Typefaces

Three type systems, each drawn as the real app (Original, by day) with the same words, so only the letters change. Today the app's buttons and menus use your Mac's own font, which is why it can look like Notes or Mail. The website you liked already uses Literata with Atkinson.

1, the safe one

Today, refined

Literata for reading, your device's own font for the interface (San Francisco on a Mac), the wordmark in Literata.

What changes

On a Mac, nothing. Elsewhere, Atkinson replaces Inter as the fallback.

Cost

About 110 KB lighter (Inter goes).

The catch

The app still looks like a different app on every device, and like none of them is nine turtles.

2, the bold one

One family, drawn by hand

Alegreya and Alegreya Sans, a serif and a sans drawn as one family from a broad-nib pen. The wordmark in Alegreya italic.

What changes

Everything: reading, interface and wordmark. The most "made by a person".

Cost

About 60 to 80 KB heavier, and the reading size must rise to about 21.5 pixels to read as well.

The catch

Its letters are smaller for their size, and its lively texture can tire over long essays.

3, my pick

Literata and Atkinson, everywhere

Literata for everything someone wrote. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for everything you press, on every device, plus its matching Mono for code. The wordmark stays in Literata: one serif word beside a sans interface.

What changes

The app finally matches the website, and stops looking like a Mac utility. Reading text doesn't change.

Cost

About 60 to 100 KB lighter overall. Macs now download one small font they used to get free.

The catch

Atkinson is a little wider than San Francisco, so long names cut off a little sooner in the sidebar.
