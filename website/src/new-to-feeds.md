## What's a feed?

Most blogs, news sites, magazines and podcasts publish a **feed**: a small,
plain file that lists their newest writing. It's been part of the web for
more than twenty years. You may have seen it called **RSS**, **Atom** or
**JSON Feed**; they're three versions of the same idea, and nine turtles
reads all three.

A **feed reader** checks the feeds you choose and gathers the new writing in
one place. Nobody decides what you see but you. There's no algorithm, and
nothing gets pushed to the top because it's loud.

## Why read this way?

- **You choose your sources.** No recommendations, no "you might also like."
- **You see everything they publish,** in the order they published it,
  not a selection picked to keep you scrolling.
- **It ends.** When you've read what's new, you're done.
- **No account with each site.** Following a blog doesn't need a sign-up,
  an email address or an app of its own.

## Where to find feeds

You usually don't need to find them at all. In nine turtles, paste the
address of a website you like, such as a blog's home page, and it finds the
feed for you.

If you'd like to look for one yourself:

- Look for a small **RSS** or **Feed** link, or the square feed symbol
  with its quarter circles, often at the very bottom of a page.
- Many sites have their feed at their address followed by `/feed`, `/rss`
  or `/atom.xml`.
- Newsletters on Substack, Ghost and similar services usually have one too.
- YouTube channels have feeds: paste a channel's address.

## Some places to start

The web is full of personal sites worth following. A few ways to find them:

- The blogs of writers, scientists, designers and makers you already
  admire, who often link to the people *they* read.
- A site's "blogroll": a list of other sites its author recommends.
- Your current reader, if you have one: most can export everything you
  follow as an **OPML** file, which nine turtles can import in one go.

## Starting in nine turtles

1. Press **Add feed** and paste a website's address.
2. If the site offers more than one feed (say, articles and comments),
   choose the one you want.
3. Its newest articles appear in your list. Read, star the ones worth
   keeping, and close it when you're done.

nine turtles checks your feeds on its own from then on, politely, and new
articles wait for you until you next open it.
