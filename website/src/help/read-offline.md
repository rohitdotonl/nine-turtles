<p class="lede">
  There's nothing to set up. Each device you use keeps a copy of your newest
  unread and saved articles, ready for when the connection goes.
</p>

## What's kept

- The first 200 articles in your Unread (or in All articles, if you've switched
  <a href="/help/unread-on-or-off/">Unread off</a>).
- Your newest 200 saved articles.

Only the words are kept. Pictures, videos and podcast episodes show a line
saying they'll appear when you're back online.

The copies are brought up to date whenever you open the app, when the
connection comes back, and when you refresh. They also make reading quicker on
a slow connection: a kept article without pictures opens straight from your
device.

## Reading offline

Just open nine turtles. The list says **You're offline. Showing the articles
kept on this device.** You can read, mark articles read or unread, save them and
undo, as usual. Everything you do is sent as soon as you're back online.

If your device is online but nine turtles doesn't answer at all (an outage,
say), it's the same, but the list says **nine turtles
can't be reached right now.** instead: your own connection is fine. If nothing
is kept on the device yet, the page says so with **Try again**, and opens by
itself once nine turtles is back.

On a very slow connection, nine turtles doesn't keep you waiting either: if it
hasn't heard back within a few seconds of opening, it shows the kept articles,
and the list says **The connection is slow.** The newest articles come in by
themselves once the answer arrives.

## What needs a connection

Search, adding someone new, Settings, refreshing and the Notes list.

## Good to know

- On an iPhone or iPad, <a href="/help/install-the-app/">add nine turtles to
  your Home Screen</a> first. Safari can clear what a website keeps if you
  haven't visited for about a week; the installed app keeps it.
- There's nothing to switch on: every device keeps copies by itself. On a
  computer other people use, sign out when you're done: that deletes the
  copies.
