<p class="lede">
  Make reading comfortable for your eyes. Every change shows at once, and
  you can change it again any time.
</p>

## While you read

Open any article and press **Aa** at its top. The panel holds everything on
this page: text size, the page and the font. To change
only the size, press <kbd>+</kbd> or <kbd>-</kbd>.

- **Text size:** six steps, from Smaller to Largest.
- **Font:** **Literata**, **Atkinson** (made to be easy to read), or your
  device's own serif.

## In Settings

Open **Settings**. Under **Look and feel**, the one thing that changes the
whole app, not just the article:

- **Page:** the page you read on. Each choice shows a small square of that
  page, so you can see it before you pick it.
  - **White:** a plain white page.
  - **Paper:** a warm cream page, easier on some eyes.
  - **Night:** a near-black page with soft white words.
  - **Match device** (where you start): White by day and Night by night,
    following your device's own setting.

Text size and font are only in **Aa**, next to the text they change.

## Everything larger

To make the whole app larger (the lists, buttons and Settings, not only
the article), use your device's own setting:

- **iPhone or iPad:** Settings › Display & Brightness › **Text Size** (or
  Accessibility › Display & Text Size › **Larger Text**). nine turtles
  follows it, as Mail and Messages do, the next time you open it.
- **Android:** Settings › Display › **Font size**. Your browser grows the
  whole page with it.
- **Computer:** zoom the browser with <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>+</kbd> on a Mac or
  <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>+</kbd> on Windows.

Aa's text size still works on top, for the article alone.

## Good to know

- These are kept on each device, so your phone and your computer can differ.
- Each page has one color, and it marks only what's yours to look at: the
  dot beside an unread article, and what a search found. It's blue on White
  and Night, and brown on Paper.
- Every article has the same comfortable line width, about 71 letters
  across. A larger text size makes the lines shorter by itself.
- Larger text sizes get a little less room between lines, as a printed
  book's larger type does, so the page never looks loose.
