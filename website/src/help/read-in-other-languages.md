<p class="lede">
  Follow people who write in Hindi, Arabic, Hebrew, Chinese, Japanese or any
  other language. There's nothing to set up: nine turtles works out what each
  article is written in, and sets it to match.
</p>

## What nine turtles does for you

- **It knows the language.** Many blogs don't say what language they're in,
  and some say the wrong one. nine turtles looks at the letters themselves,
  so your screen reader reads each article in the right voice, and your
  device draws the right letter shapes.
- **Right to left reads right to left.** Arabic, Persian, Urdu and Hebrew
  articles start on the right, title and all, with their lists and quotations
  on the right side too.
- **Each script gets room to breathe.** Hindi and other Devanagari writing,
  Arabic, Hebrew, Chinese, Japanese and Korean get more space between lines than English, so
  their marks above and below the letters never crowd. Nothing is slanted
  into a fake italic where a script has none.
- **A book face for Hindi and Arabic.** Hindi and other Devanagari writing,
  and Arabic, Persian and Urdu, are set in reading faces made to sit beside
  the one English articles use. Other scripts use your device's own fonts.
- **Names stay whole.** A writer's name or a title in another script keeps its
  own direction in the list, the sidebar and under the title.

You can also type in any language: notes, searches and folder names start
in the direction of what you type.

## Good to know

- nine turtles' own words (buttons, dates, Settings) are in English.
- Articles are never translated: you see what the writer wrote. Your browser
  can translate a page if you'd like.
- <a href="/help/search-everything/">Search</a> understands English, other
  languages written in the Latin alphabet, and Hindi. Chinese and Japanese
  aren't searchable yet.
