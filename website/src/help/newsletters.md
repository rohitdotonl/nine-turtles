<p class="lede">
  nine turtles gives you a private email address. Sign up to newsletters
  with it, and each one arrives here, read like any other article, away from
  your inbox.
</p>

## Get your address

1. Open **Settings › Newsletters**.
2. Press **Copy** beside **Your address**. It looks like
   <code>turtle-7k2xab3c@in.nineturtles.app</code>.
3. Paste it into a newsletter's sign-up form, as you would your own email.

When the first email arrives, the newsletter appears in your sidebar, named
after whoever sent it.

## Keep track of who has your address

Add a word after a plus sign, like
<code>turtle-7k2xab3c+cooking@in.nineturtles.app</code>. Email still reaches
you, and after the first one arrives, **Settings › Newsletters** lists the
word with a switch. Turn it off, and email to that word is turned away while
the rest keeps arriving.

A few sign-up forms don't accept a plus sign; use the plain address there.

## Stop a newsletter

Unfollow it as you would anyone: open its page and press
**Unsubscribe from …** at its foot. Its emails stop arriving at once. Once
it can no longer be brought back (30 days), nine turtles also tells the
sender to stop, where the newsletter allows it. If it
doesn't, use the unsubscribe link at the bottom of one of its emails.

## If spam starts arriving

Press **New address**. The old address stops working at once. **Undo**
brings it back for an hour, if you change your mind.

## Good to know

- Only email sent to this address arrives. nine turtles never sees your own
  inbox.
- Some newsletters count who opens them using hidden pictures. nine turtles
  "opens" each email once, as it arrives, so the writer sees it was received.
- Up to 200 emails a day can arrive at one address.
