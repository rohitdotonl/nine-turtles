## Is it a social network?

No. There are no profiles, followers, likes or comments, and no way to see
what other people read. It's just you and the writing, and, when you want
to, an email from you to the person who wrote it
([how](/help/write-back/)).

## Will it recommend things to read?

No. You choose every source. There's no "trending", no "for you" and no
algorithm deciding what you see first: articles arrive in the order they
were published.

## Does it send notifications?

No. nine turtles doesn't try to bring you back. New articles wait quietly
for the next time you open it. The one exception is a
[weekly email](/help/weekly-email/), if you choose it in Settings: plain
text, and off in one press.

## Can I bring my feeds from another reader?

Yes. Almost every feed reader can export what you follow as an OPML file,
and nine turtles imports it in one go, folders and all
([how](/help/bring-your-feeds/)). It exports the same way, so you can
always leave.

## Can I read it in Reeder or NetNewsWire?

Not any more. Until November 2026, other feed reader apps could sync with
nine turtles, but reading there skipped the part that matters most here:
the writer's page, your notes, and writing back. To take everyone you
follow to another app, **Settings › Your data › Download everything**
includes your list as follows.opml, a file any feed reader opens. If you miss syncing, write to
hello@nineturtles.app.

## Why do some articles show only a summary?

Some publishers put only the first paragraph in their feed, so readers
visit their site for the rest. nine turtles respects that: it shows what
the feed shares and links to the full article. It never copies the rest
from their website. When the feed marks its text as a summary, or the text
ends in "…" or a "Read more" link, the article ends with "Only the
beginning is shared here." and a link to read the rest on their site;
otherwise it's the whole article.

## Will it slow down or burden the sites I follow?

No. nine turtles checks each feed on its own rhythm: often for sites that
publish a lot, rarely for quiet ones, never more than once an hour. It
tells each site what it already has, so an unchanged feed costs them
almost nothing, and when a site asks it to slow down, it does. Sites that
announce new posts the moment they publish are heard within minutes.

## Can I read newsletters, podcasts and videos?

Yes. nine turtles gives you a private email address for newsletters, so
each one arrives here instead of your inbox
([how](/help/newsletters/)). Podcast episodes play right in nine turtles,
from the podcast's own address ([how](/help/listen-to-podcasts/)), and
YouTube videos play in YouTube's own player
([how](/help/watch-youtube-videos/)).

## Does it work on my phone?

Yes, in any modern browser, and you can add it to your home screen, where
it opens like an app of its own ([how](/help/install-the-app/)). There's
nothing to download from an app store.

## Can everyone read here?

Every screen meets the WCAG AA standard for contrast (and the stricter AAA
for article text), works with a keyboard alone and with a screen reader,
and follows your device's text size, light or dark and reduced motion
settings. More on the [accessibility page](/accessibility/).

## Is my reading private?

Yes. nine turtles shows no ads, tracks nothing, and never shares or sells
what you read. The [privacy policy](/privacy/) has the details.
