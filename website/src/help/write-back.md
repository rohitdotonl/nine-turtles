<p class="lede">
  Liked something? Tell the person who wrote it. At the end of each article
  from someone you follow is an outlined button to write back to them, by email.
</p>

Write back is for people. A publication, such as a magazine, has no Write
back button; if you'd like to write to one, open its page and, in its
settings, choose **This is: A person**. When nine turtles couldn't tell
whether someone is a person or a publication, the end of their articles
asks once instead: "Is … someone you might write back to?" Press **Yes, a
person**, and Write back takes its place.

## Write back

1. Read to the end of the article and press **Write back** (under it,
   nine turtles says who it goes to: their name as it is in your sidebar). You don't have to scroll there:
   the paper plane beside **Star** at the top of the article (on a phone,
   in the bar at the bottom) does the same, and so does <kbd>W</kbd>. In a
   list, right-click an article (or press and hold it on a phone) and
   choose **Write back**.
2. Your own email app opens with their address, and a subject line of
   "Re:" and the article's title (for a short post with no title, its
   first words). nine turtles says "Opening your email app…" under the
   button, or at the bottom of the screen if that button is out of sight
   (when you pressed the paper plane or <kbd>W</kbd>, say). If nothing
   opens, or the wrong app does, see below.
3. Write what you'd like to say, and send it as you send any email.

## If you use Gmail or Outlook.com in your browser

On a computer, your email may live in a browser tab rather than an email
app. Then pressing Write back opens nothing, or an app you don't use. Under
the button, beside "Opening your email app…", press **Didn't open?**, then
**Write in Gmail** (or **Write in Outlook.com**): the same email opens as a
new draft there, in a new tab. nine turtles remembers this on that
computer, so from then on Write back goes straight to Gmail. To go back,
press **Use your email app** under the button.

If neither works for you, press **Didn't open?**, then **Copy their
address**, and paste it into a new email. On a phone, Copy their address is
already there under the button.

## Write back about a line

Something they wrote stayed with you? Choose those words in the article.
The small menu beside them ends with **Write back about this**. Press it,
and your email starts with their words, in quotation marks, so they know
what you're answering.

If you've highlighted words in the article, **Write back** starts your
email with them too, and says so under the button: "Your email starts with
the words you highlighted."

## When nine turtles doesn't know their address

Many writers put their email address in their feed, or a "Reply by email"
link at the end of each post, and nine turtles finds it there. When it
can't, it looks once on their website, on its home page and its contact or
about page, while the box says "Looking on their site…".

- **If it finds an address,** it fills it in for you. Check it's theirs,
  then press **Write back**.
- **If it doesn't,** press **look on their site** to open their website
  in a new tab, copy the address, and paste it into **Their email address**.
  Then press **Write back**.
- **Not now** (or <kbd>Esc</kbd>) puts the box away.

nine turtles remembers it for that person, so you're asked only once. Their
page shows it under **Write to Tomas Berg**, which opens your email app to
write to them about anything, with no subject. To change it, press
**Change** beside the address.

## Good to know

- nine turtles never sends the email or sees it. It's between you and them,
  from your own email account.
- nine turtles visits a writer's site for their address only when you press
  Write back, and only once for each writer. It never visits their
  articles' pages.
- Addresses you type are yours alone: nobody else who reads that person sees
  them.
- For a newsletter, the reply goes to the address its emails come from.
