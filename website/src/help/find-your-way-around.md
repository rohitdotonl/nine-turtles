On an iPhone, tap the top of the screen (where the clock is) to go
back to the top of the list or article you're in.

## The sidebar

From the top:

- **Find a person or folder**: type a few letters of a name to narrow the
  sidebar. Under what it finds, **Search articles for “…”** (or
  <kbd>Enter</kbd>) looks for the same words in every article instead.
  <kbd>Esc</kbd> empties it.
- **Unread**, **Starred**, **Notes** and **All articles**.
- **Everyone you follow**: one page with everyone on it, for looking after
  them. Who isn't working comes first, then everyone A to Z.
- **Folders**, then **People** and **Publications**: everyone who isn't in
  a folder, A to Z. Someone with a blog and a channel has one row.
- At the foot: **+ Add**, **Keys** and **Settings**.

A small dot beside **Unread** means something new has arrived. The people
and folders below it carry no dots and no numbers: you never have a pile to
clear, and nothing calls to you from every row.

## The list of articles

Choose anything in the sidebar and its articles appear, newest first. Unread
articles are in bolder type with a dot beside them. A post with no title,
such as a short note, shows its first words instead, in plain type, up to
two lines. Under each title are who wrote it and when: "Today",
"Yesterday" or the day of the week for the past week, then the date,
written the way your device writes dates ("20 Oct" or "Oct 20"). Rest
the pointer on it to see the exact day and time. A star means you starred
it, headphones mean a podcast episode, and a small screen means a video.
Older articles load as you reach the bottom, and every list ends with
"That's everything."

**Unread** is arranged by person instead: each person's name once, with
what's new from them under it, and each article's day beside its title
(under it, when the title needs the room).
The people who posted most recently come first; publications follow, under
**Publications**. Someone with a blog and a podcast is one name. Sorted
oldest first, the people who have waited longest come first. <kbd>J</kbd>
and <kbd>K</kbd> still go from article to article, past the names, and with
a screen reader you can jump from name to name by heading.

The list's **More** menu (the three dots) holds what you can do to the whole
list: mark it all as read, refresh it, or sort it oldest first. In a
person's or publication's list it also has **Info about …**, their page, which
opens too when you press their name at the top of the list. Each article
has a menu of its own too: right-click it (on a phone, press and hold it)
to star it, mark it read or unread, write back, see the writer's page,
open it in a new tab or copy its link (on a phone, share it).
While the menu is open, the article it belongs to stays shaded, so you can
see which one you're about to change.

Each open article has its own address, so you can reload, bookmark it, or
Command-click (Ctrl-click on Windows) an article to open it in a new tab,
as you would a link.

## Reading

Choose an article and it opens on the right. Across its top are **Open
original** (the publisher's own page), **Aa** (how the text looks), **Star**,
**Write back** (a paper plane, for a person) and **More**. The writer's name
under the title opens their page. On a phone they're in the bar at the bottom: More, Aa, Write back
and Star on the left, then Previous and Next on the right, where your thumb rests. A long article opens where you stopped last time, and until you finish it, its row in the list says how far you got, such as "Half read".

A footnote's number opens its note in a small card just under the line, so
you don't lose your place; <kbd>Esc</kbd>, × or a press anywhere else closes
it. In an English article, the straight quotes, double hyphens and three dots
that many feeds send are shown as curly quotes, dashes and ellipses, as in a
printed book. The writer's words are never changed, and code stays exactly as
written.

To print an article, open it and use your browser's Print (<kbd>Command</kbd>
<kbd>P</kbd>, or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd> on Windows). Only the article
prints, in black on white, and the last line says where it came from, with
the address of the original.

When you come back with nothing open, the reading pane offers the article
you last left part of the way through, in one quiet line: "You were reading
*The long walk home*, by Ana." Press the title to carry on where you
stopped, on any device you sign in on (on a phone, the line is at the top of
the list). It goes once you open something else or finish it.

Otherwise the reading pane rests on one quiet line until you choose
something. Every list opens the same way: the newest article is simply the
first in the list, with its dot if it's unread. Nothing is marked read until
you open it.

## New articles wait for you

The list never changes while you look at it. When new articles have arrived,
a note at the bottom says **New articles**. Press <kbd>R</kbd>, or **Show**
on a phone, to bring them in. If you refresh and nothing has come in, the
note says so: "Nothing new has arrived since 14:05." Opening the app always shows the latest. On a
phone, Unread's dot catches up each time you come back to the sidebar.

## Make room to read

On a computer, the small button at the top of the sidebar hides it. For
full-screen reading, press <kbd>F</kbd>, or choose **Full-screen reading**
in an article's **More** menu: the sidebar and the list hide together, and
one button brings them back. You can also
drag the edge between two parts to make one wider; double-click the edge to
put it back.

On a computer, an article's buttons (View original, Aa, Star and the rest)
sit above its title and scroll away with it, so the whole pane is the
writing. Most have a key that works all the way down, and at the end of the article
**Star** and **View original** are there again.

On a phone, tap the words of an article once to put its top and bottom bars
away, so only the writing shows. Tap again, or read to the end, and they're
back.

## Good to know

- On a smaller window, the parts take turns: <kbd>Esc</kbd> goes back one.
- People and publications are in A to Z order. Folders are in your own
  order: drag one up or down, or use **Edit** beside **Folders**
  ([Put people in folders](/help/put-people-in-folders/)).
