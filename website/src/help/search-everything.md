  Search looks through every article nine turtles has for you, read or not,
  titles and text alike.
</p>

## Search

1. Press <kbd>/</kbd>, or choose the **Search** field above the list.
2. Type a few words. Matching articles appear, with the words picked out.

Search starts in the list you're in, shown as a small label such as
**in: Recipes ×**. Press its × to search everything instead.
<kbd>Esc</kbd> takes you back to where you were.

## How words match

- Every word you type must be in the article.
- A word also finds longer words that start with it: "walk" finds "walking".
- Words in "quotation marks" must appear together, in that order.
- Articles whose titles match come first.
- Your highlights and notes are searched too: a result found by them says
  "Highlighted" or "Your note".

## Narrow it down

Press **Search options** (the sliders beside the field) to choose where to
look, to show only unread or starred articles, or to pick a time: the past
week, the past month, or dates of your own.

Or type a filter with your words:

| Type | To find |
|---|---|
| <code>is:unread</code> | only unread articles |
| <code>is:starred</code> | only starred articles |
| <code>is:noted</code> | only articles you highlighted or wrote a note on |
| <code>feed:alpha</code> | only articles from one person or site |
| <code>folder:cooking</code> | only articles in one folder |
| <code>in:recipes</code> | only articles in one collection |
| <code>after:2026-01-31</code> | articles from after a day |
| <code>before:2026-03-01</code> | articles from before a day |

Put a name with spaces in quotation marks: <code>folder:"long reads"</code>.

## Keep a search in the sidebar

While searching, open the list's **More** menu and choose **Save search…**
It appears under **Saved searches** in the sidebar, always up to date. To
rename or delete it, open it and choose **More › Edit saved search…**

## Good to know

- Search needs a connection.
- **Mark all as read** works on what a search found.
- Search understands English, other languages written in the Latin alphabet,
  and Hindi.
