There's more good writing on the open web than ever: people writing on
their own sites, about the things they know and love, for anyone who cares
to read. Most of it reaches us, if it reaches us at all, through apps
designed to keep us scrolling, between the outrage and the ads.

nine turtles is a different way in. It's a feed reader: you tell it which
blogs and sites you'd like to follow, it checks their feeds (the small
files almost every site publishes, listing its newest writing), and it
gathers what's new in one calm place.

## Built to end

Social media is infinite, urgent and attention-grabbing. nine turtles is
meant to be the opposite: finite, calm and quiet.

- **It ends.** Your unread list is a list, not a stream. When you've read
  it, you're done, and it says so.
- **It waits.** New articles don't push into view while you read. They
  wait until you ask for them.
- **It doesn't call you back.** No notifications, no badges, no streaks,
  no "you missed this".

One question settles every choice we make, from the size of a margin to
whether a feature belongs at all: *does this make nine turtles feel more
like a book, or more like a feed?*

## Made for reading

The article is the point. It's set in Literata, a typeface designed for
long reading on screens, at a comfortable line length, by day or by night.
You can change the size, the font and the width, and that's about all
there is around the text. The look takes its lead from Apple Books, which
has spent years getting this right.

## Where it stands

The reader is finished and in daily use, on computers and phones. Behind
it are a written philosophy and a long list of automatic checks that every
change must pass (contrast, keyboard use, screen readers).

A version you can simply sign up for is planned. Until then, [the tour](/tour/) shows
what it does, and [this blog](/blog/) will say when that changes. It has a
feed, naturally.
