nine turtles was planned on 22 October 2026, under the working name
slowfeed. Everything since has happened in public, in small steps, each
one checked before it went live. Here's how the first four days went.

## 22 October: a plan, and the rules first

Before any code, the rules were written down: what nine turtles is and
isn't, the two things it would never trade away (everyone can read it;
publishers are respected), and a short, ranked list of principles to
settle every argument. Then came the skeleton, with its automatic checks
already in place.

## 23 October: a working reader

Signing in, adding feeds, folders, reading, stars, marking read with undo,
importing and exporting OPML, search, keyboard shortcuts, and phones with
an app you can add to your home screen. By the evening, version 1 was
complete.

## 24 October: closer to a book

A long day of making it calmer and clearer, guided by Apple Books: one
search field instead of two, quieter titles, sheets that open where your
eye already is, panes you can hide and resize. And a new name, nine
turtles, everywhere.

## 25 October: a home of its own

The second version began: a **river** mode for reading without counts,
instant updates from publishers that offer them, a **feed health** page
for feeds that stop working, YouTube videos in YouTube's own player,
syncing with Reeder and NetNewsWire, a second theme called Plain, and a
logo of nine small turtles. nine turtles moved to its own address,
nineturtles.app, and this website went up.

## What's next

The biggest piece of work is still ahead, and still being planned:
making nine turtles something you can simply sign up for, safely, with your reading kept entirely
separate from everyone else's. That's a careful job, and it'll be done
carefully. This blog will follow along.
