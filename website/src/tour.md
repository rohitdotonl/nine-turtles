<p class="lede">
  nine turtles has three parts, side by side on a computer and one at a time
  on a phone: your feeds, a list of articles, and the article itself.
  Everything else stays out of the way until you need it.
</p>

## Reading

The article is the point, so it gets the most care. Text is set in
Literata, a typeface made for reading on screens, at a comfortable line
length, with the publisher's name and the author in a quiet line under the
title, as in a magazine.

- **Your page, your way.** Choose the text size (six steps), the font
  (Literata, Atkinson Hyperlegible, made for low vision, or your device's
  own serif) and the line width. The **Aa** button beside every article
  (in the bar above it on a computer, below it on a phone) holds them,
  one press from the text.
- **Two themes, by day and by night.** Original, white paper with ink-blue
  marks for what's unread, or Plain, black on white with links in the
  browser's own blue. Each follows your device's light or dark setting, or
  stays the way you choose.
- **Videos play in the publisher's player.** A YouTube video plays in
  YouTube's own player, so the views stay theirs.
- **Short feeds stay short.** If a site's feed shares only a summary,
  that's what you see, with a link to read the rest on their site. nine
  turtles never fetches more than the publisher chose to share.

## Two ways to read

- **Inbox**, the usual way: things to get through. Read articles turn grey,
  feeds show how many are unread, and "Unread" is where you start.
- **River**, for when you'd rather let things flow past: no counts, no
  unread list, every title alike. nine turtles still remembers what you've
  read, in case you switch back.

Either way, when you've read everything, the list says so and stops.

## Finding things

- **Search everything you've received**, in titles and full text, in
  English, other languages written in the Latin alphabet, and Hindi.
  Matching words are marked in the results.
- **Narrow it down** to a folder, a feed, unread or starred, or a range of
  dates, from a small menu, or by typing filters such as `feed:`,
  `is:starred` or `after:2026-10-01` if you like.
- **Folders** keep feeds in order, and a feed can live in more than one.

## Adding feeds

Paste the address of any website you like. nine turtles looks at that one
page for its feed, and if the site offers several, lets you choose. You can
also paste a feed's own address, or bring all your feeds at once from
another reader as an OPML file. ([What's a feed?](/new-to-feeds/))

## Keeping up with publishers, politely

nine turtles checks each feed on its own rhythm: often for sites that
publish a lot, rarely for quiet ones, never more than every 30 minutes. It
tells each site what it already has, so an unchanged feed costs them almost
nothing, and when a site asks it to slow down, it does. Sites that announce
new posts the moment they publish (through a standard called WebSub) are
heard within minutes.

When a feed stops working, it doesn't nag you. A **Feed health** page in
Settings lists the ones that are having trouble, says what's wrong in plain
words, and can look for a site's new feed address.

On a phone, you move through one screen at a time: feeds, then articles,
then the article. Add nine turtles to your home screen and it opens like an
app of its own. Sharing an article shares the publisher's link, never ours.

## With the keyboard

Every action works with Tab, Enter and Escape. On top of that, single keys
move you through your reading: `j` and `k` for the next and previous
article, `s` to star, `m` to mark unread, `v` to open the original, `/` to
search. Press `?` at any time to see them all.

## With the apps you already use

If you like reading in Reeder or NetNewsWire, they can use nine turtles to
keep your feeds, folders, read articles and stars in step, through the same
"Google Reader" connection that FreshRSS and Miniflux offer.

## Your data stays yours

- **Export your feeds** as an OPML file, the standard every feed reader
  understands, whenever you like.
- **Export your starred articles**, with their text and links, as a JSON
  Feed file.
- **Undo** follows anything that removes something, straight away.

## Everyone can read here

Every screen meets the WCAG AA standard for contrast (and the stricter AAA
for article text), works with a keyboard alone and with a screen reader,
and follows your device's text size, light or dark and reduced motion
settings. More on the [accessibility page](/accessibility/).
