# nine turtles V1: what it does

*Agreed with the owner; agents build from it (decision 0014). Based on `docs/DESIGN.md`, which takes
precedence: if this document and DESIGN.md disagree, DESIGN.md is right and
this document has a bug.*

Each feature has two parts:
- **What it does:** the behavior, in plain language.
- **How to check it:** steps you can follow yourself in the app.

The owner's answers to the planning questions (`docs/planning/questions.md`)
are included.

A few words used throughout:
- **Feed:** the file a website publishes listing its latest writing (RSS, Atom or JSON Feed).
- **Item / article:** one piece of writing from a feed.
- **Pane:** one of the side-by-side columns on screen (feeds, items, article).
- **Vim keys:** single-letter keyboard shortcuts such as `j` and `k`, named after a classic text editor.

## 1. Sign-in

**What it does**
- nine turtles has a public web address, so it asks who you are: an **email and a password** (decision 0087). Each reader has their own.
- **The owner** (the person who set nine turtles up) signs in with their email and password like everyone else; the password alone signs nobody in (decision 0116). Leaving the email empty and pressing Sign in sends nothing: "Type your email too.", and the keyboard moves to the email field. On a brand-new nine turtles the owner has no email yet: their first sign-in, with an email nobody has and the owner's password, signs them in and saves that email as theirs (they can change it later in Settings › Readers).
- Each device stays signed in for a long time, so you rarely type it.
- Each device stays signed in for a year after it was last used. Signing out ends that device only; other devices stay signed in.
- Repeated wrong passwords are slowed down, so nobody can guess by trying thousands: after five wrong tries in a row, nine turtles asks you to wait a minute, and each further wrong try doubles the wait, up to an hour. The right password resets it, and so does an hour without wrong tries. **Wrong tries count for each account on its own** (each email, the owner's email too, so a stranger can't find out which email is the owner's), wherever they come from: a stranger guessing someone's email makes only that email wait, never anyone else. Once an account has to wait, further tries are refused at once, without checking the password. Checking a password takes a lot of memory, so only a few are checked at a time, in one line for everyone (a flood of guesses can slow every sign-in while it lasts, the owner's too, so the time taken never tells which email is the owner's). A password longer than 1,024 bytes is refused at once as a wrong password. Devices already signed in are never affected. Restarting nine turtles on Render clears every wait (`docs/operations/render-setup.md`).
- nine turtles **never says whether an email has an account**: a wrong password and an email nobody has get the same answer, in about the same time.
- The page shows the logo (the nine turtles, decision 0067) above the wordmark, one quiet line under it ("A quiet place to read."), the email field, the password field and **Sign in**, all in one centred column: each field says its name inside it ("Email", "Password", and the same to screen readers, which password managers recognise), 12px apart, and the button sits 12px under the password with the same rounded corners. The email field has the keyboard's place when the page opens. On a phone the column runs between the same 20px margins as every other screen; on a larger screen it is at most 320px wide (decision 0048).
- Messages are plain: "That email and password don't match." (with an email), "That isn't the password." (without one), or "Too many wrong tries. Try again in 2 minutes." They sit right under the fields, centred, in red words after a small warning mark, and move only the button down (the room for them is kept below it, so the fields and the title stay still); a refusal also marks the fields themselves (their edge turns red, and screen readers hear them as invalid).
- Sign out is in Settings.
- Passwords themselves are never stored, only a scrambled version that can't be turned back into them (a "hash"). The owner's is set by typing `nine-turtles set-password` in Render's Shell tab and pasting the scrambled line it prints into Render's settings (`docs/operations/render-setup.md`); until then, the owner can't sign in. A reader's is scrambled the same way when they sign up.

**Signing up** (decisions 0087 and 0115)
- **Sign-up is always open:** anyone can make an account, with no invite and no email sent to confirm it (decision 0115, the owner's choice; it replaced the Closed / Invite link / Open switch and the emailed sign-up link of decisions 0087 and 0088). The sign-in page always shows **Sign up** under the form.
- **The sign-up page** (`/sign-up`) is built like the sign-in page: the logo and wordmark, then **Email**, **Password** ("At least 12 characters." under it) and **Sign up**. Signing up makes the account at once, signs this device in and opens nine turtles. Signed in already, `/sign-up` opens nine turtles itself.
- Messages: "That doesn't look like an email address." (one @ with something either side, at most 254 characters, and only the address: no name before it and no spaces, `<` `>`, quotes, commas, semicolons, colons, brackets or backslashes), "Choose a password of at least 12 characters.", and "That email already has an account. Sign in instead." with a **Sign in** button.
- **At most 20 sign-ups are tried in any hour**, for the whole app (nine turtles sits behind Render's proxy, so it can't reliably tell one place from another), as a guard against bots. A try with an email that already has an account counts too, so nobody can keep trying. Sign-ups are handled one at a time. At most 20 accounts are made in any hour too. The 21st hears "Many people have signed up in the last hour. Try again later."
- **Nobody checks the address is really theirs** (the owner accepted this, decision 0115): someone could sign up with an address that isn't theirs. While nine turtles can send email, the address's real owner can take the account over with "Forgot your password?"; without email, they can't, and neither can a reader who forgets their password. Sign-up works with or without email. The owner should save their own email in Settings › Readers, so nobody else can sign up with it first.

**A sign-in link** (decision 0096)
- While nine turtles can send email, the sign-in page shows **Email me a sign-in link** under the form, on a line of its own above Forgot your password? and Sign up. It's for every reader, the owner too once they've saved an email; passwords keep working as before.
- It turns the page into one email field ("nine turtles emails you a link that signs you in." under it), **Send link** and **Back to sign in**; the email already typed stays, and the keyboard goes to it.
- The answer is said once, exactly as for a new password: **Check your email** (the keyboard goes to it) and "If … has an account here, a link to sign in is on its way. It works once, for 15 minutes.", whether or not the email has an account, then the same quiet "Didn't get it? Look in your spam folder." and **Send again** (nothing sent within a minute of the last link), and Back to sign in.
- The email, "Sign in to nine turtles", holds the one link (its ticket after the `#`, like the others). It opens a page built like sign-in: the logo and wordmark, "Press Sign in to read on this device." and **Sign in** (one press, so a mail program checking links can't use it up). Pressing it signs this device in and opens nine turtles; nothing else changes: the password stays, other devices stay signed in. A link used before, past its 15 minutes, sent to an address the reader has since changed, or from before a new password, says "This link has expired or was already used. Ask for a new one from the sign-in page.", with **Sign in**.
- It counts towards the same limits as a password link: at most three emails to one address in any hour (whatever they're for), and one sign-in link a minute. Without email, it isn't shown; asking anyway says "This nine turtles can't send email yet. Sign in with your password."

**A passkey** (decision 0109)
- A passkey signs you in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN instead of a password. You add one in Settings › Passkeys (section 18); passwords keep working exactly as before, for every reader.
- Where passkeys work, the sign-in page shows **Sign in with a passkey** on a line of its own under the form, above Email me a sign-in link, in the same quiet words. It opens the browser's own passkey sheet; choosing your passkey signs this device in and opens nine turtles, with no email or password typed. The email field also offers your passkeys among its suggestions, as browsers do for saved passwords (passkey autofill), where the browser can.
- Cancelling the browser's sheet says nothing. A passkey nine turtles doesn't know (one removed in Settings, or made for another nine turtles) says "That passkey isn't one this nine turtles knows. Sign in with your password."; a passkey chosen after the page's challenge ran out (five minutes) says "That took too long. Try again."
- Passkeys belong to nine turtles' public address (app.nineturtles.app). Anywhere else (slowfeed.onrender.com), and in a browser without passkeys, nothing about them is shown and the page is as before.
- Signing in with a passkey is exactly like signing in with a password afterwards: the device stays signed in for a year after it was last used. A passkey can't be guessed, so a refused one makes nobody wait. No email is sent.

**A new password** (decision 0088)
- While nine turtles can send email, the sign-in page shows **Forgot your password?** under the form (beside Sign up). It turns the page into one email field ("nine turtles emails you a link to choose a new password." under it), **Send link** and **Back to sign in**; the email already typed stays, and the keyboard goes to it.
- The answer is always the same, said once (decision 0092): the email field, its line and Send link give way to **Check your email** (the keyboard goes to it) and "If … has an account here, a link to choose a new password is on its way. It works for an hour.", in the text color. nine turtles never says whether the email has an account. Under it, quietly: "Didn't get it? Look in your spam folder." and **Send again**. Pressed within a minute of the last link it sends nothing and says "The link went less than a minute ago. Give it a minute, then send again if it hasn't come."; after that it says "Another link is on its way." Nothing on the page changes by itself. **Back to sign in** returns to the sign-in form with the email kept.
- The link (its ticket after the `#`, so it never reaches a log; the page takes it out of the address bar at once) opens a page built like sign-in: **New password** ("At least 12 characters. Your other devices will be signed out." under it) and **Set password**. It works once, within the hour, and only while the account still has the address it was sent to (as with a sign-in link). Setting it signs out every other device signed in as that reader, turns off their app password for other feed reader apps (Settings › Other apps can make a new one), and signs this device in. Passkeys stay (the owner's choice, decision 0109): if you fear someone else got in, remove any passkey you don't recognise in Settings › Passkeys. A link that has run out says "This link has expired or was already used. Ask for a new one from the sign-in page.", with **Sign in**.
- The owner can use it once they've saved an email; the new password then replaces the one in Render's settings, with or without the email, for as long as that setting stays as it is. **The owner's way back that needs no email:** putting a new `NINE_TURTLES_PASSWORD_HASH` in Render makes it the owner's password again when nine turtles restarts; the one set by email stops working, every device signed in as the owner is signed out, and any password or sign-in link not yet used stops working (`docs/operations/owner-password.md`). Passkeys stay; remove any you don't recognise in Settings › Passkeys.
- "Forgot your password?" takes as long whether or not the email has an account.
- **At most three emails go to one address in any hour**, whatever they're for, **and one link of each kind (password, sign-in, change of email) a minute**, so pressing Send link twice sends one email. Past either, the answer stays the same and nothing is sent, so nine turtles can't fill anyone's inbox. Limits count each address wherever the requests come from: nine turtles sits behind Render's proxy, so it can't reliably tell one place from another.
- A new password also ends any sign-in link not yet used.
- Without email (the settings aren't set), Forgot your password? isn't shown; asking anyway says "This nine turtles can't send email yet. Ask whoever runs it to set a new password for you."
- Emails are short plain text, signed "nine turtles": no pictures, no tracking, and no link but the one asked for.

**How to check it**
1. Open the app's address in a private browser window. You see only the email and password fields, nothing about your feeds.
2. Type your email and a wrong password several times quickly. After a few tries it asks you to wait before trying again. Leave the email empty and type your password: "Type your email too."
3. In another private window, type a made-up email and a wrong password: "That email and password don't match." Do it six times: only that email has to wait; the owner can still sign in in the first window.
4. In another private window, type your email and the right password. You're in. Close the browser, reopen the address: you're still signed in.
5. In Settings › Readers, change your email. In a private window, sign in with the new email and your password; the old one no longer works.
6. In a private window, press Sign up under the sign-in form and sign up with another email: you're in, and Settings there has no Readers. Back in your Settings, press Readers, then All readers: the new email is in the list, with "0 feeds".
7. Sign out from Settings. Reload: you're back at the sign-in page.
8. Once email is set up (`docs/operations/email.md`): on the sign-in page press Forgot your password?, type your email and press Send link. Open the link in the email, choose a new password: you're in, and another device you were signed in on asks you to sign in again.
9. In Settings › Readers, type a different email in Your email and press Save without your password: it asks for it, and your email stays as it was.
10. Sign out. On the sign-in page press Email me a sign-in link, type your email and press Send link. Open the link in the email and press Sign in: you're in, and your password still works. Open the same link again: it says it has expired or was already used.
11. At app.nineturtles.app, add a passkey in Settings › Passkeys (section 18, step 7). Sign out. Press Sign in with a passkey and choose it: you're in, with nothing typed. Sign out again and click into the email field: your passkey is among the suggestions. Your password still signs you in too. At slowfeed.onrender.com the sign-in page has no Sign in with a passkey.

## 2. First visit (empty state)

**What it does**
- Until you add your first feed, the reading area shows a welcome, centred like a page: **"A quiet place to read."** in the reading serif, one sentence ("Follow the websites you like, and read what they publish here, at your own pace."), under it a quiet link, **"New to feeds? Start here"** (underlined, in the text color, 44px tall; decision 0079), then a wide field that says **"Paste a website or feed URL"** inside it (its label for screen readers; on a small phone, or with a larger text size, these words step down from 16px to as little as 10.5px so they're never cut off (with a larger text size the field has less room, so they can be smaller than at the usual size; the owner chose fitting over size), ending in "…" only if even that doesn't fit, while what you type stays 16px) with **Add feed** beside it, and just under the field, centred like everything else in the welcome, a link, **"Import an OPML file…"**, the other way in: underlined, in the text color, a full 44px tall to touch, its words on the same margin as the heading's (on a phone, the screen's 20px margin; decision 0055) (a message about the address, when there is one, appears between the two and moves the link down, never anything above), which goes to the import described in section 16 (Settings → Your data), all in one centred column about 420px wide. The welcome has the screen to itself: where there's a reading area beside the list, the list steps aside and the welcome takes both, one empty state rather than an empty column beside it (decision 0048); the sidebar shows no find field while there's no feed to find. Where there's no reading area beside the list (a phone, or a medium window with the sidebar shown), the welcome sits in the list's place instead. Starred keeps its own (empty) list. When **Add feed** opens its sheet over the welcome, the welcome stays exactly as it was under the dim, so nothing behind the sheet moves.
- **New to feeds?** (decision 0079): the link opens a sheet (`/new-to-feeds`), built as every sheet is (on a phone, a page sheet with its X, and the main action the full width at the foot; its X goes back to the welcome). Its title is "New to feeds?"; two sentences say what a feed is ("A feed is a website's list of its newest writing. Follow a few, and what they publish gathers here for you to read. Nobody else decides what you see."); a link, **"Here's how feeds work"**, opens the website's guide (nineturtles.app/new-to-feeds) in a tab of its own; then **"A few to start with"**: a grouped list of five feeds, each row its name and one muted line on what it publishes, ending in a tick box, none ticked. The five are fixed and the same for every reader: The Marginalian, The Public Domain Review, kottke.org, Austin Kleon and NASA Image of the Day, each by its publisher's own feed address, so adding one never looks at a web page. At the foot, **Cancel** and **Add chosen feeds**. Pressing it with nothing ticked says "Choose a feed first."; otherwise the ticked feeds are added one after another, exactly as if their addresses had been pasted, with "Adding feeds… 1 of 3 done" in plain text meanwhile. One feed added opens that feed; several open Unread (All in river mode). If none could be added the sheet says so ("Those feeds couldn't be added just now. Try again later.") and keeps them ticked; if only some could, the list's note names the others. The welcome, and so this link, is gone once you follow anything; the sheet's address then opens the home view instead. Each reader on a nine turtles with several readers sees the same welcome on their own first visit.

**How to check it**
1. On a fresh installation, sign in. Beside the sidebar there's one welcome, centred, with the field, Add feed and "Import an OPML file…"; no empty list beside it, and the sidebar has no find field. Nothing tells you to press `j`. Press `a`: the sheet opens and the welcome behind it doesn't move. Press Escape, then "New to feeds? Start here": a sheet explains feeds, links to the website's guide and lists five feeds, none ticked. Tick two and press Add chosen feeds: both appear in the sidebar and Unread opens.
2. On a phone, the welcome fills the list's screen, left-aligned on the screen's margin about 96px under the top line, and it's the one heading shown: the list's title steps aside (screen readers still hear it as the screen's name).

## 3. Adding a feed

**What it does**
- **Add feed** (a plus icon and its words) sits at the very bottom of the sidebar, always in the same place, with two buttons beside it: **Keys** (a keyboard icon and its word, so Add feed and Keys read as two buttons, not one phrase; decisions 0062, 0064; in a sidebar narrowed below 15rem, the icon alone) and **Settings** (a cog), each with its name as a tooltip. The `a` key does the same as Add feed.
- Add feed opens a sheet over the list you were on (`/add-feed`): a small card titled "Add a feed" (in the same serif as every title), a line saying nine turtles finds the feed on any website, the same field as the first visit at the card's full width, and at the foot one row, 20px under the field: **Cancel** (a faint tone, no edge), then **Add feed** at the right end. The sheet opens a little above the middle of the window and is as tall as what's in it; a message appears under the field only when there is one, and the sheet grows down by its line (its top never moves). In the first-visit welcome, which is centred on the page, the field keeps room for two lines of message so nothing moves. The list and article stay in view behind it under a light dim. Cancel, Escape, or a press on the dimmed page, closes it. Add feed is an action, not a place, so it never shows the "where you are" pill. On a phone it's an iOS page sheet, built as every phone sheet is (DESIGN.md → "One phone sheet"; decisions 0052, 0055): a 10px strip of the dimmed screen shows above it, its top corners are 12px, its ground is grouped grey (the list's tone by day, the page by night) with any group on it a card (the page's color by day, a step lighter by night), its title a step smaller (20px) in the top line, an X beside the title, no back arrow and no Cancel, and **Add feed** the full width at the screen's foot. It appears at once, with no slide. When a site offers several feeds, "This site offers more than one feed. Choose one:" is set as the list's label, 20px under the field.
- You can paste either a feed address or an ordinary website address; leaving off `https://` is fine. For a website, nine turtles looks at exactly the page you pasted to find its feed ("autodiscovery"), once, because you asked. If the page names several feeds, you see their names (at most ten) and addresses as a list, each row ending in a round mark that's filled with a dot on the chosen one (the first, to begin with); choose one with a click, a tap or the arrow keys, then press **Add feed** to add it. Change the address and Add feed looks again.
- While nine turtles looks, the field says "Looking for a feed…" in plain text. No spinner.
- If it doesn't work, a plain sentence says why, never an error code: "This site doesn't seem to offer a feed. Try the feed's own address, or the site's home page.", "That site didn't answer. Try again later.", "Nothing was found at that address.", "That doesn't look like a web address.", "nine turtles can't visit that address." (an address inside a private network or on nine turtles' own computer), "That file is too big to be a feed.", or, for a reader who already follows 1,000 feeds, "You follow 1,000 feeds, as many as one reader can. Unsubscribe from one to add another." (decision 0090; nothing is fetched then). The message sits under the field in red words after a small warning mark; when the address itself is the problem, the field's edge turns red too (the words and mark mean color is never the only sign).
- Adding a feed you already follow says "You already follow this feed." with a link to it, instead of making a copy. The same address written differently (`http` or `https`, a trailing `/` or `#part`) counts as the same feed.
- The new feed appears in the sidebar and opens, its articles straight away, all unread (including older posts the feed still lists). It's fetched once on adding; new articles arriving on their own come with Milestone 8.
- Each feed has its own address (`/feeds/3`), so reloading keeps your place. An address for a feed you don't follow says so plainly ("Feed not found").
- **Adding from anywhere** (decision 0104): `/add-feed?url=<address>` opens Add a feed with that address already in the field, and nine turtles looks at that one page straight away for the feeds it names, as if you had pasted it. It **never follows anything by itself**: what it finds is shown as the usual list ("Found this feed:" over a one-row list for one feed, "This site offers more than one feed. Choose one:" for several), and only **Add feed** follows the chosen one; Cancel, Escape or the X follows nothing. Problems are said in the same sentences as above. If the address has no `url`, the first web address (`http` or `https`) inside `text`, then `title`, is used, because share menus often put the link inside a sentence. The address bar then shows plain `/add-feed`, and the shared address is used for that one opening only. Signed out, the sign-in page comes first; after signing in, Add a feed opens with the address kept. The look uses the same lookup as the website's feed finder (section 30), so while it's busy or can't be reached nine turtles says nothing and the address simply waits in the field for Add feed. Three ways lead there:
  - **Android (and any browser that supports it):** once nine turtles is on the home screen, it appears in the system share menu ("Share → nine turtles"), through the app's "share target" (the part of its manifest that tells the phone it can take shared links).
  - **iPhone and iPad:** Safari doesn't offer home-screen apps in the share menu, so an Apple Shortcuts shortcut does it instead (steps in section 20).
  - **A computer's browser:** a bookmark that opens Add a feed with the page you're on (a "bookmarklet": a bookmark that runs a line of JavaScript). Settings › Add from anywhere shows it, built from this nine turtles' own address, with a Copy button: make a new bookmark and paste it as the bookmark's address. On app.nineturtles.app it is `javascript:location.href='https://app.nineturtles.app/add-feed?url='+encodeURIComponent(location.href)`.

**How to check it**
1. Press `a` (or click Add feed), paste a blog's home page address (not its feed), press Enter. The blog appears in the sidebar with its articles.
2. Paste a direct feed address. Same result.
3. Paste an address of a page with no feed. You get a calm, plain message, not an error code.
4. Add the same feed twice. nine turtles tells you it's already there instead of creating a duplicate.
5. Open `/add-feed?url=` followed by a blog's address. Add a feed opens with the address filled in and the feed found and chosen, but the blog isn't in the sidebar until you press Add feed. Press Cancel instead: nothing is added.
6. In Settings › Add from anywhere, copy the bookmark, make a bookmark with it, and press it on a blog: Add a feed opens with that blog's address.

## 4. Layout

**What it does**
- Three panes on a wide screen: **feeds | items | article**. Two panes on a medium screen. One column on a phone (section 20).
- No layout options to choose: the layout simply adapts to the window width. Wide means 1200 pixels or more at normal size, medium 896 or more; below that, one area shows at a time, as on a phone. On a medium screen the list and article show, and showing the sidebar puts it in the article's place. On a phone-narrow screen one area shows at a time, starting with the sidebar; choosing a place in it shows that place, and Escape goes back.
- Zooming in or a larger system text size counts as a narrower window, so the layout steps down instead of anything overlapping.
- On a computer the sidebar and the article list can each be hidden and shown, with a small sidebar-shaped button or a key: `[` for the sidebar, `]` for the list, `\` for both at once. Each button sits at the right end of its own pane's top line (the sidebar's across from the wordmark, the list's after More); with its pane hidden it waits at the start of the next area's top line. With both hidden the two become one button, which brings both back. Screen readers hear "Sidebar" and "Article list"; the tooltips say what pressing does, with the key.
- On a computer the sidebar and the list can be made wider or narrower by dragging the line where they meet the next pane (or, with the keyboard on that line, the left and right arrows); a double click puts the pane back to its usual width. The sidebar stays 200–400px wide and the list never leaves the article less than 400px. Beside the article, the list is 352px wide by default below 1280px (so on a window about 1024px wide feed names and titles have room), 384px up to 1536px and 416px beyond, and it's never narrower than 280px, however it's dragged. The widths are remembered on that device. Beside it, each area's top line (the wordmark, the list's title, the article's tools) never scrolls. All the top lines are one band of the same height (56px at normal text size), so the sidebar button, the wordmark, the list's title and More, and the article's tools sit on one line across the window, and the faint lines that appear under them once something scrolls fall at the same height. A button in a top line draws its keyboard ring just inside its own edge, so the ring never touches the top of the window.
- Unread, Starred, All, Recently read and Settings each have their own web address (`/unread`, `/starred`, `/all`, `/recent`, `/settings`), so reloading keeps your place and the browser's Back button works. The front page opens Unread. Any other address opens **"Wrong turn?"** in place of the app (decision 0102; DESIGN.md → "Name and signature"): a small "404 error", eight turtles walking one way and one turned the other, "There's nothing at this address. It may have moved, or the link may have a typing mistake in it.", "If a link in this app brought you here, tell me which one and I'll fix it." signed "— Rohit", and **Back to your reading**, which opens the front page. "Tell me which one" opens the reader's email app with the address already written in. The tab says "Page not found". A well-formed address for a feed, folder or saved search you don't have still opens the list with "Feed not found" (section 3). Signed out, such an address shows the sign-in page first, then "Wrong turn?" once you sign in. The website's own 404 page is the same, with "on this site" and no button.
- Regions are told apart by background tone and space, never by lines, shadows or gradients. Small exceptions: a faint line under a top line once something has scrolled under it, a soft shadow under the note that floats at the bottom of the list, a faint line along the top of the phone's article toolbar, and, on a computer, a faint 1px line where two panes meet (the sidebar's right edge, and the list's when the article is beside it), in the same tone as the hairlines between list rows (DESIGN.md → Visual language; decision 0054). The article's bars are solid, never see-through.
- Hiding the sidebar with `[` while the keyboard is in it moves the keyboard to the list itself (the selected article, or the first), never to the list's title; with no articles, to the sidebar button.
- Nothing animates. Views switch instantly.

**How to check it**
1. Make the browser window wide, then narrower, then phone-narrow. You see three, then two, then one column.
2. Press `[` twice, then `]` twice, then `\` twice. The sidebar, then the list, then both hide and return; with both hidden one button shows.
4. Drag the line between the sidebar and the list, then double-click it. The sidebar widens, then returns to its usual width.
3. Squint at the screen: you can still tell the three regions apart, with a faint line where the sidebar meets the list and the list meets the article.
5. Open `/nowhere`. "Wrong turn?" shows, with "404 error" and the turtles; Back to your reading opens Unread.

## 5. Sidebar

**What it does**
- Fixed order, top to bottom:
  1. **Unread · Starred · All · Recently read**
  2. **Saved searches**, alphabetically, once there is one (section 14)
  3. **Collections**, alphabetically, once there is one (section 34)
  4. **Folders**, alphabetically
  5. **Feeds not in any folder**, alphabetically
  6. **Add feed**, then **Keys** (icon and word) and **Settings** as an icon, in one row
- You can't reorder things by hand; order is always alphabetical.
- At the top of the sidebar: the nine turtles logo and the "nine turtles" wordmark (a name, not a button: Tab never stops on it; the keyboard's place starts there only on a phone's feeds screen, which has no list, and otherwise on the list's title when the app opens, so screen readers begin with what there is to read), and below it a quiet **field to find a feed or folder by name** (a search icon and its words, the same slim, faintly filled field as the list's search: 36px tall with a mouse on a wide window, 44px on a touch screen or a phone, softly rounded, with no edge), shown whenever you have a feed. Folders and feeds sit under small muted section names ("Folders", then "Feeds", on a computer and a phone alike), and every feed name is in full ink; a name in Devanagari is set a touch larger, so it sits level with the others. A long feed name ends in "…" on one line, so the sidebar stays tidy; its whole name shows as a tooltip, is read in full by screen readers, and is the list's title when you open it (one line, then "…", with the whole name as its tooltip).
- Every name in the sidebar (Unread, Starred, All and Recently read, folders, feeds, and the words in the find field) starts at one left edge, after a column that holds the icons of Unread, Starred, All and Recently read, a folder's arrow and the find field's search icon; the section names (Folders, Feeds) stand further out, at the sidebar's margin. On a computer the names start 46px in and the section names at the 20px margin; a folder's feeds start a step further in (64px), so the hierarchy shows without relying on the arrow.
- Folders appear once at least one exists. Each has a small arrow button before its name, in the icon column, as in a Mac sidebar, that shows or hides the feeds in it, listed beneath one step further in than the folder's name; folders start closed, and this device remembers which you opened. Choosing a folder's name lists the articles of all its feeds (`/folders/2`). On a phone's feeds screen there's no arrow (every row there already ends in a chevron): a folder's feeds are always listed beneath it, one step in.
- The search field filters the sidebar as you type: the folders and feeds whose name contains what you typed (capitals and accents don't matter), in one list. Escape empties it.
- **Unread counts:** small, muted grey numbers next to each feed and folder (a folder's is its feeds' added up), right-aligned at the row's end, so every count, folder or feed, sits in one column. No count on "Unread", "Starred" or "All", and never a total. Above 999 shows "999+". Never colored, never a badge. A reader who'd rather not see numbers can hide them all in Settings › Reading (**Unread counts: Hide**, section 18); Unread, the dot and read styling stay.
- Your current place is marked as in the list (section 6), a rounded pill of soft neutral grey set a little in from the sidebar's edges, with no color (decision 0057), its name semibold in full ink, and, for Unread and All, its icon in full ink (Starred's star fills in green). When your place changes, its row scrolls into view in the sidebar if it was out of sight, at once and only as far as needed, never smoothly; it doesn't move when counts change. On a phone's feeds screen nothing is marked: it's where you start, not a place you're in. Under the pointer a row gets a fainter, neutral tone, never the accent.
- While Settings is open, its button at the foot of the sidebar shows as your place, with the same pill, and screen readers hear it as the current page. The shortcut list's button, while its list is open, sits on a faint ink tile instead (section 19). The fill is never the only sign (decision 0054).
- A broken feed looks like any other feed in the sidebar; it just doesn't get new items. The feed health page (section 17, in Settings › Your library) lists the feeds that aren't working, and the daily check mentions them too.
- **Recently read** (`/recent`, decision 0105), with a small clock whose rim turns back, a history sign, as its icon (Let unread go's plain clock face is another thing): the last 50 articles you opened, the one opened last first, whatever the sort order. An article counts as opened each time the reading area shows it (or, for a feed set to open on the website, its page opens), whether that marked it read or it was read already. It's a plain list and nothing more: no count, no number read, no streak, nothing about your reading habits. Only here, quiet day headings part it by when you opened each article, by your device's calendar: **Today**, **Yesterday**, then the weekday for the rest of the last week ("Sunday"), then the date ("12 October", with the year if it isn't this one); small, semibold and muted grey, as the sidebar's section names are, and screen readers hear them as headings. They're worked out when the list loads, so they never change while you look. Its More menu has only Refresh (no Mark all as read, no sort order), it never says "New articles", and searching from it searches everything, as from All. Articles of a feed you've unsubscribed from, and muted ones, aren't listed. It's yours alone: another reader of the same feeds never sees it. It isn't kept on the device for reading offline: without a connection it says "Recently read needs a connection. It shows once you're back online.", and articles opened offline don't join it. `g` then `r` opens it (section 19). Empty, it says "Articles you open will show here."
- **Up Next** (section 35, decision 0113), with a small list icon, after Recently read, only while it holds an episode: the episodes you've queued, in your order, with no count.
- **Collections** (section 34, decision 0108): under a small **Collections** heading after the saved searches, A to Z, each with a small shelf of books where the saved searches have their search icon, and no count. Screen readers hear "(collection)" after each name. The find field doesn't look in them.
- **Hiding lists** (decisions 0105, 0108, 0113): Starred, All, Recently read, Up Next, the saved searches and the collections can each be left out of the sidebar in Settings › Show in the sidebar (section 18); all show by default. Unread, folders and feeds always show, and in river mode All does too (it's home there). A hidden list still works: its address opens it, and `g s`, `g a` and `g r` still go to Starred, All and Recently read. The choice is the reader's, on all their devices.

**How to check it**
1. Add feeds whose names start with different letters. They're in alphabetical order, under the fixed views.
2. In inbox mode, a feed with unread items shows a grey number. "Unread" itself shows no number.
3. A feed with more than 999 unread items shows "999+".
4. Open two articles, then choose Recently read: the second is first, then the first. It shows no number.
5. In Settings › Show in the sidebar, untick Recently read: it leaves the sidebar, on your other devices too. Go to `/recent`: it still opens. Tick it again.
6. Put an article on a new collection (section 34): it appears under Collections in the sidebar, with a shelf icon and no number.

## 6. Item list

**What it does**
- Each row shows the article **title** (18px with a mouse, so it clearly leads), and below it the **source name and time** in quiet small text (12px, a step fainter than muted grey, and muted grey on the selected row) (just the time in one feed's own list, whose title already names it). That line is always one line: a long source name ends in "…", with the "·" right after it at the same distance as on any other row, and the time and the star always keep their place on it, never wrapping below; its figures are proportional, as in running text (decision 0048). Unread articles have a small dot in the theme's accent in a gutter of its own before the title, clear of the words, so it reads as a mark in the margin, not a bullet; every title starts at the same place, dot or no dot, lined up with the list's title and the search field. Each row has 10px above its title and 11px below its meta line, as Apple Books spaces its lists. A faint hairline parts one row from the next, from the titles' edge to where the selection's pill ends, and none shows directly above or below the selected row or the row under the pointer. No images, no site icons.
- Dates and times are shown in your device's time zone, in words that never change while you look: "14:05" for today, "23 Oct" for this year, "23 Oct 2025" before that. The time is when the publisher says they published it, or when nine turtles received it if they don't say (and never later than that, so a wrong date can't pin an article to the top).
- Newest first by default; oldest first can be chosen in Settings or in the list's More menu.
- The list's title is one line: a long name ends in "…" (its whole name is its tooltip and is read in full by screen readers), so the search field under it never moves; in a narrow list it is set a size smaller (22px rather than 24px). Beside it sits one quiet icon, **More** (…), named for screen readers and in its tooltip, as Apple Books has one per collection. It opens a small menu with **Mark all as read** (section 8; its key, `Shift+A`, shown beside it), while an article is chosen **Mark all above as read** and **Mark all below as read** (section 8; `Shift+O` shown beside the older side), in Unread also **Catch me up** (section 8), **Refresh** (section 13; its key, `r`; not while searching), the **sort order** (Newest first or Oldest first, the current one ticked and semibold), and, in a feed's or folder's own list, **Edit feed…** or **Edit folder…** (section 15; its key, `Shift+E`), or, in a collection, **Rename…** and **Delete** (section 34). A click, Enter or the down arrow opens it on its first item; the arrow keys, Home and End move through it; Escape closes it and puts the keyboard back on More; Tab or a press outside closes it too. Closing a sheet opened from it puts the keyboard back on More.
- Unread, Starred and All list articles from every feed. A feed's own list shows all its articles, read and unread.
- **A feed that isn't working says so above its own list** (decision 0106): only while the feed health page lists it (two checks in a row have failed; section 17), one quiet line in the second ink and the small size, under the search field where a search's count sits, never in the accent or red: "This feed hasn't worked since 12 October." ("since earlier today" on the day it began; the year too when it isn't this one), followed by a text button, **Find its new address**. Pressing it does exactly what Find new address does on the feed health page (section 17), in place, under the line; nothing is fetched until it's pressed (decision 0016). Once the feed has moved, the line gives way to "It now reads from its new address and works again. Its articles, stars and folders stayed." until you leave the list, and the keyboard moves to the list's title. Search results, folders and the views show no such line.
- The list has an end: it shows 50 items, and when you reach the bottom the next 50 load below by themselves, with a small grey centred "Loading…" while they arrive; nothing above moves (decision 0075). For the keyboard and screen readers there's a **"Show older articles"** button ("Show newer articles" when sorting oldest first), hidden until Tab reaches it, then a small grey outlined pill centred under the list, never in the accent. If loading fails, the button shows for everyone, to try again. When there's nothing more to show, a quiet small caption closes the list, centred under it (no line above it): **"You're all caught up."** only at the end of Unread, the one list there is to get through; **"No more results."** at the end of search results; and **"That's everything."** at the end of every other list (a feed, a folder, Starred, All, a collection, Recently read, Up Next).
- Row states, each recognizable without color:

  | State | Looks like |
  |---|---|
  | Unread | Full-strength, semibold title, and a small dot in the theme's accent before it |
  | Read | A softer second ink, regular-weight title (the title stays full-strength while it's the selected row) |
  | Selected | A rounded pill of soft neutral grey set a little in from the list's edges, with no color (the same as in the sidebar; decision 0057); the title in full ink. When you're using the keyboard, an outline is drawn just inside the pill's edge too |
  | Starred | A small filled star after the time, in light green (read out as "Starred"; decision 0057). The article's own star button is green while it's starred, and so is the Starred icon in the sidebar while you're there |

- A podcast episode's meta line ends with a small headphones icon and the episode's length ("45 min"), in the same quiet text (section 35).
- Items you read in the Unread view stay where they are (greyed) until you press `r` or leave the view, so nothing jumps under your cursor.

**How to check it**
1. Open a feed with many articles. Scroll to the bottom: "Loading…" shows briefly and more appear below, and nothing above moves. Keep going until the list ends with "That's everything." (Unread ends with "You're all caught up.", and search results with "No more results.")
2. Open an article and move on: its title turns softer and regular weight, and it doesn't vanish from the list.
3. Turn your screen to greyscale (system accessibility setting). You can still tell unread, read, selected and starred apart.
4. Open a feed listed on the feed health page: "This feed hasn't worked since …" shows above its articles with **Find its new address**. A working feed's list has no such line.

## 7. Reading an article

**What it does**
- The article opens inside nine turtles, using exactly what the publisher put in the feed, restyled consistently: headings, quotes, code, tables, footnotes and images.
- Reading font is Literata by default (Atkinson Hyperlegible and System serif, the device's own serif, as alternatives). Lines are about 65–75 characters long.
- Links inside articles are underlined in the normal text color, never blue, except in the Plain theme, where they are the browser's own blue (purple once visited), still underlined (decision 0063).
- If the feed only has a summary, nine turtles shows the summary. It never fetches the rest from the website. Under the article's closing rule, a small centred note in upright interface type (14px, the second ink) says "This feed shares only a summary.", and under it, on a row of its own at least 44px tall to press, a link to the publisher: **"Read on {publisher} ›"** ("Read the episode page on {publisher} ›" for a podcast's episode, section 35), opening the original in a new tab and the only underlined part (a fine, soft line). Each sentence stays whole on a line wherever it fits, so a narrow column breaks between them and never strands a word (with very large text on a small phone, a sentence wraps evenly rather than running off the screen); **Open original** goes there too.
- The article's top bar runs the width of the reading pane, its icons anchored to the pane's edges (not the text column, so on a very wide window they never float in the middle), each named for screen readers and in its tooltip with its key. The icons sit together at the right edge, 4px apart, and the left stays empty, as in Apple Books' reading toolbar (decision 0050): **Open original** (an arrow out of a box, tooltip "Open original (V)"), always present and always in the same place, then **Aa** (the reading panel, below), **Star** (an outline star, filled once starred) and **More** (…, "More actions" for screen readers), a small menu like the list's with **Copy link** (its key, `c`; or Share, section 21), **Mark unread** (its key, `m`, shown beside it), **Add to collection…** (its key, `l`; section 34), and, for a podcast episode, **Add to Up Next** or **Remove from Up Next** (section 35). The feed's name in the line under the title links to the publisher's site.
- The top bar is solid, in the page's own color, across the whole pane; the text scrolls below it and never shows through. A faint line shows under it only once the text has scrolled. The scrollbar's track is see-through, so its gutter is the page's own color in every theme.
- **The Aa panel** ("Reading appearance"), as in Apple Books: a small panel under the Aa button (on a phone, a sheet along the bottom of the screen, with a Close button). From the top: text size as a stepper: one flat capsule, a small A with a minus and a large A with a plus at its ends (the same six steps as `+` and `-`) and the current step's name between them, parted by hairlines (section 18); Appearance (Light, Dark or Match device) (the same Appearance and text size controls as Settings); the theme (Original or Plain); the reading font, each name written in its own face; and the line width (Narrow, Medium, Wide). Every change applies at once to the article behind it and is the same preference as in Settings › Look and feel (section 18). It isn't modal: the article stays usable. The keyboard starts on its first control; the choices are real buttons and radio buttons; Escape closes it and puts the keyboard back on Aa; a press outside it, or Tab leaving it, closes it too.
- The title is set large (about 32px at the default text size, 1.6 times the text), tight, its lines balanced so a long title wraps into even lines rather than a long line over a short one; headings inside the article are a clear step smaller (about 22px) with more room above them than below.
- The source is always credited and linked, in a quiet line about 20px under the title as in a book or magazine (the pause grows and shrinks with the text size) (14px, the second ink): the feed's name in full ink and semibold (linking to the publisher's website), the author if the feed names one, and the time.
- Once loaded, an article ends rather than stops: a short centred line after its last paragraph (for a summary-only article, after the "Read on {publisher}" note), with room below so the last lines can be read at eye level.
- Choosing an article in the list (a click, `j` / `k`, or the arrow keys while in the list) shows it in the reading area. `Enter` or `o` opens it to read: the keyboard's place moves into the article, and `j` / `k` then move from article to article; Escape goes back to the list. On a phone-narrow window the article takes the list's place, with a back button showing the list's name. `j` stops at the last item listed; older items load when the bottom of the list comes into view, as with scrolling.
- `v` opens the original in a new tab. `+` / `-` change the text size (the same setting as in Settings). Space and `Shift+Space` move the open article down and up a screen, stopping at its end (section 19).
- Links inside articles open in a new tab (links to a place inside the article, like footnotes, stay in it).
- Footnote links move to the note inside the article (and back), without leaving nine turtles.
- On a wide window the article sits in the middle of the reading area, like a page in a book, while its actions keep to the pane's right edge. On a computer at least 56px of paper stays beside the text on each side, as Apple Books keeps on a Mac; in a narrow pane the line gets shorter rather than the margins thinner (decision 0044). On a phone the text keeps the one 20px margin.
- Body text wraps normally; only the article's last paragraph avoids a lone last word, so no line is pulled visibly short (decision 0040).
- With no article chosen, the reading area shows one quiet line in its middle, "Time to read something beautiful." (decision 0064; it replaced "No article open"), in the serif and in muted grey (never the accent, no picture). When the list has something to open, screen readers also hear "Choose one from the list, or press j." (on touch screens just "Choose one from the list.").
- Images load from the publisher's site, each only as you reach it in the text, so an article with many pictures opens quickly and nothing further down is loaded until you scroll near it.
- **Reading here tells no one more than it must** (decision 0112):
  - Invisible 1×1 "tracking pixels" are removed (any image sized 0 or 1 pixel), so they're never loaded.
  - Pictures from known advertising, analytics and tracking services (a short list nine turtles keeps, such as Google Analytics, DoubleClick, Facebook's pixel, WordPress's and Mailchimp's counters and FeedBurner's) are removed too; the same site's real pictures stay.
  - Links lose their tracking tags (`utm_…`, `fbclid`, `gclid`, `mc_cid` and similar well-known ones), both the article's own link (Open original, Copy link and Share) and the links in its text. Any other part of a link stays exactly as the publisher wrote it, so it still leads to the same page.
  - When you open a link or a picture loads, the publisher isn't told it came from nine turtles (no "referrer" is sent).
  - A YouTube video plays from YouTube's privacy-enhanced address (youtube-nocookie.com), and only once you press Play.
- In a night theme, pictures are dimmed a little (never inverted) so a pale one doesn't glare on the dark page; a linked picture shows at full brightness under the pointer or the keyboard. A caption is clearly smaller than the text (13/16 of its size, with a 1.4 line height), 6px under its picture, in the second ink, which keeps 7 : 1 on the page in every theme. A table starts flush with the text's left edge, its header semibold over a fine rule and its rows over fainter ones (decisions 0050, 0053).
- **A picture can be opened larger** (decision 0107). A picture the page shows clearly smaller than it really is (at least a fifth and 40px bigger in width or height), once it has loaded, and not already a link, takes the pointer's hand cursor and is a button for the keyboard (Tab reaches it) and for screen readers, which hear "Open picture larger: {its alt text}". A picture shown at its full size, or inside a link, stays exactly as the publisher's picture. Whether a picture qualifies is looked at again whenever it loads or changes size: a wider or narrower window, a larger or smaller text size, another line width. A press, a tap, Enter or Space opens it alone on a plain card over the dimmed page (the dim of a sheet), as large as fits with a margin all round but never stretched past its own size, its alt text under it as a caption in the second ink (if it has one), a quiet underlined "Open in new tab" to the picture's own address, and an X at the top right (44px, tooltip "Close (Esc)"). Escape, the X or a press on the dim closes it, and the keyboard is back on the picture (or on the article's title, if a change of window size means it no longer opens larger). The opened picture is read in the article's language, as the article is. It appears and goes at once, with no motion, and a phone's pinch-zoom works on it. By night the picture in the article keeps its slight dimming (full brightness under the pointer or the keyboard, as a linked picture does), and the opened picture shows at full brightness, since the reader asked to look at it. A picture that hasn't loaded (offline, or the publisher's site can't be reached) isn't offered.
- Anything unsafe in the feed's content (scripts, embedded frames, code hidden in styles) is removed before display. A YouTube player embedded in an article leaves a link in its place, "Watch the video on YouTube".
- **YouTube videos play in YouTube's own player** (decision 0072). An article whose link is a YouTube video (from a channel's feed) shows, under the byline, a quiet box the shape of the video (16 : 9) in a faint tone of the text, with a round Play mark and "Play video", and no line under it (the owner removed it, 25 October 2026). Until it's pressed nothing is loaded from YouTube. Pressing it (a click, or Enter or Space) puts YouTube's privacy-enhanced player in the box's place, at the same size, playing; the keyboard moves into the player, and Tab leaves it. Screen readers hear "Play video: {title}" and, once it plays, "YouTube video: {title}". The video's description from the feed is its text; a video with none is just the video, ending with the closing rule.
- **Podcast episodes play in the browser's own audio player,** from the publisher's own file (decision 0113): a calm player panel sits under the byline, and a Now playing bar at the foot of the app while one plays (section 35).
- The next article is loaded in advance so moving to it with `j` is instant.
- **Your place is kept, like a bookmark** (decision 0060). Leave a long article part of the way through and it opens where you stopped next time, even after closing nine turtles. Only your own scrolling moves the place; it is forgotten once you reach the end (a finished article opens at its top again) or scroll back to the top. It's kept by paragraph rather than by pixels, so it holds when pictures load late or the text size changes, and it jumps there at once, never gliding. Each device keeps its own places (in the browser's storage, for the 200 most recent articles); nothing goes to the server.

**How to check it**
1. Open an article from a full-content blog: it reads well, with headings, quotes and images in place.
2. Open one from a summary-only feed: you see the summary, ending with "Read on {the publisher's name} ›" on a row of its own, which opens the publisher's page in a new tab.
3. Check that links in the text are underlined and not blue (in Original; in Plain they are blue and underlined).
4. Press `j` repeatedly: each article appears with no visible wait.
5. Press `Enter`, read, press Escape: you're back on the same row in the list. Press `+` and `-`: the text grows and shrinks.
6. Press Aa above an article: the panel opens under it. Press the large A, choose Dark, and Atkinson Hyperlegible: the article changes at once. Choose Plain under Theme: the page turns black on white and the article's links blue. There are no theme tiles. Press Escape: the panel closes and the keyboard is back on Aa. Settings › Look and feel shows the same choices.
7. Make the window very wide: Open original, Aa, Star and More stay together at the reading pane's right edge. Scroll an article: the text fades under the top bar.
8. Scroll halfway down a long article, open another, then come back (or reload the page): it opens where you stopped. Read it to the end, open another and come back: it opens at its top.
9. Open an article with a large picture (a photo from a news site or a blog usually is): the pointer becomes a hand over it. Click it: it opens alone on the dimmed page, as large as fits, its description under it, with an X and "Open in new tab". Press Escape: it closes and the keyboard's outline is on the picture. Press Enter: it opens again; click the dim around it: it closes. A small picture, or one that's a link, does nothing new.
10. Follow a YouTube channel (paste its page address, e.g. youtube.com/@name, into Add a feed) and open a video: a grey box with Play shows above the description. Press it: the video plays in YouTube's own player, in the same place. Open the next video: it waits for Play again.
11. Open an article whose link came with `?utm_source=…` (many newsletters' and news sites' feeds do this), choose Copy link and paste it: the `utm_…` part is gone, and the link still opens the same article. Hover over a link in the text that had tracking tags: they're gone too.
12. Open a long article with many pictures on a slow connection: the text shows at once, and the pictures further down load as you scroll towards them.
13. Open an article from a WordPress blog that showed a tiny stats picture before (`pixel.wp.com` or `stats.wp.com`): with the browser's developer tools on the Network tab, no request goes to those addresses.

## 8. Read state, marking unread, "mark all as read", undo

**What it does**
- An item counts as read as soon as it's shown in the reading pane, by click or by `j`/`k`.
- `m` marks the current item unread again. **Mark unread** in the article's More (…) menu does the same (in the top bar on desktop, in the bottom bar on phones).
- **Mark all as read** (`Shift+A`, or **Mark all as read** in the More (…) menu beside the list's title, section 6): happens instantly, no "are you sure?". It affects **only the items shown on screen right now** (every item listed, including older ones loaded as you scrolled). Items that arrived in the background but aren't displayed yet are never touched.
- **Undo** (`u`, or a visible **Undo**): after an undoable action, a note such as "Marked 23 articles as read" with an **Undo** button floats at the bottom of the list (nothing in the list moves), in a band of the list's own tone that the rows end above, so it never covers them (decision 0048). It stays until your next undoable action or until you leave the list. Pressing `r` doesn't clear it. After an undo it says "Undone."; there's no undoing an undo. If the undo can't reach nine turtles, it says "That couldn't be undone. Try again in a moment." Moving through articles doesn't clear it.
- **Catch me up** (decision 0097), in Unread's More (…) menu only, in inbox mode: every article in Unread from before the start of today (midnight on your device) counts as read at once, whether it's listed or not, with no "are you sure?". Today's articles stay unread. The rows on screen turn grey where they are and the counts drop; the note says "Marked 230 articles from before today as read" with **Undo**, which makes exactly those articles unread again. With nothing to catch up, it says "Nothing from before today is unread." If nine turtles' answer doesn't arrive, it says "Catching up couldn't be confirmed. Refresh to see where things stand."
- **Unread lets go by itself** (decision 0097), if you choose it in Settings › Reading (**Let unread go after: Never · 1 week · 2 weeks · 1 month**; Never is the default): an unread article older than that counts as read. It leaves Unread and the counts, and stays in its feed, All, its folders and search, read. Nothing is removed. "Older" goes by the date the list shows, so the old posts of a blog you've just added don't fill Unread either. It starts once your choice has stood for a minute (so moving through the choices with the arrow keys, past "1 week" on the way to "1 month", lets nothing go), then keeps up every half minute; choosing Never again doesn't make anything unread again. An article you mark unread again lets go once more at the next check. It carries on in river mode too, where its row is hidden, so back in inbox mode Unread is as you chose.
- **Mark all above as read** and **Mark all below as read** (decision 0103), in the list's More (…) menu while an article is chosen, and **Mark older as read** (`Shift+O`): every unread article on that side of the chosen one counts as read at once, with no "are you sure?", **including those further down the list that haven't loaded yet**. Above and below follow the list's order; older goes by date, so it is "below" when the list is newest first and "above" when oldest first, and the menu shows `Shift+O` beside that one (in search results, ordered by how well they match, older goes by date and neither shows it). The chosen article itself is left as it is. Only articles that were there when the list was loaded count: one that arrived in the background since is never touched. The list follows the same rules as everywhere (muted articles and feeds that flow past stay out of Unread), and each reader marks only their own. Rows on screen turn grey where they are; the note says "Marked 14 articles as read" with **Undo**, which makes exactly those articles unread again. With nothing unread there it says "Nothing older is unread." (or "Nothing above it is unread.", "Nothing below it is unread."). Without a connection, when part of the list isn't loaded yet it says "Marking articles not yet listed needs a connection. Try again once you're back online." Not in river mode.
- Undo reverses **the last action only**, of these: mark all as read, mark above / below / older as read, catch me up, mark unread, star/unstar, unsubscribe, delete folder.
- Read state is recorded in both reading modes, so switching modes never loses it.
- Each feed's unread number in the sidebar changes at the same moment.
- If nine turtles can't be reached when a mark is made (the connection dropped, or a new version is going live and the server is restarting), it quietly tries again for about a minute, so a short gap loses nothing and says nothing. With no connection at all, the mark waits on the device and is sent once the connection is back, the latest mark winning (section 32).
- If a mark still can't be saved, it's taken back and the note at the bottom of the list says so plainly, such as "The star couldn't be saved.", with a **Try again** button in the Undo's place. The message goes as soon as a later mark is saved (the connection is back), or when you press Try again. A failure never takes back a later action on the same article.

**How to check it**
1. Click an unread item: it turns grey and the feed's unread number drops by one.
2. Press `m`: it's unread again and the number goes back up.
3. Press `Shift+A` in a feed: everything shown turns grey at once. Press `u`: they're unread again.
4. Wait until "New articles · press r" appears, then mark all as read *without* pressing `r`. Press `r`: the new items are still unread.
5. Turn the Wi-Fi off, open an unread article, and turn it back on within half a minute: the article stays read and nothing is said. Leave it off longer: "An article couldn't be marked as read. It's still unread." appears with Try again; once the Wi-Fi is back, opening another unread article (or pressing Try again) clears it.
6. In Unread, open More (…) and choose Catch me up: everything from before today turns grey and the counts drop; today's articles stay unread. Press `u`: they're all unread again.
7. In a feed with more than 50 articles, choose the tenth and press `Shift+O`: the rows below it turn grey, the feed's count drops to 9 (the older ones not yet listed are counted too), and `u` brings them all back. Open More (…): **Mark all above as read** marks the nine above.
8. In Settings › Reading choose Let unread go after: 2 weeks. Unread now starts at two weeks ago; the older articles are still in their feeds, read. Choose Unread counts: Hide: the numbers in the sidebar go, and Unread stays.

## 9. Star

**What it does**
- One concept: **Star**. `s` (or the **Star** button in the article's toolbar) stars or unstars the current item. It can be undone like the other marks (section 8).
- Starred items appear in the **Starred** view and are kept forever.
- Stars and collections (section 34) are separate: starring an article never puts it on a collection, and putting it on one never stars it. Both keep it forever.

**How to check it**
1. Star an article: a small star mark appears on its row, and it shows up under Starred.
2. Unstar it: it leaves Starred.

## 10. Reading modes: inbox and river

*V1 had inbox mode only. River mode arrived in V2 (25 October 2026, decision 0068).*

**What it does**
- A single setting for the whole app.
- **Inbox (default):** things to get through. Unread view, read/unread styling and unread counts are all present.
- **River:** things flowing past. No Unread view, no read/unread styling, no counts. Read state is still recorded quietly.
- In river mode the sidebar shows **Starred · All** only, `g u` does nothing, and "Mark all as read" (in the More menu, and `Shift+A`) is hidden. The app opens on **All** in river mode and on **Unread** in inbox mode.
- In river mode every title in the list looks the same: full ink, regular weight, with no unread dot, and screen readers don't hear "unread". The feeds and folders show no numbers.
- The Unread address (and `/`) leads to All in river mode, without a step in the browser's history. Switching to river mode while on Unread moves you to All the same way.
- The `?` list leaves out "Go to Unread" and "Mark all as read" in river mode, and search can't be limited to Unread (there's no Unread view), though its "Show only: Unread" filter stays. "Mark unread" (and `m`) stay: read state is still recorded, and shows again back in inbox mode.
- Chosen in Settings › Reading, as **Reading mode: Inbox · River**. Each device remembers the mode it last saw, so it opens in it before the server answers; the server's answer decides.
- **One feed in river mode** (since 25 October 2026, decision 0082): in Edit feed (section 15), a feed's own **Reading mode: Inbox · River**. A river feed flows past while the app is in inbox mode: its articles never appear in Unread, it has no count (and adds nothing to its folders' counts), and its rows look as in river mode, wherever they're listed. Its own list, All, its folders and search still show everything, except a search for unread articles only. When the whole app is in river mode, every feed flows past whatever its own setting says.

**How to check it**
1. In Settings, switch to river mode. The Unread view, the counts and the grey "read" titles all disappear.
2. Read a few articles, switch back to inbox. The ones you read are shown as read.
3. In inbox mode, open a busy feed's Edit feed and choose River: its count goes, its articles leave Unread, and its list shows every title alike.

## 11. Background fetching (being polite to publishers)

**What it does**
- The server checks feeds in the background, each on its own rhythm: often for frequent publishers, rarely for quiet blogs. Never more often than every 30 minutes, and at least once a day.
- Checks are spread out over time, never all at once.
- It tells the publisher what it already has (using "ETag" and "Last-Modified", standard web labels), so unchanged feeds cost them almost nothing.
- It identifies itself clearly as nine turtles.
- If a publisher says "slow down" (HTTP 429, or 503 with a time), it waits as long as asked, up to a week (so a mistaken answer can't silence a feed for good).
- Each fetch has a time limit and a size limit.
- If a feed has moved permanently, nine turtles updates its stored address.
- A failing feed is retried less and less often (twice as long after each failure, up to once a day) and never affects other feeds. A feed that keeps failing still looks normal in the sidebar; it simply shows nothing new.
- It never visits article pages.
- **Feeds nobody opens are checked once a day** (decision 0090): a reader is *away* once nine turtles hasn't seen them for 30 days (no device of theirs opened it, no other app synced). A feed whose every follower is away is checked at most once a day. When one of them comes back (opens nine turtles, signs in or syncs), their feeds that are working are checked again straight away, a few at a time as always, unless a feed was checked in the last 30 minutes or a follower chose Hourly or Daily for it.
- **A feed can be checked less often** (decision 0082): Edit feed's **Check for new articles: Automatic · Hourly · Daily**. Automatic is the rhythm above; Hourly and Daily check that feed no more often than once an hour or once a day, for feeds that post a lot. Choosing a longer wait applies at once. WebSub announcements still arrive as they come.
- If a publisher edits an article you've already seen, the text is updated quietly and it stays read.
- The same article published in two different feeds appears in both.

**How to check it**
Most of this is checked by the automatic tests rather than by eye. You can see it working:
1. Leave the app closed for a day, then open it: the feeds you follow have new articles that arrived on their own.
2. When a feed keeps failing for three days or more, the daily check mentions it ("Some feeds keep failing"), without naming it; the feed health page (section 17) names it.

## 12. WebSub (instant updates from publishers who offer it)

*Built in V2 (25 October 2026, decision 0069).*

**What it does**
- Some publishers announce new articles the moment they publish, through a service called a "hub" (the standard is called WebSub). When a feed names a hub, nine turtles signs up with it, and the hub sends each new article to nine turtles as it's published.
- Nothing on screen changes: new articles wait for the reader as always ("New articles · press r", section 13). They simply arrive sooner.
- nine turtles checks that announcements are genuine: each feed's subscription has its own secret, and an announcement without the right signature is ignored.
- Subscriptions are renewed a day before they expire (halfway through a short one). A hub that never confirms is asked again once a week.
- Regular checking continues as a backup, at most twice a day while the hub delivers.
- It only works where hubs can reach nine turtles: the live app, at `https://app.nineturtles.app` (the setting `NINE_TURTLES_PUBLIC_URL`). A copy running on a computer without it checks feeds as before.

**How to check it**
1. Follow a blog that uses WebSub: most blogs on WordPress.com or Blogger do, and so does a YouTube channel's feed.
2. When it publishes, "New articles · press r" appears within a few minutes rather than hours.

## 13. Refresh: the view waits for you

**What it does**
- The list on screen changes only when you ask: press `r` or choose Refresh in the list's More (…) menu.
- When new items are waiting, a quiet note floats at the bottom of the list: **"New articles · press r"** (on touch screens: **"New articles"** with a **Show** button, which does the same as `r`). It shows no number. It sits at the foot of the list in a band of the list's own tone, which the rows end above, so nothing shifts when it appears and it never covers a row (decision 0048). The list asks the server about once a minute, and only about its own articles (none ever arrive in Starred).
- **Refresh** (its key, `r`, shown beside it) is in the More (…) menu beside every list's title, on phones too (section 6).
- Because fetching already happened in the background, pressing `r` is instant. It shows the list from the top again, with the new items, and updates the sidebar's unread numbers. Those numbers also wait for `r`, so nothing in the sidebar changes under you either.
- Opening or reloading the app always shows the latest state.
- No pull-to-refresh on phones; there's Refresh in the list's More menu, and Show in the "New articles" note, instead.

**How to check it**
1. Leave the app open. When the "New articles" note appears at the bottom, check the list hasn't moved.
2. Press `r`: new items appear at once, with no spinner.
3. Close and reopen the app: you see everything new without pressing anything.

## 14. Search

**What it does**
- `/` puts you in the search field, which sits under the list's title: a slim, faintly filled field with soft corners and no edge, 36px tall with a mouse on a wide window and 44px on a touch screen or a phone, the same field as the sidebar's find field. A faint search icon starts the field; the "Search options" (sliders) button sits inside it at its right end.
- Results follow a moment after you stop typing, or at once on Enter. Every word must appear; a word also finds longer words it begins ("read" finds "reading"); words in double quotes are found together.
- The search starts out limited to where you are, shown as a removable chip inside the field, e.g. `[ in: Tech × ]`, as soon as you're in the search (the field focused, its options open, or words typed). At rest the field just says "Search", since the list's title already names it. Remove the chip to search everything.
- **Search options** opens a small popover under its button, floating over the list rather than pushing it down, drawn as a menu: three sections of rows, with a checkmark at the start of each one that holds and its words semibold. **Search in**: the list you started from (marked "This list") and All feeds, plus the place now searched when it's another; **Other feed or folder** at the section's end, drawn as a menu's submenu row with a trailing › (its words wrap rather than being cut off on a narrow screen), opens a list of every view, folder and feed (decision 0048). **Show only**: Unread and Starred, each ticked on its own. **Published**: Any time, Past week, Past month or Custom, which shows two dates. The rows are radio buttons and tick boxes underneath, for keyboards and screen readers. Escape closes it and returns to its button (a second Escape closes the search); so does a press outside it, or the keyboard moving out of the search. The panel and the chips always match. The search box stays one line for the usual one or two chips: they shrink and end in "…", with their whole text in a tooltip. With three or more it wraps, so every chip stays readable.
- Titles and article text are always searched together; title matches come first.
- Power-user filters can be typed: `is:unread`, `is:starred`, `feed:name`, `folder:name`, `in:name` (one of your collections, section 34; offered only once you have one), `after:YYYY-MM-DD`, `before:YYYY-MM-DD`. Typed, `in:` is only for collections: a feed is `feed:` and a folder `folder:`, even though every place chip reads "in:" (`[ in: Tech × ]` for a folder). `feed:`, `folder:` and `in:` each say where to search, so each becomes the place chip (`in: Recipes`); searching from inside a collection starts limited to it, and the options' **Other feed or folder** list has your collections too. (The shortcut list documents the other filters; `in:` is offered as you type it, so the list still fits the window.) They're suggested as you type, in a small panel floating over the list in the family of the search options, so the results never move (↓ and ↑ choose, the chosen one marked by a bar in the text color and a faint tone, never the accent; Enter takes one; screen readers hear each as it's chosen) and turn into chips once followed by a space or Enter. A chip says its filter in plain words, as the options menu does: **Unread**, **Starred**, **After 1 Oct 2026**, **Before 1 Mar 2026**; the typed form (`is:unread`) is its tooltip, and screen readers hear it after the chip's Remove button (decision 0048). A name with spaces goes in quotes (`feed:"Garden Notes"`), or type part of it. `after:` and `before:` don't include the day itself. A filter that can't be understood stays as typed, with a plain line saying why. Backspace at the start of the field removes the last chip.
- Results look and behave like the normal item list, including "Mark all as read" in the More menu. The list's title says where they come from: **"Results in All"**, or "Results in {the feed, folder, collection, Unread or Starred}" while the search is limited to one (a saved search keeps its own name). A quiet line above the results says how many were found ("16 results", or "50+ results" while more can load). Where the search options would cover that line (a narrow list, or a phone), the options repeat it in a quiet line at their foot, so ticking a filter always shows how many it leaves (decision 0054). Refresh is hidden while searching: there's nothing new to fetch for a search.
- Matching words, in a title and in the line of text under it alike, have a faint tone of the theme's accent behind them with small rounded corners and a fine underline (so never tone alone), in the text's own weight (decision 0037). Screen readers hear them as highlighted.
- Under the title of each result whose text (not only its title) holds a searched word, up to two muted lines show that text around the first match, before the source and time: a few words before it, then on to about 90 characters, cut only between words, with "…" where text was left out, and ending in "…" if the list is too narrow for two lines. Three typed dots in the publisher's text show as one "…" too. The searched words in it are marked as in the title, and in full ink rather than muted grey. The line is plain text: nothing from the feed's HTML comes through.
- Once the field has words in it, a **×** at its end ("Clear search", a 44px target) empties the field, keeping any chips, and leaves the keyboard in it.
- Works for English, other Latin-alphabet languages, and Hindi.
- **Saved searches** (decision 0081): while a search shows results, **Save search…** in the More (…) menu opens a sheet built like Edit folder's: a **Name** (started from the searched words, or else what the chips say), a line saying in words what it searches for ("walking · In Garden Notes"), then Cancel and Save. Saved, it opens at once, with an address of its own (`/searches/3`), and is listed in the sidebar under a small **Saved searches** heading, after Unread, Starred, All and Recently read, A to Z, each with a small search icon where those have theirs and no unread count. Opening it runs the search again: its name is the list's title, and its chips and words are in the search field, so it always shows what it looks for. Changing the field changes the results for now; Escape puts it back as saved. **Edit saved search…** in the same menu renames it and saves what the field holds now; **Delete**, in red words at the sheet's far left, removes only the search, never an article, and takes you to Unread with "Deleted the saved search …" and Undo. Two saved searches can't share a name. On a phone, the sheets are the phone sheet, as Edit folder's are.
- Tab moves from the field into the open options, onto the place being searched; Escape closes search and returns you to where you were.

**How to check it**
1. In a folder, press `/`, type a word you remember from an article. The chip shows the folder; results appear with the word marked by a faint tone behind it and a fine underline, and a result whose text holds the word shows a line of that text with the word marked the same way. Press × to empty the field.
2. Remove the chip: results now come from all feeds.
3. Type `is:starred`: it becomes a chip saying "Starred", and only starred items are listed.
4. Open the options, choose Past week: a matching "After …" chip appears in the field, its date in words.
5. Search a Hindi word from a Hindi article: that article is found.
6. Press Escape: you're back exactly where you were.
7. Search for a word, choose More › Save search…, name it and Save. It opens, and appears in the sidebar under Saved searches. Go to All, then choose it in the sidebar: the same results, with the same chips. Remove a chip and press Escape: the chip comes back.
8. More › Edit saved search…: rename it and Save. Then Edit saved search… › Delete: it leaves the sidebar; Undo brings it back.
9. Type `in:` and the start of a collection's name: it's suggested; take it, and only that collection's articles are found.

## 15. Folders and managing feeds

**What it does**
- Folders are the only grouping. A feed can be in more than one folder.
- You can rename a feed, choose its folders, set its own settings, add a rule for it (**Add a rule for this feed…**, a link under its settings that opens Settings › Rules with a new rule set to the feed; section 27) and unsubscribe, from **Edit feed…** in the More (…) menu beside the item list's title when you're viewing one feed. It opens as a sheet over the feed's list (`/feeds/3/edit`) with the name, the folders as a checklist (a grouped list of 44px rows; each folder ends in a tick box, a rounded square, filled with a check when the feed is in it and empty when it isn't, square rather than round because a feed can be in several folders), whose last row is "New folder…", a text box (made when you save, with the feed in it), and at the foot one row in a Mac sheet's order: **Unsubscribe** at the far left as words in a muted red (no edge; the word says what it does, so red is never the only sign; its tooltip, and screen readers, say it removes the feed's articles, keeps the starred ones, and can be undone straight afterwards), then **Cancel** (a faint tone, no edge) and **Save** at the right end. A problem saving appears above that row in red words after a warning mark. Escape, Cancel or a press on the dimmed page goes back unchanged. On a phone it's the same iOS page sheet as Add a feed (and Edit folder; decision 0055), with the folders and Unsubscribe as cards on its grouped ground: an X beside the title (it and Escape go back unchanged), no Cancel, **Unsubscribe** as the one red row of a group of its own, and **Save** the full width at the screen's foot, at least 32px below it (decision 0052). The folders' helper line ("Optional: a new folder…") is 13px in the muted grey, 6px under the list, on its left edge.
- **A feed that isn't working** (decision 0106): only while the feed health page lists it, Edit feed starts with the same quiet line as its list ("This feed hasn't worked since 12 October.") and a text button, **Find new address**, which works exactly as on the feed health page (section 17), in place under the line. Using an address moves the feed at once, not on Save, keeping its name, folders, articles and stars; the line then says so, and the keyboard moves to the sheet's title. If others follow the old address too, the feed gets a new number and Edit feed goes to it, still the same sheet: the line still says so, and anything you typed stays. A working feed's Edit feed shows nothing of this.
- **How often it posts** (decision 0105): in Edit feed, a quiet helper line under the name (13px, muted grey, as a field's helper line) says how often the feed posts, in plain rounded words: "Posts about 12 a day.", "Posts about once a day.", "Posts about 3 times a week.", "Posts about twice a month.", "Posts less than once a month.", "Hasn't posted in 4 months.", "Hasn't posted in over a year." or "Too new to tell." (fewer than 3 articles, or under two weeks since its oldest one). The yearly clean-up (section 23) removes old unstarred articles, so a silence of a year or more is only ever "over a year", and a feed with no articles left is counted from when nine turtles started fetching it ("Too new to tell." only in its first two weeks). No chart, no decimals, no judgement. It's worked out on the server from the feed's articles of the last 90 days, by their published time (or when they arrived, if the feed gives none), over those 90 days or the feed's own history if shorter. Its line is kept from the moment the sheet opens, so nothing moves when the words arrive.
- **Each feed's own settings** (decision 0082), in Edit feed under the folders, as a grouped list named "Settings", built as Settings' rows (each name on the left with a short description straight under it in the muted grey, decision 0120; a segmented choice at the right end, under them on a narrow screen): **Reading mode: Inbox · River** (section 10), **Check for new articles: Automatic · Hourly · Daily** (section 11), **Open articles on the website** and **Pause this feed**, each a tick box row. Each row's description says what it does: "New articles go to Unread." (or, for River, "Its articles skip Unread and show no count."; while the whole app is in river mode, "The whole app is in river mode for now (Settings › Reading)."), "As often as this feed usually posts." (Hourly: "No more often than once an hour."; Daily: "No more often than once a day."), "Opens the publisher's page instead of the text here." and "New articles skip Unread until you untick it; then its newest three come back." (with since when, once paused). Each starts as every feed behaves (Inbox, Automatic, unticked), and Save keeps them with the name and folders. Following a feed again after unsubscribing starts them afresh.
- **Pause this feed** (decision 0095), for a holiday or a noisy week: while it's paused, nothing from it piles up. Its new articles still arrive (it's checked as usual, so nothing it posts is missed) and appear in its own list, its folders, All and search, but already read, so they never reach Unread or its count. What was unread before you paused it stays unread. The helper line says since when ("Paused today.", "Paused on 23 Oct."). Unticking it brings back only its newest three articles from while it was paused to Unread (your rules act on them again, so one a rule marks as read stays read); the rest stay read in its list. Pausing is yours alone: other readers of the feed see no change. Following it again after unsubscribing starts it unpaused.
- **Open articles on the website**, for feeds that share only a summary: opening one of its articles (`Enter` or `o`, or a tap on a phone) opens the publisher's page in a new tab instead, and the article counts as read. Choosing it in the list (a click, `j` / `k`) still shows its summary beside the list, so moving through the list never opens tabs. Nothing is fetched from the website by nine turtles.
- Viewing a folder, **Edit folder…** in the same menu opens a sheet in the same way (`/folders/2/edit`) to rename or delete it, built the same way: Name, then one row with **Delete** in muted red at the far left (its tooltip says the feeds stay) and Cancel and Save at the right end. On a phone, the same build as Edit feed's: an X, Delete as a red row of its own, Save at the foot.
- You can create (from "Edit feed"), rename and delete folders. Two folders can't share a name. Deleting a folder never deletes its feeds.
- Unsubscribing and deleting a folder happen at once and take you to Unread, where the line says "Unsubscribed from …" or "Deleted the folder …" with Undo (or `u`), until your next action. Undo after deleting a folder brings it back with its feeds, its rules and the saved searches limited to it, as Undo after deleting a collection does.
- Unsubscribing from a feed keeps its starred articles in Starred. Undo brings the feed back whole, read history and folders included; after your next action (or ten minutes) its other articles are gone for good from your lists. Following it again later starts it afresh, all unread.
- **Several readers on one nine turtles** (decision 0086): your feeds' names, folders, settings and your read, starred and muted marks are yours alone. Another reader who follows the same feed shares only its articles, fetched once: unsubscribing removes only your marks and leaves their feed exactly as it was, and its articles go only once nobody follows it (anyone's starred ones stay). Following a feed someone else already follows brings in its stored articles: the ones the publisher still lists unread, older ones read.

- **Your feeds** (tidying many feeds at once; decision 0080): in Settings, under **Your library**, a **Your feeds** row (ending in ›) opens a page of its own at `/settings/feeds`, built like the feed health page: the same top line with an X ("Close your feeds", or Escape) back to Settings, and one centred column.
  - It lists every feed A to Z in one grouped list, each on two lines: its name, then, in the muted grey, when it last posted ("Last posted today", "Last posted on 3 Oct 2025", or "Nothing posted yet") and the folders it's in. The group's small heading counts them ("42 feeds"). Out of Edit, a row opens that feed's list.
  - **Show**, a segmented choice above the list: **All feeds**, **Quiet for 6 months** or **Quiet for a year**. A quiet feed is one with nothing published for that long (counted from when you added it, if it has never posted). The feed health page lists broken feeds; this lists working feeds that have gone silent, so you can let them go. With none, it says "Every feed has posted within the last 6 months" (or year).
  - **Edit**, as in Apple Books, at the right of the list's heading: each row gets a tick box (the same rounded square as Edit feed's folders), and **Choose all** / **Choose none** appears beside it; Edit becomes **Done**. A bar stays at the page's foot while the list scrolls, solid in the page's color with a hairline above: how many are chosen, **Unsubscribe** in the destructive red words, and **Move to…**.
  - **Move to…** opens a sheet ("Move 3 feeds") with a choice of one, as a grouped list with a round mark at each row's end: **No folder**, each folder, and **New folder** (with a text box for its name). Each feed leaves the folders it was in and goes to the one chosen. Cancel or Escape closes only the sheet.
  - Moving and unsubscribing happen at once. A line above the list says what was done ("Moved 3 feeds to Later.", "Unsubscribed from 3 feeds.", naming the feed when there's one) with **Undo**, which the keyboard moves to, until your next action there (pressing Edit, changing Show or another move or unsubscribe ends it). Undo puts every feed back where it was in one go (a folder made just for the move goes too) or brings every feed back whole, as unsubscribing from one feed does. Edit ends after each action.

**How to check it**
1. Put a feed in two folders. It appears under both, and its unread count is right in each.
2. Rename a folder: it moves to its new alphabetical position.
3. Delete a folder: its feeds remain (under other folders or the "no folder" group).
4. Unsubscribe from a feed, then undo: it's back with its read history.
5. Star an article, then unsubscribe from its feed: the article is still in Starred.
6. Open Settings › Your feeds, press Edit, tick two feeds and Move to… a new folder: both are in it, and nowhere else. Press Undo: they're back where they were, and the new folder is gone.
7. Tick two feeds and press Unsubscribe, then Undo: both are back, with their read history.
8. Choose "Quiet for 6 months": only feeds with nothing new for six months are listed.
9. In Edit feed, tick "Open articles on the website" and Save. Choose one of its articles and press Enter: the publisher's page opens in a new tab.
10. In Edit feed, tick "Pause this feed" and Save. When it next posts, the new articles are in its own list as read, and Unread and its count don't grow. Open Edit feed again: it says when you paused it. Untick it and Save: its newest three new articles are in Unread.
11. Open Edit feed for a feed that posts every day: under its name, "Posts about once a day." For a feed added today with two articles: "Too new to tell."

## 16. OPML import and export

OPML is the standard file format feed readers use to move subscription lists between apps.

**What it does**
- **Import:** from Settings → Your data, or from the empty state. A feed that appears several times in the file becomes one feed in several folders. Feeds you already have are not duplicated (they join the file's folders). A feed another reader of this nine turtles already follows isn't stored twice: you get it with its articles so far marked read, and new ones arrive unread. Folders inside folders become the innermost one. Afterwards, a plain summary says what was added and what couldn't be read, for example "Added 120 feeds and 8 folders. Their articles arrive over the next few minutes." The feeds appear at once with the file's names; their articles arrive in the background, a few feeds at a time, as with any feed.
- **At most 1,000 feeds per reader** (decision 0090): a file that would take you past 1,000 imports nothing and says so plainly: "Nothing was imported. You follow 950 feeds, and this file would add 120 more, past the 1,000 one reader can follow." Feeds the file lists that you already follow don't count.
- **Export:** downloads an OPML file of all your feeds and folders. A feed in several folders appears under each of them.
- Your own OPML file is never stored in the code repository.

**How to check it**
1. Import your OPML file. The summary says how many feeds were added. Your folders appear in the sidebar.
2. Export, then import the export into another feed reader (or back into a fresh nine turtles). Everything is there, with the same folders.

## 17. Feed health page

*Built in V2 (25 October 2026, decision 0071).*

**What it does**
- In Settings, under **Your library**: a **Feed health** row (ending in ›) opens it in place of Settings, at `/settings/feed-health`, with the same top line and centred column. Its X ("Close feed health") or Escape goes back to Settings, with the keyboard on the Feed health row.

- A feed is **dead** after 7 days of "not found", or 3 weeks of any failure. Dead feeds are still checked once a day in case they come back, and the page says so.
- Each feed is a card (rounded, a tone off the page, the cards a little apart; decision 0118). Top to bottom: its name, a little larger and semibold; one sentence on what's wrong, leading in bold with how long: "**Not found for 8 days.** The site says the feed isn't there any more. Every one is at least 44px tall. On a narrow screen the two buttons share their row equally and Unsubscribe has a line of its own below them.
  - **Retry** checks the feed straight away and says how it went ("It works again." or "Tried again just now: it still isn't working."). If the site asked nine turtles to slow down, Retry waits as asked and says until when (the publisher floor). To fetch politely, Retry asks the publisher at most once every five minutes for each feed, whoever presses it, and never a working feed fetched in the last half hour; a press in between answers with how the last try went.
  - **Find new address** visits the feed's site once, because you asked: the home page the feed names, or else its address's own home page, and lists the feeds that page names (never guessing other addresses, decision 0016), leaving out the broken one and any you already follow. What it found shows in place, the same way wherever Find new address is offered (here, above a failing feed's list in section 6, and in Edit feed in section 15; decision 0106): **one feed** is one plain question, "Use https://… instead?", with "Its name, folders, articles and stars stay." under it and **Cancel** and **Use it**; **several** are a choice of one (a round mark, as in Add a feed, the first chosen) with **Cancel** and **Use this address**; **none** is a plain sentence ("The site doesn't name another feed. You could try again another day, or unsubscribe."). One status line says each step to screen readers ("Looking on the site…", the question, "Moving…", the result); the keyboard moves to **Use it** (which screen readers hear with the question) or to the chosen feed, and Cancel puts it back on the button that started it. Using one: the feed moves there and keeps its name, folders, articles, read marks and stars. An address you already follow, or one kept for the stars of a feed you unsubscribed from, is refused with a plain sentence saying why; feeds are never merged quietly. If another reader follows the same feed, its address stays as it is for them: you follow the new address instead, with your name, folders, settings, rules and saved searches for it, and every article you had and every mark (read, starred, muted) comes with you, as it does when the feed moves in place. Nothing of yours is cleared later; the old address simply leaves your list.
- Nothing moves while you look: a feed that works again stays where it is, saying so, until you open the page again.
- No status colors: the group's name says how bad it is, in words (decision 0071).
- When nothing is wrong, the page says "All feeds are working."

**How to check it**
1. Add a feed whose site later moves it (the browser tests use a made-up one). After two failed checks, it's listed under "Having trouble" with a plain explanation.
2. Each listed feed is a card: its name, what happened, its Address and when it Last worked, then Find new address, Retry and, at the right, Unsubscribe (on a phone, Unsubscribe on its own line). Press Retry: it's checked again immediately and the result is shown.
3. Press Find new address. When the site names one feed, it asks "Use … instead?": press Use it, and it works again, with its articles and stars. (When it names several, choose one and press Use this address.)
4. When everything works, the page says "All feeds are working."
5. After a listed feed's "Last worked" day, it says how often that feed posts, as Edit feed does.
6. Open a listed feed's own list before fixing it: the line above its articles offers **Find its new address**, which does the same there.

## 23. Your data: keeping and exporting

**What it does**
- **The yearly clean-up** (decision 0089): an article you haven't starred is removed a year after nine turtles first received it, read or not. Starred articles stay forever, and so do articles on any of your collections (section 34). The year counts from when nine turtles received the article, not when it was published, so old posts from a blog you just added stay a year too. (If other readers already follow that blog, nine turtles received its posts when they did: you get those it still keeps, not ones it has already removed.) Another reader's star keeps an article for them, not for you. A removed article never comes back as new, even while its feed still lists it. The clean-up runs each night just after the backup is safely stored, never without one, so anything it removes is in that night's copy (kept up to a year). It happens quietly; the app shows nothing about it.
- Unsubscribing removes that feed's articles and your read history for it (your starred articles stay in Starred, and articles on your collections stay on them); another reader following the same feed keeps it as it was. Undo brings them back only straight away; after your next action they're gone from the app, though the backups still hold them as they were at the last nightly copy.
- Export of starred articles as a JSON Feed file (a standard, open format other feed tools can read), from Settings → Your data → **Export starred articles**. It holds every starred article, newest first, with its text, original link and the feed it came from, including starred articles of feeds you've since unsubscribed from.
- **Export collections** (decision 0108), from Settings → Your data: every article on any of your collections as one JSON Feed file, newest first, with its text, original link and the feed it came from, as the starred export has them, and its collections' names as the article's tags. Every collection's name is in the file too, empty ones included. Nothing about your collections is locked in.
- **Import starred articles** (decision 0098), from Settings → Your data: brings your stars over from another feed reader. Choose the JSON file it exports starred (or "saved") articles in: Inoreader, Feedly, The Old Reader, FreshRSS, Feedbin and others that use the Google Reader format, or a JSON Feed file such as nine turtles' own starred export. Each article appears in Starred, read, with its text as the file has it (cleaned like any feed's), its original link and the feed it came from. An article nine turtles already has from a feed is starred as it is. A star whose feed you don't follow stays only in Starred (and search), with a link to the original; no feed is added to your sidebar and nothing is fetched for it. An article the file gives no link to the original for is left out. Importing only adds stars, so nothing needs undoing; importing the same file twice stars nothing new. Afterwards a plain summary says what happened, for example "Starred 120 articles. They're in Starred, each with a link to the original." At most 10,000 articles or 25 MB per file, and each reader keeps at most 10,000 imported articles (50 MB of their text) from all files together; a file that would go past that imports nothing and says so. A star whose file names its feed but not the feed's address keeps that feed's name; one that names no feed at all is credited to "Imported stars". Importing works after a free trial or plan has ended too, as starring does. Pocket isn't supported: it keeps whole web pages, not what a feed sent.
- **Backups** (decision 0076): every night, nine turtles stores a complete copy of everything (feeds, folders, articles, read marks, stars, settings) in storage outside Render, and another just before any update changes the database's structure. It keeps the last 30 nights, one copy a month for a year, and the last 10 taken before an update. At worst a day of reading activity is lost. Bringing a copy back, or moving to another host, is a written, tested procedure (`docs/operations/backups.md`, `docs/operations/moving-hosts.md`). Backups happen quietly in the background; the app never shows or asks anything about them. Your OPML file and the starred export remain your own safety net too.

**How to check it**
1. Export your starred articles and open the file: they're all there.
2. Export your OPML file now and then, and keep it somewhere safe.
3. Export your starred articles from another feed reader (most offer it in their settings, near OPML export) and import the file: they appear in Starred, each with a link to the original, and your sidebar stays as it was.
4. In the backup storage (Cloudflare R2), `nightly/` holds a copy from last night.
5. A year after you start, articles you never starred from before then are gone, and your starred ones are all still in Starred, and your collections still hold theirs.
6. Export your collections and open the file: each collected article is there, its collections listed as its tags.

## 26. Other apps: syncing with Reeder and NetNewsWire

*Added 25 October 2026 at the owner's request (decision 0070).*

**What it does**
- Other feed readers, such as Reeder and NetNewsWire, can use nine turtles as their sync service, through the "Google Reader API" that FreshRSS and Miniflux also offer. In the app, the owner adds a **FreshRSS** (or **Google Reader API**) account, with the server address shown in Settings (`https://app.nineturtles.app/api/greader`) and the app password; any username works. `/api/greader.php` answers too, for apps that expect FreshRSS's address.
- **What syncs, both ways:** feeds, folders and articles (with the text the feed carried, the link to the original and the feed's name, without trackers or tracking tags, as here, decision 0112); read and unread; starred. The app can also follow and unfollow feeds, rename them, put them in folders and take them out, and rename or delete folders (deleting a folder never unfollows its feeds, and from an app it has no Undo: only the grouping goes, never an article).
- **Unfollowing from an app** works as it does here: the feed is hidden at once and its unstarred articles are cleared ten minutes later. Following the same feed again from an app within those ten minutes brings it back as it was, with its articles, read marks and folders. Unlike unfollowing in nine turtles, unfollowing a second feed from an app doesn't cut the first one's ten minutes short; unsubscribing on nine turtles' own page does, as it always has (its Undo covers only the last action), so a feed an app unfollowed for you is then cleared at once (another reader's are never cleared early). A hidden feed is out of every list in the app too.
- **"Mark all as read" from an app** marks only articles that had arrived when the app last looked, never ones that arrived since, as nine turtles' own does.
- **Settings › Other apps:** **Make an app password** (ending in +) makes a 24-letter password in groups of four (`abcd-efgh-…`; no 0, 1, i, l or o) and shows it with the server address, each with a **Copy** button. The password is shown only this once; nine turtles keeps only a scrambled copy. Then **Make a new app password** (which signs out any app using the old one) and **Turn off other apps** (which signs every app out; feeds and articles are unchanged). The line under the group says what it's for and how to use it.
- The app password opens only the sync API, never nine turtles' own pages, and the owner's own password never works in an app.
- Following a feed from an app counts towards the 1,000 feeds one reader can follow (decision 0090); past it, the app is refused. Syncing counts as being here, so the feeds of a reader who reads only in another app never rest (section 11).

**How to check it**
1. In Settings › Other apps, press **Make an app password**: the address and password appear, each with Copy.
2. In NetNewsWire (Mac or iPhone), add a FreshRSS account with that address and password: your feeds and folders appear. Star an article there; refresh nine turtles: it's starred here too. Mark one read here; refresh NetNewsWire: it's read there.
3. Press **Turn off other apps**: NetNewsWire can no longer sync and asks you to sign in again.

## 27. Rules and mutes

*Built in V2 (25 October 2026, decision 0077).*

A rule says "when a new article **matches this**, **do that**", in plain fields, never code. Only what the feed holds and what you wrote are used: no guessing, no ranking.

**What it does**
- **Matches this** (every part filled in must hold): a **word or phrase**, in **the title** or in **the title or the text**, found as whole words whatever their capital letters ("AI" never matches "said"; the HTML's own words never count); an **author**'s name; and **where**: all feeds, one folder, or one feed.
- **Do that:** **Mute** (the article is kept, counts as read, and waits in no list: not Unread, All, its feed or its folder; only search, Muted articles and, if it's starred, Starred show it), **Star** (it goes straight into Starred), or **Mark as read** (it stays in its feed's list, already read). Rules act on articles as they arrive, a new feed's first ones included, in the order the rules were made.
- **Where you find it:** Settings › **Rules ›**, its own page (`/settings/rules`; decision 0120). Each rule is one plain sentence, e.g. "Mute articles with “sponsored” in the title, in all feeds.", with **Edit** and a **switch** that pauses it ("Paused." follows the sentence). **New rule…** is the group's last row. Edit feed has a shortcut, **Add a rule for this feed…**, which opens Settings › Rules with a new rule already set to that feed. Nothing about rules shows anywhere else until you make one.
- **The rule editor** is a sheet over Settings (on a phone, the phone sheet): Word or phrase, Look in (a choice of one), Author (optional), In (All feeds, then folders, then feeds), Then (Mute, Star or Mark as read, each with a line saying what it does), and Cancel and Save; an existing rule also has **Delete** in red words at the far left. Escape or Cancel closes it unchanged.
- **Asking first:** once a new rule is saved, if unread articles already match, the line under the group asks once, with the number: "Also mute the 37 unread articles that match?" with **Mute them** and **Not now**. What it did ("Muted 37 articles.") has **Undo**, which puts back exactly those articles.
- **Deleting a rule** happens at once, with **Undo** on the line under the group. What a rule already did stays done.
- **Muted articles ›** appears in the group once anything is muted, with how many. Its page (`/settings/muted`, the feed health page's build) lists the newest 200, each with its feed, its time and **Unmute**; an unmuted article comes back unread and stays on the page saying so, with Undo, so nothing moves. Its X or Escape goes back to Rules, to its row.
- **Search still finds muted articles,** with "· Muted" after the time. Marking a muted article unread unmutes it.
- **Other apps** (section 26) see a muted article as read, so it stays out of their unread lists too; marking it unread there unmutes it here.
- **When a rule's feed or folder goes** (unsubscribed for good, or the folder deleted; Undo for deleting a folder hands its rules back), the rule stays, matching nothing, and its sentence says "in a feed or folder that's gone". Editing it asks you to choose where it applies again. It never spreads to every feed.

**How to check it**
1. Settings › Rules › New rule…: "sponsored", The title, All feeds, Mute, Save. The rule shows as one sentence.
2. If unread articles already match, the line asks "Also mute the N unread articles that match?". Press Mute them, then Undo: they're back.
3. When an article with "sponsored" in its title arrives, it's in no list and not counted. Search for it: it's found, marked "· Muted". Settings › Rules › Muted articles lists it; press Unmute and it's back in Unread.
4. Turn a rule's switch off: it says "Paused." and stops acting. Edit › Delete, then Undo: it's back as it was.
5. In Edit feed, press "Add a rule for this feed…": the new rule is already set to that feed.

## 28. Newsletters as feeds

*Built in V2 (26 October 2026, decision 0091), in three parts: reading and cleaning emails (part one), each reader's own address with emails kept as articles (part two), then labels with switches, one-click unsubscribe and "opened" on arrival (part three). On only while `NINE_TURTLES_NEWSLETTER_SECRET` is set.*

**What it does**
- Settings has a **Newsletters** group, after Rules, with your private address, like `turtle-7k2xab3c@in.nineturtles.app`, made the first time you open Settings, and **Copy**. **New address** gives you another one at once; mail to the old one is refused from then on (for when spam arrives). The line under the group says so, with **Undo**, which brings the old address back (for an hour). The group isn't there while nine turtles takes no newsletters.
- You sign up to newsletters with that address. **Labels:** a word after a plus sign (`turtle-7k2xab3c+cooking@…`) still reaches you. Once email arrives at a label, it's listed in the group, under your address, with a **switch**; switched off ("Off." follows it), email to that label is turned away (it bounces), while your plain address and other labels still take email. Switch it on again to take its email again. Labels belong to an address: New address starts with none, and Undo brings the old address's back. The words under the group say how labels work and their catch: anyone with a labelled address can find your plain one by taking the label off, and a few sign-up forms don't accept a plus sign. At most 100 labels are listed per address; mail to more still arrives.
- The first email from a newsletter makes a feed for it in the sidebar, named after the sender; each email is an article, read, starred, searched and muted (rules apply) like any other. A newsletter feed is yours alone: if two readers take the same newsletter, each has their own feed and their own copies, because each copy carries personal links (decision 0091).
- Edit feed and Unsubscribe work as usual. **Unsubscribing also tells the newsletter to stop,** once Undo has passed (10 minutes), when its emails offer one-click unsubscribe (most big mailing services do): nine turtles sends that request once, for you. The sender's later emails are dropped for 30 days, even after Undo has passed, so a slow sender's last few don't bring the feed back. After that, an email from them (you signed up again, say) starts the feed afresh. A newsletter without one-click unsubscribe keeps its own unsubscribe link in each article.
- A newsletter is never checked in the background and isn't in the OPML file, which lists web feeds.
- Only mail sent to your address arrives. nine turtles never sends email, never replies, and never sees your own inbox. At most 200 emails, and 5 MB of newsletter text, a day reach one address (and 100 MB a day for everyone together, so a leaked address can't fill the disk); more are refused that day. Newsletter feeds count towards the 1,000 feeds a reader can follow: once you follow 1,000, emails from a new sender are dropped, while the newsletters you already have keep arriving.

**How an email becomes an article**
- **Which newsletter:** the mailing list's own name for itself (`List-Id`) when the email gives one, otherwise the sender's address. The feed's name is the sender's name as the email gives it (otherwise the list's name, otherwise the sender's domain).
- **The article:** its title is the subject ("No subject" when there's none), its date the email's date, and its "Open original" the email's own "View in browser" link when it has one (that link isn't repeated in the text). An email is never stored twice (its Message-ID is its fingerprint).
- **Cleaned for reading,** then made safe exactly like a feed article (section 7): the hidden preview text mail apps show in the inbox list, parts only Outlook shows, styles, pictures attached inside the email (which can't be shown) and invisible tracking images go; tables used only to lay the email out become plain blocks so the text flows; a real table of data, with headings, stays a table; links lose their tracking parts (`utm_…` and the like). A plain-text email becomes paragraphs, its web addresses links.
- **Respecting the publisher:** only what the email holds is shown. nine turtles never fetches the web version or anything else the email links to.
- **Opened on arrival** (the owner's choice, decision 0091): many senders drop readers who seem never to open their emails. So when a new email arrives, nine turtles loads its invisible tracking images (at most 3) once, as a mail app does when you open an email: the sender sees it was opened. Nothing that comes back is kept. Pictures in a newsletter load like any article's when you read it.
- **Any language:** emails in any character set are read, Chinese and Japanese ones included; an email that names its language (`Content-Language`) keeps it.

**How emails arrive:** Cloudflare Email Routing receives mail for `in.nineturtles.app` and a small forwarder (`cloudflare/newsletter-worker/`) posts each email to nine turtles' `/newsletters/incoming` with a shared secret. Without `NINE_TURTLES_NEWSLETTER_SECRET` that address doesn't exist. Emails over 10 MB are refused. Setup: `docs/operations/newsletters.md`.

**How to check it**
1. Settings › Newsletters shows your address. Copy it and send an email to it from your own email: within a minute it's a feed in the sidebar, named after you, with the email as its article.
2. Press New address, then Undo: the first address is back. Without Undo, an email to the old address bounces.
3. Send an email to your address with `+test` before the @: Settings › Newsletters lists that label with a switch. Switch it off and send another: it bounces back to you. Your plain address still works.
4. Unsubscribe from that feed and send another email: it doesn't arrive.

## 29. Paying for nine turtles

*Built in V2 (26 October 2026, decision 0094), in Dodo Payments' test mode: no real money until the owner applies to Dodo and switches to live. On only while the Dodo settings (`NINE_TURTLES_DODO_*`) are set; without them nobody pays and nothing below happens.*

**Who pays**
- **Never the owner, and never anyone with an account on the day this part went live** (26 October 2026; they are marked free for good). Readers who join after that, even while payments are still switched off, have the trial below once payments are switched on. Everyone who joins later has a **free trial**, 14 days unless the owner sets another length, from the day they joined (or from the day payments were switched on, if that's later). No card is asked for to start.
- After the trial: **one plan, everything included**, monthly or yearly, at the prices the owner sets (shown as "Price to be decided" until then). Payments are taken by Dodo Payments, the merchant of record (decision 0083): it takes the card, adds any sales tax and sends receipts. nine turtles never sees a card.

**Settings › Your plan** (for every reader but the owner, and not for readers who are free)
- One row says where you stand, in a few words with a line under it: **Free trial** ("Your trial ends on 9 November 2026."), **Monthly plan** or **Yearly plan** ("Renews on 26 November 2026.", or after cancelling "Cancelled. It lasts until 26 November 2026."), **Your last payment didn't go through** ("Update your card under Billing by 2 November 2026 to keep your plan."), or **Your free trial or plan has ended** (what still works, below).
- While you have no plan renewing: **Monthly · price** and **Yearly · price** rows (Yearly only once the owner sets up a yearly plan), each opening Dodo's checkout page in the same tab. Choosing a plan during the trial keeps the trial's remaining days (rounded up to a whole day): the first charge never comes before the day the trial would have ended. Dodo sends you back to Settings; if you've just paid, it can take a minute to show.
- Once you've bought a plan: **Billing and cancelling**, which opens Dodo's own page for your card, receipts and cancelling. Cancelling is yours to do, any time, with nobody to ask (the website's refunds page says so).
- The words under the group say Dodo takes the card and nine turtles never sees it. In test mode, one more line says no real money is taken and gives Dodo's test card number.
- Nothing about the plan appears anywhere else in the app: no banners, no countdowns, no emails about the trial (the "Never" list).

**When a payment fails or a plan ends**
- A renewal that doesn't go through keeps your plan for **7 days** while Dodo tries the card again. A cancelled plan lasts to the end of what you paid for.
- **Once the trial or plan has ended,** you still read, star, search and export everything you have, change settings, and can delete your account. You **can't add feeds** (the Add field, an OPML file or another app say "Your free trial or plan has ended. Choose a plan in Settings to add feeds again."), and your feeds are checked **once a day**, as for a reader who has been away for a month (section 11): a feed someone current also follows is still checked as usual. Newsletters keep arriving, new senders included (they cost nothing to fetch). Choosing a plan brings everything back at once: adding works again and the feeds are due for a check within minutes.
- **Deleting your account cancels your plan first.** If Dodo can't be reached just then, nothing is deleted, so nobody is charged for an account that's gone.

**The owner's All readers page** shows each reader's plan under their line in a few words ("Free trial", "Monthly plan", "Plan cancelled", "Payment didn't go through", "Plan ended"), never what they paid. Readers are told so under Account in Settings and in the privacy policy.

**How it works:** nine turtles asks Dodo for a checkout page (with the reader's email and number), and Dodo tells nine turtles about each subscription in signed messages (webhooks) at `/payments/dodo`. A message without Dodo's signature, or more than five minutes old, is ignored; one sent twice changes nothing, and neither does one older than the last kept for that subscription, or the end of an earlier subscription once the reader has bought a new one. Deleting an account waits only when Dodo can't be reached; if Dodo answers that the plan is already over, the account goes. nine turtles keeps its own record of each reader's subscription, so moving to another payment company stays possible. Setup: `docs/operations/payments.md`.

**How to check it** (test mode, after the setup steps)
1. Sign up as a new reader: Settings › Your plan says "Free trial" and when it ends.
2. Press Monthly: Dodo's test checkout opens. Pay with `4242 4242 4242 4242`, any future date and any three digits. Back in Settings, within a minute it says "Monthly plan" and when it renews.
3. Press Billing and cancelling and cancel: Settings says "Cancelled. It lasts until …".
4. Signed in as yourself (the owner), Settings has no Your plan row, and the All readers page shows the new reader's plan.

## 32. Reading offline

*Built 26 October 2026 (decision 0101): the lighter version the owner chose, words only.*

**What's kept.** While nine turtles can be reached, each device quietly keeps a copy of the reader's **first 200 unread articles** (in their sort order; in river mode, the first 200 of All) and their **newest 200 starred articles**. Only the words: each image becomes a short line, "An image shows here when you're back online", a YouTube article's player says "The video plays here when you're back online.", and a podcast episode's says "The episode plays here when you're back online." (section 35; the audio is never kept). For most readers that's a few megabytes. The copies are brought up to date when nine turtles opens, when the connection comes back, when it comes back into view after a while, and after Refresh, a few dozen at a time, a moment apart, so nothing waits for them. Nothing extra is fetched from publishers.

**Without a connection**
- nine turtles opens as usual, signed in (on an iPhone, reliably once it's added to the home screen: in a Safari tab the phone may clear the copies after about a week without a visit).
- Unread, Starred, All, each feed and each folder list the kept articles, with a quiet line above the list: "Offline. Showing the articles saved on this device." Such a list ends with "That's everything saved on this device."; one with nothing kept says "Nothing here is saved on this device. It will show when you're back online." The sidebar shows the feeds and folders as they were last seen, its counts following what's marked here.
- Articles open from their copy. One that isn't kept says "This article isn't saved on this device. It opens when you're back online."
- **Reading, marking read or unread, and starring work as usual**, with nothing said: each mark waits on the device and is sent once the connection is back. Each says when it was made, and **the latest mark wins**: a mark made offline at 10:00 doesn't undo one made on another device at 11:00. Undo works too.
- **What waits for a connection says so:** search ("Search needs a connection. It works again once you're back online."), adding a feed ("Adding a feed needs a connection. Try again once you're back online."), Settings ("Settings need a connection. They open once you're back online."), Refresh ("Refresh needs a connection. This is what's saved on this device."), Catch me up, Recently read ("Recently read needs a connection. It shows once you're back online."; section 5) and collections: the sidebar still lists them, as last seen, and opening one says "Collections need a connection. They show once you're back online." (section 34). Add to collection… needs a connection too; its sheet says it couldn't load. So does Up Next ("Up Next needs a connection. It shows once you're back online."; section 35).

**Switching it off, and privacy**
- Settings › Your data › **Keep articles for reading offline**, a switch, on by default on phones and computers alike. It's for that device only; switching it off deletes its copies at once, after sending any marks still waiting. Without a connection, Settings shows only this switch under its line.
- **Signing out deletes the copies**, after sending any marks still waiting, as does being signed out any other way (an expired sign-in, a deleted account), so nothing is left on a shared computer. When another reader signs in on the device without passing the sign-in page (an emailed sign-in, new-password or new-email link), the last reader's copies, waiting marks, chosen keys and player go before anything is shown or sent: their marks are never saved as the new reader's.

**How it works:** the copies live in the browser's own database for nine turtles (IndexedDB), in `web/src/lib/offline.ts`; the server gives up to 50 at once, words only, at `GET /api/articles/several?ids=…`. Each reader's entries remember when the read mark and the star were last set, and a late mark older than that changes nothing (ENGINEERING.md → "Data", decision 0101).

**How to check it**
1. Open nine turtles online and wait a minute. Switch on airplane mode.
2. Open Unread and Starred: the line says "Offline. Showing the articles saved on this device." and your articles are there. Open one: its words are there, with a short line where each image was.
3. Read one, star another, mark one unread. Nothing complains.
4. Try search, Add feed, Settings and Refresh: each says it needs a connection.
5. Close nine turtles and open it again, still in airplane mode: it opens with the same articles.
6. Switch airplane mode off. On another device (or after a reload), the marks from step 3 are there.
7. Sign out, then switch airplane mode on and open nine turtles: it says it can't be reached, because the copies are gone.

## 33. Connecting your own AI assistant (MCP)

*Added 27 October 2026 at the owner's request (decision 0111).*

nine turtles runs no AI itself. A reader can connect their own assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, or any other that speaks **MCP**, the open standard for letting an assistant use a program's tools), so it can find, sort and tidy things for them. It's a door, like the sync API for other apps (section 26), not a feature on any screen. It follows the standard only, with nothing specific to one company.

**Connecting**
- In the assistant, the reader adds nine turtles as a connector (an "MCP server") with the address shown in Settings › Connected assistants: `https://app.nineturtles.app/mcp`.
- The assistant opens nine turtles' **"Connect … to nine turtles?"** page (signing in first if needed). It names the assistant (as the assistant calls itself, shown as plain text), lists what it will be able to do and what it won't, says feeds it unsubscribes from are kept for 30 days, that it can be disconnected any time in Settings, and which website the reader goes back to. **Connect** and **Cancel**; either goes back to the assistant. A link that isn't valid (an unknown assistant, or a return address it didn't register) says so and goes nowhere.
- Approving is the opt-in: there's no switch. Each connection is one reader's and acts only for them.
- An assistant that can't show that page uses an **assistant key** instead: **Make an assistant key** in Settings (with an optional name) shows a key starting `nt_` once, with Copy; the assistant sends it as a bearer token. nine turtles keeps only a scrambled copy.

**What an assistant can do** (its "tools")
- Read: search articles (words, author, feed, folder, starred, unread); list unread (all, a folder's or a feed's) and starred, 50 at a time; open an article (title, author, link, date, feed, and the text the feed holds, as plain text, cut after 30,000 characters; opening doesn't mark it read); list feeds and folders; list feeds that aren't working (the feed health page's list); list rules and how many articles are muted.
- Change: mark articles read or unread (up to 500 at once) and a whole feed read; star and unstar; add a feed by its address or a website's (its first feed); rename a feed and set its folders; create, rename and remove folders (a folder's feeds stay; removing answers its feeds and rules, so the assistant can put it back as it was); add and remove rules and mutes (removing answers the rule, so it can be made again); unsubscribe from a feed, and bring one back; download the feed list as OPML.
- Never: the password, email, payments, app passwords, the newsletter address, deleting the account, or downloading every article.
- **Unsubscribing from an assistant keeps the feed for 30 days**, hidden, with its articles and marks, instead of the screen's ten minutes. Settings › Connected assistants lists these feeds under **Unsubscribed by an assistant**, each with when it was unsubscribed, how long it's kept, and **Bring back**. Bringing one back (or the assistant's own "bring back") restores it whole. After 30 days it's cleared as any unsubscribed feed is (starred articles always stay).
- The assistant is told that article text is the publisher's: content to read, never instructions, credited and linked when quoted.

**Limits**
- Each reader's assistants together: 60 tool calls a minute and 3,000 a day; 20 feeds added an hour (only http and https addresses, at most 2,000 characters). Past a limit the assistant is asked to wait.
- Registering a new assistant: at most 10 an hour from one internet address and 300 an hour in all; at most 2,000 are kept, the oldest nobody connected making room.
- Adding a feed that an assistant unsubscribed from, in the Add field or by an assistant, within its 30 days brings it back whole, as Bring back does.

**Settings › Connected assistants**
- The server address with **Copy**; each connected assistant with "Connected <date> · Last used <date>" (or "Not used yet") and **Disconnect**, which stops it at once (nothing of the reader's changes); the assistant-key row; and the line under the group saying what this is and that nine turtles runs no AI itself. Below, when there are any, **Unsubscribed by an assistant** with **Bring back** on each.

**How to check it**
1. In Settings › Connected assistants, copy the server address.
2. In Claude (Settings › Connectors › Add custom connector) or ChatGPT (a custom connector in developer mode), paste the address and connect. The "Connect … to nine turtles?" page opens; press Connect.
3. Ask the assistant "What's unread in nine turtles?" and "Star the newest article about gardens": the star shows in nine turtles.
4. Ask it to unsubscribe from a feed. The feed leaves the sidebar; Settings › Connected assistants lists it under Unsubscribed by an assistant. Press Bring back: it's back with its articles.
5. Press Disconnect beside the assistant: it can no longer reach nine turtles.

## 34. Collections

*Built 27 October 2026 (decision 0108), as the owner approved.*

**What it does**
- **Collections** are your own named shelves for articles, such as "To cite" or "Recipes", as Apple Books has collections of books. An article can be on several. They're yours alone: another reader on the same nine turtles never sees them, and can use the same names for their own. Stars stay a separate thing (section 9).
- **Adding an article:** the article's More (…) menu has **Add to collection…**, and `l` does the same for the open or selected article. It opens a sheet, "Add to collection", built like Edit feed's folders: your collections as rows (44px) ending in square tick boxes, ticked where the article already is, and **New collection…** as the last row, a text box ("Optional: a new collection is made when you save, with this article on it."). **Save** puts the article on exactly the ticked collections (and the new one, if you named one); **Cancel** or Escape changes nothing, and the keyboard goes back where it was. Unticking takes the article off a collection; the article itself stays where it was. If Save took it off any collection, the list's quiet line says "Took the article off Recipes" (or "… off 2 collections") with **Undo**, as other removals do: an article kept only by a collection would otherwise be left to the yearly clean-up. Undo puts it back on them. Inside a collection, an article you take off it keeps its row until the list is next loaded (nothing moves unless you move it). Two collections can't share a name ("There's already a collection with that name."). On a phone it's the same sheet, over the article.
- **In the sidebar,** under Saved searches (section 5), A to Z, each with a small shelf of books and no count. It can be hidden in Settings › Show in the sidebar.
- **Opening one** (`/collections/3`) lists its articles like any list: by each article's date, in your sort order (newest first unless you chose oldest first), 50 at a time. Its name is the list's title. It also lists muted articles and articles of feeds you've since unsubscribed from, as Starred does: you put them there. Nothing arrives on a collection by itself, so it never says "New articles". Empty, it says what collections are for: "Nothing in this collection yet. Articles you put here stay, even after a year. Add one from an article's More menu."
- **Its More (…) menu** has, after Mark all as read, Refresh and the sort order, **Rename…** (a sheet with its name, Cancel and Save) and **Delete**. Delete removes the collection and deletes no article at once, and takes you to Unread (All in river mode) with "Deleted the collection …" and **Undo**, which brings it back with the same articles on it, and your saved searches limited to it limited to it again. An article that only that collection kept (of a feed you've since unsubscribed from) is then found only by search, and the yearly clean-up removes it once it's over a year old, unless it's starred or on another collection.
- **Kept forever:** an article on any of your collections is never removed by the yearly clean-up (section 23), and unsubscribing from its feed keeps it, as it keeps starred ones: it stays on its collections and in search. Taking it off every collection (or deleting the collection) lets the clean-up treat it as usual again.
- **Search:** `in:name` searches one collection (section 14).
- **Exports:** Settings › Your data › **Export collections** (section 23).
- **Offline:** the sidebar lists your collections as last seen; opening one says it needs a connection (section 32).
- The article's byline doesn't list its collections: the sheet shows them in one press.

**How to check it**
1. Open an article and press `l`. Type "Recipes" in New collection… and press Enter. "Recipes" appears in the sidebar under Collections.
2. Open another article, choose More › Add to collection…, tick Recipes and Save.
3. Choose Recipes in the sidebar: both articles are there, newest first, with no number anywhere.
4. In Recipes, More › Rename…: call it "Soups" and Save. The sidebar follows.
5. More › Delete: you're back at Unread with "Deleted the collection Soups"; both articles are still in their feed. Press Undo: Soups is back with both articles.
6. Unsubscribe from the feed of an article on Soups: after the Undo has passed, the article is still in Soups.
7. In Soups, open an article, press `l`, untick Soups and Save: the line at the foot of the list says "Took the article off Soups" with Undo, and the article's row stays until you reload. Press Undo: it's on Soups again.
8. Sign in as another reader: none of these collections are there.

## 35. Podcasts

*Built 27 October 2026 (decision 0113), as the owner approved.*

**What it does**
- **A podcast is a feed like any other.** Follow it by pasting its feed (or its website) into Add a feed. An article whose feed item carries an audio file (an RSS `enclosure`, an Atom `enclosure` link or a JSON Feed `attachment` whose type is `audio/…`, or, with no type, whose address ends in .mp3, .m4a, .aac, .ogg or .opus) is an **episode**. nine turtles keeps only the file's web address (http or https), its type and size, its length (from `itunes:duration` or the feed's own media details) and its picture (the episode's, else the podcast's). **It never downloads, keeps or passes on the audio:** the reader's browser plays the publisher's file from the publisher's address, as YouTube videos play in YouTube's own player (decision 0072). A podcast followed before 27 October 2026 shows its episodes as episodes once its feed next changes (usually with its next episode): a feed that hasn't changed isn't read again.
- **In the list,** an episode's meta line ends with a small headphones icon and its length ("45 min", "1 hr 5 min"), after the time; screen readers hear "Episode, 45 min".
- **The player** sits under the byline, where a YouTube video's box would, in a calm panel in a faint tone of the text with softly rounded corners: **Back 15 seconds**, a round **Play / Pause** in the text color, **Forward 30 seconds** (the 15 and the 30 drawn inside their arrows large enough to read, about 11px, and each button's name in its tooltip), then the episode's length (only until it starts playing: then the times under the slider say it, so it steps aside) and the **speed** (1×, 1.25×, 1.5×, 2×; each press moves to the next, and this device remembers it). Under them, a slider for the position, with the time played on the left and the time left ("−12:34") on the right, in figures that keep their width. Every control is a real button or slider of at least 44px, named for screen readers; the slider says its time in words ("12 minutes 3 seconds of 45 minutes"). Nothing moves except the slider's mark and the times: no waveform, no animation. While the file starts, a quiet line says "Loading from the publisher…". If it can't be played: "This episode couldn't be played. Open the original to listen on the publisher's site." The show notes are the article's text; an episode with none ends with the closing rule. When the feed shares only a summary of them, the link under the closing rule says "Read the episode page on {podcast} ›" (section 7).
- **Keys:** none new. Space keeps scrolling the article, as everywhere (section 19); the player's buttons and slider answer to Enter, Space and the arrow keys only while the keyboard is on them.
- **It keeps playing while you read.** One episode plays at a time, for the whole app. Once one is playing (or paused), a slim **Now playing** bar sits along the bottom of the app, taking its own room so it never covers the list or the article: Back 15 seconds, Play / Pause, the episode's title and its podcast's name (a button that opens the episode) and **Close the player**. On a phone, while an article is open, it sits just above the article's toolbar, and it steps aside while the same episode's own player is on screen, so the episode never shows twice; scrolled past the player, or on another article, it's back. Closing it stops the episode and keeps your place.
- **Your place is kept, on all your devices.** Where you are is saved about every 15 seconds while playing, and whenever you pause, move the slider or leave nine turtles: on this device at once, and on the server, for you alone. Opening the episode anywhere shows where you stopped, and Play carries on from there; the newer of the two saves wins. Played (or paused, or moved with the slider) to within 30 seconds of its end, an episode is **finished**: it's marked read, and it starts from the beginning if you play it again. **Signing out** stops the episode, closes the bar and forgets this device's places and Up Next, so whoever signs in next on it starts from nothing (as offline copies are deleted, section 32).
- **The lock screen and headphones** show the episode's title, its podcast and its picture, and their play, pause and skip buttons work (where the browser offers this, the Media Session API).
- **Up Next** is your own short queue of episodes. An episode's More (…) menu has **Add to Up Next** (or **Remove from Up Next**); a quiet line confirms it. While it has something, **Up Next** shows in the sidebar after Recently read (section 5), listing the episodes in their order, with no count; empty, it leaves the sidebar ("Nothing in Up Next. Add an episode from its More menu." if its address is opened). An episode in it says so in its player ("In Up Next, 2 of 3"), with **Move up**, **Move down** and **Remove** buttons: no dragging. At either end, Move up or Move down stays put, dimmed, and does nothing, so the keyboard isn't lost. **Remove** (in the player or the More menu) leaves "Removed from Up Next." with **Undo** in the player, the keyboard on Undo; Undo puts the episode back in its place. When an episode ends, the first one in Up Next starts by itself and comes off the list, and a quiet line says "Playing next: …" (if the browser won't start it without a press: "Next: …. Press Play to start it."); when nothing is next, it says "Finished.". Playing an episode yourself also takes it off Up Next. These two automatic removals have no Undo (Add to Up Next puts one back). **Nothing moves by itself:** an open Up Next list changes only for your own Move, Remove, Undo or Refresh, so an episode that started stays listed until then; and once Up Next empties, its sidebar row stays until you next go somewhere else. Up Next keeps at most 200 episodes, and leaves out those of feeds you've unsubscribed from, in its list, its count ("2 of 3") and what plays next. Its More menu has only Refresh. It can be hidden from the sidebar in Settings › Show in the sidebar (section 18).
- **Offline** (section 32): a kept episode's notes show, and its player says "The episode plays here when you're back online." Up Next needs a connection.

**How to check it**
1. Add a podcast's feed. Its episodes show a headphones icon and their length in the list.
2. Open one and press Play: it plays, the times count, the length beside the speed steps aside, and the Now playing bar appears at the bottom (on a phone, only once the episode's own player is off the screen). Move to another article: it keeps playing.
3. Pause, then reload nine turtles (or open it on your phone): the episode opens where you stopped, and Play carries on from there.
4. Press the speed until it says 1.5×: it plays faster. Back 15 and Forward 30 move by those amounts.
5. On another episode, choose More › Add to Up Next: Up Next appears in the sidebar. Press Remove in its player, then Undo: it's back in Up Next. Play the first episode to its end (or move the slider near the end): the next one starts by itself, the first is marked read, and Up Next empties; its sidebar row stays until you open another list, then leaves.
6. Lock your phone while one plays: the lock screen shows the title and picture, and pause works there.
7. Sign out while an episode plays: it stops and the bar goes. Sign in as another reader: none of your places or your Up Next are there.
