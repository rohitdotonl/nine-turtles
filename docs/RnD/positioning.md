# nine turtles: positioning

Decided with Rohit on 1 November 2026. It replaces every earlier
positioning idea ("a reader that ends", owning the word "finished",
newsletters as the way in, "Read people, not feeds"), which were deleted
on purpose. Who it's for is in [`ideal-reader.md`](ideal-reader.md).

## In one breath

nine turtles is **a reader for the small web**: the place you go instead
of social media to read the people worth knowing, and write back to them.

## The home page, first draft

The words will change; the shape is decided.

> **Read people. Write back.**
> There are people online you'd like to know. Most of them have a blog, a
> newsletter or a channel. nine turtles keeps them in one quiet place,
> helps you write back to them, and ends when you've read what's there.
> No algorithm, no likes, no AI in between. Built on RSS, the open
> standard, so your list is always yours.

- **Headline:** "Read people. Write back." (Rohit, 2 November 2026; it
  replaced "The good part of the internet, in one quiet place."). The
  headline and the first line are kept in [`../BRAND.md`](../BRAND.md);
  this paragraph is the rest, used word for word.
- **No A/B testing yet.** It needs thousands of visitors per version.
  Before then, try lines in public posts and see which ones people repeat.
  Never test inside the app.

## What we call it, and what we don't

- **Category: "a reader for the small web".** Keep the word "reader";
  never say "feed reader" or "RSS reader" (as Apple never called the
  MacBook a laptop). "Feed", "subscribe" and "RSS" stay out of headlines
  and first lines. **RSS appears once, lower down,** only to explain that
  the reader's list is always theirs.
- **Our word is "write back",** not "finished". "A reader that ends" is
  gone as a message; ending stays true of the app, said in passing.
- "You keep who you find" is how we think about it, not a line for copy.

## Who we compete with

The real alternative is **social media at idle time** (Instagram, X,
YouTube), not other readers. Saying "RSS reader" puts us next to free
apps and loses on price; "instead of scrolling" wins on everything the
ideal reader cares about. The nearest product is the Substack app (follow
people, read, reply), but owned by one company and built for attention.
We never name a company in copy: the enemy is a behaviour.

## What makes it true

1. **People, not sources:** a writer's blog, newsletter, videos and
   podcast together under their name.
2. **Writing back is built in:** reply by email from the article,
   privately. No "Sent from nine turtles" line, ever (Rohit, 1 November).
3. **You keep who you find:** share any page from a blog into nine turtles
   and it asks "Follow this person?" (built, decision 0138). Nothing is saved as a link.
4. **It ends:** no endless scroll, no counts, nothing to check.
5. **Nobody in between:** no algorithm, no AI rewriting or ranking, no
   likes; export everything in one click.
6. **Made by a person you can write to:** Rohit is the face.

Person pages and writing back are what the headline promises, so they
are V1 musts (`docs/PLAN.md`, decision 0140).

## What we said no to

- **Read later (like Raindrop or Pocket):** no. Saving any page means
  copying the publisher's web page, which the floor forbids, and saved
  piles become the next guilt list. The star keeps articles from people
  you follow.
- **A blogroll to show (on the reader's site or a page on nine turtles):**
  no. Rohit, 2 November 2026: it feels like social media. Not to be
  suggested again.
