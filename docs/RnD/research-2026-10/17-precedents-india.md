## Part A: precedents

1. Derek Sivers, CD Baby order email. CONFIRMED (with a caveat). Sivers wrote it himself, in about 20 minutes, as the automated "your CD has shipped" email. It begins "Your CD has been gently taken from our CD Baby shelves with sterilized contamination-free gloves", mentions a packing specialist from Japan lighting a candle, and says it left "in our private CD Baby jet on this day, Friday, June 6th", and "Your picture is on our wall as Customer of the Year". Customers posted it on their sites; a Google search for "private CD Baby jet" gave over 20,000 results; Sivers says it "created thousands of new customers". Caveat: the Ferriss page does not give the year, and I only saw excerpts, not the full text. https://tim.blog/2012/05/31/the-most-successful-e-mail-i-ever-wrote/ (also listed, not read: https://searchengineland.com/how-cdbaby-built-20000-citations-with-one-e-mail-84357)
2. nownownow.com. PARTLY CONFIRMED. The About page gives no count. The home page lists 191 locations whose per-location counts sum to about 4,201 /now pages (my own addition, as fetched 2026-10-25; the page states no total). Say "about 4,000+". https://nownownow.com/ and https://nownownow.com/about
3. Brandon Sanderson, 2022 Kickstarter. CONFIRMED. Closed at more than $41.7 million; over 185,000 backers in the article text (its summary says 181,000+, so say "about 185,000"); passed Pebble's $20.3 million 2015 record in three days; 30-day campaign; four novels written in the pandemic. Also on Guinness: https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/108388-most-money-pledged-for-a-kickstarter-project (listed, not read). https://www.cnbc.com/2022/03/31/authors-record-breaking-kickstarter-campaign-closes-at-41point7-million.html
4. Tarsnap picodollars. CONFIRMED. Storage 250 picodollars per byte-month of encoded data (= $0.25 per GB-month); bandwidth 250 picodollars per byte (= $0.25 per GB). Prepaid balance, no fixed or minimum monthly fee, charged on actual usage after dedup/compression/encryption. (The pricing.html page failed; this is from the home page.) https://www.tarsnap.com/
5. Sublime Text unlimited evaluation. CONFIRMED. The download page says you can evaluate for free, a license must be bought for continued use, and "There is currently no enforced time limit for the evaluation." Nothing on nags or price on that page. https://www.sublimetext.com/download
6. MetaFilter $5. CONFIRMED. One-time US$5 to create an account (PayPal by default; fee waiver on request). Wikipedia: Matt Haughey reopened signups on 18 Nov 2004 with a $5 lifetime fee after repeated closures from growth; Time (2009) credited the fee with keeping the site "remarkably free of trolls"; about 47,691 active members as of May 2024. The stated purpose of the fee is not in the Wikipedia article. Correction to any "2001" claim: the fee dates from 2004. https://www.metafilter.com/newuser.mefi and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetaFilter
7. Craig Mod membership. CONFIRMED, with a correction. It is called the SPECIAL PROJECTS Membership Program (renamed from "Explorers Club" in July 2020; started 2019). Price: $10/month, $100/year, $1,500 lifetime; free tier for students on request. The current page says "thousands" of members; the 2023 page said "1,000+". Revenue and retention are not disclosed. "Ridgeline" appears to be the name of his newsletter (search results show craigmod.com/ridgeline/057 "The Dreaded Membership Issue"), not the program; I did not open that page. https://craigmod.com/membership/ and https://craigmod.com/membership/2023
8. Aza Raskin "behavioral cocaine". CONFIRMED. Quote: "It's as if they're taking behavioral cocaine and just sprinkling it all over your interface", which keeps users "coming back and back and back." Said to the BBC (Panorama), per BGR (2018). Raskin designed infinite scroll in 2006 while at Humanized. I did not read the BBC original. https://bgr.com/2018/07/04/social-media-addicts-us-like-cocaine/
9. Light Phone "lending story". NOT VERIFIED. I found no lending or loaning story. What I did find: founders Joe Hollier and Kaiwei Tang met in 2014 in Google's 30 Weeks incubator; early on they gave people flip phones plus a short contact list for a Saturday or weekend to test disconnecting (TechCrunch 2019). Wikipedia: Light Phone 1 raised $415,127 from 3,187 backers on Kickstarter (announced 13 May 2015); Light Phone II raised $3,513,838 from 10,732 backers. Do not cite a "lending" story unless the owner has a source. https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/04/light-phones-founders-discuss-life-beyond-the-smartphone and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_Phone

## Part B: India

### B1. UPSC aspirants
- Scale. About 11.36 lakh applicants (2022), about 13 lakh (2023), about 13.4 lakh (2024, an estimate; the source says no official figure). Final selections are about 1,000 a year (933 in 2022, 1,016 in 2023, 1,056 recommended in 2024). So roughly 1.3 million people a year prepare for about 1,000 seats. Source: https://www.pw.live/upsc/exams/how-many-candidates-applied-for-upsc-2024 (a coaching company's page; secondary).
- How they read. Search results across coaching sites (BYJU'S, Rau's IAS, Vajiram, Drishti-style blogs, The Better India topper tips) consistently name The Hindu and The Indian Express (editorials and Explained) as core daily reading, plus PIB, PRS and the monthly Yojana. I did not open these pages, so treat the pattern as UNVERIFIED in detail. Search hit examples: https://byjus.com/free-ias-prep/how-to-read-newspaper-for-ias-exam/ and https://compass.rauias.com/blog/how-read-the-hindu-newspaper-upsc/
- Feeds (checked as far as the blocks allowed):
  - PIB: real RSS 2.0 feed (e.g. https://pib.gov.in/RssMain.aspx?ModId=6&Lang=1&Regid=3), 20 items, but each item has only a title (Hindi in the version I got) and a link. No description, no full text. Read directly.
  - The Hindu: per feeder.co's catalogue, 79 working feeds, e.g. https://www.thehindu.com/feeder/default.rss and https://www.thehindu.com/opinion/editorial/feeder/default.rss. Items carry a headline, a one-sentence summary and a picture, not the full article. Secondary source; not read at thehindu.com. https://feeder.co/knowledge-base/rss-content/the-hindu-rss-feeds/
  - The Indian Express: feeds exist at https://indianexpress.com/section/explained/feed and https://indianexpress.com/section/opinion/feed (per feedspot); there is no separate editorials feed in that listing. Full text vs summary: UNVERIFIED. https://rss.feedspot.com/theindianexpress_rss_feeds/
  - PRS Legislative Research: UNVERIFIED. A PRS blog page showed no RSS link (only Facebook, Twitter, YouTube). Check prsindia.org by hand.
  - Implication: the main UPSC sources give summaries, not full text, so a reader would link out to the publisher (which fits "never scrape"). Full-text reading would not work for the biggest papers.
- Prices of prep services (secondary). IAS Gyan lists four one-year plans at Rs 1,083 / 1,667 / 3,500 / 4,000 per month, bundling tests, classes and daily current-affairs articles. https://www.iasgyan.in/subscription . I could not verify prices for Vision IAS, Insights or Unacademy; many of their daily current-affairs notes are free. UNVERIFIED.
- Takeaway: this audience is large and pays for coaching, but it wants curated notes and tests, not a calm reader. A "finite daily list" with the PIB/Hindu/Express feeds could appeal, but only as a free-form tool, and the best sources only give summaries.

### B4. Willingness to pay, to set an Indian price
- Google/Kantar "Indian News Consumer: Willingness to Pay" (study 2024): about 1 in 7 news users would pay for online news, about 1 in 5 among those who use publisher sites/apps. No price points in the page I read. https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/the_indian_news_consumer_willingness_to_pay_and_key_drivers.pdf
- Kindle Unlimited India launched 3 Sep 2015 at Rs 199/month (Rs 999 for six months, Rs 1,799 for a year), about one million titles. Today's price UNVERIFIED (search snippets showed promotional Rs 99/149 for three months). https://techenclave.com/threads/amazon-kindle-unlimited-launched-in-india-for-rs-199-a-month.176007/
- Reference points: Rs 69 a month was cited by one respondent as a news plan; UPSC prep bundles run Rs 1,000 to 4,000 a month (above). No ARPU report found. Suggested for the owner: price India separately, with a figure in the low hundreds of rupees a month or a yearly price near one or two months of Kindle Unlimited, but this is judgement, not a sourced number. Payment friction note from search: Substack payments for Indian writers have had problems (https://manoj.digitalpress.blog/monetizing-writing-on-substack-the-payment-challenges-faced-by-indian-writers-2/, not read), so check UPI/cards support for any Indian price.
