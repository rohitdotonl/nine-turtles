# nine turtles: wild idea bank (110 ideas)

Provenance note: I did no WebSearch or WebFetch, so nothing here is [V] verified. [K] means recalled from memory and unchecked, and [N] means my own invention. Check any [K] precedent before quoting it publicly.
Effort is S (a day or less), M (a week or two), L (a month or more). "Fit" is the check against the book-not-feed ethos.

## A. Radical transparency and anti-feature PR

1. **The Refusal Ledger.** [N] Publish a page listing every feature nine turtles has declined (streaks, unread counts, "trending", push). Put a bounty on it: "If we ever send you a notification, we pay you $50, and here is the contract." It uses the repo and its public decisions, which are already assets. It could earn a few hundred upvotes on HN and Reddit. Effort S. Fit: perfect. The bounty must be real and cheap to honor, because the app can't send notifications at all.
2. **Museum of Dead Social Features.** [N] A small web exhibit with a card for each retired feature (the pull-to-refresh slot machine, the red badge, Snapstreaks, Stories). Each card has a plain-language note on why it died here. It is shareable and gets linked by design and tech writers. Effort M. Fit: strong, as long as the tone stays curious and not smug.
3. **Annual "Nothing New" release.** [N] Once a year, ship a release whose notes say only "We fixed things and added nothing." It is a counter-programmed changelog and a quotable joke. Effort S. Fit: perfect.
4. **Public decisions as content.** [K] The repo already has numbered decision notes. Publish a curated "Decision of the month" in plain English, covering why no animations and why finite lists. It builds trust and links, and few products show their reasoning. Effort S. Fit: great.
5. **The build-in-public-by-a-non-coder diary.** [N] Short weekly posts titled "What I asked the agent to do and what it did." Non-coders building real software with AI is a story many people want. Effort M. Fit: good. Avoid hype and metrics theatre.
6. **Open revenue page, but slow.** [K] Baremetrics-style open startup numbers, updated once a month, not live. Live numbers would feel like a feed. Effort S. Fit: fine if monthly.
7. **"What we don't know" page.** [N] Publish open questions (pricing, India demand, what you'd cancel for). Invite email replies. Effort S. Fit: great.
8. **Anti-metrics promise.** [N] "We do not track what you read, and here is the table schema that proves it." Link the database schema. Effort S. Fit: perfect.
9. **A "Why it costs $48" receipt.** [N] An itemized yearly cost breakdown per reader (hosting, backups, the owner's time). Effort S. Fit: great.
10. **Stated end-of-life plan.** [N] Publish what happens if the owner stops: export, open code, a 12-month notice. A "built to last ten years" claim is rare. Effort S. Fit: great.

## B. Rituals, events and calendar hooks

11. **Sunday Reading Hour.** [N] A named hour (say 10-11am IST and a second slot for US) when people read at their own place, quietly, together. It has no chat and no video. People post nothing live. They only sign a visible, counted guest book afterward, once a month. Effort M. Fit: strong if the guest book isn't a feed.
12. **World Turtle Day (23 May) "Read Slowly Week."** [N] A themed week with a short reading list ("nine long reads"), turtle facts and a free week for anyone new. Effort M. Fit: good. Make the free week not time-limited per person.
13. **Read the Web Week.** [N] A yearly week where blogs on the open web are encouraged to post their "blogroll" and readers are encouraged to subscribe to nine new things. Effort M. Fit: good, since it serves writers too.
14. **Slow Reading Day on a quiet date.** [N] Pick a day (the winter solstice, or the first Sunday of January) as an annual "Day of the Long Read." One page, no event, lots of linking. Effort S. Fit: great.
15. **Digital Sabbath partner day.** [K] Piggyback on the National Day of Unplugging (March) with a "read, don't scroll" angle. Effort S. Fit: good.
16. **Finish Line card.** [N] When a reader finishes their list, show a plain "You're done for today" page with a download-as-image card ("Finished 9 of 9. Gone outside."). People who share it do so voluntarily. Effort S. Fit: strong, but never nudge sharing and never count streaks.
17. **Reading-room opening hours.** [N] A page that says "nine turtles is quietest from 6 to 8am IST." It is an anti-urgency joke that doubles as a brand voice piece. Effort S. Fit: great.
18. **Annual "Return of the Blog" festival listing.** [N] A directory page of a few hundred blogs that announced themselves. Effort L. Fit: good.
19. **Letter-writing month.** [N] In February, encourage subscribers to write one email reply to a writer they read. This builds writer goodwill. Effort S. Fit: great.
20. **Monsoon reading list.** [N] A seasonal Indian list ("rain, chai, long reads"). Seasonal content is low-effort and shareable in India. Effort S. Fit: good.

## C. Offline and physical

21. **Bookmarks in independent bookshops.** [N] Letterpress-style bookmarks with one sentence ("Read the web like a book") and a short URL. Ask 20 indie shops to leave them by the till. A bookmark is what people actually keep. Effort M. Fit: perfect.
22. **Cafe table cards.** [N] A small card for cafes and co-working spaces: "Put your phone away. Here is a reading list for the next hour." Effort M. Fit: good.
23. **Stickers for laptop lids and phones.** [K] Inspired by developer-sticker culture. A nine-turtle shell sticker with a hidden nine in the pattern. Effort S. Fit: fine.
24. **Reading room pop-up.** [N] One afternoon in a Delhi, Pune or Bangalore cafe where a phone check-in desk holds your phone and you get a chair and a printed reading list. Effort L. Fit: great, but skip a camera-heavy format.
25. **Jaipur Literature Festival outskirts.** [N] Stand outside, not inside. Hand out bookmarks and a tiny 8-page zine to people queuing. Effort M. Fit: good, if it stays low-key.
26. **College library notice boards.** [K] A well-designed A4 poster in campus libraries and hostels; it's cheap and nobody is doing it. Effort S. Fit: good.
27. **Train and metro reading cards.** [N] Short cards for commuters in Delhi, Mumbai and Bangalore metro: "40 minutes? Here are nine worth reading." Effort M. Fit: good. Avoid ad-like clutter.
28. **Little free library inserts.** [K] Leave a bookmark in street book-exchange boxes. Effort S. Fit: perfect.
29. **Printed "Reading Passport."** [N] A small card where a bookshop stamps each visit. It isn't tied to the app. Effort M. Fit: skip if it looks like gamification. Borderline.

## E. Pricing and offers

41. **Chai-a-month pricing.** [N] Frame it as "Rs 349 a year, less than a chai a week." In India that comparison is instantly understood. Effort S. Fit: great.
42. **Pay what you read.** [N] Pay by how many articles you finished. Rejected as primary pricing: it would turn reading into a counter, which conflicts with the ethos. Effort L. Fit: conflict. Listed to document why not.
43. **Pay by number of feeds.** [N] Rejected for the same reason: it nudges you to follow more or less for money.
44. **"Free if you can't pay," on trust.** [K] Some indie products do this (Plausible-style goodwill programs, Tailscale-ish honor systems). One email gets a free year; no proof. Effort S. Fit: great.
45. **Yearly price that doesn't change for you.** [K] "Price lock for life" for the first 300 readers. Not a countdown: it applies to everyone who joins before a stated size, and the number is honest. Effort S. Fit: borderline. Keep it stated as a number, never a ticker.
46. **Refund on the honest ground.** [N] "If you didn't read more in 60 days, email us and we'll refund." Few apps tie a refund to a feeling. Effort S. Fit: great.
47. **Renewal without a surprise.** [N] Email once, 14 days before renewal, with the one-click cancel link at the top. Likely earns an unusual amount of goodwill. Effort S. Fit: perfect.
48. **Pause button, not cancel.** [N] "Pause for 3 months, your list waits." Effort M. Fit: great.
49. **Family plan as a "household shelf."** [N] Two readers, separate walls, one price. Fits the multi-reader work. Effort M. Fit: good.
50. **Teachers and librarians free.** [N] Keeps goodwill with people who recommend. Effort S. Fit: good.

## F. Challenges and campaigns

51. **The Feed Fast.** [N] A 14-day challenge: delete social apps, read only nine turtles and books. Include a printable one-page guide, no leaderboards, no streak. Effort M. Fit: good, but the challenge must not count days in the app.
52. **"Read nine long things this month."** [N] A reading challenge with a printed checklist, which sits outside the app. Effort S. Fit: good; counts could slide into gamification, so keep it paper.
53. **One-week email course, "RSS in 7 emails."** [N] The newcomer course on what a feed is and why it's old and good. Most target readers have never heard of RSS, so this is the top-of-funnel. Effort M. Fit: great.
54. **Phone-free hour pledge.** [N] A signable pledge page with no number shown. Effort S. Fit: okay.
55. **"Unsubscribe Day."** [N] A yearly day when you're encouraged to cut feeds. A good PR angle: a reader app that tells you to read less. Effort S. Fit: great.
56. **Swap your For You page for a shelf.** [N] A short blog post and printable that shows what a person's day looks like. Effort S. Fit: good.
57. **"Where did my 4 hours go?" calculator.** [N] A free calculator of screen time versus books; no signup. Effort S. Fit: good; avoid guilt.
58. **Read-aloud night.** [N] Monthly, small, in-person or in a voice room where someone reads a blog post. Effort M. Fit: good.
59. **"One blog a week" campaign.** [N] Each week, one blog is recommended to everyone. Effort S. Fit: great.
60. **Old-web scavenger hunt.** [N] A puzzle across 9 small blogs with a prize book. Effort M. Fit: borderline, since it can feel like a game. Keep it playful but prize-light.

## G. Referral and sharing that fits calm

66. **Footer on exported lists.** [N] When someone exports OPML, the file name carries a quiet credit. Effort S. Fit: fine.

68. **Word of mouth by teaching.** [N] One-page handout "How to explain RSS to your mother." Effort S. Fit: good.

## Conflicts to avoid
Pay-what-you-read (#42), pay-by-feeds (#43), reader-to-reader postcards (#64) and the scavenger hunt (#60) risk gamification or social pressure. Short vertical video (#86) and streaming radio (#84) sit close to feed culture. Treat the rest as compatible if the tone stays quiet.
