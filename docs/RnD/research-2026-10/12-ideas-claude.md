# Ideas from the lead researcher (Claude)

## A. Side doors and integrations

1. **Speak Feedbin's sync language.** [N] nine turtles already syncs with Reeder and NetNewsWire through the Google Reader method. Feedbin's own method is supported by more apps, including Current. If nine turtles spoke it too, Current users who want a web and Windows version could choose nine turtles as their sync service, and each app's developer could be asked to list it by name. This is how Feedbin itself grew. Effort L. Needs Rohit's decision and a check that those apps accept a custom server.
2. **Save to Readwise, Obsidian and Notion.** [N] Your reader already pays for Readwise and Notion. A "send to Readwise" or "save to Obsidian" button, and a listing on those tools' integration pages, puts nine turtles in front of their users at the moment they think about reading. Obsidian's community plugin directory is browsed by exactly this crowd. Effort M. Needs a spec change.
3. **"Support this writer" links from the feed itself.** [N] Many feeds already include a payment or funding link (podcast feeds use a funding tag, Atom allows a payment link). Show it quietly under each article: "Support Asha's writing". Nobody shows this, writers would love the app for it and tell their readers, and it respects publishers. Effort M. Needs a spec change.
4. **Sponsor the open-source feed tools.** [N] $5 to $10 a month to NetNewsWire, Kill the Newsletter or the JSON Feed project through GitHub Sponsors. It's thanks for the commons nine turtles stands on, and sponsor lists are seen by the most influential people in the feed world. Effort S.

## B. Writers and publishers (the supply side)

5. **"Your feed is broken" letters.** [N] When feeds that readers follow fail (counted across everyone, never revealing who reads what), write to the blog's author: "Your feed has been failing since Tuesday; here's the likely fix." Writers remember who helped them, and many will mention it. Effort S per letter, once the health data exists.
6. **"Follow instead of unsubscribing" footer line for newsletter writers.** [N] Writers lose readers to inbox fatigue. Offer them a line: "Too much email? Read this in a quiet reader instead." It solves their problem and the reader's at once. Effort S.
7. **Calm design guidelines, published for other makers.** [N] Turn DESIGN.md's floors and "Never" list into a standalone guide: "How to design software that ends." Designers and makers share guides like this, and every share credits nine turtles. Effort M.
8. **The agent-built project kit.** [N] Publish Rohit's way of running a software project with AI agents (AGENTS.md, decision notes, checks, second-agent review) as a template anyone can copy. The AI-builder audience is huge and links generously; the kit's readme points to nine turtles as the living example. Effort M.

## C. Communities and movements that already exist

9. **Silent Book Club and phone-free reading parties.** [K] Silent Book Club has chapters in many cities where people meet to read quietly; groups like The Offline Club and Reading Rhythms run phone-free reading evenings. Offer organisers a free year and a short "read the web like a book" handout. These people have already chosen calm reading. Silent Book Club has about 2,000 chapters in 55+ countries (checked 25 Oct). Effort M.
10. **Calm Tech certification.** [K] The Calm Tech Institute (founded by Amber Case, who wrote Calm Technology) began certifying products as "calm". It exists (81 criteria in six categories; reMarkable and Mudita are certified), but it looks hardware-focused and the fee isn't published, so ask whether an app can apply. Effort M.
11. **Digital wellbeing coaches and "phone-free" schools movements.** [N] Coaches who help people leave social media need a "what to do instead" tool to recommend. Offer them a free account and a page they can send clients. No medical claims. Effort M.
12. **Time Well Spent and humane-tech directories.** [K] The Center for Humane Technology and similar groups keep resource lists for people trying to change their tech habits. A respectful request to be listed costs one email. Effort S.

## D. Retention that markets itself

13. **The exit that earns trust.** [N] When someone cancels, hand them their export in one click and, if they want, a short list of other good readers that might suit them better. People tell stories about companies that let them go gracefully, and some come back. Effort S.
14. **An anniversary letter.** [N] Once a year on the day someone joined, a short personal note: what changed in nine turtles this year and what was refused. No stats about their reading (those are banned). Effort S, automated.
15. **"Finished" as the screenshot.** [N] Make the end-of-list screen quietly beautiful: the picture people post after a good morning of reading. No share button, no streak. Effort S.

## E. Bigger bets for later

16. **A small team reading room.** [N] Research teams, newsrooms and small studios follow industry blogs together. A later "reading room for five" plan could raise revenue per account without ads. It changes the single-reader design, so it's a later decision, not now. Effort L.
17. **The Almanac.** [N] Once a year, a beautifully typeset book (print on demand) of essays from the open web, with every writer's permission and a share of proceeds to them. A gift object, a press story and a love letter to writers. Effort L.
18. **A Hindi version of the website.** [N] India has hundreds of millions of Hindi readers and almost no one explains calm web reading in Hindi. Start with the home page and the "new to feeds" page. Effort M with a native editor.
19. **Exam aspirants' daily news desk.** [N] UPSC and other Indian exam aspirants read the same newspapers and government sources every day and drown in apps and Telegram channels. A finite daily list of The Hindu, Indian Express, PIB and PRS feeds is a genuine fit and a very large audience in your home market. Effort M. Check which sources publish full feeds.
20. **Lottery tickets.** [N] A listener question to Cal Newport's Deep Questions podcast about calm feed reading; a note to the library that ran "Stop Doom Scrolling with RSS"; a free year for the bloggers whose posts on unread guilt are quoted most. Each costs an email and could reach thousands of the right people. Effort S.

## F. Posts that work like the Museum

21. **An Obituary for the Unread Count.** [N] A eulogy for the little number: born in email, raised by Google Reader's "1000+", died of guilt. Funny, shareable, and it argues for the finite list without a sales line. Effort S.
22. **A Field Guide to Attention Predators.** [N] Naturalist-style entries for each attention trap (Scrollus infinitus: habitat everywhere, feeds at night), with a drawing and a note on how nine turtles avoids it. Effort M.
23. **The Last Page of the Internet.** [N] A web page that genuinely ends: "You've reached the end. Go outside." A tiny, linkable joke that is also the product's promise. Effort S.
24. **"You're All Caught Up": the rise and fall.** [K] Instagram added a "You're all caught up" message in 2018, then buried it under suggested posts. A short history of software that once let you finish. Check dates before publishing. Effort S.
25. **The Price of Free.** [N] Roughly what an ad-funded social app earns from one user a year, beside $48 for a reader. Needs careful, cited numbers; striking if done honestly. Effort S.
26. **From Commonplace Books to RSS.** [N] 500 years of people keeping lists of what's worth reading, ending with nine turtles as the latest page. A visual essay. Effort M.
27. **Things We Built and Then Deleted.** [N] An honest log of features that failed the "book or feed?" test. Rare candour, loved by makers. Effort S.
28. **Letters to a Young Blogger.** [N] A riff on Rilke's letters: why write on your own site in 2026. Writers share it, and they are the people nine turtles readers follow. Effort S.
29. **30 Days of Reading Like It's 2005.** [N] Rohit's diary of reading only through feeds for a month, the good and the dull. Effort M.
30. **A Gallery of Beautiful Endings.** [N] The best "that's everything" moments in software and books, from colophons to "The End". Effort M.
