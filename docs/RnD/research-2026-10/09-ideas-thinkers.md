# 09 - Ideas from thinkers (non-SaaS marketers) for nine turtles

Provenance note: I did NOT verify anything live (no WebSearch/WebFetch was run in this pass). Every idea is [K] = recalled from my knowledge of the authors' work. Names of posts/books are well known, but quotes are paraphrased; check before quoting publicly. Effort: S = hours, M = days, L = weeks. "10 customers" = why it could plausibly yield at least 10 paying readers.

---

## A. Derek Sivers

1. **The order-confirmation email as a love letter [K].** Source: CD Baby's "your CD has been placed gently into the hands of a CD Baby employee..." email (Anything You Want). Rewrite the post-purchase and trial-welcome email as a small piece of writing in Rohit's voice: "your subscription has been placed gently in a hammock; nine turtles will now carry your reading list at turtle speed." Effort S. 10 customers: people forward and quote delightful emails; this is the one piece of copy that spreads on its own, and it costs nothing.

2. **Reply to every email, publicly say so [K].** Source: Sivers answers all mail personally. Put "Write to me; Rohit replies, usually within a day" on the pricing page and in every footer, from a real address. Effort S. 10 customers: for a $48 product bought by skeptics of apps, a visible human is the trust signal; each reply is a conversion conversation.

3. **"Hell yeah or no" as the product filter, published [K].** Source: Hell Yeah or No. Publish a short page "Things nine turtles will not build" and a "what we say no to" log. Each no is a post ("No streak counter. Here is why."). Effort S. 10 customers: it is the app's positioning stated as a decision, and readers who feel the same self-identify.

4. **"Hell yeah" onboarding question [K].** Source: same. After 2 weeks of trial, one email: "On a scale where only 'hell yeah' counts, is this for you? If not, say so and I will refund and help you export." Effort S. 10 customers: counterintuitive honesty builds word of mouth; those who say yes become advocates.

5. **/now page for nine turtles [K].** Source: nownownow.com movement. Create nineturtles.app/now (what Rohit is building this month, what he is reading, what is broken) and list it at nownownow.com. Also invite readers to put a "what I'm reading now" feed on their /now page, generated by nine turtles. Effort S-M. 10 customers: /now people are exactly the small-web, indie readers who care about calm software; the directory is a free referral surface.

6. **"Reading now" public shelf for each reader (opt-in) [K, wild].** Source: /now + Sivers' public book notes (sive.rs/book). Let a reader publish a read-only page of what they finished this week (titles and links to the publishers, nothing scraped). Page footer: "made with nine turtles". Effort L. 10 customers: a finite, non-social, non-metric way to share reading; each page is an ad in the one place where the ideal reader already is.

7. **Public book-style notes on what you read [K].** Source: Sivers' book summaries drive huge traffic. Rohit writes "ten blogs I finish each week" in the same format: short, opinionated, linked, each ending with "subscribe in nine turtles" deep link. Effort M. 10 customers: it demonstrates the use case and each blog recommended tends to retweet or link back.

8. **Start now, no funding: share the numbers [K].** Source: "Start now, no funding needed" and Sivers' open numbers. Publish monthly: readers, revenue, costs (Render, R2), hours. Effort S. 10 customers: transparency matches the open-code ethos; indie-curious readers buy to back an honest experiment.

9. **"Business is not about money, it is about making dreams come true" as a founder-story hook [K].** Source: Anything You Want. Pitch the story as a dream made possible by AI agents: a non-coder from a small town in Haryana ships a paid app. Not the founder essay (covered), but a different angle: a one-page "dream" statement on the About page plus "what I will do when it pays for a year of my time". Effort S. 10 customers: purpose-driven buyers; the stated dream gives a reason to pay beyond features.

10. **You make the rules: a "pay what you think a year of calm is worth" tier, no tricks [K].** Source: CD Baby's rules (pay weekly, no fees on certain things). Offer a plain rule set: one price, honest refund any time within a year, monthly pro-rata refunds if you stop. State it like Sivers did: "Here are our rules; they are unusual." Effort S. 10 customers: removing every fear is a differentiator in a market of subscription traps.

11. **Pitch one person at a time (Your Music and People) [K].** Source: "contact one person at a time", "follow up". Rohit writes a personal note to 100 people who have publicly written about digital minimalism or RSS (not newsletters-by-bulk), each asking a specific question rather than "buy this", and offering a free year. Effort M. 10 customers: 100 genuinely personal notes at even 10% reply and 10% convert yields 1; combined with multiplier effect of their audiences it is the best-odds early path.

12. **Go where competitors are not: libraries, writing teachers, bookstores [K].** Source: "the most successful people do the opposite", go where competitors aren't. Offer free seats to writing course instructors, MFA programs, independent bookshop newsletters, local library tech-help sessions in India and elsewhere. Effort M. 10 customers: a single teacher recommending it to a class of 30 beats 30 ad clicks.

13. **Niche of the niche: readers who want to finish things [K].** Source: Your Music and People "find the niche of the niche". Pick one micro-audience for month one: e.g. people who have a Pocket/Instapaper backlog of 500 items and feel guilt. Landing page: "Declare reading bankruptcy." Effort S. 10 customers: a sharply defined pain gets shared among precisely those people.

14. **Make it easy to say yes: one-click import from the readers they already use [K].** Source: Your Music and People. Importers for OPML, Pocket exports, Substack subscription lists, Kindle highlights (Readwise), so that day one the app is already full of what they like. Effort M-L (feasibility depends on existing import). 10 customers: the biggest drop-off is the empty-room problem; fixing it converts trials.

15. **Don't pay to play [K].** Source: Your Music and People. Publish "We do not buy ads, sponsored reviews, or placement" and stick to it; instead spend time on gifts of access. Effort S. 10 customers: consistent with the product, and makes every mention credible.

16. **"Obvious to you, amazing to others": publish how a calm RSS routine works [K].** Source: sive.rs "Obvious to you, amazing to others". Rohit already knows how feeds, folders, and weekly reading work; write 5 plain posts like "How I read 40 blogs in 30 minutes on Sunday". Effort S. 10 customers: for people who have never heard of RSS this is brand-new, and posts rank for "how to follow blogs".

17. **Ideas are a multiplier of execution: ship a tiny free artifact each month [K].** Source: sive.rs "Ideas are just a multiplier of execution". Monthly: a printable "reading week" PDF, a free curated OPML bundle of 25 great blogs on one theme, etc. Effort S per item. 10 customers: bundles are shareable and each includes "open in nine turtles".

18. **Write down everything / public notes [K].** Source: Sivers "Write down everything". Keep a public changelog and decision log (already public), with a voiced weekly note. Effort S. 10 customers: steady drip of indexable, quotable material attracts the build-in-public crowd.

19. **Slow pace is a feature: "The standard pace is for chumps" [K].** Source: sive.rs post. Announce a deliberately slower release cadence and a "no big launch day" stance; ship quietly, one improvement a month, announced in a letter. Effort S. 10 customers: matches brand; calm-software folks reward restraint with word of mouth.

20. **Marketing is just being considerate [K].** Source: Sivers' framing. Concrete: every email asks "is this considerate?" Example rule: never send more than one email per month after onboarding; unsubscribe link at top. Effort S. 10 customers: low churn and goodwill; a few will mention it unprompted.

21. **Thank-you phone/voice note to first 50 customers [K, wild].** Source: Sivers' personal touch at CD Baby. Rohit sends a 60-second voice message (or handwritten postcard from Haryana) to each of the first 50 payers. Effort M. 10 customers: ten of fifty will post it; the story "the founder mailed me a postcard from a small town in India" is memorable.

## B. Seth Godin

22. **Smallest viable audience: name it in one sentence [K].** Source: This Is Marketing. "For people who read blogs and Substacks and have lost the habit of finishing anything." Use it as the only hero line and test it on 20 people. Effort S. 10 customers: a sharp audience statement makes it easy for a member to forward to "someone like them".

23. **People like us do things like this [K].** Source: This Is Marketing. Build identity language: "Readers who finish." Offer a small, quiet badge: "I read with nine turtles" in email signatures or on a blog's /about (no social tokens). Effort S. 10 customers: identity signals recruit peers at the tribe edge.

24. **Permission marketing: the only newsletter is a good one [K].** Source: Permission Marketing. One monthly letter with a real essay, a short list of 10 great reads, and nothing else. Effort M. 10 customers: it demonstrates the product's taste before payment, builds an owned list, and converts at a high rate.

25. **Purple cow: one remarkable, absurd feature [K].** Source: Purple Cow. The app literally ends: "You're done. See you tomorrow." Make that end-of-list screen beautiful and screenshot-worthy (it exists in spirit); promote the screen. Effort S-M. 10 customers: people share the "you are done" image because no other reader does it.

26. **Tribes: leader of a small reading club [K].** Source: Tribes. Host a monthly online "slow read" session where members read the same long essay and talk (30 minutes, no slides). Effort M. 10 customers: a tribe's members are founding customers and bring friends.

27. **The minimum viable audience via a worksheet [K].** Source: This Is Marketing. Publish a one-page "Who is it for / who is it not for", in the app's marketing page, including "not for you if you want a dashboard of urgent items". Effort S. 10 customers: self-selection ups conversion and strengthens identity.

28. **Tension and status roles [K].** Source: This Is Marketing (affiliation, status, tension). The tension is "I want to read more but I am drowning." Write ad-free essay and landing copy that addresses that tension only. Effort S. 10 customers: clear tension framing yields natural quotable lines.

## C. Kevin Kelly

29. **1,000 True Fans, scaled down: 300 founding fans [K].** Source: Kelly's essay (kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans). Frame the first few hundred readers as "the first 300" with names (opt-in) on a quiet Thank-you page, no scarcity or countdown. Effort S. 10 customers: belonging to a named founding group is a reason to pay now that is not manipulation.

30. **Direct relationship with fans: personal email check-in at 30 days [K].** Source: same essay (direct relationship). "What is one thing that would make this feel more like a book?" Reply in public changelog with credit. Effort S. 10 customers: engaged feedback produces advocates.

31. **Excellent Advice for Living as lens [K].** Source: Kelly's book. Publish a small "Advice for Reading" card set (e.g. "Don't read what's urgent; read what's durable") as shareable images linking to the app. Effort S. 10 customers: quotable and shareable among Newport-ish readers.

32. **Things that fans would buy: the "Fan Edition" print of a year of reading [K, wild].** Source: Kelly's "eight things better than free" (immediacy, personalization, embodiment). Offer an annual printed "year of reading" book: titles, links, and your highlights, typeset like a real book. Effort L. 10 customers: embodiment is the premium; it is physical, gift-ready, photographed and shared.

## D. Austin Kleon

33. **Show your work: build the app in a daily/weekly public process [K].** Source: Show Your Work! Weekly "process notes" with screenshots of typography experiments, rejected designs, and a before/after. Effort S weekly. 10 customers: process is a story people follow; the open-code build is already the raw material.

34. **Share something small every day, then a weekly digest [K].** Source: Show Your Work. Short posts about a single feed or article you loved. Effort S. 10 customers: builds an audience of readers, not users.

35. **Steal like an artist: a "steal log" of what Apple Books does well [K].** Source: Steal Like an Artist. Publish credit-giving design notes ("what we stole from Apple Books, from Instapaper, from Bear"). Effort S. 10 customers: design nerds link to such write-ups; it ranks and is honest.

36. **Make a "swipe file" newsletter of best things read [K].** Source: Kleon's weekly newsletter of 10 things. Rohit's own 10-things letter, produced from his own nine turtles reading, with "read these in nine turtles" bundle link. Effort M. 10 customers: Kleon's format demonstrably grows loyal readers; every issue shows the app in use.

37. **Become a documentarian, not a guru [K].** Source: Show Your Work. Publish the actual first 100 customer emails (with consent) and what changed because of each. Effort M. 10 customers: social proof that is real and specific.

## E. Paul Jarvis, Jason Fried and DHH

38. **Company of One: stay small and say so [K].** Source: Company of One. Put "built by one person; this is a feature" on the site, plus a promise of no VC, no ads, no data sales. Effort S. 10 customers: solo-business admirers (Jarvis readers) are a reachable, likely-to-pay audience.

39. **Out-teach the competition [K].** Source: Rework. Build a free, long-form "RSS for people who read books" guide/course (email course, 7 days). Effort M. 10 customers: teaching builds the trust that makes $48 feel small.

40. **Sell your by-products [K].** Source: Rework (emulate chefs, sell by-products). By-products here: the open decision log, a design-token set, "how a non-coder built this with agents" playbook. Offer the playbook for sale or as a free lead magnet for the "AI built" curious. Effort M. 10 customers: AI-builders pay attention; some will subscribe out of loyalty.

41. **Emulate chefs: cook in public with recipes [K].** Source: Rework "Emulate chefs". Share the exact prompts and docs (AGENTS.md style) used to build. Effort S. 10 customers: widely shared among AI-for-non-coders communities.

42. **Fathom-style honesty: publish privacy and cost choices [K].** Source: Paul Jarvis' Fathom analytics brand. Publish "We have no analytics beyond a privacy-respecting counter." Effort S. 10 customers: digital-minimalism readers value it.

43. **Build an audience, not a product launch [K].** Source: Rework. Every week for six months write one piece about reading well, with no pitch; the app is the footer. Effort M ongoing. 10 customers: slower but sticky; matches the "book not feed" ethos.

## F. Ryan Holiday, Robin Sloan, Craig Mod

44. **Perennial seller: pick one "forever page" [K].** Source: Perennial Seller. Invest in one page that can still be relevant in ten years: "A manifesto for reading on the open web". Update yearly, never change URL. Effort M. 10 customers: evergreen pages compound for years; backlinks come slowly.

45. **Long tail of word of mouth: ask the first fifty for one introduction [K].** Source: Perennial Seller (word of mouth). Ask each early reader for one person's name, not a share button. Effort S. 10 customers: personal referral converts at much higher rates than any public share.

46. **An app can be a home-cooked meal [K].** Source: Robin Sloan, "An app can be a home-cooked meal" (2020). Position nine turtles as a home-cooked app for a small table; publish the "menu". Effort S. 10 customers: the essay is beloved among exactly the right readers; citing and contacting its fans is a pointed outreach.

47. **Craig Mod style membership: a quarterly letter and a year-end special project [K].** Source: Craig Mod's Ridgeline / "SPECIAL PROJECTS" membership (about 1,000+ members). For paying readers, a quarterly handwritten-feel letter plus one annual project (e.g. a curated "Best of the small web" annual). Effort M. 10 customers: reduces churn and gives a talking point.

48. **Walking-newsletter equivalent: a slow weekly Sunday issue [K, wild].** Source: Mod's walking newsletters. A Sunday "nine turtles walk": one reflection plus three links, sent from the app's own reading list, with a photo from a walk in Haryana. Effort S weekly. 10 customers: voice and place create a personal bond more than features can.

## G. Tiago Forte, Ali Abdaal, Rob Walling, Patrick McKenzie, Amy Hoy

49. **Build a second brain bridge [K].** Source: Tiago Forte. Make "send highlight to Readwise/Notion/Obsidian" prominent and write a guide "Capture from nine turtles into your second brain". Pitch it in PKM communities. Effort M. 10 customers: PKM crowd already pays for tools and loves workflow posts.

50. **Teach in public with a template [K].** Source: Forte/Abdaal approach to teaching. Release a Notion template "Reading week planner" that pairs with the app. Effort S. 10 customers: templates circulate; low-hype and useful.

51. **Pre-sell to your own list (Walling: start small, sell before build) [K].** Source: Rob Walling, Start Small Stay Small. Offer a "founding year" annual plan to the existing blog audience (rohit.onl) with a personal note. Effort S. 10 customers: warm audience converts ten times more than cold.

52. **Engineering as marketing: free tools are SEO (not repeated; but a different angle) [K].** Source: Patrick McKenzie / Thomas Ptacek. Instead of SEO tool pages: a free "reading-time budget" calculator or "how many hours of blogs do you follow" tool whose output is a personal shareable card. Effort M. 10 customers: self-referential, shareable.

53. **Charge more, say why [K].** Source: kalzumeus "Don't call yourself a programmer" / charge more. Keep $48 and explain it in a plain "why this costs $48" page (hosting, backups, one person). Effort S. 10 customers: price transparency reduces objections and signals quality.

54. **Sales safari: mine watering holes for the exact words [K].** Source: Amy Hoy, Sales Safari. Read r/RSS, Hacker News threads on "Pocket shutdown", r/Substack complaints, Readwise Reader Discord, Goodreads reviews; collect the exact complaint sentences ("I feel guilty opening my reader"). Build landing copy from those quotes, and reply helpfully in those places. Effort M. 10 customers: copy using customers' own words converts, and helpful replies reach people mid-pain.

55. **Stacking the bricks: one tiny shipped thing per week with a link [K].** Source: Amy Hoy, "stacking the bricks". Commit to weekly small public ships, each with its own mini announcement. Effort S weekly. 10 customers: steady visible momentum earns trust of watchers.

## H. Rory Sutherland and Byron Sharp

56. **Costly signal: do something visibly inefficient [K].** Source: Sutherland, Alchemy. Hand-write the first 100 welcome notes, or publicly cap the number of new readers per month so everyone is welcomed personally (no countdown, just a stated pace). Effort S-M. 10 customers: inefficiency proves care; people tell stories about it.

57. **The opposite of a good idea can also be a good idea: a reader with fewer features on purpose [K].** Source: Sutherland. Advertise "what is missing": "No unread counts. No notifications. No algorithm." as the feature list. Effort S. 10 customers: it flips the comparison table and is quoted widely.

58. **Psycho-logic: price anchor against a book, not software [K].** Source: Alchemy. Frame $48 as "the price of two hardcover books a year for a lifetime of reading material", not "$4/month". Effort S. 10 customers: a better frame changes perceived value without discounting.

59. **Distinctive assets: the turtle [K].** Source: Byron Sharp, How Brands Grow. Lock in a distinctive asset set: the turtle mark, a specific ink blue, the lowercase name, the "you are done" screen. Use identically everywhere, even on stickers and bookmarks. Effort S-M. 10 customers: consistent assets make every touchpoint recognizable, which is how small brands build mental availability.

60. **Mental availability: own one category entry point [K].** Source: Sharp. Choose "after Pocket / read-it-later guilt" as the situation; every piece of content links the moment (Sunday morning, commuting) to the brand. Effort S. 10 customers: buying is triggered by situations; owning one means you are thought of at the right time.

61. **Physical availability: be where the reader already is [K].** Source: Sharp. Ship a browser extension and iOS/Android share-sheet "add feed" that makes it nearly free to follow a blog. Effort M-L. 10 customers: easy path from "I like this writer" to "I follow them in nine turtles" is the daily conversion moment.

62. **Reach many light buyers, not only enthusiasts [K].** Source: Sharp (penetration over loyalty). Stop optimising only for the tiny devout minority; make the free trial welcoming to the casual (10-minute set-up with a starter pack of 20 blogs). Effort M. 10 customers: starter packs lower the entry barrier for the many.

63. **Bookmark as media (Sutherland: small physical cues) [K, wild].** Source: Alchemy. Tuck a turtle bookmark in the postcards, in books gifted to libraries; the bookmark says "finished? close the tab." Effort S. 10 customers: physical objects signal care; bookshop and cafe owners display them.

64. **Free pass for lapsed readers: "Return to reading" 30 days [K].** Source: Sutherland's reframe. Pitch to lapsed readers rather than hunters of features; contact people who have publicly said "I used to read more." Effort S. 10 customers: highly motivated emotional need.
