# nine turtles: the ideal reader

Changed with Rohit on 1 November 2026, replacing the note of 25 October.
Marketing threads build on this note. The longer persona ("Maya", made up)
and the questions still open are in Rohit's working doc; this is the
summary.

## Who they are

- **People who write on the small web:** the kind who pay for Bear Blog,
  Mataroa or Pika. Everyone who pays for those is a potential reader of
  nine turtles.
- They follow **people, not publications:** a few dozen blogs found through
  other writers' blogrolls, read closely, with time to take it in.
- They **reply when a post moves them,** usually by email, sometimes days
  later.
- **Comfortable with technology, not engineers:** they write in Markdown,
  set up their own domain and know what RSS is, but rarely write code.
- They judge a tool on **craft and care** first, and already pay $5–10 a
  month for small tools they love.

## What they care about and what they hate

- **Care about:** craft, people over platforms, owning their corner of the
  web, being treated as an adult, slowness as a choice, knowing who made
  the tool.
- **Hate:** feeds that never end and guilt-counting numbers, algorithms and
  AI between them and the writer, the corporate SaaS look, bloat, being
  sold to, tracking, likes and follower counts.

## In more detail (Rohit, 1 November 2026)

- **Maya** (the made-up persona; Rohit says "I am Maya"): in her 30s,
  earns, writes a blog. She used to read a book a month and reads less
  because of social media; she has quit the platforms many times and
  comes back, because nothing replaces them. She used to meet good people
  online and doesn't any more, and wants to: today she emails about two
  writers a month and wants more of those exchanges.
- **About 50 feeds,** up to 70. She finds good blogs and forgets them
  (bookmarks, X bookmarks, notes, a WhatsApp chat with herself).
- **When a post moves her:** saves it for later (and quotes it on her
  blog), shares the link with a friend or two, writes back, mostly
  privately.
- **What pulls her back to social media:** friends' Instagram posts,
  YouTube as her main source of knowledge, X for tech news. nine turtles
  makes YouTube and podcasts first-class; it doesn't chase X or
  Instagram.
- **Attention:** she loves long essays but often can't finish one; deep
  sessions that end are the goal, never minutes spent.
- **She'd cancel** if she forgot to open it and felt she wasn't getting
  value. She has subscription fatigue and weighs even $1.
- **Price:** about $5 a month for a new product; more if someone she
  trusts (Derek Sivers, Herman Martinus) vouched for it.
- **Never:** lock her in (one-click export of everything, notes too),
  pushy marketing emails, feel like just another SaaS, add to her
  information overload. A little essential tracking is fine if said
  plainly (only to know when something is broken).
- **Keeps her paying:** feeling part of someone's story. Rohit is the
  face, like Herman for Bear Blog.
- **Likes:** honesty from makers, Derek Sivers, a few Substacks, small
  easter eggs (Bear Blog's "Publish something awesome with your bear
  hands", Porkbun's "Don't like our name?").
- **Devices:** laptop first; phones and tablets get our best design, but
  never a feature that pulls (nothing to check, nothing to refresh by
  habit, no endless scrolling).

**For the copy:** never call her a writer, and never name loneliness.
Name the need (people you'd want to know, writing back) so a reader who
doesn't write feels just as at home; "writer" describes the people she
reads.

## Who it's not for

They can still use nine turtles, but we don't design or market for them:
the news junkie with hundreds of feeds, the power user who wants scraping
and filters, teams and companies, and people with no reading habit yet.

## Where to find them

On their own blogs and each other's: Bear Blog's discover page, Mataroa,
Pika, Micro.blog, Mastodon, blogrolls, webrings and small newsletters about
the indie web. They find tools through posts from bloggers they trust.
