# nine turtles: marketing

How nine turtles finds its readers. Started with Rohit on 1 November 2026,
building on [`positioning.md`](positioning.md) and
[`ideal-reader.md`](ideal-reader.md). Nothing here is decided until it
says "Rohit's call". This is the one place for marketing: it replaces the
marketing parts of the October research (deleted 1 November) and of
[`strategy-notes.md`](strategy-notes.md).

## The proposal (1 November 2026, waiting on Rohit)

Claude's proposal, with eight calls for Rohit at the end, is in the Claude
Doc "Marketing: a proposal to argue with"
(https://claude.ai/artifact/BD72V6WxPZ3QaEHg9dVjFB). In short:

- **Market by doing what the app helps with, in public:** read people and
  write back. Rohit's blog (rohit.onl) is the main channel.
- **Write back to about five small-web writers a week,** about their post,
  never pitching the app.
- **"How I read the web" interviews** as the series other people share.
- **A warmer [aboutfeeds.com](https://aboutfeeds.com/):** "how to follow
  the people you like", with a link bloggers can put on their own sites.
- **Dropped:** the power-user guide as cornerstone, a site about RSS
  tools, a series of comparison pages, free tools for now, r/rss as a
  channel, SEO how-to guides, Product Hunt.
- **Launch by gates, not a date:** warm up now; the first 100 by personal
  invite; one public day built on one essay (Show HN on the side); then a
  weekly habit. "Write to me" instead of a waitlist form.

**Rohit's calls:** (none yet)

## Worth raising before deciding

Points from the October research that push against the proposal:

- **Invites without theatre.** The founder research warns against
  "exclusive waitlists" and invite queues. Invites must be personal and
  real, never scarcity.
- **Compounding versus one by one.** On 25 October Rohit preferred
  marketing that compounds (search, free tools) over talking to people one
  by one. The proposal leans on one-to-one writing back.
- **Comparison and "alternative" pages** are among the few pages still
  winning clicks, and AlternativeTo's Feedly, Inoreader and Pocket pages
  are what AI assistants cite. AlternativeTo takes no closed betas, so
  listings wait for public sign-up. At least: list on AlternativeTo,
  SaaSHub and Slant at launch.
- **Free tools** are what survives AI answers (moderate evidence).
- **Reddit:** weak vendor data links a Reddit presence with more ChatGPT
  mentions. The only argument for it.

## Kept from the October research

### People to write to as a peer

Never with a pitch.

- **Calm-reader makers who wrote about calm reading in 2026:** Manton Reece
  (Micro.blog; in February 2026 he said he's building a reader whose items
  fade), Terry Godier (Current; newsletter "Intermittent Dispatches"),
  Brent Simmons (NetNewsWire), Nick Heer (Pixel Envy), Alan Levine (Cog
  Dog).
- **Write-ups where nine turtles is exactly the subject:** James G's list
  of minimal readers on Artemis, and Moonwriting's Bear Blog post "Artemis
  and Current: bringing calmness into RSS".
- **Blogs and roundups that fit Maya:** Shellsharks' weekly indie-web
  "scrolls", Dan Q, Robin Rendle, Matt Webb, Kottke, Michael Tsai (he
  collects every reader launch).
- **Small-web platforms** (a friendship first, a free year or a follow link
  maybe later, a no accepted): Herman (Bear Blog, about 5,500 hand-approved
  blogs), Manton (Micro.blog), Buttondown, omg.lol, Write.as, Pika, Ghost,
  Kagi Small Web.

### rohit.onl as the main channel

- **Small changes:** a footer or colophon line, a line on /now and /uses,
  an "I made it" paragraph on /about, and dates on the post list (it has
  none).
- **Submit rohit.onl, the person, not the product,** to blogroll.org, Kagi
  Small Web (a pull request to kagisearch/smallweb), ooh.directory, the
  512KB Club and IndieWeb webrings.
- **A /blogroll page** with a downloadable feed list (never the owner's
  file in this repository). Blogrolls are coming back: Dave Winer's
  blogroll standard is used by Micro.blog; one writer found 20 blogrolls
  led to 150 sites.
- **The archive already speaks to Maya:** "What it means to read a book",
  "Two teaspoons of information, please.", "AI is killing the open web".
- **Pace honestly.** Rohit posted 2 to 8 times a year, with a pause since
  March 2026. One post a month is a big step up; a series that's abandoned
  does more harm than none.
- **Share each post** with one line on Mastodon and Bluesky. Not X.

### Ideas that build "write back"

- **Letter-writing month (February):** invite readers to write back to one
  writer they read.
- **"Your feed is broken" letters** to bloggers whose feed fails, counted
  across readers and never saying who reads what.
- **"Support this writer" links** from the feed's own payment link (needs
  a spec change).
- **Sponsor** NetNewsWire, Kill the Newsletter or JSON Feed on GitHub
  Sponsors ($5–10 a month).
- **Posts:** "Letters to a Young Blogger" (after Rilke), "The Blogroll Is
  Back", "Still Here: blogs that have published for 20 years" (the blogs
  named tend to share it).
- **Blogrolls:** import any blogroll in one click, and a yearly blogroll
  week.

### Founder precedents worth quoting

- **Feedbin:** break-even at about 100 payers, reached in 3 weeks; the
  Reeder developer adding Feedbin sync "put it on the map"; no free tier.
  Being the sync service inside other apps is a channel of its own.
- **Bear Blog:** growth from one Hacker News post plus Herman's own blog.
  "99.9% of people will not contribute to anything they can receive for
  free."
- **Craig Mod:** thousands of members at $100 a year, from direct, rare
  asks.
- **Readwise:** an extra 30-day trial for Forte Labs readers. The model
  for community codes ("tell us you came from X").
- **Pinboard:** flat at 23–29k users for years, and fine. **Plausible:**
  churn just under 2% a month.
- **Show HN:** small launches got 100–500 visits and almost no payers.

### Welcoming the first 100

A short welcome note in Rohit's voice, then a handwritten postcard from
India (about $150 for 100), asking each for one sentence he may quote.
Precedent: Derek Sivers' CD Baby email.

### Newsletters

- **Dense Discovery:** about 38,000 design-minded readers, 61% open rate,
  classified ads from $89, next slots January 2027 (the only verified
  price). **Tedium** (Ernie Smith): about 10–12k readers, price on request.
- A $500 test across 4 to 8 tiny newsletters (Reletter lists many), each
  with its own link, once sign-up and price are live. Better still: swap a
  free year for a mention.

### Being named by AI assistants

- **One plain facts page:** what it is, who makes it, the price, what it
  does and doesn't do.
- **Let the AI robots in** (OpenAI, Perplexity, Anthropic) in the website's
  robots file, add structured data, verify with Google and Bing. One
  founder found Cloudflare silently blocking robots he wanted.
- **Check by hand once a month:** about 20 questions ("calm RSS reader",
  "Feedly alternative without AI", "follow blogs without social media",
  "read newsletters outside my inbox") in ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity,
  Gemini and Google's AI Mode. Pages they cite become next month's list.
- **Add "an AI assistant"** to "how did you hear about us?".

### Website basics

- A sitemap, a feed for the website's blog, "don't index" on
  app.nineturtles.app and slowfeed.onrender.com, Google Search Console and
  Bing Webmaster Tools.
- **Design galleries** fit "craft first": Minimal Gallery, Typewolf, Lapa
  Ninja, siteinspire. Never pay for "featured".
- **About 15 free listings that last** (AlternativeTo, SaaSHub, Slant,
  Peerlist, Indie Hackers), not a 100-directory sweep. Skip the Apple App
  Store for now.
