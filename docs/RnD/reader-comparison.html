How other feed readers compare with nine turtles

Research notes, 26 October 2026. Nothing here is decided and nothing was built.

Start here

You asked what four well-known feed readers do that nine turtles does not, and where they do the same thing better. I read the public source code of Feedbin, Miniflux, NetNewsWire and FreshRSS and compared them with our own docs/SPEC.md.

Every idea is a card. Each card says what the app does, why it might matter to a reader, what nine turtles does today, and my take. Every card also has two labels:

Fits It sits comfortably inside our rules (calm, no animation, accessible, never fetching article pages).
Needs a decision It could work, but it is a design or product choice for you first.
Clashes It breaks a rule in DESIGN.md or AGENTS.md. Listed so we can say no on purpose, not so we build it.
Already covered We already do this, or something very close.

Effort is a rough guess: Small is a few hours, Medium is a day or two, Large is a milestone of its own.

The short version. nine turtles is already ahead on the things this project cares about most: reading comfort (the Literata font, the Aa panel, line width), accessibility, reading offline, plain-language settings, a page for broken feeds, rules and mutes, saved searches, importing stars, and the calm brief itself. The other readers are ahead on a handful of small conveniences, mostly keyboard and privacy details, and Feedbin is ahead on one big area we skipped, podcasts. Most of what is worth borrowing is small.

How we use it. We learn from ideas only. These are other people's programs under their own licences, so nothing is copied and nothing should be. And I read code, not running apps, so how something feels is my guess. Where I say "probably already covered", a quick look in the app is the first step before anyone builds.

What to look at first

When several apps arrive at the same idea independently, that is the best sign it is worth having. These five are all small and all fit our rules.

  1. Mark older, or above and below, as read (Feedbin F1, NetNewsWire C1)
    Two apps agree. It is the single most natural way to triage, and we already have Undo to make it safe. Small effort.
  2. Space to read onward, n for next unread (Feedbin F10, NetNewsWire C2)
    The key people expect. It would make reading a whole inbox a one-key job. Small effort.
  3. Keys for folders and copying the link (Feedbin F24, NetNewsWire C3)
    Pure keyboard polish. Small effort.
  4. Privacy hygiene (Miniflux B1, B2, B4; NetNewsWire C4)
    Four small checks: strip tracking tags from links, send no referrer when leaving, play YouTube without cookies, and block known tracker domains. Our docs mention none of these except the 1x1 pixels, so the first step is just to look at what the app does today.
  5. Test real Substack and Ghost feeds (Feedbin F16)
    Add a few, look at them, fix what looks off. Cheap and it directly serves the book-like feeling.

Feedbin

https://github.com/feedbin/feedbin

Feedbin is a paid feed reader that has been polished for more than ten years, and its code is public. It is a web app written in Ruby on Rails, with a separate crawler, a search server and a lot of machinery behind it, because it serves many thousands of readers. For us it is mostly a source of small, well-tested conveniences, and a few bigger product questions.

F1

Mark everything above, or below, as read

FitsEffort: Small (a few hours)
What it is
From any article in the list, Feedbin can mark every article above it as read, or every article below it. It sits right next to "mark all as read" in the code.
Why it might matter
This is how people actually clear a backlog. You read down the list until you lose interest, and then you want to say "everything after this point is not for me" in one move. Today nine turtles only offers "mark all", which is all or nothing.
What nine turtles does now
We have "Mark all as read" with Undo (SPEC section 8). We have nothing for a part of the list.
My take
Best first candidate. NetNewsWire has the same idea (C1), so two apps agree it is worth having. It would reuse the Undo we already have, so it stays safe.
F2

A "Recently read" list

Needs a decisionEffort: Small to Medium (a few hours to a day)
What it is
Feedbin keeps a short list of articles you opened lately, on a screen of its own.
Why it might matter
Solves "I closed that article by accident and now I cannot find it". Marking something unread covers part of that, but not "show me what I was just reading".
What nine turtles does now
Nothing. Once you read an article it just drops out of Unread.
My take
Fits if kept very plain: the last 50 articles, newest first, no counts and no streaks. DESIGN.md forbids reading statistics, so it must be a list, never a measurement of the reader. Miniflux has the same thing under the name History (B7).
F3

Show what changed when a publisher edits an article

Needs a decisionEffort: Medium (a day or two)
What it is
Feedbin keeps the first copy of an article next to the latest one. If the text changed it can show an inline version with the removed words struck out and the new words marked.
Why it might matter
Publishers quietly fix typos, change headlines and sometimes rewrite whole paragraphs. For a reader who cares about the writing, "this was edited, and here is how" feels like a printed erratum, which suits a book-like app.
What nine turtles does now
We keep only the latest version of an article.
My take
A real product choice. It stores a second copy of every changed article, so the database and the backups grow. If built, show it only on request inside the article, never as an "updated" badge in the list.
F4

Send to other services (Instapaper, Kindle, Pinboard, Mastodon and more)

ClashesEffort: Medium for each service
What it is
A settings page where the reader switches on share targets such as Instapaper, Kindle, Pinboard, Raindrop, Mastodon, Micro.blog and email. Pocket is on the list too, but Pocket has closed.
Why it might matter
Some people keep a read-it-later tool or a notes tool next to their reader, and these buttons save them copying a link by hand.
What nine turtles does now
Copy link on a computer, the system share sheet on a phone, and nothing platform-specific (SPEC section 21).
My take
Clashes with SPEC section 21, and platform share buttons are on the "Never" list. Listed so we can say no on purpose.
F5

Email an article to someone

Needs a decisionEffort: Small to Medium
What it is
An article can be emailed from inside the app, with a reply-to address so the friend can answer.
Why it might matter
A friendly way to say "you will like this".
What nine turtles does now
We send email only for accounts (sign-in links and so on) and share only the publisher's link.
My take
The founding rule is "share the publisher's link, not our copy". An email that carries just the title and the original link would fit; one that carries the article's text would not.
F6

Needs a decisionEffort: Large (a milestone of its own)

Why it might matter
Many people who use a feed reader also follow a few podcasts, because podcasts are just feeds with an audio file attached.
What nine turtles does now
Nothing. SPEC does not describe audio at all.
My take
This is a product decision, not polish. Our floor says to play media in the publisher's own player, so the most we could do is a plain audio control. Nothing urgent.

My take
Strong candidate. NetNewsWire does exactly the same thing (C2).

F16

Special clean-up for Substack and Ghost posts

FitsEffort: Small for each platform
What it is
Feedbin's article pipeline has small filters for particular publishing platforms. For Substack it keeps only the post body and drops the page furniture a feed sometimes carries. For Ghost it restyles "bookmark cards" so they do not look broken.
Why it might matter
Substack and Ghost are two of the most common places the writing people follow comes from. If posts from them look slightly wrong, the whole app feels less like a book.
What nine turtles does now
Our sanitiser makes feed content safe and readable generally (SPEC section 7). I have not checked how Substack or Ghost posts look.
My take
Good, cheap work: add a few real Substack and Ghost feeds, look at them, and fix what looks off. It only restyles what the feed already contains, so it respects the floor.

Miniflux

https://github.com/miniflux/v2

Miniflux is a minimal, very fast reader written in Go. It is the closest to nine turtles in spirit: a short feature list, a plain interface, and most of the extras tucked into Settings. It is aimed at people who run their own server, so some of what it offers (fetching article pages, 25 integrations, custom scripts) is exactly what we have decided not to do. The valuable parts are its privacy habits and a few careful details.

B1

Strip tracking tags from links

FitsEffort: Small (a few hours)
What it is
Links in articles often end with marketing tags such as utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign or fbclid. Miniflux removes the known ones. It also follows FeedBurner redirect links to find the real address.
Why it might matter
When you press Copy link or Open original you hand over, or visit, an address that tells the publisher's analytics which feed you came from. A clean link keeps our promise to share "the publisher's original link" and removes the marketing noise.
What nine turtles does now
I searched the SPEC for utm and tracking parameters and found nothing, so I believe we leave links as the feed wrote them. Worth confirming in the app.
My take
Good privacy hygiene. Strip only a fixed list of well-known tags so a link that really needs its query still works.
B2

Don't tell the publisher where you came from

FitsEffort: Small (a few hours)
What it is
Every link that leaves Miniflux has rel="noopener noreferrer", and the whole site sends a no-referrer policy header.
Why it might matter
Without this, a browser tells the destination website which page you were on. It is a small leak of information about the reader, and it costs nothing to close.
What nine turtles does now
SPEC does not mention it. We may or may not already send it.
My take
Serves the floor "never put the reader at risk". First step: look at what the app sends today.
B3

A picture proxy, as an option

FitsEffort: Large (a milestone of its own)
What it is
Miniflux can load article pictures through its own server, so the publisher's image host never sees the reader.
Why it might matter
With pictures loaded straight from publishers, as we do today (SPEC section 7), a publisher's image server can see the reader's address and the time they read. A proxy hides that. Feedbin has the same idea (see the cross-cutting questions below).
What nine turtles does now
Pictures load from the publisher's site; only 1x1 tracking pixels are removed.
My take
Strongest answer to reader tracking, but it uses bandwidth and cache space on our one small server. A decision for the owner, not a quick fix.
B4

FitsEffort: Small (a few hours)

Why it might matter

What nine turtles does now

My take
Quick check: if we are not already on the no-cookie address, switching is small.

NetNewsWire

https://github.com/Ranchero-Software/NetNewsWire

NetNewsWire is a free Mac and iPhone reader, written in Swift and loved for how it feels. Because Apple Books is our design reference, it is the best match for how a calm native reading app behaves. It is not a web app, so some things (widgets, Shortcuts) are native-only. I read its code and bundled files; I have not run it, so how anything feels is inference.

C4

NetNewsWire is not ahead of us on typography. Literata and the Aa panel are more considered than its theme list. It is a good model for how the keyboard does most of the moving and nothing outside the window nags, which is what we aim for.

FreshRSS

https://github.com/FreshRSS/FreshRSS

FreshRSS is built for people who run their own server. It has the longest feature list of the four and a huge number of settings, which is the opposite of calm. We read it as a checklist, to see what the most feature-hungry readers ask for, and mark what fits.

D1

Your own labels on articles

Needs a decisionEffort: Medium (a day or two)
What it is
You can tag any article with your own labels, separate from folders.
Why it might matter
Lets a reader file an article under "to cite" or "recipes" and find it later.
What nine turtles does now
Stars and folders only. Folders hold feeds, not articles.
My take
A second way to sort adds weight. A single idea in the Apple Books spirit, something like Collections for starred articles, might fit. Owner's call.
D2

Keep-how-many rules per feed

Needs a decisionEffort: Medium (a day or two)
What it is
A feed can keep only its newest N articles, or those from the last three months. Stars are always kept.
Why it might matter
Stops a noisy feed from filling the library.
What nine turtles does now
One yearly clean-up of unstarred articles over a year old (decision 0089).
My take
More granular, but SPEC chose few settings.

Needs a decisionEffort: Small (a few hours)

Why it might matter
Saves pressing a key.
What nine turtles does now
Our "Let unread go after" setting (decision 0097).
My take
"Nothing moves unless you move it" argues against. Ours is the calmer version.

ClashesEffort: Large (a milestone of its own)

Why it might matter

What nine turtles does now
Not possible by design.
My take
Clashes with "never scrape". Do not build.

ClashesEffort: Small (a few hours)

Why it might matter

What nine turtles does now
Feed health shows trouble.
My take
Reading statistics are a "Never". A plain line such as "this feed has not posted in three months" belongs on Feed health instead.

Bigger decisions for you

None of these is urgent. Each changes what nine turtles is, so they are yours to weigh.

  • A picture proxy (Feedbin, Miniflux B3)
    Pictures currently load from the publisher's own servers, so they can see when and from where you read. A proxy would hide that. The cost is bandwidth and storage on our single small server, and it is a larger piece of work. A decision for the owner.
  • Podcasts (Feedbin F6)
    A whole area nine turtles has skipped. Nothing urgent, but it is the largest gap against Feedbin.
  • Showing what an edited article changed (Feedbin F3)
    A book-like touch (an erratum) that grows the database.
  • Labels or collections (FreshRSS D1)
    A way to file articles beyond stars. The one feature here that changes how people organise.
  • Passkey sign-in (Miniflux B6)
    Already in the "Not in V1" list. A timing question.
  • A warmer, paper-toned theme (Feedbin F9, NetNewsWire C5)
    A pure taste call.

Clear noes

All four apps do some of these. Each breaks a rule we set, so the sensible answer is to say no on purpose:

  • Sending articles to other services, or share buttons for them (all four)
  • Saving or scraping full web pages (Feedbin, Miniflux, NetNewsWire, FreshRSS)
  • Push notifications, and widgets with unread counts
  • Pictures in the list, and tinting rows with logo colours
  • Reading statistics
  • Public pages and anonymous reading
  • Custom scripts and plug-in systems

What this research did not do

  • It read each app's public code, not the running product, so how things feel is guessed from the code.
  • It did not test nine turtles against each point. Where I wrote "probably", the first step is a quick look in our app.
  • It did not cover Feedbin's, Miniflux's or FreshRSS's separate mobile apps, or paid-only back ends.
Lives in the repository as docs/RnD/reader-comparison.html. Research only; the owner's yes is needed before any idea becomes a change.
