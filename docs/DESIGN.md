# nine turtles: UI & UX design philosophy

> **nine turtles is a reader for the small web: a quiet place to read the people worth knowing, and write back to them.**

*What changed and when: each change has its own note in [`decisions/`](decisions/README.md), and the first line of each note says what it decided. (A list here clashed whenever two changes were made at once, decision 0114.)*

## Mission

Help people leave social media and return to reading the open web. Social media is **infinite, urgent and attention-grabbing**; nine turtles is **finite, calm and quiet**.

**Tiebreaker:** the book question ("more like a book, or more like a feed?") was retired by the owner on 1 November 2026 (decision 0132). A new one will be tested in UI and UX trials before it's adopted; the candidate is *"does this feel like a letter from someone, or a feed from something?"* Until then, decide by the floors and the principles.

## What nine turtles is, and isn't

**nine turtles is** a reader for the small web: the writing people publish on their blogs, newsletters, channels and podcasts, read through RSS, Atom and JSON Feed. Each reader has their own feeds and reading; several readers can share one nine turtles without ever seeing each other's (approved by the owner for going multi-user, decision 0086).

**nine turtles is not:**
- **A read-later app:** it doesn't save arbitrary web pages.
- **A discovery engine:** no recommendations, trending or "you might like." You choose your sources. (One exception, approved by the owner for going multi-user: the first-visit welcome's "New to feeds?" offers a short, fixed list of five starter feeds, the same for everyone, none ticked, gone once you follow anything; decision 0079. And its "Or try one" shows three fixed example addresses, a blog, a newsletter and a YouTube channel, chosen by the owner; decision 0198.)
- **A social network:** no profiles, followers, likes or comments.
- **A podcast app:** no podcast directory, downloads, charts or listening stats. (A podcast's episodes play in a calm player under the byline, from the publisher's own file, with your place kept, approved by the owner on 27 October 2026, decision 0113; its Up Next queue was removed in step 5 of the owner's review, decision 0170. YouTube videos play in YouTube's own player, since 25 October 2026.)
- **An email app:** it never sends email for you or sees your inbox. (Newsletters can arrive as feeds through a private address of their own, approved by the owner for going multi-user; decision 0091.)
- **A web scraper:** it shows what publishers put in their feeds (second floor).
- **A permanent archive:** unstarred articles are kept for one year (the yearly clean-up, built 25 October 2026, decision 0089).
- **A shared space:** readers on one nine turtles never see each other's feeds, marks, searches or reading (decision 0086).

## Design reference: our own design language (being written)

On 1 November 2026 the owner dropped Apple Books as the design reference (decision 0131, replacing decision 0059). nine turtles will have **its own design principles and design language**, with design as a first-class part of the product; the goal: **cited for taste and coherence, recognisable from one screenshot.** The principles in Part 1 are our own (decision 0133); the design language (colors, type, accent, the turtles' role, the brand idea) is being decided with the owner through mockups, and until it is, the current look stays.

**Design comes first** (decision 0132): design is the most important thing in nine turtles. Agents are proactive about it and bring up broken or better design even outside the task at hand; design, UI and UX decisions are tried in mockups, a safe and a bold choice each, preferably running locally on the owner's Mac. Words alone don't settle a look.

- **Until the design language is settled:** the floors first, always; then the rest of this document as it stands. The current look (much of it first modelled on Apple Books) stays until the new language replaces it; don't change things only to come closer to Apple Books, and don't start redesign work without the owner.
- **What's fixed:** the three-pane structure (sidebar, list, page; one at a time on a phone), the owner's choice (decision 0134): it is one of the most reliable designs a reader has. Design experiments happen inside the third pane, the article's, which may become more wholesome and memorable. The logo stays: the same nine turtles, redrawn a little heavier in November 2026 ([`BRAND.md`](BRAND.md)).
- **The brand sheet,** [`BRAND.md`](BRAND.md) (decision 0139), is one page with every brand decision: name and words, typefaces, color and themes, the logo and app icon, the turtles' only job, icons, empty screens, emails and the website. It is part of this document, with its standing; every change a reader can see follows it, and a new brand decision goes there first.
- **Who may change what:** the mission, the floors, the ranked principles and the "Never" list change only with the owner's yes.

## Part 1: The philosophy

Every decision in Part 2 traces back to these. Reason from them rather than debating new questions from scratch.

**Two floors** and **ten ranked principles.** (A third floor, never lose the reader's data or put them at risk, is engineering's: [`ENGINEERING.md`](ENGINEERING.md) → "The floor".) Floors are not ranked: nothing can trade them away. Between ranked principles, **the higher one wins**.

### The first floor: everyone can read here

No amount of calm or minimalism justifies text some people can't read. Everything else in this document may change; this never does. When a design idea conflicts with it, we find another way to do the idea (the owner, 1 November 2026).

- **Contrast:** WCAG AA minimum everywhere; aim for AAA in article text. This includes muted text (read items, counts, source names).
- **Never color alone:** every state also has a non-color signal (a bar, a weight, a word).
- **Fully keyboard-operable:** standard Tab / Enter / Escape with a visible focus ring are the floor; vim keys are a power feature on top.
- **Screen readers:** panes are landmarks; the selected item and state changes ("marked as read") are announced; each article carries its language.
- **Respect system settings:** text size, reduced motion, high contrast, light/dark.
- **Touch targets:** at least 44px on mobile, however dense the design. Where the main pointer is a mouse or trackpad, sidebar rows may be 32px; anything a finger is the main pointer for gets 44px.

**Test:** can someone use every feature with only a keyboard, only a screen reader, or at 200% text size?

### The second floor: respect the publisher

Publishers are the reason there's anything to read.

- **Show what the publisher put in the feed.** Never scrape the full article. A summary-only feed shows the summary and "View original." A newsletter shows only what its email holds; its web version is never fetched. (Its invisible tracking images are loaded once when it arrives, so its sender knows it reached a reader: the owner's choice, decision 0091.)
- **Always credit the source** and link to the original.
- **Fetch politely:** on each feed's own rhythm, with ETag / Last-Modified (and WebSub, since 25 October 2026).
- **Visit a site only when the reader asks, and never an article's page.** There are four visits: to find a feed when the reader adds one (the page they give, and if it names none, its home page and a few usual feed addresses); when they press Write back and no address is known, to look once on the writer's home page and one contact or about page it links to, never twice for the same writer; and when someone asks the website's blog checkup about a blog, to find its feed the same way and read its home page and, only if that shows no address, one contact or about page it links to, with any one site looked up at most a few times in half an hour, as the feed finder is; and when Add finds no feed and the reader presses **Write to** under "Ask them for a feed", to read that site's home page and, only if that shows no address, one contact or about page it links to, under the same limits and only a few times in half an hour for one reader. Never in the background. (The owner's yes to the Write back visit, 3 November 2026, decision 0168; the blog checkup, step 12 of the owner's review plan, decision 0178; Ask them for a feed, the owner's "ship" of the no-dead-ends mockups, 6 November 2026, decision 0212.)
- **Shared links go to the publisher,** never to nine turtles.
- **Embedded media plays in the publisher's own player** (e.g. YouTube's), so views stay theirs; **an audio file the feed names plays from the publisher's own address in a simple player**, so every listen still reaches them (the owner's wording, 27 October 2026, decision 0113).

**Test:** would a publisher be glad their feed is read in nine turtles?

### The ranked principles

Adopted with the owner on 1 November 2026 (decisions 0133 and 0134), replacing the seven principles first modelled on Apple Books. Where each one comes from (the owner's design field guide: Norman, Rams, Victor, Reichenstein, Tufte, Vignelli, Fukasawa and Hara, and Rams' ten principles in their updated form) is in [`RnD/design-principles.md`](RnD/design-principles.md).

#### 1. Nothing pulls
nine turtles never tries to bring you back or keep you longer. It succeeds when you've read what you wanted and closed it. The one thing that reaches out, the weekly email, is chosen by the reader, ends, and turns off in one press.
- *Explains:* no notifications, no counts or streaks, no pull-to-refresh, manual refresh, a list that ends (older items load as you reach the bottom, decision 0074, but only from what you follow).
- **Test:** would the reader be glad it happened, even on a week they didn't open the app? Would a social media company want it? If yes, be suspicious.

#### 2. The writer comes first
The article and the person who wrote it are the product; nine turtles only helps the reader and the writer meet. The text is the interface, and our tools step back around it.
- *Explains:* in-app reading, serif by default, no images in the list, the publisher's name leading the byline, reader controls one press from the text (the Aa panel), links that don't compete with the text.
- **Test:** does this make the writer clearer, or put us in front of them?

#### 3. Clear at a glance
Every screen says what it's for, where you are and what you can do there, in the reader's words, without a tour. It answers the reader's question before they ask, from what the app already knows, and asks only as a last resort. Familiar ways are kept unless broken on purpose (see "Breaking a principle on purpose"). Quiet must never become hidden.
- *Explains:* regions separated by tone, one accent with one meaning, words wherever an icon isn't universal, the search scope chip, plain names.
- **Test:** squint (are the regions still distinct?) and five seconds (can someone say what the screen is for and where to act?).

#### 4. Forgiving
Mistakes are cheap. Undo instead of "Are you sure?"; a message says what happened and how to fix it; nothing the reader typed is thrown away; destructive actions sit apart from frequent ones.
- *Explains:* Undo after every removal, a refused field keeping what was typed, oxblood words for what removes something.
- **Test:** if the reader slips, can they get back in one press, without reading a dialog?

#### 5. Honest by default
Never look more capable, urgent or free than it is. Say what things do and cost; leaving is as easy as joining. Said plainly, never preached (see "Honest by default: a public promise" in Part 2).
- **Test:** would we be comfortable if the reader saw exactly how this works?

#### 6. Cohesive
One system: the same thing always looks, behaves and is named the same, everywhere, so the reader learns it once (see "One system" in Part 2).
- **Test:** does this introduce a new way of doing something the app already does?

#### 7. Made by a person, to the last detail
Care shows in the details, beauty is a goal in itself, and a person's voice is in it: warm, never cute. Every state is designed: empty, loading, offline, error, a very long name, another script, 200% text.
- **Test:** could this screen belong to any other app? Is any state left to chance? Is it beautiful, not just correct?

#### 8. Instant and still
Every press is answered within about a tenth of a second, visibly, without motion. Changes show as you make them, with no Apply buttons. Layout, positions and content change only through the reader's action.
- *Explains:* no spinners for what the app already has, the next article prefetched, act immediately and confirm quietly (a word changing, such as "Starred"), the Aa panel changing the article as you choose, no transitions, no feedback through motion (no springs, no pull-to-refresh), the fixed layout, "mark all as read" only affecting what you've seen.
- **Test:** does anyone ever wait for the interface, press Apply, or see something move by itself?

#### 9. Power is findable, never in the way
Keys and advanced tools speed up what's visible; nothing exists only as a key. A beginner never trips on them and can find them when needed.
- *Explains:* the shortcut list, keys named in tooltips, Not working in Everyone you follow, progressive disclosure in the sidebar; a command menu is planned (`RnD/design-principles.md`).
- **Test:** can a beginner find this when they need it, and ignore it until then?

#### 10. Less, but better, and built to last
As little design as possible: if removing something wouldn't make the task harder, remove it. Nothing that looks dated next year: no trends, no glass, no decoration that pretends. Light enough to run well on an old phone for ten years, with few outside parts. The reader's data is theirs, and they're free to leave.
- **Test:** would removing this hurt? Will it still look right in ten years?

(Respect for the publisher is the second floor, above all of these.)

**Breaking a principle on purpose** (the owner, 1 November 2026): a principle can be broken consciously when the result is clearly better, never by accident. Every break is written in the Exceptions log below with its reason and its limits; if it can't be justified there, it doesn't ship.

### Rules about the rules

1. **The design language settles questions of look and feel** once it's written (see "Design reference"); until then this document does. The floors always come first.
2. **Exceptions are written down** in the Exceptions log below: where, why and within what limits. Floors have no exceptions; only the owner can change a floor's wording, through a note in `docs/decisions/`.
3. **New features go through the floors and principles in order** and stop at the first "no":
   1. Does it break a floor? → no.
   2. Is it useful: does it serve reading people or writing back to them? → if not, no.
   3. Does it pull people back or keep them longer than they wanted? → no.
   4. Does it put us in front of the writer, or hurt reading? → no.
   5. Is it clear at a glance? If not, can it be found when needed and stay out of the way?
   6. Is it forgiving, honest and cohesive, and is every state of it designed?
   7. Is it instant, and does it leave everything still that the reader didn't move? If not, redesign until it is.
   8. Is it as little as it can be, and will it still look right in ten years?
   9. Does it challenge the usual way for a good reason, or only copy what others do? Copying is fine where the usual way serves the reader; changing it needs a written reason.
4. **Scope is checked too.** A feature that would make nine turtles one of the things under "nine turtles is not" is out, however good.
5. **Every change the reader sees passes the critique checklist** (decision 0135), before its pull request and again in review. It turns the principles into questions an agent can answer from a screenshot or a mockup; a "no" is fixed, or written in the Exceptions log as a deliberate break:
   - Can someone tell, in five seconds, what the screen is for and where to act? Does every action have a visible sign (a word, a button's shape, an icon with its name), with keys and gestures only as shortcuts? (3, 9)
   - Does the screen answer the reader's main question with no press, using what the app already knows? (3)
   - Are the words the reader's own, and familiar ways kept, or is the break written down? (3)
   - Does every press get an instant, visible answer without motion, and does anything longer than a second say what's happening? (8)
   - Can a slip be undone in one press, and does every message say how to fix the problem, keeping what was typed? (4)
   - Would removing any element make the task harder? If not, remove it. Is the chrome quieter and smaller than the text? (10, 2)
   - Are price, terms and the way out as visible as the offer, with no pre-ticked box and no fake urgency? (5)
   - Does it use only the system's spacing, type sizes, colors and names? (6)
   - Is the article text readable at every size: line length, line height, contrast? Is nothing on a busy or see-through background? (2, 10, first floor)
   - Are the empty, loading, offline, error, very long, other-script and 200%-text states designed, and does it work by keyboard and screen reader? (7, first floor)
   - Is it beautiful, not just correct, and would it still look right in ten years? (7, 10)
   - Does it pull? Would a social media company want it? (1)

**Worked example: AI summaries.** They put something between the reader and the writer (2), but a power user (9) might want them to triage 500 feeds. Result: never in the reading pane; possibly an opt-in, hidden triage tool in the list. The philosophy answered *where*, not yes or no.

**Worked example: fetch the full article when a feed has only a summary.** It would help reading (2) but breaks the publisher floor. Floors aren't ranked against principles, so the answer is no: show the summary and "View original."

### Never

- Notifications of any kind. The one exception is the weekly email: chosen by the reader in Settings, plain text, off in one press (principle 1, decision 0140)
- Badges, streaks, reading stats or any gamification (no reading-time estimates, no "year in reading")
- Algorithmic ranking ("for you"), or filling a list with anything you didn't subscribe to, so it never ends. (Older items loading as you reach the bottom of a list is allowed, decision 0074: the list still ends.)
- In-app likes, comments or social features
- Branded share buttons for specific platforms
- Full-article scraping from publishers' websites
- Rearranging what people have learned, once the look settles (see principle 3)
- Decoration that pretends: glass, blur, gradients, fake depth, motion for delight; shadows except under things that float, as Apple draws them (see Visual language → Rules). The article's bars are solid, never see-through (decision 0040). Honest, handmade touches, such as a still drawing or a signature, are welcome at the edges, never in the reading pane (decision 0132)

### Visual language

#### The system

The whole system on one page (decision 0244, after the structure and discipline critique of 10 November 2026). | Part | The values | Named exceptions |

#### Rules
- **Regions are separated by tone and space, and where two panes meet, by one hairline.** The sidebar is a tone of its own; the list and the article share the page and are parted by the pane line alone (decision 0221). Other lines and shadows appear only where they do a job, never as decoration:
  - **Lines between panes** (decision 0054): on a computer only, where two panes meet (the sidebar's right edge, and the list's when the reading pane is beside it): 1px, the line tone (the text color at 10%), as the list's row hairlines. Tone alone barely parts the panes by night, and the list and the article share the page's color, so between them this line is the edge (decision 0221).
  - **Hairlines inside a page:** between rows that do different jobs (rows in a grouped list, the parts of the text-size capsule; decision 0055), faint, in a tone of the text color, inset from the left in a grouped list. The article list has none: space alone parts its rows (decision 0123). A field's own edge is a border, not a line between regions.
  - **A line down an open folder's feeds** (decision 0123): in the sidebar, 1px in the line tone, under the folder's name, so its feeds read as its contents, as a Mac source list shows nesting.
  - **A hairline under a scrolled top line** (decision 0028): only once a pane's content has scrolled under it, and above the shortcut list's foot while more lies below, so text never looks cut off.
  - **A hairline above Choose…'s bar** (decision 0080): on Everyone you follow, while Choose… is on, the bar of actions at the page's foot is solid in the page's color, with the same line above it, so a row scrolling under it reads as scrolling, never as cut off.
  - **A hairline above the phone's article toolbar** (decision 0040): the bar is solid in the page's color, and the line stops a line of text looking sliced where it meets the bar. Both article bars are solid, never see-through. A tap on the text puts both away and another brings them back, with nothing sliding and the text staying where it was (decision 0242).
  - **Lines that say what was just done** (SPEC section 8): the list's floating note and the lines under a group in Settings share one build: the words, then Undo or Try again (or, for Write back's note, its ways on: Write in Gmail and the like, then Copy their address, decision 0259) as underlined semibold words in the ink (never a bordered button, never the accent, still 44px), then a × (Close the note). A list that couldn't load says so in oxblood with the alert icon and Try again in the same underlined words. While the list is out of sight (an article on a phone, the list hidden on a computer), the same note floats 12px above the article's foot (above a phone's toolbar), centred, appearing at once (decision 0229).
  - **A wait says so** (decision 0230): anything that keeps the reader waiting more than half a second says what's coming, in words where it will appear, in that place's own quiet type (a list's muted serif caption, a muted line under an article's byline, a video's own box): "Loading Outdoors…", "Loading the article…", "Starting the video…". Never a spinner, a skeleton or a fade; what arrives takes the line's place.
  - **A press is felt** (decision 0229): while a finger or mouse button is down on a row or button, a still line tone (the ink at 10%) behind it (the main action steps to the full ink), coming and going with the finger, never faded; the browser's own tap flash is off.
  - **A soft shadow under things that float:** the note at the bottom of the list, a sheet, the Aa panel, the More menus, the shortcut list, a dragged row and "Skip to articles" while the keyboard is on it, the one shadow and a faint edge each (decisions 0028, 0048, 0244). Nothing that sits in the page casts a shadow, except the 1 to 2px lift under a segmented control's chosen segment (decision 0042), never animated.
  - **A dim behind a sheet** (decisions 0031, 0039): only behind a sheet, the shortcut list or an opened picture, black at 18% by day and 45% by night, never animated; it closes with Escape or a press on it. On a phone it shows as the 10px strip above a page sheet (decision 0055).
- **Type does the hierarchy:** size and weight, never color alone or badges (the unread dot is the one mark, in the Exceptions log).
- **One accent, one family of meanings:** a page has one accent (ink blue on White, old-ink brown on Paper, pale blue on Night, decision 0176) and no second color: a starred article's star is drawn in the page's ink, filled when starred and an outline when not, so the shape says it (decision 0142). The accent marks what's *yours to look at*: what's unread (the dot) and what a search found (a faint tone behind the words) (decision 0037). Where you are is a plain grey pill with no color, its sidebar icon in ink (decision 0057). Nothing else uses it: never a button, a link, a focus outline or decoration.
- **Icons where the convention is an icon** (close, clearing the search field, back, search, settings, the sidebar toggle, the list's More (…), and the article's view original, Aa, star, write back's paper plane (a person's) and More (…); in the sidebar's icon column, each fixed list's sign; an episode row's headphones, and the podcast player's back 15, Play / Pause and forward 30, decision 0113; a video row's small screen with a play mark, decision 0149; and, at the left of each row on Settings' first page, a drawing for that setting, decision 0120; how icons are drawn is in BRAND.md → Icons), each named in its tooltip with its key and to screen readers, so the tools around the text stay quiet (decisions 0034, 0038). **Words** for + Add (the plus icon and its word), menu items, the phone's back button (with its arrow) and the main action of a form.
- **Fewer, steadier toolbars** (decision 0038): **one More (…) per place**, holding the less frequent actions as words (the list's Edit, Refresh, Mark all as read and sort order; the article's Copy link or Share and Mark unread, each with its key). **What's done most is one press away** (decision 0214): Write back, the reason to follow a person, is an icon beside Star, not a word in More. **A row's own menu** (a right-click, a long press, or the Menu key) is More's menu for one article, opened where it was pressed, with no keys printed, so the list's commonest marks need no opening. **A bar's controls keep to its pane's edges,** their icons about 20px in (lined up with the sidebar button), one group at each end, so they never float in the middle of a wide window.
- **The YouTube box** (decisions 0072 and 0149, `docs/SPEC.md` section 38): a still panel in a faint tone of the text, 12px corners (a panel that holds, decision 0244), 16 : 9 (a Short's 9 : 16, at most 20rem wide), with the video's title in the reading serif at the large size, semibold and centred, over a round Play mark in the text color and "Play video" or "Resume from 4:00". With large text it grows taller rather than cut its words. Its chapters sit under it as plain 44px rows, the time underlined in tabular figures, then the name.
- **The podcast player** (decision 0113, `docs/SPEC.md` section 35) is a still panel under the byline in a faint tone of the text, 12px corners, as the YouTube box is: one round Play / Pause in the text color (never the accent), back 15 and forward 30 as quiet line icons with their figures large enough to read (about 11px, semibold, inside the arrow), the length (until it starts playing, then the times say it) and the speed in small words, and a plain slider (its mark in the text color) with the times under it in tabular figures. No waveform, artwork, progress ring or animation. While one plays, a slim **Now playing** bar sits at the foot of the app on the tone one step off the page with a faint top line, taking its own room (it never floats over text): back 15, Play / Pause, the title over the podcast's name, and Close. On a phone it sits just above the article's toolbar, and steps aside while the same episode's own player is on screen, so the episode never shows twice (decision 0118).
- **A person's or publication's page** (decisions 0146, 0163 and 0254, `docs/SPEC.md` section 36), the same for both, one page with no Edit mode, opens in the reading area beside their list, where an article would, under the article's bar (solid, the page's color, a hairline under it once the page scrolls) with an **×** at its right end, where an article's ••• is (on a phone, a screen of its own with ‹ back); otherwise the reading area beside a list keeps the quiet line, as every list does. Their name at the top of their list ends in a quiet › ("Alpha ›"), and their name under an article's title is underlined at rest as every link is (the edge tone, 65%), so both read as the way in. It reads as a book does: title page, pages, small print. It opens with the keyboard on its title (no ring, as in a sheet) rather than ringing its X. It only lists (choosing keys of your own went with decision 0170). Nothing moves or animates.
- **Fields:** a 1px edge in a soft mix of the text color (still 3 : 1 against what's around it, checked automatically), over a faint fill, 8px corners. In use, the edge steps aside for one 2px ring in the softened text color (65%, as on rows), 1px clear of the field, so it is never the heaviest line on the screen; it keeps at least 3 : 1 against the field and every surface a field sits on in every theme by day and night, checked automatically (decision 0055). A refused field's edge turns oxblood, beside the message's alert icon and words in the same oxblood: never a thicker ring, never color alone. While the keyboard is in a refused field, the outline takes the oxblood edge's place too, so there is only ever one line (decision 0042). Room for a message is kept only where the page is centred on screen (sign-in, sign-up, the welcome): below the main action (on the sign-in pages, at the column's foot, decision 0122), so a message arriving under the field moves only what's under it (decisions 0045, 0048); in a sheet the sheet grows by its line.
- **The sign-in pages** (decision 0122; sign-in, sign-up, Forgot? and the page its link opens), the usual pattern Apple's own sign-in sheets use: one centred column (at most 320px; the phone's 20px margins) under the logo and wordmark. Each field's name is real text above it, as in a sheet (small second-ink words on the field's left edge, 8px over it), never a placeholder alone; a field's helper line sits under it as a sheet's does. Sign-up's one optional question (decision 0155) is a list to choose from built as a field, with the same edge, corners and ring. The password's name sits 24px under the email field, with a small **Forgot?** (the meta size, underlined, in the text color) at the right end of its line: small to the eye, 44px tall and wide to touch, its box reaching up into that room, never over a field. The password field holds a quiet **Show** at its right end (the small second-ink words, no edge, the field's full 44px height; it becomes **Hide**), so what's typed can be checked (decision 0191). One main action (Sign in, Sign up, Email me a link, Set password) at the column's full width; a second way in (Sign in with a passkey, Back to sign in after Check your email) is an edged button under it, never filled; the way to the other page ("New here? Sign up", "Have an account? Sign in") is a line of words with its link, centred, at the foot. The room for a message is kept at the column's foot, so a message arriving moves only what's under it.
- **A switch** (decision 0077), as Apple draws one, only where something is on or off (as Settings › Unread): a capsule track and a round knob, never animated. On, the track is filled in the text color with the knob at the right; off, an empty track with a muted edge and the knob at the left, so the knob's side says which as well as the fill. Never the accent or green. 44px to press.
- **Section names are the one label** (decision 0244, replacing decision 0123's small spaced capitals): semibold words in full ink, sentence case, at the meta size (13px, 15 on a touch screen), the sidebar's own since decision 0207, everywhere a group is named, so Folders can carry a small muted **Edit** (the same size, regular weight) at its line's end without the heading looking quieter than its button. A folder in the sidebar is a plain row starting where the fixed lists' icons start, with no icon of its own; its show-or-hide arrow waits at the row's right end, and its open feeds have a faint 1px line down their left, in the line tone.
- **Arranging the sidebar** (decision 0207): dragging marks only where something will land, and nothing slides: the row lifted stays in place at 40% and a small copy of its name (in the floating surface, with its shadow) follows the pointer; a folder's landing place is a 2px line in the text color with a small ring at its start, as a Mac draws it; a folder or list that will take someone is the strong tone (a dashed outline in forced colors), never an outline in normal colors (that's the keyboard's ring) and never the accent. In Edit, each row's handle is the **grip** icon (three short lines), in the muted grey, where its dot or arrow was. The note at the sidebar's foot is the list's note, in a band of the sidebar's tone. While a person or publication is held, the foot's Pause and Unsubscribe (decision 0248) are words alone on faint tiles (the fill tone, 8px corners, 44px tall), Unsubscribe in the oxblood, and the one under the pointer turns the line tone rather than the drop tone, which would take the oxblood words below AA; and while a link or a feed list from outside is held over the window, one line on the same faint tile at the top of the people and folders says what letting go does. Held over someone else's row (decision 0250), that row takes the drop tone and the lifted name changes to what letting go makes ("One person with Maya Chen"); an article lifted from the list is its title in the same lifted look, and Starred takes the drop tone under it.
- **The Aa panel** (decision 0123) is one quiet line per setting: its name in the one label in a fixed column at the left, its choices after it. Text size is the stepper; Page four 24px swatches, each the page it gives (White, Paper, Night, and half White, half Night for Match device), with a faint edge and its name under it, the chosen one ringed in the text color and its name semibold (decision 0176); the font plain words in the second ink, the chosen one semibold in full ink and underlined (2px), so it never shows by color alone.
- **Hiding is a list of words** (decision 0201, which replaced the one-sentence editor of decision 0123): a field with Add and three choices for how long at the top of the group, then one row per word, the word over a muted line of whose and how long, with Remove at its right. As the reader types, the field's group shows what the word would hide before Add: a semibold line of how many, the newest five as titles in the reading face over a muted who-and-where line, spaced apart with no rules between them (decision 0216).
- **Small menus and panels float under their button:** the list's and the article's More menus and the Aa panel, in the same family, a rounded card with one soft shadow, closed by Escape (back to the button), a press outside, or the keyboard leaving them. On a phone the Aa panel is a sheet along the bottom of the screen with a Close button, and the article's More menu opens upwards from the bottom bar, reading from the button outwards: its first item is drawn nearest the button, where the finger is (decision 0243).
- **One search field:** the sidebar's find field and the list's search are one slim capsule, as Apple Books draws its search: 36px tall with a mouse on a wide window, 44px on touch or a phone, 8px corners, the fill tone (the text color at 5%) and no edge, a search icon at the size beside a word in the icon column and the interface's text size; the keyboard's outline marks the one in use. The list's waits behind a Search button (a magnifier) beside More until it's asked for, on a computer as on a phone, so only the sidebar's shows at rest (decision 0242). A chip inside it is 24px tall and round; its Remove button is still a full 44px to press. Its options menu floats on the raised surface (decision 0051, replacing 0041's unfilled find field).
- **One column for the reading area** (decision 0219, after Vignelli): on a computer, whatever the reading area shows (an article, a person's page and its settings) hangs from one column: the article's left edge, right edge and line length, its room for a scrollbar, and its first line just under the article's bar (`web/src/reading-column.css`). A page of interface text there (their page) keeps its own lines to 36rem inside it, so they stay short at large text sizes. A new thing shown there takes the same column.
- **No motion.** Views switch instantly, and feedback is instant and visible without moving (principle 8).
- **One system** (principle 6, decisions 0133 and 0134, after Vignelli): one spacing scale, a short type scale, and the same thing always looks the same and is named the same everywhere. A new size, spacing, color or name is added to the system here first, never as a one-off.

- **Links in articles** are underlined in the text color, **never blue**, on every page (decision 0176).

On a day page it is the page's own tone with a thin 1px edge in the edge tone (the text color at 65%) and its small lift; on Night it is filled with the text color at 65%, its semibold words in the page's color. Never the accent.

Text stays at 7 : 1, muted grey at 4.5 : 1 and the unread dot at 3 : 1 on it, and a dark pill, or a day pill on a warm pane, keeps its pane's temperature (all checked automatically). A row under the pointer gets the fill tone (the text color at 5%), never the accent, always lighter than the pill; so does a list row while its own menu is open, on touch screens too (decision 0214).

On rows it is the text color softened (65%, still at least 3 : 1 on every area and on the pill, checked automatically; fields use the same tone, see "Fields"), drawn 2px inside their rounded edge so it never reaches over the hairlines or the pane's edge; the pill's fill always stays. #### Themes
Themes are named by the page they give (decision 0176): one setting, **Page**, with **White** (a plain white page), **Paper** (warm cream, warm-black words) and **Night** (near-black, soft white words), and **Match device** (the default), which shows White by day and Night by night, live, as the device switches. Each page has one color, its accent, for the unread dot and a search's faint tone: White ink blue, Paper old-ink brown, Night pale blue. No page's dot is green or red: red (oxblood) means only "this removes something" or "this went wrong", and red and green are the colors most often confused. Earlier sets (Apple Books' six, decision 0046; Original and Plain, decisions 0056 and 0063; the Braun themes, decisions 0136 and 0145) are gone. Before nine turtles' script runs, a page is White, or Night on a dark device. Every page meets the same contrast floors, checked automatically.

| Use | Typeface | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reading (default) | **Literata** | Made for long-form reading on screens |
| Reading (alternate sans) | **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next**, the interface's own face | Made for legibility, including low vision |
| Reading (alternate serif) | **System serif**: the device's own (New York on Apple devices, Georgia on most others) | The owner's site rohit.onl's font (decision 0063); nothing to download |

| Code in articles | **Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono** | The interface's monospaced sibling, so code sits with the rest (decision 0141) |

- All fonts are free and served by nine turtles itself (no third-party font services); System serif is already on the device.
- **Other scripts:** Devanagari and Arabic letters are set in reading faces that match Literata, Noto Serif Devanagari and Noto Naskh Arabic, which follow Literata in the serif stack and are downloaded only when a page shows those letters (decision 0227, [`BRAND.md`](BRAND.md) → "Typefaces"). Every other script uses the fonts already on the device.
- **Each script set with its own settings** (decision 0223, `web/src/scripts.css`): the reading column, sizes and the reader's font stay the same; line height 1.8 for Devanagari, Arabic, Chinese, Japanese and Korean, 1.7 for Hebrew; the title not tracked in, its line height opened (1.4 Devanagari, 1.5 Arabic, 1.3 Hebrew, 1.35 Chinese, Japanese and Korean), Arabic text a tenth larger; quotations upright and emphasis a step heavier where a script has no italics (Chinese, Japanese and Korean: emphasis dots); Japanese line breaks strict. Right-to-left articles share one right edge, byline included.
- **A fixed type scale by role** (the whole system is in "The system" above), so each level of importance looks different at a glance (size, weight and ink together, never color):
  - meta 13px (times, source names, hints, buttons' words, helper text, a group's label); 15px on a touch screen;
  - interface 15px (sidebar names); 17px on a touch screen, as a phone's own apps use. The article list's titles are this size in the reading serif on a touch screen and 19px with a mouse, with room around each row, like a book's contents page (decision 0128). A post with no title shows its opening words there instead, in the same serif and size but regular weight, two lines and then "…", so it never poses as a title (decision 0196);
  - base 16px (text boxes, running interface text);
  - large 20px (section headings inside a screen);
  - heading 24px, in the reading serif (every place's title; the list's is one line that ends in "…", at every pane width); the sidebar's wordmark is 22px in the second ink on a computer (the logo's lockup, Exceptions log) and 28px on a phone's feeds screen;
  - display 48px (sign-in wordmark).
- Article text is set by the reader: six steps, 16 to 28px, 20px by default (a tenth smaller on a phone). The article's title is 1.9 times the text size (38px by default), in regular weight and Literata's display cut, as a book sets a chapter's title (decision 0246), with tight leading (1.1) and its letters tracked in only a little, so they never touch (decision 0047), its lines balanced so a two-line title reads as two even lines, as Apple Books sets one (decision 0044, replacing 0040's lone-word rule); its headings step down clearly: 1.3 times (26px by default), 1.1 times (22px), then the text's own size, all semibold (decision 0246), with more room above than below, so the title is always clearly the largest thing on screen and a heading never competes with it (decision 0038).
- **The byline is quiet:** the meta size (13px, 15 on a touch screen) in the interface font (decisions 0050, 0244) and the second ink, a clear pause under the title (five-eighths of the title's size, about 24px by default, decision 0047), with the publisher's name leading in full ink and semibold. For someone followed the name opens the page about them, underlined only on hover, with no "›" (the "·" follows it; decision 0214).
- **In an article read offline** (decision 0101), each image is a short line in the interface font, small and in the second ink, as the end note is, so it never reads as the author's words; the list's "Offline. Showing the articles saved on this device." sits above the list in the muted meta size, as a search's count does. A failing feed's "This feed hasn't worked since …" sits in the same place, in the second ink and the small size, its button a text button in the same ink: information, never an alarm, so no accent and no red (decision 0106).
- **The cards for who isn't working** (decisions 0118 and 0186), at the top of Everyone you follow: each a card in the grouped lists' grey, 12px corners, 16px inside, 12px apart. Its name (about 16px, semibold), one sentence on what happened (its lead in semibold, "Nothing has come through since 1 Nov.", then the reason in plain words, line height 1.5), then, under a hairline, Find new address as the main filled button and Unsubscribe in oxblood words at the right end. Every target 44px.
- **Recently unsubscribed** (decision 0240), the last group of Everyone you follow, drawn as Connected assistants' rows were: the name in the interface face, under it the small muted line "Unsubscribed today · kept until 8 Dec", **Bring back** as a small ordinary button at the right (on a narrow screen it drops under the words, at the right), and one muted line under the group. No color, no count, no icon: it's a shelf, not a warning.
- **Spacing:** the scale in "The system" (4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64px).
- **Weights:** two, regular and semibold (600), each upright and italic, for both reading and the interface (decision 0246, which retired the medium weight Settings' rows had): semibold for page titles, headings and bold words in articles, in either reading font; a writer's bold inside italics is drawn from its own font files, never slanted by the browser. The article's own title is regular, at its large size. Nothing heavier, so a calm page never shouts. Semibold marks unread titles, where you are in the sidebar, the one main action in a toolbar or sheet, and a destructive action's words. Unread titles are 600 (decision 0037).
- **Three inks:** text (titles, where you are), a second ink (read titles, quieter actions); feed names in the sidebar are in full text color and muted grey (meta).
- **Line length and spacing:** one column of 57 "ch" (about 71 letters) at most; on a laptop with the list open, about 53 letters; on a phone, about 40 (decision 0187). Line spacing follows the reader's text size: 1.6 at the smallest, 1.55 at the usual size, easing to 1.5 from the second-largest up, as a book sets larger type closer (a twentieth less on a phone; never under 1.5 anywhere, WCAG's line-spacing guideline; decision 0246).
- **Faces of our own** (decision 0246, `web/src/fonts.css`): Literata's display cut (its optical size for big type, made from Google's variable Literata) for every title of 24px and more; a copy of Atkinson's zero with its slash taken out for every date in the interface (each a `<time>`, or a date inside a sentence, found and marked by `dated.ts`), where 0 can't be mistaken for O (addresses and codes keep the crossed zero); and stand-ins, the device's Georgia or Noto Serif, Arial or Roboto, resized to take the same room, so nothing moves while the fonts arrive.
- **Every part of a post** (decision 0246, `web/src/article-text.css`): a writer's highlighting in a soft tone of the ink (never the browser's yellow or the accent); keys as small key caps; a definition's term semibold with its meaning indented under it; a table's digits all one width; footnotes' numbers with room to press, underlined as every link in an article is, and their notes a step smaller in the second ink, the way back (↩) drawn as a mark, never an emoji; on Apple's browsers, a quote mark opening a paragraph hangs just outside the column. A footnote's number opens its note in a card just under the line, the column's width, in the floating family, with no movement; an English article's straight quotes, double hyphens and three dots are shown as a typesetter would set them, never inside code (decision 0247).
- **An article's lists hang in the margin** on a computer (decision 0218): their numbers and bullets sit left of the text, so a list's words start on the same edge as every paragraph, as a set book does. On a phone, and for a list inside a list, they stay inside.
- **On paper** (decision 0236): printing an article prints it alone, as a page of a book: black ink on white in every theme, pictures at full brightness, a highlight keeping its line under its words and its words at the end, nothing of the app's own (no buttons, players or Write back), and a 10pt credit line at the end naming the publisher, the writer, the date and the original's address.

### Voice

nine turtles speaks like **a friend who writes well: plain, warm, a little dry, never selling.** Unhurried and never urgent ("Nothing new yet," never "You're missing out!"). It treats the reader as an adult: helpful and careful, never talking down. Labels say what things are and do, and text with a job (a password hint, an error) stays plainly functional. No exclamation marks, no hype. **Warm, never cute** (decision 0134): warmth comes from true, specific facts, said plainly (names, dates, the writer's own words: "Mira Okafor hasn't posted since 12 Aug."), never from invented feelings or stories about people. Short, never verbose. "Calm" and "quiet" are true of nine turtles but aren't its brand: every reader says them (decision 0132).

- **Who speaks:** "I" when Rohit speaks (the website, emails, About, the 404 page, the pricing page). Never "we": nine turtles is one person with no investors. Where "I" would be awkward, no pronoun at all; the app itself speaks without one ("Nothing new yet.").
- **Every email and every line of copy is a chance to win the reader over,** so each gets real time and care.
- **Emails and empty screens** follow [`BRAND.md`](BRAND.md) ("Emails"; "Empty screens and the ends of lists"); the exact sizes of an empty list are in `spec/06-item-list.md`.

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Welcome to nine turtles! Let's get started" | "Hello. Who would you like to read?" |
| "You're all caught up!" | "That's everything." |
| "Upgrade to Pro to unlock" | "This part is for paying readers. Here's why it costs money." |
| "We're sorry to see you go" | "Done. Your account is paused; nothing's deleted. Thanks for reading." |
| "Our team is working on it" | "I'm on it. — Rohit" |

### Honest by default: a public promise

Said plainly on the website, never preached: honesty is how nine turtles behaves, not its selling point (decision 0132).

- Leaving is as easy as joining: pause or cancel in one place, one press, no survey you must fill in.
- Yes and no look the same weight. No pre-ticked boxes, no "No thanks, I like paying more."
- No fake urgency: no countdowns, no "only today", no "3 people are viewing this".
- The price, the trial and what happens when it ends are on one screen, before you start; prices adjusted by country are said plainly.
- Export everything, notes too, in one press, any time, even after you stop paying.
- What's measured is listed in plain words, and it's only what tells us something broke.
- A public page of what changed, in Rohit's words, mistakes included.

### Easter eggs: found, never shown

A few small surprises, for the reader who looks (decision 0132): still (words or a still drawing, never animation or sound), never in the reading pane or between the reader and an article, never a pop-up and never a reward for using the app more, always readable by a screen reader and never the only way to do something, and rare. Text with a job stays functional; a joke never makes an instruction less clear. The 404 page already is one and stays as it is.
