# 0319: Accessibility, round 2: names in full at large text

**In one line:** After the second accessibility check (19 November 2026, in Safari's engine as well as Chrome's), at large text names in the sidebar and a phone's Home and who wrote it under a list's titles wrap rather than ending in "…", an icon alone in its button grows only to 32px and a list's Search and More keep the page's margin, Settings' Help gets a 44px place to press on a touch screen, and an article's tables that only lay it out are read for their words, not as data.

*19 November 2026. From the second accessibility report. The owner picked all five fixes ("fix all five as you suggested").*

**What changes.**
1. **Names at large text.** At an iPhone's largest text a phone's Home cut people's names to "Ana Rodri…" (67 names on the test account), and a list cut who wrote it to "Deepa… · De…". With `data-text="large"` (decision 0313) a name in the sidebar or Home (`.place-name`, also on the sidebar's "Search articles for …" row, whose words are the reader's own) and a list row's writer and publication (`.row-feed-name`) wrap onto more lines, and the date moves down a line when there's no room. At large text this sets aside decision 0048's "always one line" for the row's meta line: at twice the size one line held only a few letters of each name. `tests/large-text.spec.ts` now fails if any of them is cut short, on every phone screen and on Home with a whole library.
2. **Icons at large text.** An icon alone in a button grew to fill its 44px button edge to edge, and a list's Search and More were pulled 24px toward the edge, so More touched the screen's edge. The icon now grows to 32px at most, and the pair is pulled by its usual 12px.
3. **Help.** On a touch screen Settings' Help link, one short word, was 31px wide, and Keyboard shortcuts' was the same; both get an unseen 44px place to press around them. The rule is shared with a footnote's number (decision 0313) in `taps.css`, as `.press-room` for any short link.
4. **Layout tables.** Some feeds carry their email's HTML, where a byline sits in a table inside a table, and a screen reader read it as data ("table, 2 rows"). `heard.ts` marks such a table `role="presentation"`: one holding or held in another table, or of one column, with no heading cells, header row or caption. A table with headings, or of several columns on its own, stays a table. Such a table is drawn as the plain blocks it holds, one under another (article-text.css), as emailed newsletters already flatten theirs on arrival (newsletter.rs): a browser ignores `presentation` on anything the keyboard can reach, so a layout table laid out 600px wide would otherwise have become a wide table, a stop for the keyboard, and a table to a screen reader again. `markWide` never marks one.

**Considered and not taken.** Flattening those tables on the server, as newsletters are: it would only reach articles fetched from now on. Keeping names on one line and showing the whole name on a long press: nothing else in the app hides words behind a gesture.

**Revisit if** a feed's real data table is read without its rows (a headed table never is), or a reader at large text finds a long sidebar too long to scan with names on two lines.
