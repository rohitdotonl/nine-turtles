# 0261: Adding someone leaves no chores

**In one line:** + Add files someone in a folder while it asks "… is…" (starting on the folder it opened over, with New folder… for a new one), so there is no separate "New folder" button; a site's comments feed is left out when its posts' feed is there; feeds up to 10 MB are read, and one too big is said as that, not as "no feed"; "You already follow this." makes the main button "Go to …"; a site with no feed is said calmly, not as an error; and a quiet line under the field gives the reader their newsletter address

*11 November 2026.*

Rohit asked for a critique of adding a person or publication, in the
manner of Jony Ive, and whether nine turtles should have an "add folder"
option at all. He reads with folders (122 of his 127 feeds are in one),
yet every add landed under People, to be dragged into a folder after.

**Folders: no "New folder" button.** An empty folder is something to
manage, not something to read. A folder comes into being with someone in
it: while adding them (here), from their page's Folders, or from Move
to… (section 15). The choice sits in the question that already files
them ("… is…" A person or A publication): **Folder**, a native list of
No folder, New folder…, a dividing line, then each folder in the
reader's order (New folder… near the top, as "New Playlist" leads Apple
Music's list, at Rohit's suggestion; the Move to… sheet and the
folder list on someone's page put it first too, so it's in one place
everywhere). New folder… shows a field for its
name; Done with no name says "Name the new folder first." It starts on the folder the sheet opened over, else No folder,
so nothing is filed unless the reader was already there. Closing the
sheet keeps what it showed, as it keeps the guess of person or
publication. Once followed, their folders open in the sidebar, so the
reader sees where they went. The welcome, where nobody has folders yet,
doesn't ask.

**Comments feeds.** A WordPress blog names its posts and its comments
("Maya » Feed", "Maya » Comments Feed"), so every WordPress blog asked
which to follow. Nobody follows a blog for its comments. `lookup` now
leaves out a feed whose address has a `comments` part or whose name says
"Comments Feed", "Comments on" or "Comments for", while a feed that isn't
comments is left; a blog with one posts feed is then followed without a
question. A site offering only comments keeps them. A blog and its
podcast are still both offered (decision 0206).

**Too big.** danluu.com's feed holds every post, 6.6 MB, over the 5 MB
limit, and the usual-address search moved on past it and said nine
turtles "couldn't find a way to follow this site", telling the reader to
ask the writer for a feed they already have. The limit is now 10 MB (the
same as a newsletter's email), and a usual address too big to read is
said as "Their feed is too big for nine turtles to read." when nothing
else is found. That problem no longer marks the field: the address is
right.

**Smaller things.** "You already follow this." makes the sheet's main
button **Go to Julia Evans** (the welcome keeps its link). "nine turtles
couldn't find a way to follow this site…" is in the second ink without
the warning mark, and the field isn't marked: it isn't the reader's
mistake. Ask them for a feed loses its heading and its "Many sites can
add one in minutes" line, which said again what the sentence above says;
the Write to button follows the sentence. The tiles' words are balanced,
so no word is left alone on a line. Under the field, while nine turtles
takes newsletters, one quiet line, "A newsletter without a website? Use
your nine turtles address", opens the address with Copy in place.
