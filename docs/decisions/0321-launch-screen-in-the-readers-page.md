# 0321: The installed app opens on the reader's page, never white for Night

**In one line:** The home-screen app's launch screen, and the moment before the styles arrive, take the page this device shows (White, Paper or Night): plain pictures of each page for every iPhone and iPad screen, one web app manifest per page for Android, and the first script points them at the reader's page.

*20 November 2026. Left over from the consistency round of 19 November 2026 (it was a bigger change than that round's fixes); the owner allowed this thread to merge once checks pass.*

**What was wrong.** A reader on Night who opened nine turtles from the home screen saw a white screen first. An iPhone or iPad shows a white launch screen when a web app gives it no picture of exactly its screen's size, and nine turtles gave none; Android builds its launch screen from the web app manifest, whose background was always White; and until the styles arrived, the browser painted its own default, which follows the device, not the reader's page.

**What changes.**
1. **Launch pictures** (`web/public/launch/<page>/<width>x<height>.png`, made by `scripts/launch-screens.mjs`): a plain picture in each page's color for each current iPhone screen (upright) and iPad screen (either way up), named in `web/index.html` with the screen size each fits (`apple-touch-startup-image`; iOS uses them only with `apple-mobile-web-app-capable`, now in the page too). Each is a one-color picture of a few hundred bytes, and only the phone's own size is ever downloaded. Plain, with no turtles or words: the page itself is what the reader sees next, so opening looks like the page appearing, with nothing moving (DESIGN.md: no animation).
2. **A manifest per page** (`manifest.webmanifest` for White, `manifest-paper.webmanifest`, `manifest-night.webmanifest`): the same app (`"id": "/"`), its background and bar in that page's color. `launch.test.ts` checks they differ only there, and that every picture is its screen's size and its page's color.
3. **Pointed at the reader's page** by the page's first script (its fingerprint updated in `server/src/security.rs`) and by `preferences.ts` (`launchAs`) whenever the page changes, so they follow the latest choice. Match device takes the device's day or night at that moment. The same script sets the page's `color-scheme` tag to the reader's day or night, so what the browser paints before the styles arrive matches too.

**Limits.** A phone reads the launch screen when nine turtles is added to the home screen (Android may refresh it later, on its own), so it shows the page chosen then: a reader who changes page, or who installed before this, removes nine turtles from the home screen and adds it again. iOS may still show a very short white flash between its launch screen and the page on a dark device (a WebKit bug, reported 2026, that no page can work around). When Apple ships a new screen size, add it to `scripts/launch-screens.mjs`, run it, and paste the links it prints into `web/index.html`.
