# 0300: An article ends once, in small capitals; the podcast's 15 and 30 get room

**In one line:** Where the ◆ ends the writer's words, the closing rule under it steps aside; Save and View original under it keep their icons, their words in the reading face's spaced capitals at the meta size; and the podcast player's 15 and 30 are redrawn with room inside their arrows.

> **Partly replaced by [0302](0302-article-top-and-end.md):** Save and View original no longer wait at the end, so their spaced capitals went with them.

*17 November 2026. Tracker item 11 ("why the New Yorker feels posh"). The owner asked for a posh, New Yorker-like reading page, turned down the magazine version ("Don't make it a magazine") and asked for something that serves short notes, YouTube videos and podcasts as well as long essays. He saw ten versions, then close-ups of five suggested changes, and chose number 5 only, the ending. He also asked for the podcast player's 15 and 30, which touched their arrows, to be fixed.*

**What changes.**
1. **One ending.** Where the ◆ ends the writer's words, the closing rule under it steps aside, so the article ends once. Articles with no ◆ (a summary, a video's description) keep their closing rule.
2. **Save and View original as a book's last page.** They keep their icons; their words are in the reading face's spaced capitals at the meta size, a little further apart.
3. **The 15 and 30.** Drawn inside a larger circling arrow with room to spare, semibold, never touching the line.

**A rule changed.** DESIGN.md → "The system" said never small spaced capitals (decision 0244). The end's two words are a named exception there and in `scripts/check-system.mjs`: the article's own furniture, in the reading face, not a group's label, which stays `.label`.

**Considered and declined.** The magazine (a rubric, a drop cap, the first line in capitals). Also shown and not chosen: a nameplate above the title, finer and wider players, a video's description set smaller, no lonely last words (decision 0040 keeps that for the last paragraph), the day in full, the name in spaced capitals, warm paper.

**Revisit if** readers miss the closing rule, or the capitals feel loud beside Write back.
