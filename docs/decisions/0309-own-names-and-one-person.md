# 0309: People by their own names, and "One person?"

**In one line:** a person is called by their own name when the articles that carry a name all carry the same one (unsigned ones no longer stop it), and when two follows share one website nine turtles asks once, "One person?", with Join them and Keep apart, which is remembered.

*18 November 2026. From the second whole-app critique (round two, "what we agreed"): the owner chose option A for names (a person's own name alone, no blog title after it in lists) and agreed the "One person?" question. Built and shipped together, without the try-on-the-Mac step, at the owner's yes.*

**What changes.**
1. **Names** (`writers::kind::name_by_author`, SPEC section 36). Before, a blog was called by its writer's name only when every article was signed; one unsigned post kept the blog's title ("Reading Room" for Asha Rao's blog). Now unsigned articles don't count against the name: if the articles that carry a name all carry the same one, that's what the person is called. Everything else stays: a reader's own name always wins, publications and feeds that name nobody keep their title, a role such as "admin" is never a name, and only a person still called by exactly the feed's own title is renamed. People already followed get it once at their feed's next check (structure change 55 sets `name_pending` again, as structure change 35 did). In lists their name stands alone; the blog's title stays on their page, under "where they publish".
2. **"One person?"** (`server/src/writers/alike.rs`, `web/src/lib/OnePerson.svelte`, structure change 55). Two follows sharing one website are often one person's (a blog and its links feed, a blog and a podcast on the same site). nine turtles asks once, in one box a tone off the page like who isn't working, at the top of Everyone you follow and in Add's question when the second is followed: **Join them** joins them as + Add a source does, with Undo on Everyone you follow; **Keep apart** goes in a new `kept_apart` table, so the same two are never asked about again. One website is one host without "www."; on a site many people share (Medium, GitHub, YouTube and the like) it's the whole address, so two writers there are never taken for one. Two publications from one site (a newspaper's sections) aren't asked about. This changes decision 0163's "nine turtles never asks": it still never joins by itself, and asks only where the evidence is strong (the same website), only once.

**Considered and not taken.** Joining same-site follows automatically (the reader should say; a site can host two people). The blog's title as a quiet second line under the name in lists (option B, mocked): more to read on every row for something their page already says. Asking about two follows on one blog host (kai.medium.com and lena.medium.com differ by host already; medium.com/@kai and medium.com/@lena by path).

**Revisit if** readers are asked about two follows that plainly aren't one person (a shared host missing from `SHARED_HOSTS`), or if the half-signed rule names a blog after a guest writer.
