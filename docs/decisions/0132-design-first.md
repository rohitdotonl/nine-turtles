# 0132: Design first, and the first settled parts of our own language

**In one line:** Design first: agents proactive about design, decisions tried in mockups (safe and bold, preferably locally); accessibility never traded; the book tiebreaker retired; "calm" not the brand; a warm voice with "I" from Rohit and never "we"; honesty a public promise; easter eggs found, never shown; "no decoration that pretends"

*1 November 2026.*

**Decision.** The owner settled these while the new design principles are
discussed (`docs/RnD/design-principles.md`):

- **Design is the most important thing in nine turtles.** Agents are
  proactive about design and bring up broken or better design even when it
  isn't the task at hand. Design, UI and UX decisions are tried in mockups
  (a safe and a bold choice each), preferably running locally on the
  owner's Mac, and the agent asks the owner first whether to work that way.
- **Accessibility is never traded.** Everything else in the design may
  change; when an idea conflicts with accessibility, we find another way
  to do the idea.
- **The book tiebreaker is retired.** Its replacement ("a letter from
  someone, or a feed from something?") is tested in UI and UX trials
  before it's adopted.
- **"Calm" and "quiet" aren't the brand.** They stay true of the app, but
  every reader says them.
- **Voice:** a friend who writes well (plain, warm, a little dry, never
  selling), treating the reader as an adult. "I" when Rohit speaks, never
  "we"; no pronoun where "I" is awkward. Every email and line of copy gets
  real care.
- **Honesty is a public promise,** stated plainly and never preached.
- **Easter eggs are found, never shown.** Functional text stays functional.
- **"No decoration" becomes "no decoration that pretends":** honest,
  handmade touches are allowed at the edges.

**Still open:** the principles themselves, the brand idea, the design
language (colors chosen after long discussion: how many, for what, as few
as possible), and the turtles' role, all to be decided with mockups.

**Why.** The owner's ideal reader judges a tool on craft and care, and
nine turtles' edge is design (decision 0131). Rules written for a quiet
Apple-like app ("book or feed", "no decoration", a neutral voice) made it
feel made by nobody in particular.
