# 0180: A shorter list of keys, and searching is typing words

**In one line:** A shorter list of keys: two columns in the order that matters, no find field or filters in it, `o`, `[`, `]`, `Shift+E`, `g f`, `n` and `Shift+N` cut, `N` for a note, `\` moved to `F`, Shift J / K walking every sidebar row, Shift written as a word on every computer, and typed search filters removed: searching is typing words.

**Date:** 5 November 2026. **Decided by:** the owner, with the owner's critic.

## What changed

- **New:** `G` then `N` goes to Notes. Tooltips say "Write back to Tomas (W)" and "Star (S)" or "Unstar (S)".
- **Keys cut:** `o` (Enter opens), `[` and `]` (`F` hides both; each pane keeps its button), `Shift+E`, `g` then `f`, `n` and `Shift+N` (next / previous unread), `Shift+H`. They do nothing now.
- **Home and End** still work with no row: everyone already expects them (in a list they choose the first and last article). Any other key that works unlisted breaks the "one list of keys" rule (decision 0103), which is why `n` was cut and not hidden.
- **The list** is two columns in the order that matters, with no find field (it replaces the find of decision 0062) and no search filters. `?` isn't printed: whoever reads the list has found it, and the Keys button's tooltip names it. The foot has only Help. Group headings are ink and semibold; spacing alone parts the rows.
- **The welcome tip** teaches the heart of the app: "Press J to read. Press W to write back."
- **Keys look the same on every computer:** capitals, and Shift as the word instead of ⇧ (this replaces how decision 0055 prints Shift). In the list, two caps, [Shift] [A]; in menus, plain grey capitals, "Shift A"; in tooltips, "(Shift A)". Key caps are only for the list and the welcome tip.
- **Typed search filters are gone** (`is:`, `feed:`, `folder:`, `after:`, `before:`). Each repeated the sidebar or the search options, which keep every one of those choices.
- **The Keys button stays,** icon and word, because a newcomer learns that shortcuts exist from seeing it.

- **Keys easier to remember:** full-screen reading moves from `\` to `F`, and `\` does nothing. Row names carry their letter where they can: View original (V, it was Open original, everywhere), Info about this person (I, and "Info about …" in the More menu and the tooltip; the page itself keeps "About …" for screen readers), Refresh (show new articles) (R), Highlight chosen words (H), Add a note (N, freed by the unread keys; it was Shift H). The group heading says how: "Go to: press G, then…". J, K, G and / stay: readers of other apps already know them. Arrow keys aren't listed.
- **Shift J / K walk the sidebar as shown** (this changes decision 0110): every row, top to bottom, Unread, Starred, Notes and All and folders included, so a reader can jump to a folder and read everyone in it. A folded folder's people are passed over. `E` ("Expand / collapse this folder") stays, as the keyboard's way to open a folder Shift J landed on. `Enter` keeps its one meaning, opening an article. No keys move between panes.

## Why

The list had too many rows and none pointed at what nine turtles is for. A list short enough to take in at a glance needs no search box; a feature nobody can find is dead weight (principles: less, but better; power is findable, never in the way).
