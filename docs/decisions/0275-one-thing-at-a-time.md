# 0275: Read one thing at a time, on a computer too

**In one line:** One thing at a time is how nine turtles reads on a computer, for every reader, with no setting: the list alone (beside the sidebar, its rows in a centred column), then the article alone in the whole window with "‹ <list name>" and Escape going back; it replaces the trial of decision 0269 and full-screen reading (`f`, decision 0241), and amends the three panes of decision 0134.

> **Partly replaced by [0277](0277-one-column.md):** the list's column is no longer centred beside the sidebar; it starts where an article's text starts.

*15 November 2026. The owner, after living with the trial: "I tried and I like Read one thing at a time. Implement it."*

**What changes.** The trial's behaviour (decision 0269, point 2) becomes the
only way a wide or medium window reads, for everyone:
- nothing open: the sidebar, as the reader has it (shown by default on a
  medium window too), and the list alone in the rest of the window, its
  title, search and rows in a centred column 42rem wide with its edges;
- an opened article fills the whole window, with **‹ Unread** (the list's
  own name; screen readers hear "Back to Unread") at the start of its bar
  and its tools at the end. It, Escape and the browser's Back go back to
  the list as it was, the keyboard on the row that was open. Opening is a
  step in the browser's history, as on a phone, and reloading at an
  article's address opens it alone;
- j, k and the arrows move to the next and previous article while reading;
- the list's notes that matter while reading (Undo or Try again for the
  article, and "nine turtles was updated. Reload to continue.") show at the
  article's foot, since the list is off screen;
- a person's or publication's page and their settings fill the whole
  window too, with their × back to their list;
- Settings, Everyone you follow and Add stay as they were. A phone is
  unchanged: it already read this way.

**What goes, in the same change.** The trial's switch in Settings (and
what kept it on the device); full-screen reading (`f`, its item in the
article's More menu, its line in the keyboard's cheat sheet), which has
nothing left to hide; the list's width handle and its 400px guard for the
article, since the list is never beside the article now; and the empty
reading area, which never shows now, with its quiet line ("Time to read
something beautiful.", decision 0064) and the one-time key tip under it
("Press J to read. Press W to write back.", decision 0062). "You were
reading …" (decision 0175) sits at the top of the list instead, as on a
phone, and the first visit's welcome takes the list's place. The sidebar keeps
its button and its width handle. Nothing about the layout is stored on the
server; only the sidebar's width (and, across an update's reload, whether
it was hidden) stays on the device. The old device keys (the list's width,
the key tip's "seen", the trial's switch) are left in browsers that have
them, on purpose: nothing reads them, they hold no personal data, and
clearing them would be code kept only for that.

**DESIGN.md.** "What's fixed" now says: still three areas (sidebar, list,
page), seen two places one at a time on a computer, the list, then the
article; one at a time on a phone. That is the owner's yes, given in the
message above, to change what decision 0134 fixed.

**Why.** On a laptop the article got about half the window beside the
sidebar and the list (the owner's review of flow 4, "Reading on a laptop").
Alone, it has the room a book page has; the writer comes first (principle
2), and one way of reading on every device is more cohesive (principle 6).

Replaces [0269](0269-one-thing-at-a-time-trial.md) and the full-screen
reading part of [0241](0241-less-but-better.md); amends
[0134](0134-ten-principles.md) (three panes).
