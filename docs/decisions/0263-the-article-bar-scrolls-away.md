# 0263: The article's bar scrolls away on a computer

**In one line:** On a computer the article's top bar (View original, Aa, Star, Write back, More) is the article's first line and scrolls away with its title; Star and View original wait again at the article's end, and what was just done floats at the pane's foot. A phone keeps its bars, and its Aa sheet tells of the tap that hides them.

*13 November 2026. The owner's choice (option A of a now-vs-after picture), shipped at his word.*

**What happened.** Rohit asked whether the bar at the top of the article
pane had to stretch across the screen and stay there while he read. It
covers only the article pane, but in full-screen reading that pane is the
whole window, so the bar was a 56px blank strip with a line right across.
Its buttons are rarely pressed in the middle of an article, and each has
a key.

**What changed.**
- **Computer:** the bar sits above the title and scrolls up with it, so
  about two more lines of the writing show in every layout, and no line
  is drawn at the window's top. The keys (`v`, `s`, `w`, `c`, `m`…) work
  all the way down.
- **At the end:** under the closing rule and above Write back, **Star**
  and **View original** wait as quiet buttons with their words, since
  that's where they're wanted. View original is left out when the note
  above already says "Read the rest on…".
- **What was just done** ("Link copied.", "Highlighted.", "Note saved."),
  which sat in the middle of the bar, now floats at the pane's foot in the
  list's own note, with its ×, so it's seen wherever you are in the article.
- **Phone:** unchanged. Its reading is short and its bars are used often
  (Back, Next, Star), so they stay until a tap puts them away (decision
  0242). Hiding them on scroll, as Kindle does, was weighed and declined
  with the owner: a Kindle's main gesture is turning the page, while here
  you leave or move on often. The tap was hard to discover, so the Aa sheet
  now ends with one quiet line: "Tap the text to hide the bars. Tap again
  to bring them back."

**Weighed.** Keeping the bar but dropping its strip in full-screen reading
only (the icons alone, in the empty margin) kept the tools in reach but
changed nothing with the sidebar and list open. A bar that hides as you
scroll down and returns as you scroll up would slide, which the no-motion
rule forbids.
