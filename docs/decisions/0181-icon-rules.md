# 0181: Icon rules: clear at a glance, one meaning each, outlines for shapes

**In one line:** Icon rules on the brand sheet: clear at a glance, one icon per meaning, familiar signs first, simple not empty, outlines for shapes and solid only for tiny marks, judged at 18px; twelve Settings icons redrawn (Newsletters an envelope with a feed sign, Hiding an eye with a line through it)

*4 November 2026.*

**Decision.** Icons follow seven rules, written on the brand sheet
(`docs/BRAND.md` → "Icons"): clear at a glance, one icon per meaning,
familiar signs first, simple but not empty, outlines for shapes and solid
only for tiny marks, judged at real size, one hand. They replace "one solid
part only where the real object has one".

**What changed.** Twelve of Settings' icons were redrawn under them:
Feed health (an outlined heart with its beat), Add from anywhere (an
outlined bookmark and a plus), Newsletters (an envelope with a feed's sign,
since Weekly email has the plain envelope), Readers (both heads outlined),
Your plan (a card with one stripe), Passkeys (a key with one tooth),
Connected assistants (a robot without its mouth), Hiding (an eye with a line
through it, since the sliders open search options), Show in the sidebar (the
sidebar's window with a check, since the plain one is the sidebar button),
Install the app (a phone with an arrow down), Font and line width (text
lines under a two-way arrow, since a pen's nib reads as writing) and
Settings' Unread row (a page with a new dot). The palette's paint and the
headphones' ear cups stay solid: they are too small to outline. The unread
dot in the list is unchanged.

**Why.** The owner disliked the solid fills; outlining small shapes looked
odd, and simplifying too far lost meaning ("a pupil is what makes it an
eye"). The owner chose each icon from three versions shown at real size,
and the rules came out of those choices.
