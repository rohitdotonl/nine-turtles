# 0302: The article's end loses its second Save and View original; the ◆ stands alone; the title rises into the bar

**In one line:** Save and View original live only in the computer's top bar, which never goes; the ◆ ends an article on a line of its own, centred; the title gets more room above it (32px, 48px on a computer); and the bar's running title is set in the title's own face and rises from the bar's edge as far as the reader scrolls, instead of fading in.

*17 November 2026. The owner asked why Save and View original sit at the article's end when the bar above always holds them, for more room above the title, for the ◆ on its own centred line, and for the bar's title to be in the title's typeface and to look as if the title itself slides under the bar and comes out on it.*

**Why the end buttons went.** They came with decision 0263, when a computer's bar scrolled away and the end was the only place left to reach them. Decision 0298 made the bar stay, and the end kept them by habit: two copies of the same two buttons. A phone never had them (its bars hold both and come back at the end).

**What changes.**
1. **No Save or View original at the end.** Write back alone follows the ◆ (or the closing rule). The spaced capitals that came with them (decision 0300's named exception) go too.
2. **The ◆ on a line of its own, centred,** under the last words or picture, as a book ends a chapter; never after code or a table, as before.
3. **Room above the title:** 32px on a phone, 48px on a computer, from 12px.
4. **The running title** in the title's face at 17px, regular, muted grey. Once the title's foot has gone under the bar, its copy rises from the bar's bottom edge into the middle, step for step with the scroll, and sinks back the same way. Nothing moves unless the reader scrolls, so it's not motion in principle 8's sense, and the fade (decision 0298's exception, `fadeIn.ts`) is removed. With reduced motion the copy is simply there once the title has gone.

**Revisit if** readers look for Save at the end, or the rising title catches the eye while reading.
