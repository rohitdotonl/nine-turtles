# Founder playbooks: verified numbers and tactics (research 19)

Date of research: 2026-10-25. 30 of 30 WebFetch calls used. Marked VERIFIED (page read), SEARCH-ONLY (seen in search result titles/snippets, page not read) or UNVERIFIED/NOT FOUND. Where a page did not give a number, that is stated; nothing is filled in from memory.

## Headline for nine turtles

1. Almost every durable small subscription here grew by one small, honest, repeatable thing: an opinionated post that landed on Hacker News once (Plausible, Bear Blog), a distribution partner (Feedbin and Reeder), or plain word of mouth (Kagi, Buttondown, Plausible). None used ads.
2. Public numbers are mostly absent. Only Pinboard (a full table), Plausible (a few MRR points), HEY (125k signups in 11 days) and Kagi (50k members, June 2025) gave hard figures. Treat the rest as qualitative.
3. Realistic scale for a calm paid reader: Pinboard held about 23-29k active users and $160-260k/yr revenue for eight years with no funding. Feedbin hit break-even at about 100 paying customers within 3 weeks. 100-800 payers at $48 = $4.8k-$38k/yr. That is plausible but is a "Pinboard-sized" business, not a Kagi-sized one.

## 1. Feedbin (Ben Ubois)

- VERIFIED, [Feedbin's First Year](https://feedbin.com/blog/2014/03/11/feedbins-first-year): launch costs about $170/month; break-even target just over 100 paying customers; the one-year goal was met in three weeks; about $1.62 profit per user per month after card fees. Launched March 2013, two days before Google announced Reader's shutdown. No subscriber count or revenue given.
- VERIFIED, [Changelog #240](https://changelog.com/podcast/240): $3/month. Ben says more customers signed up before the Reader shutdown than after (his own claim). The Reeder developer asked to support Feedbin and sync via its API, which Ben says "put Feedbin on the map." Ben went full-time in early 2014. Open source; no freemium (features not held back). Target: web professionals and technical readers.
- Not found: any later subscriber or revenue numbers.
- Tactics that mattered: (a) being a sync backend that a popular reader app supports; (b) low price, no free tier; (c) open source as a trust signal; (d) reliability and speed.
- Copy: be the nicest reader that existing apps and the OPML/feed ecosystem can plug into; make the economics tiny (about 100 payers covers costs). Avoid: expecting a Reader-shutdown-style event; that was luck of timing.

## 2. NewsBlur (Samuel Clay)

- VERIFIED but weak, [forum thread](https://forum.newsblur.com/t/an-outsized-thank-you-and-a-small-worry/4569): about 8,000 premium subscribers on the homepage counter at the time (undated in the text); a commenter doubted income exceeded expenses. No revenue posts found; the thread links a 2013 revenue graphic and blog post that were not read.
- [TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/07/30/yc-backed-newsblur-takes-feed-reading-back-to-its-basics) appeared in search (YC-backed); not read.
- Copy: a public premium-subscriber counter is a cheap honesty and trust device. Avoid: relying on this as evidence of profitability; it is not.

## 3. Readwise

- VERIFIED, [Why We're Bootstrapping Readwise](https://blog.readwise.io/why-were-bootstrapping-readwise/) (24 Jul 2018): 30-day free trial, then a "small monthly fee"; moved from freemium to paid-only; beta users kept their old version free "as a thank you." No user, revenue or price numbers.
- VERIFIED, [Forte Labs interview](https://fortelabs.com/blog/interview-with-readwise-founders/) (Oct 2018): Readwise ran a partner promotion for Forte's readers (an extra 30 days free on top of 30). No numbers.
- NOT FOUND: "first 1,000 paying users" timeline, price changes. Do not quote any figure for Readwise from this research.
- Tactic that mattered: a trusted writer's audience (Praxis, Forte Labs) with a partner-specific trial extension; free trial, not freemium.
- Copy: a free trial plus partner-audience offers to writers you respect. Avoid: copying "AI" or growth hacks; nothing verified here.

## 4. Matter, Glass, HEY

- HEY, VERIFIED, [TechCrunch, 6 Jul 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/07/06/oh-hey/): about 125,000 signups in the first 11 days (per Jason Fried); $99/yr base. Caveat: Basecamp had a large existing audience and a high-profile App Store fight. Not transferable to a solo writer.
- Matter, VERIFIED, [TechCrunch, 6 Oct 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/06/matter-raises-7-million-series-a-to-build-a-better-reading-app/): invite-only beta to public beta; no user numbers; $2M seed and $7M Series A. Free at that point. Venture-funded; do not use as a model.
- Glass, VERIFIED, [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/features/glass-celebrates-its-one-year-anniversary-we-ask-them-whats-next): launched 2021, $4.99/month or $29.99/year (iOS), no member counts disclosed. Positioning: a calm, ad-free, subscription home for a niche (photographers), sold as a secondary account and not a replacement for Instagram.
- Copy from Glass: pick a niche that cares (writers/readers of independent blogs) and say plainly "you pay, so you are not the product." Avoid: invite-only waitlist theatre (this conflicts with the project's no-dark-patterns stance).

## 6. Buttondown (Justin Duke)

- VERIFIED, [What we shipped in 2025](https://marketing.buttondown.com/blog/2025): revenue +61%, active authors +45%, unique subscribers emailed +72% (2025 vs 2024). No absolute MRR. "We rely simply and merely on word of mouth." Cash-flow profitable; no big ad spend; data export always available.
- NOT FOUND: MRR milestone series (searches pointed to jmduke.com posts that were not read). A getlatka.com page appeared in the search results; not read.
- Copy: easy data export as a trust and retention argument (nine turtles already has OPML); publish growth in percentages even if you will not publish absolutes.

## 7. Bear Blog (Herman Martinus)

- VERIFIED, [Running a blogging platform](https://herman.bearblog.dev/running-a-blogging-platform/): about 5,500 blogs at the time of writing; income is sponsorship (no revenue figure given); initial signups from a Hacker News launch of a weekend project, then readers of his own blog; custom domains and styles triggered another wave; offering custom domains and beta features to sponsors ("sponsorware") caused a sharp rise in sponsors; he writes "99.9% of people will not contribute to anything they can receive for free." Upgrade rate not given. (The page is from an early period; current numbers not found.)
- Copy: the own-blog audience as the first channel; reserve a small set of useful extras for paying supporters and say so. Avoid: assuming free-tier-to-paid conversion above a tiny fraction; Herman himself says it is tiny.

## 8. Craig Mod

- VERIFIED, [membership page](https://craigmod.com/membership/2023): "1,000+" members. $10/month, $100/year, $1,500 lifetime. Billing via Memberful and Stripe. Free student tier by email, no proof needed. Previously "Explorers Club" until July 2020, then "Special Projects". No revenue number.
- VERIFIED, [Ridgeline 057](https://craigmod.com/ridgeline/057/) (Feb 2020): a year into the program; goal "100 new members in February"; he asks directly and explains that the work is "supported 100% by paid memberships and book and print sales"; perks are wallpapers, discounts, members-only newsletter, office hours. A 5,000-word essay on running the program exists (not read).
- Lesson: about 1,000 members came from a long-built, free, high-quality newsletter audience with a direct, rare, honest ask. The earlier-built audience is the asset.
- Copy for the owner (a writer): the owner's own writing and audience are the channel; one candid ask per month; a free student tier.

## 10. Kagi

- VERIFIED, [50k post](https://blog.kagi.com/50k) (June 2025): 50,000 members including over 5,000 family plans and about 200 teams; "more than doubling our members since January 2024"; 50k came faster than 20k; "word-of-mouth marketing and community recommendations"; about 40 staff.
- VERIFIED, [help.kagi.com/company](https://help.kagi.com/kagi/company): launched June 2022 as paid; bootstrapped 2018-2023 with about $3M from the founder (so not a good analogy for cost base); about $2.5M from about 93 community angels in 2023-24.
- VERIFIED, [feedback thread](https://kagifeedback.org/d/4682-improvement-to-live-stats-chart): 20k and 50k are milestones (undated). Public live stats at kagi.com/stats (not read).
- Copy: public live stats and milestone posts that thank members; family plan. Avoid: claiming Hacker News drove Kagi; not established in what I read.

## 11. Plausible

- VERIFIED, [Indie Hackers interview, Aug 2023](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-400-mo-to-188-000-mo-in-3-years-marko-saric-of-plausible-tells-us-how-he-did-it-SqKJHAGgPOdpFsplEbhk): from about $400 MRR (under 100 paid users) to $188,000 MRR; over 11,000 paying users; ARPU under $20; churn just under 2% monthly. The post "Why you should stop using Google Analytics on your website" hit the HN front page and "changed traction" but no traffic or signup numbers are given. About two posts a week for several months in year one; reposted to HN, Indie Hackers and communities; most paid users came by word of mouth; no ads, affiliates, broadcast mailing or sales calls; open source.
- Copy: one strong, opinionated, honest essay about why you made this ("the reading you do is not a feed"), written for readers, plus consistent writing. Avoid: expecting the same: Plausible's audience (people with websites) is larger and more motivated to adopt and pay than readers of blogs, and the product replaces a free tool with a clear enemy.

## 12. Show HN outcomes (data points, small n)

- VERIFIED, [IH post-mortem, health data](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/front-page-of-hn-the-full-postmortem-traffic-lessons-surprises-cbe9e0a7f6): launch day about 468 active users (350 from HN/GitHub); the next days 100-150 a day; about 500+ unique HN visitors, about 6k page views; zero direct conversions. Title that worked was developer-benefit, not product-focused. The post's date was not stated.
- VERIFIED, [IH, 118 visitors, 0 signups](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/118-visitors-0-signups-and-the-two-bugs-i-only-found-by-digging-cc665c94e4) (Aug 2026): about 118 unique visitors in 8 days; 2 reached register; 0 signups; the Show HN was flagged within 25 minutes; analytics goal was broken; no paying customers.
- Search-only (not read): gwern.net/HN, a "Show HN playbook 2026" page (failed to load), Vincent Schmalbach on a year of HN traffic.
- Takeaway: Show HN is a lottery with a short spike and usually zero to few paid conversions; for readers who are not developers its fit is weak. Tag links, test the signup funnel, and have a simple "try it free" path.

## What nine turtles should copy, in one list

1. Be good enough for a small group to tell friends (Kagi, Buttondown, Plausible all credit word of mouth). Make the share path a plain, quiet link.
2. Publish one honest founding essay (Plausible/Bear Blog pattern) and post it once on HN, Lobsters, and relevant blog/RSS communities, and expect hundreds of visits, not thousands of payers.
3. Use the owner's own writing audience as the first 100 (Craig Mod; Herman's own blog).
4. Free trial, no freemium, no lock-in, export always (Readwise paid-only trial, Buttondown export, Feedbin).
5. Partnerships with RSS-ecosystem projects and writers' communities (Feedbin and Reeder, Readwise and Forte). Offer a partner trial extension.
6. Publish simple public numbers (Kagi stats, Pinboard table, NewsBlur counter), and say when growth is flat. Pinboard shows flat is fine.
7. Cost model: Feedbin needed about 100 payers to break even in 2013; at $48/yr nine turtles needs far fewer payers to cover a small host.

## What to avoid

- Scarcity timers, fake counters, lifetime-deal stunts, invite-queue theatre, revenue-screenshot bragging.
- Believing a single launch will carry it: the verified Show HN data points show zero or near-zero conversions.
- Venture-style comparisons (Matter, HEY, Kagi's founder funding).
