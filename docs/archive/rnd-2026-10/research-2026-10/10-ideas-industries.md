# Idea bank 10: lessons from industries far from SaaS (nine turtles)

Provenance: every item is [K] (recalled from my own knowledge). I ran no WebSearch or WebFetch, so nothing is [V]. Verify any specific fact (dates, prices, names) before it goes in public copy. Effort: S = hours, M = days, L = weeks+.

## A. Indie musicians and bands

1. **Bandcamp Friday for readers.** [K] Bandcamp waives its cut on one Friday a month, and fans plan around it. Translation: once a quarter, publish "what nine turtles earned and where it went" (hosting, Rohit's time, a donation to a small publisher or feed author). Then ask readers to nominate small writers to feature. No discount and no deadline, only a ritual people look forward to. Effort S. Why 10 customers: transparency about money is rare, and the Newport crowd reads it as integrity.
2. **Amanda Palmer "ask, don't sell".** [K] Her Kickstarter and Patreon worked through a direct, vulnerable ask to people she already knew. Translation: Rohit writes a plain "I'm a writer from Haryana, I built this with AI, it costs $48, here's why" email to his own blog readers and Twitter/LinkedIn contacts. Effort S. Why: the first 10 customers are almost always people who already know him.
3. **Radiohead pay-what-you-want, inverted.** [K] In Rainbows (2007) let fans choose the price. Translation: a "name your price between $24 and $96" annual option, with $48 as the default and the suggested amount. Public code means no one is trapped. Effort S. Why: some will pay more, and the option signals trust. Risk: it may anchor lower, so test it with a tiny cohort.
4. **Mailing list as the real asset.** [K] Musicians who survive own their email list, not their Spotify followers. Translation: from day one, a monthly letter (not a newsletter treadmill) that works as nine turtles' own "liner notes". Effort S. Why: owned audience is the only channel that doesn't die.
5. **Liner notes: the "about this release" page.** [K] Fans read liner notes to feel closer to the work. Translation: each app release gets a short, warm "liner notes" entry (why we changed X, what we left out), written like a record sleeve. Effort S. Why: it turns the changelog into content people forward.
6. **House concerts / Sofar Sounds.** [K] Small secret gigs in living rooms build a fanatical core of 20 to 50 people. Translation: a "reading room" hosted over video, or in person in Delhi or Chandigarh. Ten people each bring one article they loved and read a paragraph aloud, with no slides. Effort M. Why: a few hosts become evangelists, and each brings friends.
7. **Limited-run merch with meaning.** [K] Bands fund tours with a small, well-made object. Translation: not scarcity pressure, just an always-available, small-batch object (a bookmark, a tea-stained notecard "reading log") shipped with a yearly plan. Effort M. Why: physical objects get photographed and shared. Keep it never "sold out" to stay honest.
8. **Tour the small towns.** [K] Bands built following by playing small rooms for years. Translation: Rohit's "small-town India writes" angle: feature one independent Indian blogger per month with an interview, sent to their audience too. Effort M. Why: each featured writer shares the piece with their own readers.
9. **Fan-funded album credits ("Thanks" list).** [K] Backers appear in credits. Translation: an optional, opt-in "Early readers" page (first names or handles) in the app's About. Effort S. Why: people like to be named, and it is a quiet kind of proof.

## B. Authors and book marketing

10. **ARCs to reviewers, not influencers.** [K] Publishers send advance copies to a handful of trusted early readers. Translation: send 30 free "advance reader" accounts, hand-picked, to people who write about reading, with only a request for honest feedback. Effort M. Why: a thoughtful post by a trusted reader beats ads, and 10 paid conversions from 30 is plausible.
11. **Blurbs.** [K] Books carry quotes from authors the reader respects. Translation: ask 5 to 10 bloggers whom the audience reads to use it for two weeks and give one honest sentence; put them on the landing page with a link to their blog. Effort M. Why: borrowed trust is the main barrier.
12. **Author tour = "launch week of letters".** [K] Authors tour bookshops. Translation: one week of guest posts and podcast chats, scheduled for the same days, each ending with the same plain link. Effort M. Why: a concentrated week produces a spike you can learn from.
13. **Serialisation: release the story in installments.** [K] Dickens, and now Substack serial fiction. Translation: publish "How I built an app without code" as 8 weekly chapters, each ending with the app. Effort M. Why: serial format keeps people returning without being a hook-style feed.
14. **Brandon Sanderson's Kickstarter model.** [K] A huge, transparent pre-order with tiered pledges and honest updates. Translation: a "founding readers" tier of $96 for the first 200 people, with a named page and a say in a yearly roadmap vote. Effort S. Why: some people pay more to be part of the building, and the roadmap is public anyway.
15. **Goodreads-style shelf sharing.** [K] People share what they have read. Translation: a read-only public page listing a reader's "finished" articles (opt-in, no counts, no followers) that they can post. Effort M (needs a feature decision, so ask the owner). Why: it lets readers recommend nine turtles by showing a shelf, and it matches the book metaphor.
16. **BookTok-like "reading shelf" video.** [K] Short videos of people with books drive sales. Translation: calm 30-second screen recordings of finishing a list, with the "you're done" state as the payoff. Effort S. Why: "a feed that ends" is visual and surprising.
17. **Street teams.** [K] Authors recruit a small group of superfans with early access. Translation: a 25-person "first readers" circle with a private monthly note from Rohit and a direct line to him. Effort S. Why: 25 attentive people refer more than 2,500 casual ones.
18. **Author newsletters that recommend others.** [K] Cross-recommendations among authors are a main growth channel. Translation: a "Recommendations" swap with 10 small newsletters in the same world, each recommending nine turtles plainly. Effort M. Why: swap partners share the same ideal reader.
19. **The "first chapter free" sample.** [K] Free excerpts sell books. Translation: a no-signup demo reading list of 10 great pieces that you can finish in 20 minutes, showing the full experience. Effort M. Why: the "finished" feeling is the product, and a demo delivers it in one sitting.
20. **Book club format.** [K] Book clubs make reading social and regular. Translation: a monthly "one essay, one hour" read-along, where everyone reads the same piece and discusses it on a call. Effort M. Why: it builds a weekly habit and a community that needs the tool.

## C. Indie magazines and zines

21. **Delayed Gratification "slow journalism".** [K] The quarterly revisits events after the dust settles and sells on that stance. Translation: lean hard on "slow" as the stance, a quarterly "what we read, a season in review" page. Effort S. Why: a clear stance gets shared.
22. **The Browser: curated, paid, small.** [K] A handful of curated links daily, paid subscription, trusted taste. Translation: Rohit publishes a weekly "five worth reading" list inside the product, free to read on the website. Effort S. Why: curation is proof that the product delivers calm reading.
23. **Monocle's shops and radio.** [K] The magazine extended into shops and a radio station so the brand is a world. Translation: a small "Reading life" shelf on the site (tea, notebooks, pens that Rohit actually uses), no affiliate links. Effort S. Why: lifestyle adjacency attracts the right reader.
24. **Kinfolk gatherings.** [K] The magazine runs small dinners. Translation: host a "slow reading dinner" or chai evening in a few cities, 8 people each. Effort M. Why: intimate and photogenic, and 8 per event is already meaningful.
25. **Lapham's Quarterly: one theme, old texts.** [K] Each issue gathers historical writing around one theme. Translation: a monthly themed reading list drawn from public RSS archives (e.g. "On Leisure"), shared as an OPML. Effort S. Why: shareable, and OPML import shows the product works.
26. **Zine fairs.** [K] Tables at zine fairs sell to exactly the enthusiasts. Translation: make a 12-page zine, "How to read the web slowly", and give it away at local fairs and bookshops with a QR to nineturtles.app. Effort M. Why: physical object plus right crowd.
27. **Subscription letters.** [K] Paid letters sent monthly by mail. Translation: for annual subscribers, one hand-signed letter from Rohit each season (email by default, paper on request). Effort S. Why: attention to individuals is retention.
28. **"Issue zero".** [K] Magazines publish a free first issue to prove themselves. Translation: a single public page "nine turtles, issue zero" that shows a finished reading list, rather than marketing copy. Effort S. Why: shows, not tells.

## D. Restaurants, cafes, hospitality, tea and kirana

29. **Omakase: let the chef choose.** [K] The diner trusts the chef. Translation: an "Omakase list" mode where Rohit hand-picks 7 feeds a week for new readers. Effort S. Why: people with no RSS knowledge need a starting point.
30. **Remember the regulars.** [K] The good cafe knows your order. Translation: a one-line personal reply to every new paying reader in the first 48 hours, asking what they like to read. Effort S. Why: at under 300 customers it scales, and it creates fans.
31. **Menus as storytelling.** [K] Great menus describe dishes with care. Translation: write the pricing and settings pages like a menu, with short descriptions of each setting's purpose. Effort S. Why: copy that feels like the app.
32. **Family meal.** [K] Staff eat together before service. Translation: a "family meal" post each month showing how the app was made, what failed, with the AI agents' role. Effort S. Why: behind-the-scenes builds affection.
33. **Chai-stall conversation.** [K] The tea seller builds trust through daily presence and a conversation. Translation: a standing "chai hour" on a fixed weekly time, a public video room where anyone can ask Rohit anything. Effort S. Why: the ritual is reliable and personal.
34. **Kirana "udhaar" trust.** [K] The neighborhood shop extends credit to regulars. Translation: a no-card 30-day trial, then pay what you decide (no auto-charge). Effort M. Why: removes risk and lets goodwill work.
35. **Tea subscription gift.** [K] Tea clubs send a small packet that comes with a story. Translation: partner with a small Indian tea estate: a tea sample with the annual plan, tied to a "read with tea" essay. Effort M. Why: a sensory object is memorable and shareable.
36. **The "first sip" experience.** [K] A great cafe's first cup sets the mood. Translation: the first-run screen offers only one thing: choose 5 feeds and read the first calm piece. Effort M. Why: first minutes decide conversion.
37. **Word-of-mouth cards at the counter.** [K] Restaurants leave small cards for friends. Translation: after a reader finishes a list, a quiet, optional note: "Know someone who'd like this? Here's a link", with no reward. Effort S. Why: a gentle ask at a happy moment.

## E. Hobby communities, retreats, memberships

38. **Hobonichi yearly ritual.** [K] The planner ships in autumn, and buyers plan around it. Translation: a yearly "reading year" ritual: each January a "Year in reading" page (private, finite, no stats pressure) and a fresh start. Effort M. Why: a yearly moment gives a reason to renew and share.
39. **JetPens-style expert guides.** [K] Stationery sellers earn trust with honest reviews. Translation: publish "10 feeds to start with" guides per theme (essays, science, India, craft) as OPML downloads. Effort S. Why: useful, linkable, and exactly what a new reader needs.
40. **Mechanical keyboard groupbuys.** [K] Communities pre-order custom products together. Translation: a founding-members "group buy" where 100 readers buy together and share a public roadmap. Effort S. Why: community co-ownership.
41. **Film photography revival.** [K] The constraint (36 frames) is the appeal. Translation: market the constraint: "a list of 20, then you are done", shown beside a roll of film. Effort S. Why: constraint-as-feature is proven in the hobby.
42. **Vinyl revival.** [K] People buy records for the ritual of listening. Translation: "sit down and read" rituals: a Sunday-morning reading mode with a tea timer (a user's own setting, not gamified). Effort M. Why: ritual is the product.
43. **Slow Food movement.** [K] Name an enemy (fast food) and a positive alternative. Translation: a one-page "Slow Reading Manifesto" with ten short principles others can sign or adapt. Effort S. Why: manifestos spread.
44. **Digital Sabbath.** [K] Sabbath Manifesto and Tiffany Shlain's tech shabbats. Translation: a "Sunday reading" guide and a downloadable one-page poster. Effort S. Why: finds the audience already looking.
45. **Vipassana dana (donation-based).** [K] Courses are free, funded by past students giving, with nobody paid to teach. Translation: "pay it forward": readers can fund a year for someone who can't afford it, and the page shows how many were gifted. Effort M. Why: aligns with values and helps price-sensitive Indian readers.
46. **Retreat format.** [K] Retreats sell by setting and a short silence. Translation: a quarterly 1-day "reading retreat" online: a quiet room with a timer, no chat, 4 hours. Effort S. Why: delivers the benefit and is a funnel.
47. **Yoga studio intro offers.** [K] Studios give "first week free" and class packs. Translation: honest 14-day free access with no card; later, a 3-month pack for the unsure. Effort S. Why: lowers the commitment of $48.
48. **Gym "class packs".** [K] Prepaid bundles reduce friction. Translation: a 6-month plan at $27 next to the $48 annual. Effort S. Why: a lower upfront price captures cautious buyers.
49. **Wine and coffee clubs.** [K] Monthly surprises from a curator. Translation: a "Reader's pick" monthly: one new feed each month sent to members to try, with Rohit's note. Effort S. Why: delight and discovery without a feed.
50. **Local news and public radio pledge drives.** [K] Members pay because they value what they use. Translation: twice a year, a calm, honest "how we're doing" note and one ask; never banners in the app. Effort S. Why: the ask is predictable and respectful.
51. **Museum membership.** [K] Members get free entry and a sense of belonging. Translation: members get a "member's card" page, a yearly printed bookmark and a say in the roadmap. Effort S. Why: belonging.
52. **Little Free Libraries.** [K] Tiny boxes on streets, with a mapped registry. Translation: a "Little Free Reading List": anyone can publish a public OPML bundle at a short URL ("nineturtles.app/shelf/name") that others can take. Effort M. Why: distribution via sharing, and each shelf is a doorway.
53. **Open-source foundations.** [K] Funding via sponsors and transparent governance. Translation: public finances page (revenue, costs, Rohit's pay) updated monthly. Effort S. Why: trust and press-worthy.
54. **Wikipedia fundraising.** [K] Honest, plain banners from the founder. Translation: one annual letter in the app's footer for 2 weeks, written by Rohit; no countdowns. Effort S. Why: works because it's sincere.
55. **Church or community weekly gathering.** [K] A weekly rhythm creates habit. Translation: the Sunday "weekly edition" email: what your list collected this week, finishing on Sunday. Effort M. Why: a habit loop without manipulation.

## F. Brands with a story

56. **Indian D2C nostalgia (Paper Boat).** [K] Sells childhood memories with story-led ads. Translation: a story series on Indian reading memories: Chandamama, Champak, school library. Short essays asking readers to share theirs. Effort S. Why: emotionally resonant and shareable in India.
57. **Bombay Shaving Company.** [K] Direct tone, content around a category. Translation: a regular, practical "how to read without distraction" content series. Effort S. Why: educates people who don't know what RSS is.
58. **Japanese craft brands.** [K] Quiet confidence, "monozukuri" craftsmanship and care. Translation: a page describing how the product is made with the craft details (typography choices, margins) like a maker's note. Effort S. Why: craft attracts people who value design.
59. **Muji's no-brand brand.** [K] Plain, anonymous, quality-led. Translation: no logo splash, one consistent look, product names without hype. Effort S. Why: restraint is the identity.
60. **Apple "Think Different".** [K] Sold a worldview, not specs. Translation: a short film or text, "Here's to the readers", celebrating people who read slowly. Effort M. Why: a worldview gets shared.
61. **Moleskine's legendary notebook myth.** [K] The notebook of Hemingway and Chatwin; the myth sold the product. Translation: a true story, not an invented one: the lineage of the reading list from commonplace books to RSS. A visual essay. Effort M. Why: gives the product history and depth.
62. **Penguin paperbacks.** [K] Cheap, consistent covers created a recognisable asset. Translation: a consistent visual template for every share image, post and zine (one palette, one typeface). Effort S. Why: recognition compounds.
63. **Everyman's Library and Folio Society.** [K] Beautiful editions people collect. Translation: "Collected editions" of the year's best essays from feeds, with permission, as a beautifully typeset PDF for members. Effort L. Why: gift-worthy and tangible; needs publisher consent.

## G. Boring cash-cow online businesses

64. **MetaFilter's $5 signup.** [K] A small one-time fee kept the community good. Translation: say plainly that the price is a filter: "$48 keeps this quiet". Effort S. Why: price as a promise of no ads.
65. **Something Awful's $10 fee.** [K] Paid accounts ended spam and funded the site. Translation: no free tier exists, which is the feature; say it in copy. Effort S. Why: clear positioning.
66. **Pinboard's rising price.** [K] Price rises with each new member, rewarding early joiners. Translation: a "price ladder" with a transparent, honest schedule: early readers keep their price for life. Effort S. Why: genuine early-adopter reward, not scarcity theater (state the rule publicly).
67. **Tarsnap's picodollar pricing.** [K] Absurdly precise, honest pricing earned nerd love. Translation: a "reading cost" line: $48 is about 13 cents a day, shown as tea-sized "less than a cup of chai a month". Effort S. Why: a memorable price frame.
68. **Sublime Text's unlimited trial.** [K] The trial never expires, and people pay out of respect. Translation: let the free trial run on a read-only basis after expiry (existing lists still readable), with a quiet "buy" in settings. Effort M. Why: reduces resentment and keeps the door open.
69. **Fastmail and Hey: paid email as a principle.** [K] "You are the customer." Translation: a page "How we make money: only from you". Effort S. Why: the reader's trust.
70. **Directories and Craigslist.** [K] Simple lists, locally focused, little design. Translation: a public directory of recommended small-web writers (with permission), ranked by nothing. Effort M. Why: attracts writers whose readers then arrive.
71. **Mailing-list businesses.** [K] A single weekly email with a clear promise can monetize a few thousand readers. Translation: Rohit's weekly letter "Slow Sunday" is the top of the funnel for the app. Effort S. Why: the most reliable channel at this scale.

## H. Wild and unproven

72. **A "Do nothing" day.** [K] Wild: once a year, nine turtles shows a quiet page on a chosen day and sends no email. Press may notice. Effort S. Why: on-brand and memorable. Unproven.
73. **A reading room in a chai shop.** [K] Leave a bound copy of a weekly printed list at a local cafe with a QR code. Effort M. Why: reaches unreached locals and tests offline.
74. **Handwritten onboarding note.** [K] The first 100 customers get a handwritten postcard from Rohit (note: postcards were covered elsewhere, so treat as a variant: a handwritten thank-you only). Effort M. Why: remembered.
75. **Public "unread" amnesty.** [K] A yearly day to mark all unread as read without guilt, with a ritual post. Effort S. Why: relieves backlog guilt, which is a real pain and a strong hook for lapsed readers.
