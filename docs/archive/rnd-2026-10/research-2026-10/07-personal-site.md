## 2. How founders used personal sites (what worked, quantified where I could)

Honest caveat: the quantification I could verify is thin. Mechanisms are well attested; numbers mostly are not re-verified here.

- **Bear Blog / Herman Martinus [V].** Bear started as a weekend project and "just happened to explode on HackerNews"; about 5,500 blogs at the time of his post; no deliberate marketing; growth came from HN plus readers of his own blog; spikes again when features (custom domains/styles) landed; sponsors rather than ads, he notes 99.9% won't pay for what is free. His review of the "discovery feed" takes 15 to 20 minutes a week. Lesson: a personal blog post telling the build story, plus one honest HN hit, was the whole launch. https://herman.bearblog.dev/running-a-blogging-platform/
- **Patrick McKenzie (kalzumeus) [S/B].** Wrote long, searchable, generous essays on running Bingo Card Creator (annual "year in review" posts list SEO/traffic/revenue openly) and "marketing for people who would rather be building stuff". Mechanism: public numbers + teaching posts compound via search and HN, and led to consulting/Starfighter/Stripe work. Sources: https://kalzumeus.com/2013/04/24/marketing-for-people-who-would-rather-be-building-stuff and https://kalzumeus.com/2011/12/21/bingo-card-creator-etc-year-in-review-2011. Specific figures not verified this session.
- **Derek Sivers [S/B].** Popularised /now pages (nownownow.com directory) and posts short, opinionated, no-tracking essays; sold CD Baby for a reported ~$22M [B]. Relevant: Rohit is reading Sivers right now, which is a natural hook for an email/post exchange. https://sive.rs/
- **Brent Simmons / NetNewsWire [S].** Blog (inessential.com) and a decades-long reputation in the feed-reader world; NetNewsWire returned to him from Black Pixel in 2018, and the community learned of it via blogs (MacStories, Michael Tsai) rather than ads. https://www.macstories.net/news/black-pixel-transfers-netnewswire-to-brent-simmons/ ; https://mjtsai.com/blog/2018/08/31/netnewswire-comes-home. Lesson: the feed-reader audience is small, blog-native and concentrated in a handful of influential bloggers; getting those few to mention you is worth more than ads.
- **Manton Reece / Micro.blog [S].** Launched through a Kickstarter plus a book, announced on his own blog and picked up by MacStories (https://www.macstories.net/linked/manton-reece-launches-campaign-for-microblogging-service-and-book/). Lesson: the founder's own blog was the launch channel; the product's category (blogging) matched the channel.
- **Pieter Levels, Marc Lou, Justin Jackson, Daniel Vassallo, Arvid Kahl [S/B].** The "build in public" family. Marc Lou's own story: "0 to $65k/month in 2 years" (https://marclou.beehiiv.com/p/my-solopreneur-story-0-to-65k-month-in-2-years, unread). Summary article claiming a "10-year distribution strategy" for build in public: https://www.softwareseni.com/building-in-public-the-10-year-distribution-strategy-behind-solo-founder-revenue/ (unread). Caution: these founders sell to other makers on X; their audience is the customer. Nine turtles' customers are readers, not makers, so copy the transparency, not the channel (X).
- **Simon Willison [S/B].** Built a huge following by posting daily, small, link-style notes about LLMs on a plain blog with a feed, aggregated on HN. A 2025 analysis of top HN bloggers exists (https://feedbagel.com/post/analysis-of-2025s-most-popular-hacker-news-bloggers-and-their-success-factors; unread). Lesson: frequent small posts under a consistent topic beat rare polished ones, and it is a topic with an existing appetite.
- **Maggie Appleton, Andy Matuschak, Gwern, Tom MacWright, Robin Rendle, Kevin Kelly, Paul Graham, Paul Jarvis, Jason Fried [B].** Mostly "ideas first, product later" authors; their products (if any) ride on a reputation for thinking. Applicable only partly: Rohit's essays-about-reading could play that role slowly. I did not verify numbers for any of these.

**Pattern across all of them:** (1) the writing is useful or interesting on its own, not an ad; (2) there is a feed and plain URLs, so it is syndicated and found by people who read blogs (the same people who use feed readers); (3) one outside amplifier (HN, MacStories, Kickstarter) did the launch work; (4) it compounds over years, not weeks. For a 100 to 200 customer goal, the realistic version is the Bear Blog pattern: one honest build-story post, one outside amplifier, a permanent link in the footer.

## 3. Content Rohit could create (ranked by payoff/effort)

Everything below is a page or post on rohit.onl (or nineturtles.app/website), each with a plain link to nine turtles.

1. **"A writer, not a programmer, built a reader with AI agents" (flagship essay).** What: the honest story: why he wanted a calmer reader, how he directed agents, what he specified, what broke, what it cost, what he thinks about the open web. Why: it is a rare, true, 2026-shaped story, and the repo's public docs, decision records and changelog are proof, not claims. The "non-programmer built it" angle is already a crowded genre on Substack/Medium (examples: https://kp.substack.com/p/from-shoulder-pain-to-app-store-in, https://lizplosser.substack.com/p/i-vibe-coded-a-wellness-app, https://platformer.news/claude-code-review-web-design [S]), so the difference must be the unusual proof: a paid product with decision records, backups, accessibility floors, not a toy. Lead with the product ethos, not the AI: "AI built it" alone is a low-differentiation headline now; "I wanted a book, not a feed" is the hook. Effort: 1 long post (a few days of writing). Payoff: highest; this is the post that gets submitted to HN, newsletters and podcasts. Also a Show HN candidate; see risk note on HN and AI-built projects.
2. **A public blogroll / "What I read" page with OPML export.** What: /blogroll listing the feeds he follows, grouped, with a download link to the OPML and "open in nine turtles" instructions. Why: blogrolls are a well-known IndieWeb convention (see blogroll.org submission page [S]: https://blogroll.org/submit-a-blog), it demonstrates the product's purpose (reading deliberately), and it links outward to bloggers who tend to notice and link back (a generous, non-spammy source of backlinks and goodwill). Effort: 1 to 2 hours; payoff: moderate, and evergreen. Caution from AGENTS.md: never commit his OPML to the repo; publishing it on his personal site is his choice and separate.
3. **/now update plus "What I'm reading in nine turtles" monthly.** His /now is already live (updated 31 Aug 2026). Add one line: "I read it in nine turtles". Effort: minutes. Payoff: small but constant; nownownow.com is the directory of /now pages [B].
4. **/uses entry for nine turtles.** Add "Reader: nine turtles (I made it)". Effort: minutes. /uses pages are browsed by exactly this kind of audience [B].
5. **Colophon/footer link.** "Made with 11ty. Read it in nine turtles." Both sites use Eleventy, which is also a convenient content-sharing path (shared partials). Effort: minutes.
6. **Public build log / "decisions" digest.** Convert the repo's changelog (docs/changes) and decision notes into a monthly "what changed and why" post. Why: the repo already generates this, so the marginal effort is editing, not writing; posts like Kalzumeus's yearly reviews show transparency compounds. Effort: low (monthly 30 min); payoff: slow, steady, SEO-neutral but trust-building.
7. **Essays on attention and reading, in his existing voice.** He already has "What it means to read a book", "Two teaspoons of information, please." and "AI is killing the open web". Writing 3 to 5 more per year in this vein and linking each to the product where it naturally applies is the lowest-risk way to reach the Newport/Clear/Sivers audience, because they search for and share this kind of piece. A "reading diet" post (what he follows, how many feeds, why he keeps inbox-zero finite) fits the product thesis. Effort: ongoing; payoff: slow compounding.
8. **"Digital minimalism from a small town in India."** A differentiator: most of the digital-minimalism canon is US-based; a first-person account from Mandi Dabwali (low bandwidth, Linux Mint, Android ebook reader, walking) is genuinely uncommon. Don't make it exotic; make it specific and warm.
9. **Transparent cost/revenue page (/open).** What it costs to run on Render Starter + R2 + domain; subscribers (real number, even if small), expenses. Why: open-startup pages are a long-standing indie tradition (Kalzumeus yearly reviews as precedent). Risk: early numbers will look tiny; frame as "day N". Effort: low once, then monthly. Payoff: trust, press hooks ("a 100% transparent solo reader").
10. **"100 days to 100 readers."** A numbered series (short posts, mostly numbers and what he tried). Why: it gives the audience a reason to subscribe to his feed (and so to a feed reader), and creates a natural day-100 post that is shareable. Effort: high (daily or weekly discipline). Recommendation: do it weekly, not daily; the founder's own pattern (8 posts in 2025, then a pause) says daily would fail. A series that fails publicly is worse than none.
11. **"How I direct AI agents" playbook.** Spec-first workflow, decision records, second-agent review, the "floors" in AGENTS.md. This is the most useful piece for the builder audience (AI newsletters/podcasts) and the one least likely to convert readers into customers; do it for reach, not revenue.

## 5. Distribution with minimal effort (POSSE)

POSSE = publish on your own site, syndicate elsewhere.

- **Own site first.** The Atom feed already exists; keep it full-content. Add dates to the list pages (currently missing) and a visible "subscribe in a reader" line.
- **Where to syndicate (for this audience and story), ranked:**
  1. **Hacker News** (one-time, for the flagship essay or a Show HN). It launched Bear Blog [V]. Be aware that HN readers are sceptical of AI-built projects; lead with the repo, docs and constraints, not hype. I did not find reliable data on how such posts perform (search returned individual blog posts, not statistics).
  2. **LinkedIn** (good for the "AI-assisted builder" story and for his content-marketing network; low effort: paste the post's first paragraph and link). Plausible but unverified for conversion.
  3. **Bluesky/Mastodon** (the audience that reads blogs and RSS skews there, and both can be fed from the Atom feed by automation; post a link and a line, no engagement farming).
  4. **X** (most build-in-public founders live here, but the nine turtles reader does not; skip or post-only).
- **Cadence that fits his history (2 to 8 posts a year):** one post a month is a step-change; plan on a two-week queue, no daily posts.
- **Automation without lock-in:** a feed-to-social tool or simple script; keep nothing only on a platform. Consistent with the project's no-lock-in rule.

## 6. Risks

- **Attention split.** He is one person with a day job in content marketing. The honest budget is about 2 hours a week of non-product work. Priority order: items 3 to 5 (minutes), then the flagship essay (item 1), then item 2. Skip the daily series.
- **Credibility as a founder in India.** Not a real liability for this audience; arguably an asset (distinct voice). Real risks are pricing/payments trust and perceived reliability, handled by the public docs, backups and the open repo. Don't lean on "AI did everything"; own the decisions.
- **"AI-built" backlash.** Some readers (particularly anyone who agrees with his own post "AI is killing the open web") may see an AI-built reader as inconsistent. Address it directly in the flagship essay: that post is already on his site and is the best preemptive honesty.
- **Privacy.** The About page already discloses age, hometown and employer type. Fine, but keep customer data, revenue per user, and credentials out of public numbers. The repo is public: never commit his OPML or personal reading list (AGENTS.md hard rule); publishing a curated blogroll on his own site is a separate, deliberate choice.
- **Authenticity.** His strongest asset is the voice (warm, self-deprecating, no pitch). A sales-y product page on the same site would break it. Keep nine turtles as one honest paragraph plus a link, in the "More" menu, and let posts earn the clicks.
- **Cadence risk.** A public series he abandons (the 2026 pause is already visible) hurts more than not starting.
