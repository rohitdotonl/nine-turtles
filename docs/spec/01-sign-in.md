# 1. Sign-in

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- nine turtles has a public web address, so it asks who you are: an **email and a password** (decision 0087). Each reader has their own.
- **The owner** (the person who set nine turtles up) signs in with their email and password like everyone else; the password alone signs nobody in (decision 0116). Leaving the email empty and pressing Sign in sends nothing: "Type your email too.", and the keyboard moves to the email field. On a brand-new nine turtles the owner has no email yet: their first sign-in, with an email nobody has and the owner's password, signs them in and saves that email as theirs (they can change it later in Settings › Account, by a link to the new address, as every reader does; decision 0125).
- Each device stays signed in for a long time, so you rarely type it.
- Each device stays signed in for a year after it was last used. Signing out ends that device only; other devices stay signed in.
- Repeated wrong passwords are slowed down, so nobody can guess by trying thousands: after five wrong tries in a row, nine turtles asks you to wait a minute, and each further wrong try doubles the wait, up to an hour. The right password resets it, and so does an hour without wrong tries. **Wrong tries count for each account on its own** (each email, the owner's email too, so a stranger can't find out which email is the owner's), wherever they come from: a stranger guessing someone's email makes only that email wait, never anyone else. Once an account has to wait, further tries are refused at once, without checking the password. Checking a password takes a lot of memory, so only a few are checked at a time, in one line for everyone (a flood of guesses can slow every sign-in while it lasts, the owner's too, so the time taken never tells which email is the owner's). A password longer than 1,024 bytes is refused at once as a wrong password. Devices already signed in are never affected. Restarting nine turtles on Render clears every wait (`docs/operations/render-setup.md`).
- nine turtles **never says whether an email has an account**: a wrong password and an email nobody has get the same answer, in about the same time.
- The page shows the logo (the nine turtles, decision 0067) above the wordmark, one quiet line under it ("Read people. Write back.", the headline; decision 0142), then, in one centred column (decision 0122): **Email** and its field; **Password** and its field, with a small **Forgot?** at the right of the Password name's line while nine turtles can send email; **Sign in**, the one main action, 12px under the password with the same rounded corners; where passkeys work, **Sign in with a passkey** under it as a quieter, edged button; and under those, "New here? **Sign up**". Each field's name is real text above it, on the column's left edge, 6px over the field (never only a placeholder), and the password's name sits 24px under the email field, so each name stays with its field. Forgot? is small to the eye but 44px tall and wide to touch, its box reaching only into the room above the Password name's line. The keyboard goes Email, Password, Show, Forgot?, Sign in, Sign in with a passkey, Sign up. The email field has the keyboard's place when the page opens, and tells the browser it is an email (`autocomplete="email"`; where passkeys are offered, `username webauthn`, which passkey autofill needs) and the password field `current-password`. On a phone the column runs between the same 20px margins as every other screen; on a larger screen it is at most 320px wide (decision 0048).
- **The password field on each sign-in page has a Show button** at its right end, inside the field (decision 0191): on sign-in, sign-up, confirming the email and choosing a new password. (Settings' "Your password" checks, before changing your email or adding a passkey, don't: that's a password you just typed to get in.) Typing 12 characters you can't see, on a phone, causes mistakes. Show turns the dots into what's typed and becomes **Hide**; screen readers hear "Show password" (or "Show new password"). It's a quiet button in the second ink, never the accent, the field's full 44px tall, and the keyboard reaches it after the field. What's typed stays as it is, and the page opens with the dots each time.
- Messages are plain: "That email and password don't match." (with an email), "That isn't the password." (without one), or "Too many wrong tries. Try again in 2 minutes." They sit right under the fields, centred, in oxblood words after a small warning mark, said to screen readers as they appear, and move only what's under them down (the room for them is kept at the column's foot, so the fields and the title stay still); a refusal also marks the fields themselves (their edge turns oxblood, and screen readers hear them as invalid).
- Sign out is in Settings.
- Passwords themselves are never stored, only a scrambled version that can't be turned back into them (a "hash"). The owner's is set by typing `nine-turtles set-password` in Render's Shell tab and pasting the scrambled line it prints into Render's settings (`docs/operations/render-setup.md`); until then, the owner can't sign in. A reader's is scrambled the same way when they sign up.

**Signing up** (decisions 0087, 0115, 0154 and 0195)
- **Sign-up is always open:** anyone can make an account, with no invite (decision 0115). The sign-in page always shows "New here? **Sign up**" under the form.
- **The sign-in page has its own address, `/sign-in`** (decision 0206), as sign-up has `/sign-up`. Signed out, any other address shows the sign-in page too, and after signing in opens where it pointed. Signing in at `/sign-in`, or visiting `/sign-in` or `/sign-up` while signed in, opens nine turtles' home, never "Wrong turn?".
- **The address is confirmed first** (decision 0154, the owner's choice): while nine turtles can send email, signing up makes nothing until the reader follows a link emailed to the address, so nobody can make an account with an address that isn't theirs.
- **The sign-up page** (`/sign-up`; the website's **Start reading** comes straight here, decision 0191) is built like the sign-in page (decision 0122): the logo and wordmark, then **Email** (`autocomplete="email"`), **Password** (`new-password`, "At least 12 characters." under it on the field's left edge), each named above its field, then the optional "How did you hear about nine turtles?" (section 41), then **Sign up** and "Have an account? **Sign in**" (which goes to `/sign-in`). Signed in already, `/sign-up` opens nine turtles itself.
- **Check your email.** Pressing Sign up turns the form into **Check your email** (the keyboard goes to it) and "A link is on its way to … Open it in this browser to finish signing up with one press (anywhere else, it asks your password). It works once, within 24 hours.", in the text color. Under it, quietly, as after Forgot?: where the address is a Gmail, Outlook or Hotmail, iCloud or Proton one, a link in words to its inbox ("Open Gmail", "Open Outlook", "Open iCloud Mail", "Open Proton Mail"; it opens in a new tab, so this page and its Send again stay; nothing opens by itself; decision 0195), then "Didn't get it? Look in your spam folder." and **Send again** (within a minute of the last link it sends nothing and says to give it a minute; after that, "Another link is on its way."). Then **Use another email**, an edged button, which returns to the form with the email kept, to fix a mistyped address. Nothing on the page changes by itself.
- **The email**, "Confirm your email for nine turtles", holds the one link (`/finish-sign-up`, its ticket after the `#`, as every link's is) and says that if you didn't ask, ignoring it makes no account. It opens a page built like sign-in: the logo and wordmark, "Confirm your email.", then **Confirm email** (nothing happens until it's pressed, so a mail program checking links can't use the link up). **In the browser that signed up, that's all** (decision 0193): pressing Sign up left a random secret in that browser (a cookie the page itself can't read, sent only to sign-up, gone after the link's 24 hours; nine turtles keeps only a scrambled copy), and the link with that secret is proof enough, so the keyboard is on Confirm email and nothing is typed. **Anywhere else** (another browser or device, or once the secret has gone) it asks **Password** first (`current-password`, the keyboard in it, "The one you chose when you signed up." under it), with, quietly under the button, "Forgot it? **Sign up again**", which opens the sign-up page. The password is asked so that only whoever signed up can finish: a stranger who signs up with your address can't make the account theirs, or yours with their password. The stranger's browser holds a secret, but they never get the link; you get the link, but your browser holds no secret of theirs, so you're asked yours. Either way, confirming makes the account, signs this device in and opens nine turtles straight away, on its welcome.
- A wrong password says "That isn't the password you chose when you signed up." under the field (marked as invalid) and the link keeps working; wrong tries are slowed down as sign-in's are ("Too many wrong tries. Try again in 2 minutes."). Signing up more than once with the same address (say, after choosing another password) sends no new email within the minute, and any link sent works with any of the three newest passwords chosen. A link used before or past its 24 hours says "This link has expired or was already used. Sign up again for a new one.", with **Sign in**; so does every other link for the address once the account is made. If the address got an account meanwhile: "That email already has an account. Sign in instead." (When the link can't be used, the form gives way to the message, and the keyboard goes to it.) If the hour's 100 accounts are made: "Many people have signed up in the last hour. Try this link again later.", and the same link works later.
- Messages on the sign-up page: "That doesn't look like an email address." (one @ with something either side, at most 254 characters, and only the address: no name before it and no spaces, `<` `>`, quotes, commas, semicolons, colons, brackets or backslashes), and "Choose a password of at least 12 characters."
- **Sign-up never says whether an address has an account** (decision 0195, replacing what decision 0115 accepted). Signing up with one answers "Check your email" exactly as a new address does, and that address gets an email instead of a link: "You already have a nine turtles account", saying someone (hopefully them) asked to sign up with it, nothing new was made, and to sign in at nine turtles' address with their password or press Forgot? there; if it wasn't them, nothing has changed. Its one link is nine turtles' own address, never a ticket. It keeps the same limits as a sign-up link (one a minute, three emails to one address in any hour). Only where nine turtles can't send email (on someone's own computer) does the page say "That email already has an account. Sign in instead." (the Sign in under the form takes them there).
- **At most 100 sign-ups are tried in any hour**, for the whole app (nine turtles sits behind Render's proxy, so it can't reliably tell one place from another), as a guard against bots. A try with an email that already has an account counts too, so nobody can keep trying. Sign-ups are handled one at a time. At most 100 accounts are made in any hour too (raised from 20 by decision 0195, so a busy day isn't turned away; an account is made only once its link is followed). The 101st hears "Many people have signed up in the last hour. Try again in an hour." Sign-up links keep the limits every emailed link keeps (below): three emails to one address in any hour, one a minute.
- **Without email** (the settings aren't set), signing up makes the account at once, signs this device in and opens nine turtles, with its address not confirmed. Nobody then checks the address is really theirs.
- **Readers who signed up before addresses were confirmed** count as confirmed (the owner's choice, decision 0154). An address is also confirmed when its reader follows a link to a changed address or a sign-in link from Forgot?.

**Forgot? A link that signs you in** (decisions 0096 and 0122)
- While nine turtles can send email, the sign-in page shows **Forgot?** at the right of the Password name's line. It's for every reader, the owner too once they've saved an email. (It replaced the separate "Email me a sign-in link" and "Forgot your password?", decision 0122: one way back in, which also lets you choose a new password.)
- It turns the page into **Email** and its field (the email already typed stays, and the keyboard goes to it), "We'll email you a link that signs you in. You can choose a new password after." under the field, **Email me a link**, and **Back to sign in**, which returns to the sign-in form with the email kept.
- The answer is always the same, said once (decision 0092): the form gives way to **Check your email** (the keyboard goes to it) and "If … has an account here, a link is on its way. It works once, within 15 minutes.", in the text color, whether or not the email has an account. Under it, quietly: the same link to a Gmail, Outlook, iCloud or Proton inbox as after signing up, then "Didn't get it? Look in your spam folder." and **Send again**. Pressed within a minute of the last link it sends nothing and says "The link went less than a minute ago. Give it a minute, then send again if it hasn't come."; after that it says "Another link is on its way." Nothing on the page changes by itself. Then **Back to sign in**, an edged button.
- The email, "Sign in to nine turtles", holds the one link (its ticket after the `#`, so it never reaches a log; the page takes it out of the address bar at once). It opens a page built like sign-in: the logo and wordmark, "Press Sign in to read on this device." and **Sign in** (one press, so a mail program checking links can't use it up). Pressing it signs this device in; the other devices stay signed in and the password stays as it is.
- Then, in the same place: **You're signed in** (the keyboard goes to it), "Choose a new password, if you like.", **New password** (`new-password`, "At least 12 characters. Your other devices will be signed out." under it), **Set password**, and **Not now**, which opens nine turtles with nothing changed. Set password does what a password link does (below): every other device signed in as that reader is signed out, any link not yet used stops working, and this device stays signed in and opens nine turtles. The choice lasts an hour, only on this page, and only while the account keeps the address the link went to; nine turtles keeps it in memory only, so a restart ends it too. After that, Set password says "That took too long, so your password stays as it is. To choose a new one, sign out and press Forgot? again.", with **Open nine turtles**.
- A link used before, past its 15 minutes, sent to an address the reader has since changed, or from before a new password, says "This link has expired or was already used. Ask for a new one from the sign-in page.", with **Sign in**.
- Without email (the settings aren't set), Forgot? isn't shown, as the old links weren't; asking anyway says "This nine turtles can't send email yet. Ask whoever runs it to set a new password for you."

**A passkey** (decision 0109)
- A passkey signs you in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN instead of a password. You add one in Settings › Passkeys (section 18); passwords keep working exactly as before, for every reader.
- Where passkeys work, the sign-in page shows **Sign in with a passkey** under Sign in, a quieter button edged in the muted grey and not filled, the column's full width and 44px tall (decision 0122). It opens the browser's own passkey sheet; choosing your passkey signs this device in and opens nine turtles, with no email or password typed. The email field also offers your passkeys among its suggestions, as browsers do for saved passwords (passkey autofill), where the browser can.
- Cancelling the browser's sheet says nothing. A passkey nine turtles doesn't know (one removed in Settings, or made for another nine turtles) says "That passkey isn't one this nine turtles knows. Sign in with your password."; a passkey chosen after the page's challenge ran out (five minutes) says "That took too long. Try again."
- Passkeys belong to nine turtles' public address (app.nineturtles.app). Anywhere else (slowfeed.onrender.com), and in a browser without passkeys, nothing about them is shown and the page is as before.
- Signing in with a passkey is exactly like signing in with a password afterwards: the device stays signed in for a year after it was last used. A passkey can't be guessed, so a refused one makes nobody wait. No email is sent.

**A new password** (decisions 0088 and 0122)
- A new password is chosen after Forgot?'s link (above). The older password link, "A new password for nine turtles", is no longer offered on the sign-in page, but one already sent keeps working for its hour (and its route stays, for a page left open from before).
- That older link (its ticket after the `#`, so it never reaches a log; the page takes it out of the address bar at once) opens a page built like sign-in: **New password** ("At least 12 characters. Your other devices will be signed out." under it) and **Set password**. It works once, within the hour, and only while the account still has the address it was sent to (as with a sign-in link). Setting it signs out every other device signed in as that reader and signs this device in. Passkeys stay (the owner's choice, decision 0109): if you fear someone else got in, remove any passkey you don't recognise in Settings › Passkeys. A link that has run out says "This link has expired or was already used. Ask for a new one from the sign-in page.", with **Sign in**.
- The owner can use it once they've saved an email; the new password then replaces the one in Render's settings, with or without the email, for as long as that setting stays as it is. **The owner's way back that needs no email:** putting a new `NINE_TURTLES_PASSWORD_HASH` in Render makes it the owner's password again when nine turtles restarts; the one set by email stops working, every device signed in as the owner is signed out, and any password or sign-in link not yet used stops working (`docs/operations/owner-password.md`). Passkeys stay; remove any you don't recognise in Settings › Passkeys.
- Forgot? takes as long whether or not the email has an account.
- **At most three emails go to one address in any hour**, whatever they're for, **and one link of each kind (password, sign-in, change of email, sign-up) a minute**, so pressing Email me a link twice sends one email. Past either, the answer stays the same and nothing is sent, so nine turtles can't fill anyone's inbox. Limits count each address wherever the requests come from: nine turtles sits behind Render's proxy, so it can't reliably tell one place from another.
- A new password also ends any sign-in link not yet used, and any other offer to choose one.
- Emails are short plain text, signed "Rohit" with "nine turtles" on the line under it (decision 0142): no pictures, no tracking, and no link but the one asked for. Each one that holds a link opens and ends the same way (BRAND.md → "Emails", decision 0200).

**How to check it**
1. Open the app's address in a private browser window. You see only the email and password fields, nothing about your feeds.
2. Type your email and a wrong password several times quickly. After a few tries it asks you to wait before trying again. Leave the email empty and type your password: "Type your email too."
3. In another private window, type a made-up email and a wrong password: "That email and password don't match." Do it six times: only that email has to wait; the owner can still sign in in the first window.
4. In another private window, type your email and the right password. You're in. Close the browser, reopen the address: you're still signed in.
5. Once email is set up, in Settings › Account press Change… beside Email, type a new email and your password, and press Send link. Open the link in the email and press Confirm email. In a private window, sign in with the new email and your password; the old one no longer works.
6. In a private window, press Sign up under the sign-in form and sign up with another email you can read: "Check your email". Open the email "Confirm your email for nine turtles", type the password you chose, press Confirm email, then Open nine turtles: you're in, and Settings there has no Readers. Open the same link again: it says it has expired or was already used. Back in your Settings, press Readers, then All readers: the new email is in the list, with "0 feeds".
7. Sign out from Settings. Reload: you're back at the sign-in page.
8. Once email is set up (`docs/operations/email.md`): on the sign-in page each field has its name above it. Type your email, press Forgot? (beside Password) and press Email me a link. Open the link in the email and press Sign in: "You're signed in". Choose a new password and press Set password: you're in, and another device you were signed in on asks you to sign in again.
9. In Settings › Readers there is only All readers ›: no email box. Your email is in Settings › Account.
10. Sign out. On the sign-in page press Forgot?, then Email me a link. Open the link in the email, press Sign in, then Not now: you're in, and your password still works. Open the same link again: it says it has expired or was already used.
11. At app.nineturtles.app, add a passkey in Settings › Passkeys (section 18, step 7). Sign out. Press Sign in with a passkey and choose it: you're in, with nothing typed. Sign out again and click into the email field: your passkey is among the suggestions. Your password still signs you in too. At slowfeed.onrender.com the sign-in page has no Sign in with a passkey.
12. Once email is set up, sign up in a private window with the email you already use: "Check your email", as for a new address, and that inbox gets "You already have a nine turtles account" with no link in it. With a Gmail address, "Open Gmail" sits above "Didn't get it?" and opens Gmail in a new tab.
