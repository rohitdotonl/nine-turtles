# 1. Sign-in

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- nine turtles has a public web address, so it asks who you are: an **email and a password** (decision 0087). Each reader has their own.
- **The owner** (the person who set nine turtles up) signs in with their email and password like everyone else; the password alone signs nobody in (decision 0116). Leaving the email empty and pressing Sign in sends nothing: "Type your email too.", and the keyboard moves to the email field. On a brand-new nine turtles the owner has no email yet: their first sign-in, with an email nobody has and the owner's password, signs them in and saves that email as theirs (they can change it later in Settings › Readers).
- Each device stays signed in for a long time, so you rarely type it.
- Each device stays signed in for a year after it was last used. Signing out ends that device only; other devices stay signed in.
- Repeated wrong passwords are slowed down, so nobody can guess by trying thousands: after five wrong tries in a row, nine turtles asks you to wait a minute, and each further wrong try doubles the wait, up to an hour. The right password resets it, and so does an hour without wrong tries. **Wrong tries count for each account on its own** (each email, the owner's email too, so a stranger can't find out which email is the owner's), wherever they come from: a stranger guessing someone's email makes only that email wait, never anyone else. Once an account has to wait, further tries are refused at once, without checking the password. Checking a password takes a lot of memory, so only a few are checked at a time, in one line for everyone (a flood of guesses can slow every sign-in while it lasts, the owner's too, so the time taken never tells which email is the owner's). A password longer than 1,024 bytes is refused at once as a wrong password. Devices already signed in are never affected. Restarting nine turtles on Render clears every wait (`docs/operations/render-setup.md`).
- nine turtles **never says whether an email has an account**: a wrong password and an email nobody has get the same answer, in about the same time.
- The page shows the logo (the nine turtles, decision 0067) above the wordmark, one quiet line under it ("A quiet place to read."), then, in one centred column (decision 0122): **Email** and its field; **Password** and its field, with a small **Forgot?** at the right of the Password name's line while nine turtles can send email; **Sign in**, the one main action, 12px under the password with the same rounded corners; where passkeys work, **Sign in with a passkey** under it as a quieter, edged button; and under those, "New here? **Sign up**". Each field's name is real text above it, on the column's left edge, 6px over the field (never only a placeholder), and the password's name sits 24px under the email field, so each name stays with its field. Forgot? is small to the eye but 44px tall and wide to touch, its box reaching only into the room above the Password name's line. The keyboard goes Email, Password, Forgot?, Sign in, Sign in with a passkey, Sign up. The email field has the keyboard's place when the page opens, and tells the browser it is an email (`autocomplete="email"`; where passkeys are offered, `username webauthn`, which passkey autofill needs) and the password field `current-password`. On a phone the column runs between the same 20px margins as every other screen; on a larger screen it is at most 320px wide (decision 0048).
- Messages are plain: "That email and password don't match." (with an email), "That isn't the password." (without one), or "Too many wrong tries. Try again in 2 minutes." They sit right under the fields, centred, in red words after a small warning mark, said to screen readers as they appear, and move only what's under them down (the room for them is kept at the column's foot, so the fields and the title stay still); a refusal also marks the fields themselves (their edge turns red, and screen readers hear them as invalid).
- Sign out is in Settings.
- Passwords themselves are never stored, only a scrambled version that can't be turned back into them (a "hash"). The owner's is set by typing `nine-turtles set-password` in Render's Shell tab and pasting the scrambled line it prints into Render's settings (`docs/operations/render-setup.md`); until then, the owner can't sign in. A reader's is scrambled the same way when they sign up.

**Signing up** (decisions 0087 and 0115)
- **Sign-up is always open:** anyone can make an account, with no invite and no email sent to confirm it (decision 0115, the owner's choice; it replaced the Closed / Invite link / Open switch and the emailed sign-up link of decisions 0087 and 0088). The sign-in page always shows "New here? **Sign up**" under the form.
- **The sign-up page** (`/sign-up`) is built like the sign-in page (decision 0122): the logo and wordmark, then **Email** (`autocomplete="email"`), **Password** (`new-password`, "At least 12 characters." under it on the field's left edge), each named above its field, then **Sign up** and "Have an account? **Sign in**". Signing up makes the account at once, signs this device in and opens nine turtles: no email is sent and there is no "Check your email" (decision 0115). Signed in already, `/sign-up` opens nine turtles itself.
- Messages: "That doesn't look like an email address." (one @ with something either side, at most 254 characters, and only the address: no name before it and no spaces, `<` `>`, quotes, commas, semicolons, colons, brackets or backslashes), "Choose a password of at least 12 characters.", and "That email already has an account. Sign in instead." (the Sign in under the form takes them there).
- **At most 20 sign-ups are tried in any hour**, for the whole app (nine turtles sits behind Render's proxy, so it can't reliably tell one place from another), as a guard against bots. A try with an email that already has an account counts too, so nobody can keep trying. Sign-ups are handled one at a time. At most 20 accounts are made in any hour too. The 21st hears "Many people have signed up in the last hour. Try again later."
- **Nobody checks the address is really theirs** (the owner accepted this, decision 0115): someone could sign up with an address that isn't theirs. While nine turtles can send email, the address's real owner can take the account over with "Forgot?"; without email, they can't, and neither can a reader who forgets their password. Sign-up works with or without email. The owner should save their own email in Settings › Readers, so nobody else can sign up with it first.

**Forgot? A link that signs you in** (decisions 0096 and 0122)
- While nine turtles can send email, the sign-in page shows **Forgot?** at the right of the Password name's line. It's for every reader, the owner too once they've saved an email. (It replaced the separate "Email me a sign-in link" and "Forgot your password?", decision 0122: one way back in, which also lets you choose a new password.)
- It turns the page into **Email** and its field (the email already typed stays, and the keyboard goes to it), "We'll email you a link that signs you in. You can choose a new password after." under the field, **Email me a link**, and **Back to sign in**, which returns to the sign-in form with the email kept.
- The answer is always the same, said once (decision 0092): the form gives way to **Check your email** (the keyboard goes to it) and "If … has an account here, a link is on its way. It works once, within 15 minutes.", in the text color, whether or not the email has an account. Under it, quietly: "Didn't get it? Look in your spam folder." and **Send again**. Pressed within a minute of the last link it sends nothing and says "The link went less than a minute ago. Give it a minute, then send again if it hasn't come."; after that it says "Another link is on its way." Nothing on the page changes by itself. Then **Back to sign in**, an edged button.
- The email, "Sign in to nine turtles", holds the one link (its ticket after the `#`, so it never reaches a log; the page takes it out of the address bar at once). It opens a page built like sign-in: the logo and wordmark, "Press Sign in to read on this device." and **Sign in** (one press, so a mail program checking links can't use it up). Pressing it signs this device in; the other devices stay signed in and the password stays as it is.
- Then, in the same place: **You're signed in** (the keyboard goes to it), "Choose a new password, if you like.", **New password** (`new-password`, "At least 12 characters. Your other devices will be signed out." under it), **Set password**, and **Not now**, which opens nine turtles with nothing changed. Set password does what a password link does (below): every other device signed in as that reader is signed out, their app password for other feed reader apps is turned off, any link not yet used stops working, and this device stays signed in and opens nine turtles. The choice lasts an hour, only on this page, and only while the account keeps the address the link went to; nine turtles keeps it in memory only, so a restart ends it too. After that, Set password says "That took too long, so your password stays as it is. To choose a new one, sign out and press Forgot? again.", with **Open nine turtles**.
- A link used before, past its 15 minutes, sent to an address the reader has since changed, or from before a new password, says "This link has expired or was already used. Ask for a new one from the sign-in page.", with **Sign in**.
- Without email (the settings aren't set), Forgot? isn't shown, as the old links weren't; asking anyway says "This nine turtles can't send email yet. Ask whoever runs it to set a new password for you."

**A passkey** (decision 0109)
- A passkey signs you in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN instead of a password. You add one in Settings › Passkeys (section 18); passwords keep working exactly as before, for every reader.
- Where passkeys work, the sign-in page shows **Sign in with a passkey** under Sign in, a quieter button edged in the muted grey and not filled, the column's full width and 44px tall (decision 0122). It opens the browser's own passkey sheet; choosing your passkey signs this device in and opens nine turtles, with no email or password typed. The email field also offers your passkeys among its suggestions, as browsers do for saved passwords (passkey autofill), where the browser can.
- Cancelling the browser's sheet says nothing. A passkey nine turtles doesn't know (one removed in Settings, or made for another nine turtles) says "That passkey isn't one this nine turtles knows. Sign in with your password."; a passkey chosen after the page's challenge ran out (five minutes) says "That took too long. Try again."
- Passkeys belong to nine turtles' public address (app.nineturtles.app). Anywhere else (slowfeed.onrender.com), and in a browser without passkeys, nothing about them is shown and the page is as before.
- Signing in with a passkey is exactly like signing in with a password afterwards: the device stays signed in for a year after it was last used. A passkey can't be guessed, so a refused one makes nobody wait. No email is sent.

**A new password** (decisions 0088 and 0122)
- A new password is chosen after Forgot?'s link (above). The older password link, "A new password for nine turtles", is no longer offered on the sign-in page, but one already sent keeps working for its hour (and its route stays, for a page left open from before).
- That older link (its ticket after the `#`, so it never reaches a log; the page takes it out of the address bar at once) opens a page built like sign-in: **New password** ("At least 12 characters. Your other devices will be signed out." under it) and **Set password**. It works once, within the hour, and only while the account still has the address it was sent to (as with a sign-in link). Setting it signs out every other device signed in as that reader, turns off their app password for other feed reader apps (Settings › Other apps can make a new one), and signs this device in. Passkeys stay (the owner's choice, decision 0109): if you fear someone else got in, remove any passkey you don't recognise in Settings › Passkeys. A link that has run out says "This link has expired or was already used. Ask for a new one from the sign-in page.", with **Sign in**.
- The owner can use it once they've saved an email; the new password then replaces the one in Render's settings, with or without the email, for as long as that setting stays as it is. **The owner's way back that needs no email:** putting a new `NINE_TURTLES_PASSWORD_HASH` in Render makes it the owner's password again when nine turtles restarts; the one set by email stops working, every device signed in as the owner is signed out, and any password or sign-in link not yet used stops working (`docs/operations/owner-password.md`). Passkeys stay; remove any you don't recognise in Settings › Passkeys.
- Forgot? takes as long whether or not the email has an account.
- **At most three emails go to one address in any hour**, whatever they're for, **and one link of each kind (password, sign-in, change of email) a minute**, so pressing Email me a link twice sends one email. Past either, the answer stays the same and nothing is sent, so nine turtles can't fill anyone's inbox. Limits count each address wherever the requests come from: nine turtles sits behind Render's proxy, so it can't reliably tell one place from another.
- A new password also ends any sign-in link not yet used, and any other offer to choose one.
- Emails are short plain text, signed "nine turtles": no pictures, no tracking, and no link but the one asked for.

**How to check it**
1. Open the app's address in a private browser window. You see only the email and password fields, nothing about your feeds.
2. Type your email and a wrong password several times quickly. After a few tries it asks you to wait before trying again. Leave the email empty and type your password: "Type your email too."
3. In another private window, type a made-up email and a wrong password: "That email and password don't match." Do it six times: only that email has to wait; the owner can still sign in in the first window.
4. In another private window, type your email and the right password. You're in. Close the browser, reopen the address: you're still signed in.
5. In Settings › Readers, change your email. In a private window, sign in with the new email and your password; the old one no longer works.
6. In a private window, press Sign up under the sign-in form and sign up with another email: you're in, and Settings there has no Readers. Back in your Settings, press Readers, then All readers: the new email is in the list, with "0 feeds".
7. Sign out from Settings. Reload: you're back at the sign-in page.
8. Once email is set up (`docs/operations/email.md`): on the sign-in page each field has its name above it. Type your email, press Forgot? (beside Password) and press Email me a link. Open the link in the email and press Sign in: "You're signed in". Choose a new password and press Set password: you're in, and another device you were signed in on asks you to sign in again.
9. In Settings › Readers, type a different email in Your email and press Save without your password: it asks for it, and your email stays as it was.
10. Sign out. On the sign-in page press Forgot?, then Email me a link. Open the link in the email, press Sign in, then Not now: you're in, and your password still works. Open the same link again: it says it has expired or was already used.
11. At app.nineturtles.app, add a passkey in Settings › Passkeys (section 18, step 7). Sign out. Press Sign in with a passkey and choose it: you're in, with nothing typed. Sign out again and click into the email field: your passkey is among the suggestions. Your password still signs you in too. At slowfeed.onrender.com the sign-in page has no Sign in with a passkey.
