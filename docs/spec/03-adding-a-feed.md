# 3. Adding a feed

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- **Add feed** (a plus icon and its words) sits at the very bottom of the sidebar, always in the same place, with two buttons beside it: **Keys** (a keyboard icon and its word, so Add feed and Keys read as two buttons, not one phrase; decisions 0062, 0064; in a sidebar narrowed below 15rem, the icon alone) and **Settings** (a cog), each with its name as a tooltip. The `a` key does the same as Add feed.
- Add feed opens a sheet over the list you were on (`/add-feed`): a small card titled "Add a feed" (in the same serif as every title), a line saying nine turtles finds the feed on any website, the same field as the first visit at the card's full width, and at the foot one row, 20px under the field: **Cancel** (a faint tone, no edge), then **Add feed** at the right end. The sheet opens a little above the middle of the window and is as tall as what's in it; a message appears under the field only when there is one, and the sheet grows down by its line (its top never moves). In the first-visit welcome, which is centred on the page, the field keeps room for two lines of message so nothing moves. The list and article stay in view behind it under a light dim. Cancel, Escape, or a press on the dimmed page, closes it. Add feed is an action, not a place, so it never shows the "where you are" pill. On a phone it's an iOS page sheet, built as every phone sheet is (DESIGN.md → "One phone sheet"; decisions 0052, 0055): a 10px strip of the dimmed screen shows above it, its top corners are 12px, its ground is grouped grey (the list's tone by day, the page by night) with any group on it a card (the page's color by day, a step lighter by night), its title a step smaller (20px) in the top line, an X beside the title, no back arrow and no Cancel, and **Add feed** the full width at the screen's foot. It appears at once, with no slide. When a site offers several feeds, "This site offers more than one feed. Choose one:" is set as the list's label, 20px under the field. A YouTube video's or playlist's address asks about its channel the same way ("This video is by … Follow …?", section 38).
- You can paste either a feed address or an ordinary website address; leaving off `https://` is fine. For a website, nine turtles looks at the page you pasted for the feeds it names ("autodiscovery"), once, because you asked. **If the page names none, it tries a few more places** (decision 0165), one at a time, stopping at the first that works: the usual feed address under the page you pasted, if it's a page inside a site (`medium.com/@someone/feed`), so a blog inside a bigger site finds its own feed first; then the site's home page and the feeds it names; then the usual addresses at the top of the site (`/feed`, `/feed.xml`, `/rss`, `/rss.xml`, `/index.xml`, `/atom.xml`, Wix's `/blog-feed.xml`, Blogger's `/feeds/posts/default`). At most ten addresses besides the page, never two at once, never in the background and never an article's page; a site that asks to slow down or stops answering isn't asked again.
- **When a site offers several feeds** (decision 0165), nine turtles looks inside each one, once, one after another (at most ten, and for at most 12 seconds in all), and lists them, each row ending in a round mark that's filled with a dot on the chosen one (the first, to begin with). Each row says what's inside: the feed's name; its newest title, in the reading serif and the second ink; then, small and muted, how often it posts, in the words of Edit feed ("Posts about twice a week", decision 0105), and its address as a person says it (`maralind.example/feed`, no `https://` or `www.`). A feed that isn't there or isn't a feed is left out, and so is one whose newest posts are the same as a feed above it (the same posts in another format, say RSS and Atom). If only one feed is left, it's followed straight away, without asking. A feed not reached in time is still listed, by its name and address. Choose one with a click, a tap or the arrow keys, then press **Add feed** to add it: the feed just looked at is used, so the publisher isn't asked again. In a sheet a long list scrolls inside itself, so Cancel and Add feed stay in view; on a phone the page scrolls. Change the address and the list goes (it belonged to the old one), and Add feed looks again.
- **A feed made by someone else's service** (RSS Bridge, OpenRSS, a newsletter-to-feed tool) is followed like any other feed address (decision 0160). nine turtles itself never builds a feed from a web page; what such a service does is between it and the publisher.
- While nine turtles looks, the field says "Looking for a feed…" in plain text. No spinner. Closing Add a feed meanwhile (Cancel or Escape) leaves you where you went: a feed found after that still joins the sidebar, but doesn't open.
- If it doesn't work, a plain sentence says why, never an error code: "This site doesn't seem to offer a feed, on this page or in the usual places. If you know the feed's own address, paste that.", "That site didn't answer. Try again later.", "Nothing was found at that address.", "That doesn't look like a web address.", "nine turtles can't visit that address." (an address inside a private network or on nine turtles' own computer), "That file is too big to be a feed.", or, for a reader who already follows 1,000 feeds, "You follow 1,000 feeds, as many as one reader can. Unsubscribe from one to add another." (decision 0090; nothing is fetched then). The message sits under the field in oxblood words after a small warning mark; when the address itself is the problem (not the site, the connection or the reader's plan), the field's edge turns oxblood too (the words and mark mean color is never the only sign).
- Adding a feed you already follow says "You already follow this feed." with a link to it, instead of making a copy. The same address written differently (`http` or `https`, a trailing `/` or `#part`) counts as the same feed.
- The new feed appears in the sidebar and opens, its articles straight away, all unread (including older posts the feed still lists). It's fetched once on adding; new articles arriving on their own come with Milestone 8.
- Each feed has its own address (`/feeds/3`), so reloading keeps your place. An address for a feed you don't follow says so plainly ("Feed not found").
- **Adding from anywhere: "Follow this person?"** (decisions 0104 and 0138): `/add-feed?url=<address>` opens one question instead of Add a feed's field. The sheet's title (on a phone, the page's) is **"Follow <name>?"**, with the name the person's feed gives itself (usually theirs or their site's), and "Follow this person?" until it's known; under it, their site as a person says it ("jane.example"), then "Lately" (small, in the second ink) over the titles of their three newest pieces in the reading serif, as plain text, not links; and one row at the foot: **Not now** (a faint tone, no edge), then **Follow** at the right end. On a phone there's no Not now (the X beside the title leaves) and Follow is the full width at the screen's foot. The screen never says "feed" or "RSS". The keyboard starts on the title, so a screen reader hears the question first. nine turtles looks at that one page straight away for what it names, but **never follows anything by itself**: only **Follow** follows, and it follows the site's own feed (the first one the page names, without asking which: one question, one press). Not now, Escape or the X follows nothing. While it looks, a line says "Looking…" (no spinner) and Follow can't be pressed yet; once the name is known, screen readers hear the question again with it (the keyboard reached the title before the name arrived). The browser tab says the question too. Problems are said in plain words without "feed" ("This site doesn't offer a way to follow it from here.", "That site didn't answer. Try again later.", …); Follow stays locked after one that trying again can't fix, and can be pressed again after one that asks to try again (no answer, no connection, a passing trouble). Pressing Follow for someone already followed takes you to them. When the look can't be made (busy, or out of reach) nothing is said, and Follow still works through the ordinary add. After following, their page opens, as after Add feed. Nothing is saved as a link, and there is no read-later. If the address has no `url`, the first web address (`http` or `https`) inside `text`, then `title`, is used, because share menus often put the link inside a sentence. The address bar then shows plain `/add-feed`, and the shared address is used for that one opening only: Add a feed opened later starts empty. Signed out, the sign-in page comes first; after signing in, the question opens with the address kept. The look uses the same lookup as the website's feed finder (section 30). Three ways lead there:
  - **Android (and any browser that supports it):** once nine turtles is on the home screen, it appears in the system share menu ("Share → nine turtles"), through the app's "share target" (the part of its manifest that tells the phone it can take shared links).
  - **iPhone and iPad:** Safari doesn't offer home-screen apps in the share menu, so an Apple Shortcuts shortcut does it instead (steps in section 20).
  - **A computer's browser:** a bookmark that opens Add a feed with the page you're on (a "bookmarklet": a bookmark that runs a line of JavaScript). Settings › Add from anywhere shows it, built from this nine turtles' own address, with a Copy button: make a new bookmark and paste it as the bookmark's address. On app.nineturtles.app it is `javascript:location.href='https://app.nineturtles.app/add-feed?url='+encodeURIComponent(location.href)`.

**How to check it**
1. Press `a` (or click Add feed), paste a blog's home page address (not its feed), press Enter. The blog appears in the sidebar with its articles.
2. Paste a direct feed address. Same result.
3. Paste an address of a page with no feed. You get a calm, plain message, not an error code.
4. Paste a blog's page that doesn't name its feed but has one at `/feed` (many WordPress sites inside bigger sites do). The blog is followed.
5. Paste a site that offers several feeds (a WordPress blog's home page usually offers its posts and its comments). Each row shows its newest title and how often it posts; the comments feed reads as comments.
6. Add the same feed twice. nine turtles tells you it's already there instead of creating a duplicate.
7. Open `/add-feed?url=` followed by a blog's address. A sheet asks "Follow <the blog's name>?" with the site under it, but the blog isn't in the sidebar until you press Follow. Press Not now instead: nothing is added.
8. In Settings › Add from anywhere, copy the bookmark, make a bookmark with it, and press it on a blog: "Follow <the blog's name>?" opens for that blog.
