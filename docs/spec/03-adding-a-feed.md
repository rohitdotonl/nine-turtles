# 3. Adding a feed

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- **+ Add** (a plus icon and the word Add; screen readers and its tooltip say "Add a person or publication", decision 0163) sits at the very bottom of the sidebar, always in the same place, with two buttons beside it: **Keys** (a keyboard icon and its word, so Add and Keys read as two buttons, not one phrase; decisions 0062, 0064; in a sidebar narrowed below 15rem, the icon alone) and **Settings** (a cog), each with its name as a tooltip. The `a` key does the same as + Add.
- + Add opens a sheet over the list you were on (`/add`; the old `/add-feed` still works): a small card titled "Add a person or publication" (in the same serif as every title), the same field as the first visit at the card's full width, labelled "Their website's address" (the owner's core path, point 2), and at the foot one row, 20px under the field: **Cancel** (a faint tone, no edge), then **Add** at the right end. The sheet opens a little above the middle of the window and is as tall as what's in it; a message appears under the field only when there is one, and the sheet grows down by its line (its top never moves). In the first-visit welcome, which is centred on the page, the field keeps room for two lines of message so nothing moves. The list and article stay in view behind it under a light dim. Cancel, Escape, or a press on the dimmed page, closes it; an address typed and not added waits in the field for next time, except after Cancel, which clears it (on a phone, which has no Cancel, the reader clears the field; decision 0235). Adding is an action, not a place, so it never shows the "where you are" pill. On a phone it's an iOS page sheet, built as every phone sheet is (DESIGN.md → "One phone sheet"; decisions 0052, 0055): a 10px strip of the dimmed screen shows above it, its top corners are 12px, its ground is grouped grey (the tone one step off the page by day, the page by night) with any group on it a card (the page's color by day, a step lighter by night), its title a step smaller (20px) in the top line, an X beside the title, no back arrow and no Cancel, and **Add** the full width at the screen's foot. It appears at once, with no slide. Only where nine turtles is installed on the phone, one quiet line sits under the field: "Reading them in another app? Share the page to nine turtles." (12px, in the faintest grey that keeps 4.5 : 1; decision 0206). When a site offers several feeds, "This site offers more than one feed. Choose which to follow:" is set as the list's label, 20px under the field. A YouTube video's or playlist's address asks about its channel the same way ("Follow …?", section 38). Adding is cut short after 60 seconds, before anything in front of nine turtles gives up on it, and then says "The site didn't answer. Try again later." (decision 0213).
- You can paste either a feed address or an ordinary website address; leaving off `https://` is fine. For a website, nine turtles looks at the page you pasted for the feeds it names ("autodiscovery"), once, because you asked. **If the page names none, it tries a few more places** (decision 0165), one at a time, stopping at the first that works: the usual feed address under the page you pasted, if it's a page inside a site (`medium.com/@someone/feed`), so a blog inside a bigger site finds its own feed first; then the site's home page and the feeds it names; then the usual addresses at the top of the site (`/feed`, `/feed.xml`, `/rss`, `/rss.xml`, `/index.xml`, `/atom.xml`, Wix's `/blog-feed.xml`, Blogger's `/feeds/posts/default`). At most ten addresses besides the page, never two at once, never in the background and never an article's page; a site that asks to slow down or stops answering isn't asked again.
- **When a site offers several feeds** (decision 0165), nine turtles looks inside each one, once, one after another (at most ten, and for at most 12 seconds in all), and lists them, each row ending in a tick box, the first ticked (decision 0206). Each row says what's inside: the feed's name; its newest title, in the reading serif and the second ink; then, small and muted, its address as a person says it (`maralind.example/feed`, no `https://` or `www.`), on one line that ends in "…" when it's too long, never cut off or broken mid-word (decision 0191). A feed that isn't there or isn't a feed is left out, and so is one whose newest posts are the same as a feed above it (the same posts in another format, say RSS and Atom). If only one feed is left, it's followed straight away, without asking. A feed not reached in time is still listed, by its name and address. How often a feed posts is never said (decision 0206). Tick any number with a click, a tap or Space, then press **Follow**: each ticked feed is followed in turn ("Following… 1 of 2 done", no spinner), from the copy just looked at, so the publisher isn't asked again. Two or more followed together become **one person** (section 36; a blog and its podcast, say), named as the first, and their list opens with nothing more asked; one followed asks "… is…" as a single feed does. Nothing ticked says "Tick at least one first."; feeds that couldn't be followed while others were are named in the list's line ("Comments couldn't be followed: …"); if none could, the problem is said and the list stays. In a sheet the foot's Add reads **Follow** while feeds are offered, and a long list scrolls inside itself, so Cancel and Follow stay in view; on a phone the page scrolls. In the welcome the field's own Add steps aside while feeds are offered, and **Cancel** (which puts them away, the keyboard back in the field) and **Follow** sit at the list's foot, 20px under it, at their usual size; on a phone, Follow alone, the list's full width. In the welcome, the list, and what was found and the two tiles after following, are grouped cards a tone off the page, on the field's own edges, the same on a phone, a medium window and a wide one (the list's place is on the page itself, decision 0221). A YouTube playlist's question (section 38) stays a choice of one, each row ending in a round mark; a video's has one row, with no mark, which Follow's description reads out. Change the address and the list goes (it belonged to the old one), and Add looks again.
- **A feed made by someone else's service** (RSS Bridge, OpenRSS, a newsletter-to-feed tool) is followed like any other feed address (decision 0160). nine turtles itself never builds a feed from a web page; what such a service does is between it and the publisher.
- While nine turtles looks, the field says "Looking for a feed…" in plain text. No spinner. Closing the sheet meanwhile (Cancel or Escape) leaves you where you went: a feed found after that still joins the sidebar, but doesn't open.
- If it doesn't work, a plain sentence says why, never an error code: "nine turtles couldn't find a way to follow this site. You could write to them and ask if they have a feed or a newsletter." (the owner's core path, point 3), "That site didn't answer. Try again later.", "Nothing was found at that address.", "That doesn't look like a web address. An address looks like example.com.", "nine turtles can't visit that address." (an address inside a private network or on nine turtles' own computer), "That file is too big to be a feed.", or, for a reader who already follows 1,000 feeds, "You follow 1,000 feeds, as many as one reader can. Unsubscribe from one to add another." (decision 0090; nothing is fetched then). The message sits under the field in oxblood words after a small warning mark; when the address itself is the problem (not the site, the connection or the reader's plan), the field's edge turns oxblood too (the words and mark mean color is never the only sign).
- **A mistyped address gets its one obvious repair** (decision 0234), worked out on the device and never by visiting anything. Pressing Add with an address that has one says so in the same oxblood line, the field marked and kept exactly as typed, and nothing is looked up: "That doesn't look like a web address. Did you mean gardenletters.blog, with a dot?" (a comma, semicolon or space where the dot goes), "… Did you mean https://gardenletters.blog?" (a mistyped start: `htps://`, `https;//`, `http//`, `https:/`), or the same for doubled dots, a dot or comma left at the end, quotes or brackets brought along by a paste, and endings no site has (`.con`, `.cmo`, `.ocm`, `.comm`, `.cpm` → `.com`; `.ogr`, `.prg` → `.org`; `.nte` → `.net`; real endings such as `.co` are left alone). An email address pasted in says "That's an email address. Did you mean their website, gardenletters.blog?" (not for a Gmail, Outlook, iCloud, Yahoo or Proton address, whose site isn't theirs). Under it, one everyday edged button, **Use gardenletters.blog**, puts the repair in the field and looks it up. Nothing is ever changed by itself: typing in the field takes the offer away, and pressing Add again with the same address looks it up as typed. An address nine turtles can't read and can't repair says "That doesn't look like a web address. An address looks like example.com."
- **Ask them for a feed** (decision 0212): under "nine turtles couldn't find a way to follow this site…", always, a small heading, "Ask them for a feed", the line "Many sites can add one in minutes, once someone asks." and an everyday bordered button with the paper plane, **Write to june.example** (the site's name, without "www."). Pressing it opens a box in place, as Write to does on a person's page (section 37): "Looking on their site…" while nine turtles reads the site's home page and, only if that shows no address, the one contact or about page it links to (under the feed finder's limits, and only a few times in half an hour for one reader; past that, the box asks as when the site couldn't be checked); what it finds fills "Their email address" ("Found on their site. Check it, then press Write."), else "Find it on their site" (the page given, in a new tab) and the field to paste it in. **Write**, an everyday bordered button (Add stays the sheet's one solid button), opens the reader's own email app to that address with the subject "A feed for june.example?" and a short request they can change; **Not now** closes the box. nine turtles keeps nothing. Pasting or adding again clears it.
- **A site with no feed may be a newsletter that only comes by email** (decision 0198): under "nine turtles couldn't find a way to follow this site…", while nine turtles takes newsletters (section 28), a small heading, "If it's a newsletter", the line "Sign up with your own nine turtles address, and each issue arrives here instead of your inbox.", the reader's own address in a field that can't be typed in (selected when focused) with **Copy** beside it where Add is, and **"Open their site to sign up"**, a link to the address they pasted, in a new tab. Pasting or adding again clears it.
- Adding a feed you already follow says "You already follow this." with a link to it, instead of making a copy. The same address written differently (`http` or `https`, a trailing `/` or `#part`) counts as the same feed.
- **"Maya Chen is…"** (the owner's core path, point 1, decision 0183). Once someone is found, the address field goes and the sheet's title becomes **"Maya Chen is…"**: their name, or the blog's title when the feed gives no name. Under it, what was found, in a quiet grey box: their site as a person says it ("mayachen.example"), and their newest title in the reading serif. Then two tiles side by side, each with an icon, its name in semibold and its meaning under it: **A person** (the person icon): "Someone you might write back to."; **A publication** (the newspaper icon): "Anything you only read or watch, and won't write back to." nine turtles' guess is chosen to start (section 36) and the keyboard is on it; the arrow keys change it. The chosen tile has a 2px ink edge, so it shows by its shape, never by a tint alone. Then "You can change this later on their page." and **Done**. In the welcome the question has its own heading in the same words, with Done under it; on a phone, the page's title says it and Done is the full width at the foot. The name in the question is the person's own when the feed signs every article with one author's name (section 36); answering A publication names it by the feed's own title instead.
- The new feed appears in the sidebar and opens, its articles straight away. **Only its newest five arrive unread** (decision 0194): everything older the feed still lists is in its own list, already read, so following someone who posts a lot never floods Unread. Everything they post afterwards arrives unread, however much. It's fetched once on adding; after that, new articles arrive on their own (section 11). The same goes however it's followed: + Add, the welcome's starters, Follow from anywhere, + Add their newsletter, videos or podcast, an assistant, and an imported file (section 16).
- Each person and publication has their own address (`/people/3` or `/publications/3`, section 36), so reloading keeps your place. An address for someone you don't follow says so plainly.
- **Adding from anywhere: "Follow this person?"** (decisions 0104, 0138 and 0177): `/add-feed?url=<address>` (or `/add?url=…`) opens one question instead of the Add sheet's field. The sheet's title (on a phone, the page's) is **"Follow <name>?"**, with the name the person's feed gives itself (usually theirs or their site's), and "Follow this person?" until it's known; under it, their site as a person says it ("jane.example"), then "Lately" (small, in the second ink) over the titles of their three newest pieces in the reading serif, as plain text, not links; and one row at the foot: **Not now** (a faint tone, no edge), then **Follow** at the right end. On a phone there's no Not now (the X beside the title leaves) and Follow is the full width at the screen's foot. The screen never says "feed" or "RSS". The keyboard starts on the title, so a screen reader hears the question first. nine turtles looks at that one page straight away for what it names, but **never follows anything by itself**: only **Follow** follows, and it follows the site's own feed (the first one the page names, without asking which: one question, one press). Not now, Escape or the X follows nothing. While it looks, a line says "Looking…" (no spinner) and Follow can't be pressed yet; once the name is known, screen readers hear the question again with it (the keyboard reached the title before the name arrived). The browser tab says the question too. Problems are said in plain words without "feed" ("This site doesn't offer a way to follow it from here.", "That site didn't answer. Try again later.", …); Follow stays locked after one that trying again can't fix, and can be pressed again after one that asks to try again (no answer, no connection, a passing trouble). Pressing Follow for someone already followed takes you to them. When the look can't be made (busy, or out of reach) nothing is said, and Follow still works through the ordinary add. After following, their list opens, as after Add, and its note says so: "You're following Maya Chen." with **Undo**, which unfollows them and goes back to the home list ("You're no longer following Maya Chen."; decision 0230). The same note follows Add and the welcome. Nothing is saved as a link, and there is no read-later. If the address has no `url`, the first web address (`http` or `https`) inside `text`, then `title`, is used, because share menus often put the link inside a sentence. The address bar then shows plain `/add`, and the shared address is used for that one opening only: + Add opened later starts empty. Signed out, the sign-in page comes first; after signing in, the question opens with the address kept. The look uses the same lookup as the website's feed finder (section 30). Three ways lead there:
  - **Android (and any browser that supports it):** once nine turtles is on the home screen, it appears in the system share menu ("Share → nine turtles"), through the app's "share target" (the part of its manifest that tells the phone it can take shared links).
  - **iPhone and iPad:** Safari doesn't offer home-screen apps in the share menu, so a ready-made Apple Shortcut, **Add to nine turtles**, does it instead (section 20).
  - **A computer's browser:** a bookmark that opens the question with the page you're on (a "bookmarklet": a bookmark that runs a line of JavaScript). Settings › Install the app on any device shows it, on its Computer tab, as a button, **Add to nine turtles**, built from this nine turtles' own address, to drag to the bookmarks bar (decision 0190: the raw bookmark and its Copy are gone; without a bookmarks bar, + Add takes any address). On app.nineturtles.app it is `javascript:void(location.href='https://app.nineturtles.app/add?url='+encodeURIComponent(location.href))`.

- **Dropping a link** (decision 0248): on a computer, a page's address dragged from another browser tab or app and let go anywhere on nine turtles, or a link dragged out of an article and let go on the sidebar, opens the same "Follow <name>?" question, for that address. While it's held, a line at the top of the sidebar's people and folders, on a faint tile, says "Drop to follow this site. Onto a folder to file them there.", and the folder under the pointer takes the drop tone (section 5). Let go on a folder, the question says "They'll go in Cooking." under the site, and once followed they're in that folder. Only the dropped page is looked at, as if it had been pasted into + Add (decision 0016); nothing is followed until Follow. A link nudged and let go inside the article opens nothing; nine turtles' own links (a list's rows), an address that isn't a web page's, a drop into a text field and a drop while a sheet is open are left alone. If they can't be put in the folder, they're followed all the same and the note after "You're following …" says so.

**How to check it**
1. Press `a` (or click + Add), paste a blog's home page address (not its feed), press Enter. The field gives way to "… is…" with their site, their newest title and two tiles, a guess chosen; press Done. The blog appears in the sidebar, under People or Publications, with its articles.
2. Paste a direct feed address. Same result.
3. Paste an address of a page with no feed. You get a calm, plain message: "nine turtles couldn't find a way to follow this site. You could write to them and ask if they have a feed or a newsletter." Under it, "Ask them for a feed" with Write to and the site's name: press it, and the address on their site (or its contact page) fills the field; press Write and your email app opens with a short request.
4. Paste a blog's page that doesn't name its feed but has one at `/feed` (many WordPress sites inside bigger sites do). The blog is followed.
5. Paste a site that offers several feeds (a WordPress blog's home page usually offers its posts and its comments). Each row shows its newest title and address, never how often it posts; the comments feed reads as comments. The first is ticked. Tick the second too and press Follow: one person appears in the sidebar with both, and their list opens. In the welcome on a computer, the heading doesn't move as the list appears.
6. Add the same feed twice. nine turtles tells you it's already there instead of creating a duplicate.
7. Open `/add-feed?url=` followed by a blog's address. A sheet asks "Follow <the blog's name>?" with the site under it, but the blog isn't in the sidebar until you press Follow. Press Not now instead: nothing is added.
8. In Settings › Install the app on any device, on the Computer tab, drag **Add to nine turtles** to the bookmarks bar and press it on a blog: "Follow <the blog's name>?" opens for that blog. Pressing the button inside Settings only says to drag it.
9. On a computer, drag a link out of an article onto a folder in the sidebar: "Drop to follow this site." shows while it's held; let go and "Follow …?" opens, saying "They'll go in …". Press Follow: they're in that folder. Drop a link anywhere else and press Not now: nothing is followed.
