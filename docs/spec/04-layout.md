# 4. Layout

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- **One thing at a time** (decision 0275): three areas, **sidebar | list | page**, and on a computer as on a phone (section 20) you read one thing at a time. On a computer (a wide or medium window), with nothing open, the sidebar (always shown, on a medium window too, decision 0282) and the list alone in the rest of the window, the list's title, search and titles in **a column as wide as the article's** (decision 0277; the reading column, as wide as the reader's Aa makes it), **centred in the area beside the sidebar** so the page is balanced (decision 0278). Where the sidebar leaves too little room for the column, it keeps the list's own 32px edge. Its scroll bar stays at the window's edge. Settings, Everyone you follow and the pages under Settings centre their own column there too (section 18). An opened article fills the whole window, the sidebar and the list away, with **‹ Unread** (the list's own name; screen readers hear "Back to Unread") at the start of its bar and its tools at the end, standing over the text: the way back on the text's left edge, the last tool's icon on its right edge (decision 0277). It, Escape and the browser's Back go back to the list as it was, with the keyboard on that article's row; opening is a step in the browser's history, and reloading at an article's address opens it alone. j and k (and the arrows) move to the next and previous article while reading. A person's or publication's page and their settings fill the whole window the same way, with their × back to their list (section 36). Settings, Everyone you follow and Add show beside the sidebar, as the list does. There is no setting for any of this.
- No layout options to choose: the layout simply adapts to the window width. Wide means 1200 pixels or more at normal size, medium 896 or more; below that, one area shows at a time, as on a phone. On a phone-narrow screen one area shows at a time, starting with the sidebar; choosing a place in it shows that place, and Escape goes back.
- Zooming in or a larger system text size counts as a narrower window, so the layout steps down instead of anything overlapping.
- On a computer the sidebar can't be hidden, and has no button to hide it (decision 0282): with one thing at a time, it already steps away whenever an article or a person's page opens, and comes back with the list.
- On a computer the sidebar can be made wider or narrower by dragging the line where it meets the list (or, with the keyboard on that line, the left and right arrows); a double click puts it back to its usual width. It stays 200–400px wide, and the width is remembered on that device. The sidebar's and the list's top lines (the wordmark, the list's title) never scroll; an article's tools start its first line and scroll away with its title (decision 0263). All the top lines are one band of the same height (56px at normal text size), so the wordmark, the list's title and More sit on one line across the window, and the faint lines that appear under them once something scrolls fall at the same height. A button in a top line draws its keyboard ring just inside its own edge, so the ring never touches the top of the window.
- Unread, Starred, Notes, All articles, Everyone you follow and Settings each have their own web address (`/unread`, `/starred`, `/notes`, `/all`, `/following`, `/settings`), so reloading keeps your place and the browser's Back button works; an open article's address sits under its list's (`/unread/mira-chen/a-slow-letter-29`, section 6, decision 0233). A person or publication is at their name and number (`/jim-nielsen-54`) and a folder at `/folders/friends-4` (decision 0281); only the number is read. The browser tab names where you are ("Unread · nine turtles"), and, while an article is open, the article ("The pleasure of a slow letter · nine turtles"; an untitled post's opening words), so the tab, the window and the browser's History say what's being read (decision 0231). The front page opens Unread. Any other address opens **"Wrong turn?"** in place of the app (decision 0102; DESIGN.md → "Name and signature"): a small "404 error", eight turtles walking one way and one turned the other, "There's nothing at this address. It may have moved, or the link may have a typing mistake in it.", "If a link in this app brought you here, tell me which one and I'll fix it." signed "— Rohit", and **Back to your reading**, which opens the front page. "Tell me which one" opens the reader's email app with the address already written in. The tab says "Page not found". A well-formed address for a person or publication or folder you don't have still opens the list with "Not found" or "Folder not found" (sections 3 and 36), also for a person's page (and the old Edit and source-settings addresses, which open it). Signed out, such an address shows the sign-in page first, then "Wrong turn?" once you sign in. The website's own 404 page is the same, with "on this site" and no button.
- Regions are told apart by background tone and space, never by lines, shadows or gradients: the sidebar is toned, and the list and the article share the page's own color (decision 0221). Small exceptions: a faint line under a top line once something has scrolled under it, a soft shadow under the note that floats at the bottom of the list, a faint line along the top of the phone's article toolbar, and, on a computer, a faint 1px line where the sidebar meets the list, in the same tone as the hairlines between list rows (DESIGN.md → Visual language; decision 0054). The article's bars are solid, never see-through.
- Nothing animates. Views switch instantly.

**How to check it**
1. Make the browser window wide, then narrower, then phone-narrow. On a computer the sidebar and the list show; on a phone, one screen at a time. On a wide window, the list sits in the middle of the space beside the sidebar. Open an article: ‹ Unread sits above its title and More above its right edge. Open Settings: its column is centred beside the sidebar too.
2. Open an article on a computer: it alone fills the window, with "‹" and the list's name at its top left. Press Escape (or that button, or the browser's Back): the list is back as it was, the keyboard on that article's row. Open it again and reload: it opens alone. Press `f`: nothing happens.
3. On a computer, look for a button to hide the sidebar: there is none, and the sidebar shows whenever a list does.
4. Drag the line between the sidebar and the list, then double-click it. The sidebar widens, then returns to its usual width.
5. Squint at the screen: you can still tell the sidebar from the list, with a faint line where they meet.
6. Open `/nowhere`. "Wrong turn?" shows, with "404 error" and the turtles; Back to your reading opens Unread.
7. Open an article: the browser tab says its title, then "· nine turtles". Go back to the list (or, on a computer, to Settings): the tab says the place again.
