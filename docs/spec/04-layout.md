# 4. Layout

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- Three panes on a wide screen: **feeds | items | article**. Two panes on a medium screen. One column on a phone (section 20).
- No layout options to choose: the layout simply adapts to the window width. Wide means 1200 pixels or more at normal size, medium 896 or more; below that, one area shows at a time, as on a phone. On a medium screen the list and article show, and showing the sidebar puts it in the article's place. On a phone-narrow screen one area shows at a time, starting with the sidebar; choosing a place in it shows that place, and Escape goes back.
- Zooming in or a larger system text size counts as a narrower window, so the layout steps down instead of anything overlapping.
- On a computer the sidebar can be hidden and shown with one small sidebar-shaped button, at the right end of its top line across from the wordmark; with the sidebar hidden it waits at the start of the next area's top line. The list has no button of its own (decision 0241, which took away a second button drawn the same): **full-screen reading**, `f` or "Full-screen reading" in an article's More menu, hides the sidebar and the list together, and then the one button, "Sidebar and article list", brings both back. Screen readers hear "Sidebar"; the tooltips say what pressing does, with the key.
- On a computer the sidebar and the list can be made wider or narrower by dragging the line where they meet the next pane (or, with the keyboard on that line, the left and right arrows); a double click puts the pane back to its usual width. The sidebar stays 200–400px wide and the list never leaves the article less than 400px. Beside the article, the list is 352px wide by default below 1280px (so on a window about 1024px wide feed names and titles have room), 384px up to 1536px and 416px beyond, and it's never narrower than 280px, however it's dragged. The widths are remembered on that device. Beside it, the sidebar's and the list's top lines (the wordmark, the list's title) never scroll; the article's tools start on the same line and scroll away with its title (decision 0263). All the top lines are one band of the same height (56px at normal text size), so the sidebar button, the wordmark, the list's title and More, and the article's tools sit on one line across the window, and the faint lines that appear under them once something scrolls fall at the same height. A button in a top line draws its keyboard ring just inside its own edge, so the ring never touches the top of the window.
- Unread, Starred, Notes, All articles, Everyone you follow and Settings each have their own web address (`/unread`, `/starred`, `/notes`, `/all`, `/following`, `/settings`), so reloading keeps your place and the browser's Back button works; an open article's address sits under its list's (`/unread/articles/29`, section 6, decision 0233). The browser tab names where you are ("Unread · nine turtles"), and, while an article is open, the article ("The pleasure of a slow letter · nine turtles"; an untitled post's opening words), so the tab, the window and the browser's History say what's being read (decision 0231). The front page opens Unread. Any other address opens **"Wrong turn?"** in place of the app (decision 0102; DESIGN.md → "Name and signature"): a small "404 error", eight turtles walking one way and one turned the other, "There's nothing at this address. It may have moved, or the link may have a typing mistake in it.", "If a link in this app brought you here, tell me which one and I'll fix it." signed "— Rohit", and **Back to your reading**, which opens the front page. "Tell me which one" opens the reader's email app with the address already written in. The tab says "Page not found". A well-formed address for a person or publication or folder you don't have still opens the list with "Not found" or "Folder not found" (sections 3 and 36), also for a person's page (and the old Edit and source-settings addresses, which open it). Signed out, such an address shows the sign-in page first, then "Wrong turn?" once you sign in. The website's own 404 page is the same, with "on this site" and no button.
- Regions are told apart by background tone and space, never by lines, shadows or gradients: the sidebar is toned, and the list and the article share the page's own color (decision 0221). Small exceptions: a faint line under a top line once something has scrolled under it, a soft shadow under the note that floats at the bottom of the list, a faint line along the top of the phone's article toolbar, and, on a computer, a faint 1px line where two panes meet (the sidebar's right edge, and the list's when the article is beside it), in the same tone as the hairlines between list rows (DESIGN.md → Visual language; decision 0054). The article's bars are solid, never see-through.
- Hiding the sidebar while the keyboard is in it moves the keyboard to the list itself (the selected article, or the first), never to the list's title; with no articles, to the sidebar button.
- Nothing animates. Views switch instantly.

**How to check it**
1. Make the browser window wide, then narrower, then phone-narrow. You see three, then two, then one column.
2. Press the sidebar's button twice: the sidebar hides and returns. There is no button on the list. Press `f`: the sidebar and the list hide; the one button left brings both back. An article's More menu has Full-screen reading too.
4. Drag the line between the sidebar and the list, then double-click it. The sidebar widens, then returns to its usual width.
3. Squint at the screen: you can still tell the three regions apart, with a faint line where the sidebar meets the list and the list meets the article.
5. Open `/nowhere`. "Wrong turn?" shows, with "404 error" and the turtles; Back to your reading opens Unread.
6. Open an article: the browser tab says its title, then "· nine turtles". Go back to the list (or, on a computer, to Settings): the tab says the place again.
