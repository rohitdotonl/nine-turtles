# 23. Your data: keeping and exporting

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- **The yearly clean-up** (decision 0089): an article you haven't starred is removed a year after nine turtles first received it, read or not. Starred articles stay forever. The year counts from when nine turtles received the article, not when it was published, so old posts from a blog you just added stay a year too. (If other readers already follow that blog, nine turtles received its posts when they did: you get those it still keeps, not ones it has already removed.) Another reader's star keeps an article for them, not for you. A removed article never comes back as new, even while its feed still lists it. The clean-up runs each night just after the backup is safely stored, never without one, so anything it removes is in that night's copy (kept up to a year). It happens quietly; the app shows nothing about it.
- Unsubscribing removes that feed's articles and your read history for it (your starred articles stay in Starred); another reader following the same feed keeps it as it was. Undo straight afterwards, or Bring back under Recently unsubscribed for 30 days, brings them back (section 15, decision 0240); after that they're gone from the app, though the backups still hold them as they were at the last nightly copy.
- **Download everything** (decision 0251), from Settings → Your data and the delete sheets (section 18): one .zip file, named for the day (`nine-turtles-2026-11-11.zip`), that opens as a folder of the same name. Offered from the first day, even with nothing kept yet, and after a trial or plan has ended. Inside:
  - **index.html**, which opens in any browser with or without nine turtles or the internet: "Your reading", the day it was saved, **People you follow** (A to Z, each name once, linked to their website, with any note on them), **What you kept** (every article you starred, highlighted or wrote a note on, newest first under each year, with who wrote it, the day, and "Starred" or "Your notes"; with nothing kept, "Nothing yet. Star an article, or highlight words in it, and it's kept here."), and **For other apps** (the three files below, with what each is for).
  - **articles/2026/…html**, one page per kept article: its title, byline and day, "Read it where it was published" (the original's link), the text as the publisher sent it, then **Your highlights and notes**. Plain HTML, its style inside, no scripts and no fonts to fetch; light or dark as the device is set. Pictures stay on the publisher's site: they show when online, and their descriptions stand in when not.
  - **follows.opml**: who you follow, in their folders (section 16).
  - **starred.json**: every starred article as a JSON Feed file (a standard, open format other feed tools can read), newest first, with its text, original link and the feed it came from, including starred articles of feeds you've since unsubscribed from.
  - **notes-and-highlights.md**: your notes and highlights as one Markdown file (section 42).
- **Import starred articles** (decision 0098), from Settings → Your data: brings your stars over from another feed reader. Choose the JSON file it exports starred (or "saved") articles in: Inoreader, Feedly, The Old Reader, FreshRSS, Feedbin and others that use the Google Reader format, or a JSON Feed file such as nine turtles' own starred export. Each article appears in Starred, read, with its text as the file has it (cleaned like any feed's), its original link and the feed it came from. An article nine turtles already has from a feed is starred as it is. A star whose feed you don't follow stays only in Starred (and search), with a link to the original; no feed is added to your sidebar and nothing is fetched for it. An article the file gives no link to the original for is left out. Importing only adds stars, so nothing needs undoing; importing the same file twice stars nothing new. Afterwards a plain summary says what happened, for example "Starred 120 articles. They're in Starred, each with a link to the original." At most 10,000 articles or 25 MB per file, and each reader keeps at most 10,000 imported articles (50 MB of their text) from all files together; a file that would go past that imports nothing and says so. A star whose file names its feed but not the feed's address keeps that feed's name; one that names no feed at all is credited to "Imported stars". Importing works after a free trial or plan has ended too, as starring does. Pocket isn't supported: it keeps whole web pages, not what a feed sent.
- **Backups** (decision 0076): every night, nine turtles stores a complete copy of everything (feeds, folders, articles, read marks, stars, settings) in storage outside Render, and another just before any update changes the database's structure. It keeps the last 30 nights, one copy a month for a year, and the last 10 taken before an update. At worst a day of reading activity is lost. Bringing a copy back, or moving to another host, is a written, tested procedure (`docs/operations/backups.md`, `docs/operations/moving-hosts.md`). Backups happen quietly in the background; the app never shows or asks anything about them. Download everything remains your own safety net too.

**How to check it**
1. Download everything, open the zip, and open index.html: who you follow and every starred or highlighted article are there; open one, and its text, link and your highlights are on its page. Turn off the internet: it still opens.
2. Download everything now and then, and keep it somewhere safe.
3. Export your starred articles from another feed reader (most offer it in their settings, near OPML export) and import the file: they appear in Starred, each with a link to the original, and your sidebar stays as it was.
4. In the backup storage (Cloudflare R2), `nightly/` holds a copy from last night.
5. A year after you start, articles you never starred from before then are gone, and your starred ones are all still in Starred.
