# 43. The blog checkup (on the website)

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

*Built 4 November 2026 (decision 0178), step 12 of the owner's review. A free page at nineturtles.app/blog-checkup/, for writers, with no account.*

- **"Is your blog easy to follow and write to?"** One field, **Your blog's address**, and **Check my blog**; "Your blog's home page works best." The lede: "Paste your blog's address. See what a feed reader sees: your feed, your name, and a way for readers to write to you. If anything's missing, it shows how to fix it on your platform." Linked from the website's footer (**Blog checkup**, after Feed finder) and from Write back's Help page.
- While it looks, "Looking at example.com…". Then one line sums up ("Readers can follow you and write to you.", or what's missing), and the checks, each a heading and one line that starts in words, never color alone: **Yes.**, **Not yet.**, **None.** (for the optional one) or **Couldn't look.**
  - **Easy to follow:** **Your feed** (its address and its newest post's date) and **Your name** (the name the feed gives its writer: the feed's author, an RSS editor's name, or the one name all its posts give; a name that's only the blog's title doesn't count).
  - **Easy to write to,** "Three ways, from enough to best. One is all a reader needs.": **Enough: a contact page** (the contact or about page the home page links to, and whether the home page or that page shows an email address, never which: the checkup mustn't hand out addresses a site hid from bots; an address on the home page alone is enough too), **Best: your email in your feed** (as Write back reads it, section 37; a feed whose posts give several people's addresses is told so), and **Optional: Reply by email on each post** ("That's fine: it's optional, never required.").
- **The fixes:** under each check that isn't met, how to fix it on Bear Blog, WordPress, Ghost and "Your own site (Hugo, Jekyll, Eleventy and others)". When the blog's platform shows (its home page's or feed's generator, or a bearblog.dev, wordpress.com or ghost.io address), its fix comes first and the others are under **Using something else?**; otherwise all are shown. Only what's known to be true is said: where no setting is known, it says so ("I don't know of a setting for this on Ghost") and points to the ways that do work.
- **Problems** are a plain sentence in the status line, as the feed finder's are (section 30). When the blog's guard against bots turns the checkup away, it says feed readers may be turned away too, and points to the publishers' page (decision 0258). No feed found isn't a problem: the feed check says "Not yet" and the others still run.
- **What it visits** (the second floor): it finds the feed exactly as the feed finder does (the page given, once; if it names none, the home page and the usual feed addresses, one at a time; each of a site's feeds once), then reads the blog's home page (the one the feed names, else the page given; the page given isn't fetched again when it's the home page) and, only if that shows no email address, the one contact or about page it links to (as Write back does, decision 0168). Never a post's page. At most 8 seconds for the home and contact pages. It shares the feed finder's memory and limits: each answer remembered half an hour, any one site looked up at most six times in half an hour, nothing about who asked kept. A checkup whose site didn't answer is remembered for five minutes, as a failure is.
- The page's address carries what was asked (`?address=…`), so a checkup can be shared or reloaded. Below the results: what it looks at, and a link to For publishers.
