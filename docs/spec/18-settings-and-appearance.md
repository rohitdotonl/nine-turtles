# 18. Settings and appearance

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- Settings opens in place of the list and reading area; the sidebar stays. Its X (named "Close settings") or Escape goes back to where you were.
- Laid out as Apple Books' settings are: one centred column, no wider than 560px, of grouped lists. Each group is a rounded block a tone off the page (by night a clear step lighter than the page, the text color mixed 7% into it, so it never melts into it), with its name above it in small uppercase muted letters and, where it helps, a line explaining it below; its rows are at least 44px tall, parted by hairlines inset from the left.
- **A shorter first page** (decision 0120): Settings itself keeps only the everyday settings, and everything else is a row ending in › that opens a page of its own in place of Settings. In order:
  - **Reading:** Reading mode, Unread counts, Let unread go after, Sort order. "These apply on all your devices." under the group.
  - **Look and feel:** Appearance, Text size, and **Font, theme and line width ›** (with the reading font's name before the ›). "These apply to this device only…" under the group.
  - **Your library:** Your feeds › (section 15), Feed health › (section 17), Show in the sidebar ›, Your data ›.
  - **More:** Keyboard › (only on a screen with a keyboard), Add from anywhere ›, Rules ›, Newsletters › (while nine turtles takes them), Other apps ›, Connected assistants ›, Passkeys › (where they work).
  - **Your nine turtles** (the owner only, with a small "Only you" beside its name): Readers ›.
  - **Account:** Your plan › (while nine turtles takes payments, for readers who aren't free, with where they stand before the ›), Email, Sign out, Delete data…, and for every reader but the owner Delete account…; under it the lines below and the version.
- **Each row** has an icon at its left, a line drawing in the muted grey (never the accent), lined up with the first line of its name whether or not a description sits under it: Reading mode a book, Unread counts a 9 in a ring, Let unread go after a clock, Sort order two arrows, Appearance a palette, Text size an A with a plus, Font, theme and line width a pen's nib, Your feeds a feed's waves, Feed health a heart with a pulse, Show in the sidebar a window with a sidebar, Your data a tray with an arrow, Keyboard a keyboard, Add from anywhere a bookmark with a plus, Rules sliders, Newsletters a newspaper, Other apps a plug, Connected assistants a robot's head, Passkeys a key, Readers two people, Your plan a card, Email an envelope, Sign out a door with an arrow, Delete data and Delete account a bin.
- **Each setting's name** is about 0.98rem, weight 500, in the text color; a short description sits straight under it, about 0.8rem in the muted grey (line height 1.35): Reading mode "An Unread list, with counts of what's waiting." (in river mode, "Everything flows past. No Unread list, no counts."), Unread counts "Hide keeps Unread, without the numbers.", Let unread go after "Older unread counts as read. It stays in its feed, All and search." Rows are 12px above and below on a computer and 14px on a phone. On a computer a row's control sits at its right end, every control the same width; on a phone it sits under the name and description, the width of the row.
- **Settings' own pages** each have their own address, `/settings/font`, `/settings/sidebar`, `/settings/data`, `/settings/keyboard`, `/settings/add-from-anywhere`, `/settings/rules`, `/settings/newsletters`, `/settings/other-apps`, `/settings/assistants`, `/settings/passkeys`, `/settings/readers` and `/settings/plan`, so a reload keeps you there and the browser's Back and Forward work. Each opens in place of Settings with **‹ Settings** on a line above its title (no X); it and Escape go back to Settings, and the keyboard returns to the row that opened the page. The page's title names its group, so the group's own name above it is left to screen readers. Screen readers hear each row's name and "Opens its own page."
- **Look and feel** (this device only): Appearance, theme (Original or Plain), reading font (Literata, Atkinson Hyperlegible, shown as "Atkinson", or System serif), text size (six steps from Smaller to Largest, Medium by default), line width (Narrow, Medium or Wide: about 65, 71 or 78 characters in Literata; Atkinson Hyperlegible's narrower letters fit more). Appearance and text size are on Settings itself; theme, reading font and line width on **Font, theme and line width**, whose first row is a sample paragraph (inside the group, its words on the rows' words, a hairline under it) that shows how articles will look. Changes apply at once (decision 0055). The same preferences are in the Aa panel above every article (section 7), and the lines under the groups say so.
- **Theme:** Original (the default): white paper by day, near-black by night, with an ink-blue accent for unread dots and search matches, and green for starred stars (decisions 0056, 0057). Or Plain: the owner's site rohit.onl's bare look, black on white (white on near-black by night), the unread dot and search matches in the text color, and links in articles in the browser's blue, purple once visited, underlined (decision 0063). Chosen in Settings and the Aa panel. The phone's own bar above the page takes the showing version's color too.
- **Appearance:** Light, Dark or Match device (the default). Light and Dark always show the day or night version; Match device follows the device's own light or dark setting, and switches with it while nine turtles is open. A theme chosen in an earlier version becomes Original; its Appearance is kept (decision 0056).
- Sort order, Appearance, reading font and line width are rows of choices on a faint track (a "segmented control"), chosen with a click, a tap or the arrow keys (they're radio buttons underneath). The chosen one is raised: the page's own color (in dark themes, a clear step lighter than its track) with a small shadow under it and no edge, and its word is semibold, so it shows without color. Each reading font's name is written in that font.
- **Text size** is a stepper, as in Apple Books: one flat capsule on the same faint track as the segmented controls, a small A with a minus and a large A with a plus at its ends and the step's name between them, each part split from the next by a hairline and nothing raised, so it never reads as a choice with something chosen (decision 0055); one step each press, the name said aloud when it changes; at either end the A that can't go further fades to the muted grey. 44px tall to touch. It's the same control as in the Aa panel.
- **Reading:** the reading mode, inbox or river (section 10); in inbox mode, **Unread counts** (Show or Hide: hidden, the sidebar shows no numbers, and Unread stays) and **Let unread go after** (Never, 1 week, 2 weeks or 1 month: older unread counts as read, section 8; decision 0097); then newest or oldest first. Each row's description says what it does. River mode has no Unread, so the two in-between rows step aside there.
- **Show in the sidebar** (decisions 0105, 0108, 0113), its own page (`/settings/sidebar`): a tick box row each for **Starred**, **All**, **Recently read**, **Up Next** (section 35; it shows only while it holds an episode), **Saved searches** and **Collections** (the same rounded-square tick boxes as Edit feed's), all ticked by default. Unticking one takes it out of the sidebar at once (section 5). The line under the list says Unread, folders and feeds always show (and All in river mode), and that a hidden list still opens from its address and its keys.
- **Keyboard** (decision 0110; only on a screen with a keyboard), its own page: one row, **Change keys…**, ending in ›, which opens the shortcut list ready to change keys (section 19). The line under it says you can give any shortcut a key of your own, or none, that your keys follow you to every device, and that ? shows them all.
- **Your data**, its own page (the welcome's "Import an OPML file…" opens it too): first the **Keep articles for reading offline** switch (section 32, on by default, for this device only), then rows that act: **Import OPML…** and **Import starred articles…** (each opens the file chooser; each ends in ›), **Export OPML**, **Export starred articles** and **Export collections** (each downloads a file; each ends in a download mark), in the text color. What the files are is said under the group; what an import did appears there too.
- **Your library:** **Your feeds ›** opens Your feeds, for tidying many feeds at once (section 15), and **Feed health ›** opens the feed health page (section 17). Both keep their own top line with an X, as before; it and Escape go back to Settings, to the row that opened them.
- **Add from anywhere** (decision 0104), its own page: two rows, each an address in the muted grey with **Copy** at its right end, as Other apps shows its server address: **Bookmark for your browser** (the bookmarklet, section 3) and **Address for Apple Shortcuts** (`…/add-feed?url=`, section 20), both built from this nine turtles' own address. "Bookmark copied." (or how to copy it by hand) is said under the group. The line under the group says how to add a feed from the page you're on: share it on Android, the bookmark on a computer, a shortcut on iPhone and iPad, and that you choose whether to add what's found.
- **Rules**, its own page: the reader's rules, New rule… and Muted articles › (section 27). Muted articles' X and Escape go back to Rules.
- **Newsletters**, its own page, while nine turtles takes them: the reader's private address (section 28).
- **Other apps**, its own page: the app password other feed readers sync with (section 26).
- **Connected assistants**, its own page: the address to give an AI assistant, the assistants connected, an assistant key, and feeds an assistant unsubscribed from (section 33).
- **Readers** (the owner only; decisions 0087, 0088), its own page (`/settings/readers`): **All readers ›**, with how many there are, opens the All readers page. The line under the group says so, and that nobody sees what anyone else reads. The owner's own email is under Account, and changes by Change email like every reader's (decision 0125).
- **The All readers page** (the owner only, `/settings/readers/all`; decision 0090), opened from Settings › Readers › All readers, built like Feed health and Muted articles (its title, "All readers", and an X; it and Escape go back to Readers):
  - **This nine turtles:** rows of a name and a figure: Readers, Feeds (each counted once, however many follow it), Articles kept, Database (its size, such as "96 MB"), Backups ("On, latest 25 October 2026", "On, none yet" or "Off") and Email (On or Off). The line under it says each feed is fetched once, each reader can follow up to 1,000 feeds, and feeds whose readers have all been away for 30 days are checked once a day (with how many are now).
  - **Needs a look:** plain sentences for what the owner can fix: backups off, email off, and how many feeds have failed for three days or more (each reader sees theirs on their own Feed health page); otherwise "Nothing needs a look." Below it, the problems nine turtles noted since it last started (the newest ten, newest first, each with its time), in its own technical words, which the line under them says an agent can explain.
  - **The readers** ("3 readers"): the owner first, then the newest. Each row: their email (or "No email yet"), "(you)" beside the owner's, and in the muted grey "Joined 25 October 2026 · 42 feeds", with "Last here today" (or "Last here 24 October 2026": only the day) under it (decision 0093). When it applies, a line in the second ink: "Follows as many feeds as one reader can." or "3 feeds keep failing." How many feeds, never which, and nothing about what anyone reads; the line under the group says so. The day each was last here is the one nine turtles already knows for resting feeds (section 11); it's noted at most once every 24 hours, so it can be a day behind, and nothing more is recorded for it.
  - The page only lists; it changes nothing. While nine turtles takes payments, each reader's line also says their plan ("Free trial", "Monthly plan", …; section 29), never what they paid.
- **Passkeys** (every reader; decision 0109), its own page, shown only where passkeys work (nine turtles' public address, in a browser that has them; section 1). The line under the group: "Sign in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN instead of a password. Adding a passkey asks for your password. Your password keeps working, so removing a passkey never locks you out."
  - Each passkey is a row: its name, "Passkey added 27 October 2026" until the reader gives it one, with "Added 27 October 2026 · Last used today" (or "· Not used yet") under it in the muted grey, and at its right end **Rename** and **Remove** (in red words, its tooltip saying it removes the passkey and the password still works). Oldest first.
  - **Rename** turns the row into a text box with the name (its placeholder the "Passkey added …" name) and Cancel and **Save**; Enter saves, Escape cancels, and the keyboard returns to Rename. An empty name goes back to "Passkey added …". At most 100 characters ("That name is too long. Keep it under 100 characters.").
  - **Remove** takes the passkey away at once: it no longer signs in. The line under the group says "Removed “…”. Your password still works." with **Undo**, which has the keyboard's place and brings it back (for ten minutes; after that it's gone for good).
  - **Add a passkey** (ending in +) is the group's last row. Pressed, it becomes a "Your password" box with Cancel and **Continue** (the keyboard goes to the box); Cancel, or Escape in the box, puts Add a passkey back with the keyboard on it, and Settings stays open; the right password opens the browser's own sheet to make the passkey, and the new passkey appears in the list with "Passkey added. Next time, choose Sign in with a passkey on the sign-in page." under the group. An empty box says "Type your password first, to add a passkey."; a wrong password "That isn't your password." (the box is emptied), with the same waits as signing in. Cancelling the browser's sheet says "No passkey was added."; a device that already has one for this reader, "This device already has a passkey for you." (one just removed doesn't count: that device can add one again at once) Each reader keeps at most 20 ("You have as many passkeys as nine turtles keeps. Remove one to add another.").
  - Each reader's passkeys are theirs only: they sign in only as that reader, and nobody else sees or changes them.
- **Account:** first, while nine turtles takes payments, **Your plan ›** (section 29). Then **Email**, the email this reader signs in with (or "No email yet") in the muted grey under the word, with **Change…** at its right end while nine turtles can send email (decision 0088). Then **Sign out** as a row of its own, its icon a small door mark, in the text color (not red: signing out removes nothing). Then **Delete data…** in red words, for every reader, the owner too (decision 0117); then, for every reader but the owner, **Delete account…** in red words. Under the group, for every reader but the owner, "Whoever runs this nine turtles can see your email, when you joined, how many feeds you follow and the day you were last here. Never which feeds or what you read." (decision 0093); then the version. (Keeping articles comes in V2.)
- **Change email** (a sheet; on a phone, the phone sheet): **New email** and **Your password** (so a device left signed in can't hand the account to someone else), "A link goes to the new email. Your email changes once it's opened." under them, then Cancel and **Send link**. A wrong password says "That isn't your password." with the same waits as sign-in. Sent, the sheet closes, the keyboard returns to Change…, and the group says "A link is on its way to … Open it within 24 hours to use that address; until then, your email stays as it is." Opening the link and pressing **Confirm email** changes it ("Your email is now …. Sign in with it from now on.", with Continue), and the old address gets a short email saying so. An address another reader has gets no link, and the answer is the same; the try still counts towards the reader's three changes an hour (the fourth hears "Enough links have gone out for now. Try again in an hour."), so that can't tell either. Without email, Change… isn't shown and the group says changing your email needs nine turtles to send email.
- **Delete your account?** (a sheet; on a phone, the phone sheet; never for the owner): the one "Are you sure?" (the owner's choice, decision 0088). It says what goes (feeds, folders, read and starred articles, collections, saved searches, rules, app password, passkeys, email and password) and that it can't be undone, and first offers **Export OPML**, **Export starred articles** and **Export collections** as download rows. Then **Delete account** in red words at the far left and Cancel at the right; on a phone, Delete account is the one red row of a group of its own and the X closes. Deleting removes the reader and everything theirs at once, with no waiting period and no undo (the owner accepted this, as for unsubscribing; the nightly backups still hold it), signs this device out and shows the sign-in page. Escape, Cancel or the X keeps everything, and the keyboard returns to Delete account….
- **Delete your data?** (a sheet; on a phone, the phone sheet; for every reader, the owner too): the one "Are you sure?" (the owner's choice, decision 0117), built as Delete your account? is. It says what goes (feeds, folders, read and starred articles, collections, saved searches, rules), that it can't be undone, and that the account stays, and first offers the same three downloads. Then **Delete data** in red words. Deleting empties the reader's account at once, with no undo (the nightly backups still hold it): every feed is let go as unsubscribing lets it go, so a feed nobody else follows stops being checked. The reader stays signed in, with their email, password, passkeys, app password, connected assistants, settings, plan and newsletter address; a newsletter they still followed starts its feed again with its next email, and one they had stopped stays stopped. This device's saved copies go too, and the app opens again at its start, empty, with the welcome. Escape, Cancel or the X keeps everything, and the keyboard returns to Delete data….
- **Status:** which version of nine turtles you're using. When a newer version is live (nine turtles notices from the server's answers), it says "A new version is ready" with a **Reload** button. A tab left open notices within a few minutes, or as soon as you come back to it. Then the next time you go to another place (Unread, a feed, Settings, or Back), you arrive there in the new version: the screen was changing anyway, so nothing moves under you. It waits while a mark is still being saved or an Undo is on offer, so neither is lost. New versions are never swapped in while you're reading. If a tab left open across an update can't carry on, it says so plainly in the note at the bottom of the list (where "New items" appears): "nine turtles was updated. Reload to continue.", with a **Reload** button.
- Fonts are served by nine turtles itself; nothing is loaded from font services. Other scripts such as Hindi use the fonts already on your device.
- **Shared across your devices:** reading mode, unread counts, letting unread go, sort order, which lists the sidebar shows, and the keys you chose (section 19). **Per device:** Appearance, font, text size and line width (a phone and a laptop often want different sizes). `+`/`-` change the same text-size setting.

**How to check it**
1. Switch your computer to dark mode: nine turtles follows, at once, in the night version. Choose Appearance Light in Settings: it stays light regardless of the system.
2. Change the reading font and text size: the article updates immediately.
3. Look at the browser's network list (or ask an agent to): no requests to Google Fonts or any other font service.
4. Once email is set up, sign in as a reader you made (not the owner). In Settings › Account press Change…, type another email you can read and your password, and press Send link. Your email in Settings stays as it was until you open the link and press Confirm email; then it shows the new one, and the old address has a note.
5. Still as that reader, press Delete account…: the sheet offers the three downloads first. Press Escape: nothing changes. Press it again, then Delete account: you're at the sign-in page, and that email and password no longer sign in. Signed in as the owner, there is no Delete account.
6. As a reader with some feeds, press Delete data…, then Delete data: the app opens again with no feeds, folders or articles, still signed in with the same email, at the start rather than in Settings.
7. As the owner, open Settings › Readers › All readers. The top group shows how many readers, feeds and articles there are, the database's size, and whether backups and email are on; "Needs a look" names anything to fix. A test reader you made appears in the list with how many feeds they follow, never which feeds, and "Last here today". Escape goes back to Readers, with the keyboard on All readers.
8. At app.nineturtles.app, open Settings › Passkeys and press Add a passkey. Type your password and press Continue; your device asks for your fingerprint, face or PIN, and the passkey appears as "Passkey added" and today's date. Press Rename, call it "Laptop" and press Enter. Press Remove: it's gone, and Undo brings it back. At slowfeed.onrender.com, Settings has no Passkeys group.
9. Open Settings: it shows only Reading, Look and feel (Appearance, Text size and Font, theme and line width ›), then Your library, More and Account, each row with its grey icon on the line of its name. Press Rules ›: the address is `/settings/rules`, the title is Rules with ‹ Settings above it. Press Escape: Settings is back, with the keyboard on Rules. Press the browser's Back and Forward: Rules comes and goes.
10. On a phone, open Settings: each setting's choices sit under its name and description, across the screen.
