# 6. Item list

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- Each row shows the article **title** in the reading serif, as a book's contents page sets its chapters (19px with a mouse, so it clearly leads; decision 0128), and below it the **source name and time** in quiet small text (12px, spaced a little: the source name in muted grey, the time a step fainter, and both muted grey on the selected row; decision 0123) (just the time in one feed's own list, whose title already names it). That line is always one line: a long source name ends in "…", with the "·" right after it at the same distance as on any other row, and the time and the star always keep their place on it, never wrapping below; its figures are proportional, as in running text (decision 0048). Unread articles have a small dot in the theme's accent in a gutter of its own before the title, clear of the words, so it reads as a mark in the margin, not a bullet; every title starts at the same place, dot or no dot, lined up with the list's title and the search field. Each row has 22px above its title, 8px between title and meta line, and 28px below the meta line, and no line parts one row from the next: space alone does (decisions 0123 and 0128). No images, no site icons.
- **A post with no title** (a short note, as many people's own sites send) shows its **opening words once** where a title would be (decision 0196): in the reading serif at the title's size, never semibold even while unread (so it doesn't pose as a title; the unread dot still marks it), wrapping to two lines and then "…". No line of its text under it. In search results, the line of text around the searched word shows only when the opening doesn't already show a searched word (marked in the opening as in a title), so a row always says why it was found; a Notes row still shows the reader's highlight or note. A post with no words at all (a picture alone) says "Untitled". The opening is plain text from the feed, the same words the article begins with; the server sends only what two lines can show (200 characters). The Hidden articles page shows it the same way.
- **A publication's writer** (the owner's core path, point 4, decision 0183): an article from a publication whose feed names who wrote it starts that line with them: "Lena Fischer · Garden Letters · 1 Nov" (in the publication's own list, "Lena Fischer · 1 Nov"), in every list and in search. A person's articles don't, since the person's name already says who; nor does one whose writer is the publication's own name.
- Dates are shown in your device's time zone, in the words a person would say (decision 0218): "Today", "Yesterday", the weekday for the rest of the past week ("Thursday"), then "23 Oct" for this year and "23 Oct 2025" before that. Days are counted by the calendar, not by 24-hour stretches. The words never change while you look: "Today" becomes "Yesterday" only when the list next loads. The exact day and time ("Sunday, 8 November 2026 at 06:00") is in the date's tooltip, and the same words are used under an article's title, in Start reading and on a person's page. The time is when the publisher says they published it, or when nine turtles received it if they don't say (and never later than that, so a wrong date can't pin an article to the top).
- Newest first by default; oldest first can be chosen in Settings or in the list's More menu.
- The list's title is one line: a long name ends in "…" (its whole name is its tooltip and is read in full by screen readers), so the search field under it never moves; in a narrow list it is set a size smaller (22px rather than 24px). Beside it sits one quiet icon, **More** (…), named for screen readers and in its tooltip. It opens a small menu with **Mark all as read** (section 8; its key, `Shift+A`, shown beside it), **Refresh** (section 13; its key, `r`; not while searching), the **sort order** (Newest first or Oldest first, the current one ticked and semibold), and, in a person's or publication's own list, **Info about Alpha** (their page, section 36; its key, `i`), or in a folder's, **Edit folder…** (section 15). A click, Enter or the down arrow opens it on its first item; the arrow keys, Home and End move through it; Escape closes it and puts the keyboard back on More; Tab or a press outside closes it too. Closing a sheet opened from it puts the keyboard back on More.
- Unread, Starred and All articles list articles from every feed. A feed's own list shows all its articles, read and unread.
- **A feed that isn't working says so above its own list** (decision 0106): only while Everyone you follow lists it as not working (two checks in a row have failed; section 15), one quiet line in the second ink and the small size, under the search field where a search's count sits, never in the accent or oxblood: "This feed hasn't worked since 12 Oct." ("since earlier today" on the day it began; the year too when it isn't this one), followed by a text button, **Find its new address**. Pressing it does exactly what Find new address does on Everyone you follow (section 15), in place, under the line; nothing is fetched until it's pressed (decision 0016). Once the feed has moved, the line gives way to "It now reads from its new address and works again. Its articles, stars and folders stayed." until you leave the list, and the keyboard moves to the list's title. Search results, folders and the views show no such line.
- The list has an end: it shows 50 items, and when you reach the bottom the next 50 load below by themselves, with a small grey centred "Loading…" while they arrive; nothing above moves (decision 0075). For the keyboard and screen readers there's a **"Show older articles"** button ("Show newer articles" when sorting oldest first), hidden until Tab reaches it, then a small grey outlined pill centred under the list, never in the accent. If loading fails, the button shows for everyone, to try again. When there's nothing more to show, a quiet caption closes the list, on the row titles' left edge 40px under the last row, in the reading serif at the body size and the muted grey, with 96px of room under it so the list visibly stops (no line above it; decision 0142): **"No more results."** at the end of search results, and **"That's everything."** at the end of every other list, Unread included (decision 0187). Where the reader has just finished a list that still has unread articles in it, **Mark all as read** waits 12px under the caption, an everyday bordered button, the 96px of room now under it (decision 0214): it marks the articles listed, as More's does (section 8), with Undo, and goes once none is unread. Not after search results, nor with Unread off (section 10).
- **A row's own menu** (decision 0214): a right-click on a computer, a long press (half a second, held still) on a touch screen, or the Menu key or Shift+F10 with the keyboard on the row opens a small menu where it was pressed (under the start of the title, by keyboard), kept inside the window, like More's: **Star** or **Unstar**, **Mark as read** or **Mark as unread** (not with Unread off), then, for someone still followed, **Write back** (a person only; it opens the article and starts writing back, section 37) and **Info about {name}** (their page, section 36). No keys are printed in it: each has its key and place elsewhere. The keyboard starts on its first choice; the arrow keys, Home and End move; Escape or a choice gives the keyboard back to the row; Tab or a press outside closes it. Nothing opens or is chosen by opening it, and the tap that ends a long press doesn't open the row. While the menu is open its row keeps the pointer's faint tone (an edge in high-contrast modes), as a mouse's hover already shows, so it's clear which article the menu acts on; on a touch screen nothing else would show it. It is never the selection pill, which means where you are. Each mark has its Undo, as anywhere (section 8).
- Row states, each recognizable without color:

  | State | Looks like |
  |---|---|
  | Unread | Full-strength, semibold title, and a small dot in the theme's accent before it |
  | Read | A softer second ink, regular-weight title (the title stays full-strength while it's the selected row) |
  | Selected | A rounded pill of soft neutral grey set a little in from the list's edges, with no color (the same as in the sidebar; decision 0057); the title in full ink. When you're using the keyboard, an outline is drawn just inside the pill's edge too |
  | Menu open | The pointer's fainter tone, never the pill, while the row's own menu is open, on touch screens too |
  | Starred | A small filled star after the time, in the ink (read out as "Starred"; decision 0143). The article's own star button is filled, in full ink, while it's starred; the Starred icon in the sidebar stays an outline, even while you're there (decision 0182) |
  | Part read | While your place in it is kept (section 7), a few words after the time in the source name's muted grey: "· Just begun", "· A third read", "· Half read", "· Two-thirds read" or "· Nearly finished", by how much of it has been in view; "· Part read" for a place kept on another device, which can't say how far (decision 0220). Words, never a bar or a number; they go when you reach the end or scroll back to the top, as the place does |

- A podcast episode's meta line ends with a small headphones icon and the episode's length ("45 min"), in the same quiet text (section 35).
- **An empty list** (decision 0142) has one shape everywhere: a short fact in the reading serif at the row titles' size (the second ink), and, only when there's something to do, how to start in the interface's text (15px, muted) 8px under it; at most 300px wide, on the list's left edge, 40px down from where the list starts (under its search field), never centred, and no picture. The words: a feed **"Nothing from this feed yet."** with "Last checked today at 14:20." (or "Last checked on 3 Oct.") once it has been checked; a folder **"Nothing in this folder yet."**; Unread **"Nothing unread."**; Starred **"Nothing starred yet."** with "Press the star on any article to keep it here."; anywhere else "No articles yet."; and a search that found nothing says what it looked for and where: **"Nothing found for “walking” in Starred."** ("Nothing found in Unread." with only filters). When the search looked in one place, not everywhere, an everyday bordered button under it, **Search all articles**, searches the same words everywhere at once (decision 0212).
- Items you read in the Unread view stay where they are (greyed) until you press `r` or leave the view, so nothing jumps under your cursor.

**How to check it**
0. Right-click a row (or long-press it on a phone, or press Shift+F10 on it): Star, Mark as read, Write back and Info about them. While the menu is open, its row keeps a faint tone (lighter than the selected row's pill); it goes when the menu closes. Star: the row has a star, and nothing opened. At the end of a list with unread articles, press Mark all as read under "That's everything.": all are read, and Undo brings them back.
1. Open a feed with many articles. Scroll to the bottom: "Loading…" shows briefly and more appear below, and nothing above moves. Keep going until the list ends with "That's everything." (Unread ends the same way, and search results with "No more results.")
2. Open an article and move on: its title turns softer and regular weight, and it doesn't vanish from the list.
3. Turn your screen to greyscale (system accessibility setting). You can still tell unread, read, selected and starred apart.
4. Open a feed listed as not working at the top of Everyone you follow: "This feed hasn't worked since …" shows above its articles with **Find its new address**. A working feed's list has no such line.
5. Open a long article, scroll part of the way and stop: a moment later its row says how far ("· Half read"). Read to the end: the words go (decision 0220).
