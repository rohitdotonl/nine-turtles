# 6. Item list

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- Each row shows the article **title** in the reading serif, as a book's contents page sets its chapters (19px with a mouse, so it clearly leads; decision 0128), and below it the **source name and time** in quiet small text (12px, spaced a little: the source name in muted grey, the time a step fainter, and both muted grey on the selected row; decision 0123) (just the time in one feed's own list, whose title already names it). That line is always one line: a long source name ends in "…", with the "·" right after it at the same distance as on any other row, and the time and the star always keep their place on it, never wrapping below; its figures are proportional, as in running text (decision 0048). Unread articles have a small dot in the theme's accent in a gutter of its own before the title, clear of the words, so it reads as a mark in the margin, not a bullet; every title starts at the same place, dot or no dot, lined up with the list's title and the search field. Each row has 22px above its title, 8px between title and meta line, and 28px below the meta line, and no line parts one row from the next: space alone does (decisions 0123 and 0128). No images, no site icons.
- Dates and times are shown in your device's time zone, in words that never change while you look: "14:05" for today, "23 Oct" for this year, "23 Oct 2025" before that. The time is when the publisher says they published it, or when nine turtles received it if they don't say (and never later than that, so a wrong date can't pin an article to the top).
- Newest first by default; oldest first can be chosen in Settings or in the list's More menu.
- The list's title is one line: a long name ends in "…" (its whole name is its tooltip and is read in full by screen readers), so the search field under it never moves; in a narrow list it is set a size smaller (22px rather than 24px). Beside it sits one quiet icon, **More** (…), named for screen readers and in its tooltip, as Apple Books has one per collection. It opens a small menu with **Mark all as read** (section 8; its key, `Shift+A`, shown beside it), while an article is chosen **Mark all above as read** and **Mark all below as read** (section 8; `Shift+O` shown beside the older side), in Unread also **Catch me up** (section 8), **Refresh** (section 13; its key, `r`; not while searching), the **sort order** (Newest first or Oldest first, the current one ticked and semibold), and, in a feed's or folder's own list, **Edit feed…** or **Edit folder…** (section 15; its key, `Shift+E`), or, in a collection, **Rename…** and **Delete** (section 34). A click, Enter or the down arrow opens it on its first item; the arrow keys, Home and End move through it; Escape closes it and puts the keyboard back on More; Tab or a press outside closes it too. Closing a sheet opened from it puts the keyboard back on More.
- Unread, Starred and All list articles from every feed. A feed's own list shows all its articles, read and unread.
- **A feed that isn't working says so above its own list** (decision 0106): only while the feed health page lists it (two checks in a row have failed; section 17), one quiet line in the second ink and the small size, under the search field where a search's count sits, never in the accent or red: "This feed hasn't worked since 12 October." ("since earlier today" on the day it began; the year too when it isn't this one), followed by a text button, **Find its new address**. Pressing it does exactly what Find new address does on the feed health page (section 17), in place, under the line; nothing is fetched until it's pressed (decision 0016). Once the feed has moved, the line gives way to "It now reads from its new address and works again. Its articles, stars and folders stayed." until you leave the list, and the keyboard moves to the list's title. Search results, folders and the views show no such line.
- The list has an end: it shows 50 items, and when you reach the bottom the next 50 load below by themselves, with a small grey centred "Loading…" while they arrive; nothing above moves (decision 0075). For the keyboard and screen readers there's a **"Show older articles"** button ("Show newer articles" when sorting oldest first), hidden until Tab reaches it, then a small grey outlined pill centred under the list, never in the accent. If loading fails, the button shows for everyone, to try again. When there's nothing more to show, a quiet small caption closes the list, centred under it (no line above it): **"You're all caught up."** only at the end of Unread, the one list there is to get through; **"No more results."** at the end of search results; and **"That's everything."** at the end of every other list (a feed, a folder, Starred, All, a collection, Recently read, Up Next).
- Row states, each recognizable without color:

  | State | Looks like |
  |---|---|
  | Unread | Full-strength, semibold title, and a small dot in the theme's accent before it |
  | Read | A softer second ink, regular-weight title (the title stays full-strength while it's the selected row) |
  | Selected | A rounded pill of soft neutral grey set a little in from the list's edges, with no color (the same as in the sidebar; decision 0057); the title in full ink. When you're using the keyboard, an outline is drawn just inside the pill's edge too |
  | Starred | A small filled star after the time, in light green (read out as "Starred"; decision 0057). The article's own star button is green while it's starred, and so is the Starred icon in the sidebar while you're there |

- A podcast episode's meta line ends with a small headphones icon and the episode's length ("45 min"), in the same quiet text (section 35).
- Items you read in the Unread view stay where they are (greyed) until you press `r` or leave the view, so nothing jumps under your cursor.

**How to check it**
1. Open a feed with many articles. Scroll to the bottom: "Loading…" shows briefly and more appear below, and nothing above moves. Keep going until the list ends with "That's everything." (Unread ends with "You're all caught up.", and search results with "No more results.")
2. Open an article and move on: its title turns softer and regular weight, and it doesn't vanish from the list.
3. Turn your screen to greyscale (system accessibility setting). You can still tell unread, read, selected and starred apart.
4. Open a feed listed on the feed health page: "This feed hasn't worked since …" shows above its articles with **Find its new address**. A working feed's list has no such line.
