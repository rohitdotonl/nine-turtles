# 22. Everyone can read here (accessibility)

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- Contrast meets WCAG AA everywhere (an international accessibility standard) and AAA for article text, in every theme, including grey text.
- Nothing relies on color alone.
- Screen readers find the sidebar, the list and the article as named regions, hear which item is selected, and hear changes such as "marked as read" (said when an article is opened, though nothing about it is written on screen). Each article is marked with its language so it's pronounced correctly: the item's language if given, otherwise the feed's, when the article's letters agree with it; when most of its letters are in a script one language usually stands for (Devanagari, Arabic, Hebrew, Chinese, Japanese…), that language. Scripts many languages share (Latin, Cyrillic) are never guessed: an article there that names no language is left unmarked (decision 0223, section 24).
- Follows the system's text size, reduced motion, high contrast and light/dark settings. On an iPhone or iPad, the system's text size (Settings › Display & Brightness › Text Size, and Larger Text in Accessibility) grows the whole app, as it does Mail and Messages: the sidebar, lists, buttons, Settings and the article together, up to twice its size (a smaller setting leaves it as designed, so touch targets stay 44px); Aa's text size stays the reader's own control for the article, on top (decision 0252). It's read when nine turtles opens and each time the reader comes back to it. Android's browsers and a computer's zoom already scale the whole page by themselves.
- **Laid out for large text** (decision 0313): at one and a half times the usual text size or more (an iPhone's largest sizes, or a browser's own text size set larger), every screen still fits the width of a phone: a row's buttons drop under its words, a choice's or a button's words wrap inside it, Text and page puts each setting's name over its choices, Keyboard shortcuts puts each action's keys under it, titles grow a little less than the words they head so their longest word still fits, a writer's list leaves their small picture out of its title, and icons grow with the words beside them. A person's name on their page is capped as the other big titles are, and an article's headings stay smaller than its title. An icon's button stays a 44px square. On a computer the sidebar is wider to match. At the usual sizes nothing changes. At 400% zoom (a window both short and narrow), Everyone you follow's Choose bar no longer stays at the foot of the screen; it sits at the list's end, so it never covers the rows. A phone on its side keeps it in view.
- **Landmarks:** on a computer, the list and the article are the page's main content and the sidebar sits beside it, so a screen reader's "jump to main" goes past the sidebar; on a phone, one screen at a time, the whole screen is. A box that asks something (One person?) or says a feed isn't working has a heading for its title.
- **Small taps made bigger:** on a touch screen, a footnote's number, and its way back, has a 44px area to press around it, unseen, and the field for a new assistant key is a full 44px row.
- **The keyboard never hides:** pressing Tab past Text and page's last choice closes it, so the keyboard never lands on something under it.

**How to check it**
1. Zoom the browser to 200%: everything still works and nothing overlaps. On an iPhone, set the largest Larger Text size: every screen still fits, with nothing cut off at the right edge.
2. On an iPhone, set Text Size larger, then come back to nine turtles: the sidebar, list and buttons are larger too.
3. Turn on your phone's or computer's screen reader (VoiceOver, TalkBack or NVDA). Move through items: it reads the title and says when things are marked as read.
4. The automatic checks run contrast and accessibility tests on every screen in every theme, and open every phone screen at twice the text size to check nothing goes past the right edge; a pull request can't pass if they fail.
