# 7. Reading an article

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- The article opens inside nine turtles, using exactly what the publisher put in the feed, restyled consistently: headings, quotes, lists, code, tables, footnotes and images. On a computer a list's numbers or bullets hang in the margin, so its words start on the same left edge as the paragraphs; on a phone, where the margin is narrow, they stay inside (decision 0218).
- Reading font is Literata by default (Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and System serif, the device's own serif, as alternatives). Lines are about 65–75 characters long.
- Links inside articles are underlined in the normal text color, never blue, on every page (decision 0176).
- If the feed has only the title (no text and no summary, and it isn't a video or an episode), the same note under the closing rule says "Only the title is in the feed.", with **"Read it on {publisher} ↗"** as the way on (decision 0305).
- If the feed only has a summary, nine turtles shows the summary. It never fetches the rest from the website. A summary is what the feed marks as one (Atom's and JSON Feed's summary, without the full text beside it). RSS has one place for an article's text, which many blogs fill with the whole post, so there it counts as a summary only when it shows it was cut: it ends in "…" or "[…]", or ends with a "Read more" or "Continue reading" link, or it is a teaser: one line of bare words, no longer than 300 characters, with no markup at all (no paragraph, link or line break), under a title of its own that isn't the same words, as some sites send a post's subtitle (never a title-less post such as Bluesky's, nor a podcast episode's notes), or it ends with "The post … appeared first on …", which the Yoast SEO plugin adds to a WordPress excerpt (decision 0306); otherwise it is the whole article, which ends with no note (decision 0218). Under the article's closing rule, a small centred note in upright interface type (the meta size, 13px or 15px on a touch screen, the second ink) says "Only the beginning is shared here." (the owner's core path, point 5), and under it, on a row of its own at least 44px tall to press, a link to the publisher: **"Read the rest on {publisher} ↗"**, named by the site's own title ("Read the rest on Garden Letters", even when its writer is called Hal Brennan), or its address when it has none, ("Read the episode page on {publisher} ↗" for a podcast's episode, section 35), its arrow the open-in-new-tab icon, opening the original in a new tab: the page's one main button, filled with a deep tone of the ink, 44px tall (decision 0298; an underlined link before). Each sentence stays whole on a line wherever it fits, so a narrow column breaks between them and never strands a word (with very large text on a small phone, a sentence wraps evenly rather than running off the screen); **View original** goes there too.
- The article's top bar runs the width of the reading pane; on a computer its ends stand over the text column (decision 0277, replacing the pane's edges), its first and last icons mirroring each other over the text's two edges, so on a very wide window the tools stay within reach of the words. Each icon is named for screen readers and in its tooltip with its key. On a computer the bar is two and two (decision 0299): on the left **‹**, the way back to the list ("Back to {list}" for screen readers and in its tooltip, decision 0275), and **View original ↗** (an arrow pointing out, with no box; tooltip "View original (V)"), always present and always in the same place; on the right **Save** (an outline bookmark, filled once saved) and **More** (…, "More actions" for screen readers). The two sides are the same width, so the bar's middle is the text's middle. On a phone the bar holds ‹ and the list's name and View original ↗, and Share sits in the bottom toolbar (section 20). **Share** is Apple's box with an arrow on Apple devices, three linked dots elsewhere (decision 0293); on a computer it copies the link ("Copy link", key `c`), and where the device has a share menu, it opens that (section 21). More is a small menu like the list's, each item with its icon before its words (decision 0293): on a computer first **Copy link** (or **Share**, decision 0299), then **Highlight…** (its key, `h`, shown beside it on a computer; section 42, decision 0326), **Text and page** (the reading panel, below), **Mark as unread** (its key, `m`, shown beside it), **Add a note…** (section 42), then, for someone followed, apart, **Info about {name}** (their page, section 36; its key, `i`) and **Hide articles about…** (section 27). The name in the line under the title opens their list (below, decision 0289).
- Write back is never in a bar (decision 0298, replacing 0214's paper plane): it ends the article (section 37), is on their page, and `w` starts it anywhere.
- On a computer the top bar stays at the top of the pane (decision 0298, replacing 0263's bar that scrolled away). It is solid, in the page's own color. Its buttons never move or go as you read (decision 0299). Once the title has scrolled up under it, a hairline shows under the bar and its middle, the text's true middle, names the article: the title alone, as the writer wrote it, one line in the title's own face (17px, regular) and the muted grey, cut short with …. It rises from the bar's bottom edge into place step for step with the scroll, so the title seems to pass under the bar and come out on it, and sinks back the same way (decision 0302); nothing moves unless the reader scrolls, and with reduced motion it's simply there once the title has gone. Screen readers don't hear it twice. Save and View original are only in the bar, never again at the article's end: the bar is always there (decision 0302). What was just done ("Link copied.", "Highlighted.", "Note saved.") floats at the reading area's foot in the list's note, with its × (section 8), since the bar may be out of sight; a list note that arrives later ("Saved." with Undo) takes its place. The Aa panel closes when the article scrolls. A person's page has the same bar (section 36). On a phone the top bar stays put (section 20). The scrollbar's track is see-through, so its gutter is the page's own color in every theme.
- **The reading panel** ("Text and page", the name on its menu item, its title on a phone and its name to screen readers; the Aa panel), as in Apple Books: **Text and page** in More (…) opens it, under More (on a phone, a sheet along the bottom of the screen, with a Close button). One quiet line per setting, its name in the one label at the left (decisions 0123, 0244). From the top: text size as a stepper: one flat capsule, a small A with a minus and a large A with a plus at its ends (the same six steps as `+` and `-`) and the current step's name between them, parted by hairlines and nothing raised, so it never reads as a choice with something chosen (decision 0055); at either end the A that can't go further fades to the muted grey; 44px tall to touch; the page as four swatches, each the page it gives (White, Paper, Night, and half White, half Night for Match device), its name written under it, the chosen one ringed and its name semibold (decision 0176); and the reading font, each name written in its own face, as plain words, the chosen one semibold and underlined. There's no line width: every article has the same one (decision 0187). Every change applies at once to the article behind it. Text size and font live only here (decision 0241); Page is in Settings › Look and feel too (section 18). It isn't modal: the article stays usable. The keyboard starts on its first control; the choices are real buttons and radio buttons; Escape closes it and puts the keyboard back on More; a press outside it, or Tab leaving it, closes it too.
- A post with no title (decision 0196) opens without one: the byline leads and the words follow, never a big "Untitled". Screen readers still hear a heading, its opening words, which the top bar also shows once scrolled (decision 0312).
- The title is centred, as a book opens a chapter, in Literata's display cut at regular weight, its lines balanced so a long title wraps into even lines; it is **sized by its length** (decision 0294): a short title (under 45 characters, Chinese, Japanese and Korean ones counting double) at 3.2 times the text (64px at the default size; 2.5 times on a phone), tight; a medium one (under 85) at 2.5 times (2 on a phone); a long one at 2 times (1.6 on a phone) with looser lines, so a hundred-letter title never fills the first screen. From 48rem up it reaches a little past the text column on each side (at most 4rem). Headings inside the article speak in the title's voice (decision 0295): on the text's left edge, in the title's face at regular weight, stepping down by size alone (about 31px, 24px on a phone, then 24px, then the text's size in semibold), with two or more times the room above them as below. A quotation is upright, in the text's full ink and size, set off by a 2px line in the strong tone at its start (on the right in a right-to-left article), as the quotes under "Your highlights" are; a writer's own quotation marks are kept as they are (decision 0296). A **pull quote**, a line the writer repeats big (marked by Substack, WordPress or Ghost), is centred in the title's face at 1.4 times the text (1.25 on a phone, a step under a heading), between two short rules like the byline's, its named source under it small and grey (decision 0297).
- The source is always credited and linked, under the title, centred, as on a book's title page (decision 0294; the pause is seven-tenths of the title's size): their picture, a small circle, then **"{feed's name}"** alone in the reading face, at the interface's size (15px, or 17px on a touch screen), in full ink and regular weight; when the feed names an author besides, **"{author}"** on a line of its own and under it **"*in* {feed's name}"**, each line centred, the "in" in italic and the second ink, so the head stays even whatever the names' lengths and the publication is never taken for a second writer (decision 0325, replacing decision 0323's "{author} / {feed's name}", which replaced "by {author} in {feed's name}"); screen readers hear "by {author} in {feed's name}", the "by" there but unseen; then the day on a line of its own, 12px, in the list's faint grey, so the name leads; a short centred rule closes the block. The feed's name is a link (for someone followed, one that opens their list, everything of theirs, newest first, section 36, with "‹ Article" at its top back to the article, tooltip "Everything by {name}" (decision 0289; their page is a press on from there, at the top of their list, or **Info about {name}** in More, or `i`), underlined, 44px tall to touch without making its line taller; decision 0214. A link, not a button, so a long name wraps as the words around it do and the line reads as one sentence; Command-click or a middle click opens their list in a new tab; decision 0260. Their website is a press away there, and View original and "Read the rest on {publisher}" link the publisher's own pages here; otherwise their website, or plain words when there's none). The author is named only when it isn't the name already shown ("Hal Brennan", never "Hal Brennan" over "*in* Hal Brennan"). The picture is the one from section 36, "Their picture" (decision 0285).
- Once loaded, an article ends rather than stops: a short centred line after its last paragraph (for a summary-only article, after the "Read the rest on {publisher}" note), with room below so the last lines can be read at eye level. Under it, for someone you follow, **Write back** (section 37), with the same room above it as under it (decision 0301). For someone who could be either (section 36, decision 0264), in its place, once: "Is {name} someone you might write back to?" with two buttons, **Yes, a person** and **No, a publication** (44px, wrapping on a phone), a group named by the question. Yes makes them a person, and Write back takes the question's place with the keyboard on it; No settles them as a publication and the question goes. Either says so in the reading area's note, with Undo. It stays quietly at the end of their articles until answered, then never again; it's never a pop-up, never in the toolbar, and never printed.
- In the list, `j` / `k` and the arrow keys choose a row; a click or `Enter` opens its article, alone in the window (section 4, decision 0275): the keyboard's place moves into the article, and `j` / `k` then move from article to article; Escape, or the back button showing the list's name, goes back to the list. `j` stops at the last item listed; older items load when the bottom of the list comes into view, as with scrolling.
- `v` opens the original in a new tab. `+` / `-` change the text size (the same setting as in the Aa panel). Space and `Shift+Space` move the open article down and up a screen, stopping at its end (section 19).
- Links inside articles open in a new tab (links to a place inside the article, like footnotes, stay in it).
- A footnote's number opens its note where you are (decision 0247): a small card just under the line, the column's width, in the text's face a step smaller, with the number on its left and × on its right. It's a dialog named "Footnote 2"; the keyboard goes into it, and Escape or × closes it and gives the keyboard back to the number; a press anywhere else, or Tab past it, closes it too. It appears and goes with no movement. The notes stay at the article's end as the writer put them, and a note's own way back (↩), or any other link to a place in the article, still moves there. A footnote is known by its shape, as the cleaning keeps no class names on them: a link to another place in the same article that is a superscript, holds one, or is only a short number or mark; its note is that place, or the paragraph or item around it when the place is only a word or the note's own number. The card's note is set as the article's text is (links underlined, code and keys as in the text), and leaves out the note's way back and the number the note starts with. The card opens in view, moving the page no more than it must; it closes when the column's width changes (a new text size, a resized window) or another article opens.
- An English article's typewriter marks are set as a typesetter would when it's shown (decision 0247): straight quotes become curly ones facing the right way (“ ” ‘ ’; a quote that starts a paragraph, item or line opens), an apostrophe curls (it’s, the ’90s), two hyphens become a dash (—) and three dots an ellipsis (…); three or more hyphens, or four or more dots, are left alone. Code, keys and preformatted text are never touched. An article in another language, or one whose feed names one, keeps its marks as sent, since its quotes are its own (« », „ “). Only what's shown changes: the stored article, search, and what an assistant reads of the article keep the writer's own characters. A highlight keeps its words as they were shown, so one made now holds the curly marks (an assistant reading notes sees them so), and a highlight is found whichever way its marks were kept.
- On a wide window the article sits in the middle of the reading area, like a page in a book, while its actions stand over the text's right edge (decision 0277). On a computer at least 64px of paper stays beside the text on each side, as Apple Books keeps on a Mac; in a narrow pane the line gets shorter rather than the margins thinner (decision 0044). On a phone the text keeps the one 20px margin.
- Body text wraps normally; only the article's last paragraph avoids a lone last word, so no line is pulled visibly short (decision 0040).
- With no article open there is no reading area: the list has the window beside the sidebar (section 4, decision 0275), so the empty reading area's quiet line, "Time to read something beautiful." (decision 0064), and its one-time tip (decision 0062) are gone with it.
- Images load from the publisher's site, each only as you reach it in the text, so an article with many pictures opens quickly and nothing further down is loaded until you scroll near it.
- **Reading here tells no one more than it must** (decision 0112):
  - Invisible 1×1 "tracking pixels" are removed (any image sized 0 or 1 pixel), so they're never loaded.
  - Pictures from known advertising, analytics and tracking services (a short list nine turtles keeps, such as Google Analytics, DoubleClick, Facebook's pixel, WordPress's and Mailchimp's counters and FeedBurner's) are removed too; the same site's real pictures stay.
  - Links lose their tracking tags (`utm_…`, `fbclid`, `gclid`, `mc_cid` and similar well-known ones), both the article's own link (View original, Copy link and Share) and the links in its text. Any other part of a link stays exactly as the publisher wrote it, so it still leads to the same page.
  - When you open a link or a picture loads, the publisher isn't told it came from nine turtles (no "referrer" is sent).
  - A YouTube video plays from YouTube's privacy-enhanced address (youtube-nocookie.com), and only once you press Play.
- On Night, pictures are dimmed a little (never inverted) so a pale one doesn't glare on the dark page; a linked picture shows at full brightness under the pointer or the keyboard. A caption is in the interface's small type, centred under its picture and no wider than the text, 12px under it, in the second ink, which keeps 7 : 1 on the page in every theme (decision 0298, replacing 0053's serif). A table starts flush with the text's left edge, its header semibold over a fine rule and its rows over fainter ones (decisions 0050, 0053), its digits all one width so numbers line up. A writer's highlighting is a soft tone of the ink, keys are small key caps, a definition's term is semibold with its meaning indented under it, and a footnote's number has room to press, underlined as every link in an article is; a list of footnotes is a step smaller, in the second ink, and its ↩ is never drawn as an emoji. Bold inside italics is the font's own bold italic. Line spacing eases as the text gets larger: 1.6 at the smallest size, 1.5 at the two largest, and never under 1.5, on a phone too (decision 0246).
- **Pauses get room** (decision 0298). Space comes in three steps: paragraphs, then 1.75em around a picture, a quotation, code or a table, then 2.5em around a section break. A section break is the short centred rule of the byline and the end, never a full-width line, and a line that's only a run of dashes, stars or the like ("------", "* * *") is drawn the same (its marks stay in the text, hidden). Under the writer's last words (or last picture, or words a feed sent with no paragraph around them, decision 0306) a small ◆ in the second ink ends the article, on a line of its own, centred (decision 0302), and stands for the closing line, so the article ends once (decision 0300); never on a summary or a video's description, never after code or a table, and never copied, highlighted or read aloud. On a computer, pictures and code may use up to 5rem past the text on each side, as far as the pane has room, and a picture never grows past its own size. Code keeps each line whole: it never wraps, sits on a soft tint, and where a line runs past an edge a faint shadow at that edge says it scrolls.
- **A picture can be opened larger** (decision 0107). A picture the page shows clearly smaller than it really is (at least a fifth and 40px bigger in width or height), once it has loaded, and not already a link, takes the pointer's hand cursor and is a button for the keyboard (Tab reaches it) and for screen readers, which hear "Open picture larger: {its alt text}". A picture shown at its full size, or inside a link, stays exactly as the publisher's picture. Whether a picture qualifies is looked at again whenever it loads or changes size: a wider or narrower window, a larger or smaller text size, another line width. A press, a tap, Enter or Space opens it alone on a plain card over the dimmed page (the dim of a sheet), as large as fits with a margin all round but never stretched past its own size, its alt text under it as a caption in the second ink (if it has one), a quiet underlined "Open in a new tab" to the picture's own address, and an X at the top right (44px, tooltip "Close (Esc)"). Escape, the X or a press on the dim closes it, and the keyboard is back on the picture (or on the article's title, if a change of window size means it no longer opens larger). The opened picture is read in the article's language, as the article is. It appears and goes at once, with no motion, and a phone's pinch-zoom works on it. By night the picture in the article keeps its slight dimming (full brightness under the pointer or the keyboard, as a linked picture does), and the opened picture shows at full brightness, since the reader asked to look at it. A picture that hasn't loaded (offline, or the publisher's site can't be reached) isn't offered.
- Anything unsafe in the feed's content (scripts, embedded frames, code hidden in styles) is removed before display. A YouTube player embedded in an article leaves a link in its place, "Watch the video on YouTube". Any other embedded frame (a map, a Vimeo video, a CodePen) leaves a quiet line in the interface's small type, "Something from openstreetmap.org is embedded here. Open it" with the open arrow, its link opening the frame's own address in a new tab (said aloud, 44px to press), so "Here's the map:" never points at nothing; an invisible frame or a known tracker's leaves nothing (decision 0260; articles that arrived before keep what they had).
- **What didn't arrive says so** (decision 0260). A picture that fails to load becomes the same quiet line as a picture in an offline copy: "A picture didn't load: *The harbour at dawn*." (its description, when the writer gave one) and "See it on the original" with the open arrow (a new tab, said aloud, 44px to press), never the browser's broken-picture sign. Pictures with no description that didn't load one after another, with nothing between, share one line: "11 pictures didn't load." and "See them on the original" (decision 0318); one with a description keeps its own line. A table wider than the text column scrolls sideways, as it always has, with a thin bar where the system shows one, and a quiet line under it: "Scroll the table sideways to see all 14 columns." ("Swipe" on a touch screen); the keyboard can reach it (Tab) to scroll it with the arrow keys. A table that fits has neither. A table that only lays the article out (one holding or held in another table, or of one column, with no headings or caption, as a feed carrying its email's HTML has) is read by a screen reader for its words, not as a table of data, and drawn as the plain blocks it holds, one under another, never wide (decision 0319).
- **YouTube videos play in YouTube's own player** (decision 0072). An article whose link is a YouTube video (from a channel's feed) shows, under the byline, a box the shape of the video (16 : 9; a Short's stands up) drawn in ink on the page, with only a round Play mark and "Play video" (or "Resume from 4:00") (decision 0324); no picture of the video, and no title in it (the page's own is just above); its Chapters and Description, folded under it, picking up where you stopped and closing at the end are in section 38. Until it's pressed nothing is loaded from YouTube. Pressing it (a click, or Enter or Space) puts YouTube's privacy-enhanced player in the box's place, at the same size, playing; the keyboard moves into the player, and Tab leaves it. Screen readers hear "Play video: {title}" and, once it plays, "YouTube video: {title}". The video's description from the feed is its text, shown while Description is open under the box (section 38); a video with none is just the video, ending with the closing rule.
- **Podcast episodes play in the browser's own audio player,** from the publisher's own file (decision 0113): a calm player panel sits under the byline, and a Now playing bar at the foot of the app while one plays (section 35).
- **Printing an article prints the article** (decision 0236). With an article open, the browser's Print (Command P, Ctrl P) prints it alone, black on white in every theme, as a page of a book: its title, byline, words, pictures and the reader's highlights, and at its end a credit line, "From Reading Room, by Asha Rao, 20 Oct 2026. The original: https://…", so the paper says where it came from. Nothing of the app's own is printed: no sidebar, list, toolbar, buttons, players or Write back. With no article open, the page prints as it looks. The credit line is only on paper.
- The next article is loaded in advance so moving to it with `j` is instant.
- **Your place is kept, like a bookmark** (decision 0060), and its row in the list says how far while it is (section 6, decision 0224). Leave a long article part of the way through and it opens where you stopped next time, even after closing nine turtles. Only your own scrolling moves the place; it is forgotten once you reach the end (a finished article opens at its top again) or scroll back to the top. It's kept by paragraph rather than by pixels, so it holds when pictures load late or the text size changes, and it jumps there at once, never gliding. Each device keeps its own places (in the browser's storage, for the 200 most recent articles).
- **The same opening in every list** (decision 0220, replacing 0210's Start reading). When nothing is open and there's nothing to pick up (below), the list starts with its first row, whatever the list and whether its first article is read or not. The newest article waits in the list as every other does: no label, no larger title, no opening paragraph and no Start reading, on a phone or a computer. Choosing its row opens it, one press, and only that marks it read.
- **Pick up where you left off** (decision 0175). The one article you last left part of the way through is also remembered for you on the server, with its place, so every device you sign in on can offer it. When you come back and nothing is open, a quiet line at the top of the first list you see offers it: "You were reading *The long walk home*, by Ana.", in the same muted serif, the title a link underlined in the text color (never the accent). The name is the one you gave the person (section 36), else the article's own author; with neither, the title alone ("You were reading *The long walk home*."). Pressing the title opens the article in its feed's list, at the place you stopped, even on a device that never had it open. Small to the eye, the title is 44px tall to touch. The line is never a pop-up, a count or a notification, and it goes once you open another article (or this one), finish it or scroll it back to its top; opening another article also lets that place go for every device (each device's own bookmark for it stays). An article whose feed you've left, or that you've hidden, isn't offered. **Only when you come back, only on that first list** (decision 0292): coming back means opening nine turtles, or returning to it, after half an hour or more away (each device writes down when you were last there, so a refresh or a quick look at another app isn't coming back; a device you've never used counts). The line shows on the first list you see then, whichever list it is, only when that list is the first screen (from a phone's feeds screen, the list you choose), and goes once you go to another list or page; it doesn't come back until you've been away again.
- While an article's text is on its way (it wasn't fetched ahead, and the connection is slow), after half a second one muted line in the reading face under the byline says "Loading the article…"; the text takes its place (decision 0230). No spinner.
- If an article's text can't be loaded (and nothing is saved on this device), the reading area says "This article couldn't be loaded." (announced to screen readers) with a **Try again** button, which asks for it again and keeps the keyboard on the article's title. If it failed because the reader's sign-in has ended, it says "You're signed out, so the article couldn't be loaded." with **Sign in** instead, never a Try again that can't work (decision 0312).

**How to check it**
1. Open an article from a full-content blog: it reads well, with headings, quotes and images in place.
2. Open one from a summary-only feed: you see the summary, ending with a dark button, "Read the rest on {the publisher's name} ↗", which opens the publisher's page in a new tab.
3. Check that links in the text are underlined and not blue, on every page.
4. Press `j` repeatedly: each article appears with no visible wait.
5. Press `Enter`, read, press Escape: you're back on the same row in the list. Press `+` and `-`: the text grows and shrinks.
6. Choose More › Text and page above an article: the panel opens under More. Press the large A, choose Night, and Atkinson Hyperlegible: the article changes at once. Choose Paper: the page turns warm cream, its links still the text's color. Each swatch has its name under it: White, Paper, Night, Match device. There is no Theme row. Press Escape: the panel closes and the keyboard is back on More. Settings › Look and feel shows the same choices.
7. Make the window very wide: ‹ and View original ↗ stay above the text's left edge, Save and More above its right edge. Scroll an article on a computer: the bar stays, its buttons don't move, and once the title has gone under it the title, in its own typeface, rises into the bar's middle as you scroll, exactly over the middle of the text, over a hairline. More starts with Copy link. Read to the end: the ◆ sits on its own centred line, with Write back under it and no Save or View original. Press `c`: "Link copied." floats at the pane's foot.
8. Scroll halfway down a long article, open another, then come back (or reload the page): it opens where you stopped. Read it to the end, open another and come back: it opens at its top.
8a. Scroll halfway down a long article and reload the page: with nothing open, the top of the list says "You were reading {title}, by {writer}." Press the title: it opens where you stopped. Sign in on a phone: the same line sits at the top of the list there too, and opens it at the same place. Open a different article instead and reload: the line is gone, on every device.
8c. With an article to pick up, go to another list (a feed, Saved): the line is gone there and doesn't come back on the first list either. Reload: still gone. Come back after half an hour away (or set this device's `nine-turtles.here` back by half an hour): the line is on the first list you see.
8b. With nothing to pick up, open Unread with nothing open: the list's first row looks like every other, as in All articles, on a phone too. Choose it: the article opens, and only now counts as read.
9. Open an article with a large picture (a photo from a news site or a blog usually is): the pointer becomes a hand over it. Click it: it opens alone on the dimmed page, as large as fits, its description under it, with an X and "Open in a new tab". Press Escape: it closes and the keyboard's outline is on the picture. Press Enter: it opens again; click the dim around it: it closes. A small picture, or one that's a link, does nothing new.
10. Follow a YouTube channel (paste its page address, e.g. youtube.com/@name, into + Add) and open a video: a box drawn in ink with Play shows, with Chapters and Description closed under it. Press it: the video plays in YouTube's own player, in the same place. Open the next video: it waits for Play again.
11. Open an article whose link came with `?utm_source=…` (many newsletters' and news sites' feeds do this), press Share (Copy link) and paste it: the `utm_…` part is gone, and the link still opens the same article. Hover over a link in the text that had tracking tags: they're gone too.
12. Open a long article with many pictures on a slow connection: the text shows at once, and the pictures further down load as you scroll towards them.
13. Open an article from a WordPress blog that showed a tiny stats picture before (`pixel.wp.com` or `stats.wp.com`): with the browser's developer tools on the Network tab, no request goes to those addresses.
14. Open an article and print it (Command P): the preview shows only the article, black on white, ending with "From …, by …, 20 Oct 2026. The original: https://…". No sidebar, list, buttons or Write back (decision 0236).
15. Open an article by someone who could be either and read to its end: "Is … someone you might write back to?" Press **Yes, a person**: Write back is there instead, with the keyboard on it, and the note says so with Undo.
