# 7. Reading an article

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- The article opens inside nine turtles, using exactly what the publisher put in the feed, restyled consistently: headings, quotes, code, tables, footnotes and images.
- Reading font is Literata by default (Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and System serif, the device's own serif, as alternatives). Lines are about 65–75 characters long.
- Links inside articles are underlined in the normal text color, never blue, on every page (decision 0176).
- If the feed only has a summary, nine turtles shows the summary. It never fetches the rest from the website. Under the article's closing rule, a small centred note in upright interface type (14px, the second ink) says "Only the beginning is shared here." (the owner's core path, point 5), and under it, on a row of its own at least 44px tall to press, a link to the publisher: **"Read on {publisher} ›"**, named by the site's own title ("Read on Garden Letters", even when its writer is called Hal Brennan), or its address when it has none, ("Read the episode page on {publisher} ›" for a podcast's episode, section 35), opening the original in a new tab and the only underlined part (a fine, soft line). Each sentence stays whole on a line wherever it fits, so a narrow column breaks between them and never strands a word (with very large text on a small phone, a sentence wraps evenly rather than running off the screen); **View original** goes there too.
- The article's top bar runs the width of the reading pane, its icons anchored to the pane's edges (not the text column, so on a very wide window they never float in the middle), each named for screen readers and in its tooltip with its key. The icons sit together at the right edge, 4px apart, and the left stays empty, as in Apple Books' reading toolbar (decision 0050): **View original** (an arrow pointing out, with no box, tooltip "View original (V)"), always present and always in the same place, then **Aa** (the reading panel, below), **Star** (an outline star, filled once starred), **Write back** (a paper plane, tooltip "Write back (W)", for a person only, never a publication; section 37, decision 0214) and **More** (…, "More actions" for screen readers), a small menu like the list's with **Copy link** (its key, `c`; or Share, section 21), **Mark unread** (its key, `m`, shown beside it), **Add a note…** (section 42) and **Hide articles about…** (section 27). The name in the line under the title opens the page about them (below).
- The top bar is solid, in the page's own color, across the whole pane; the text scrolls below it and never shows through. A faint line shows under it only once the text has scrolled. The scrollbar's track is see-through, so its gutter is the page's own color in every theme.
- **The Aa panel** ("Reading appearance"), as in Apple Books: a small panel under the Aa button (on a phone, a sheet along the bottom of the screen, with a Close button). One quiet line per setting, its name in small spaced capitals at the left (decision 0123). From the top: text size as a stepper: one flat capsule, a small A with a minus and a large A with a plus at its ends (the same six steps as `+` and `-`) and the current step's name between them, parted by hairlines (section 18); the page as four swatches, each the page it gives (White, Paper, Night, and half White, half Night for Match device), its name written under it, the chosen one ringed and its name semibold (decision 0176); and the reading font, each name written in its own face, as plain words, the chosen one semibold and underlined. There's no line width: every article has the same one (decision 0187). Text size and Page are the same preferences as in Settings. Every change applies at once to the article behind it and is the same preference as in Settings › Look and feel (section 18). It isn't modal: the article stays usable. The keyboard starts on its first control; the choices are real buttons and radio buttons; Escape closes it and puts the keyboard back on Aa; a press outside it, or Tab leaving it, closes it too.
- The title is set large (about 32px at the default text size, 1.6 times the text), tight, its lines balanced so a long title wraps into even lines rather than a long line over a short one; headings inside the article are a clear step smaller (about 22px) with more room above them than below.
- The source is always credited and linked, in a quiet line about 20px under the title as in a book or magazine (the pause grows and shrinks with the text size) (14px, the second ink): the feed's name in full ink and semibold (for someone followed, a button that opens the page about them, section 36, as their name at the top of their list does, tooltip "Info about {name} (I)", underlined on hover, 44px tall to touch without making its line taller; decision 0214. Their website is a press away there, and View original and "Read on {publisher}" link the publisher's own pages here), the author if the feed names one (only when it isn't the name already shown: "Hal Brennan · 19 Oct", never "Hal Brennan · Hal Brennan"), and the time.
- Once loaded, an article ends rather than stops: a short centred line after its last paragraph (for a summary-only article, after the "Read on {publisher}" note), with room below so the last lines can be read at eye level. Under it, for someone you follow, **Write back to {their name}** (section 37).
- Choosing an article in the list (a click, `j` / `k`, or the arrow keys while in the list) shows it in the reading area. `Enter` opens it to read: the keyboard's place moves into the article, and `j` / `k` then move from article to article; Escape goes back to the list. On a phone-narrow window the article takes the list's place, with a back button showing the list's name. `j` stops at the last item listed; older items load when the bottom of the list comes into view, as with scrolling.
- `v` opens the original in a new tab. `+` / `-` change the text size (the same setting as in Settings). Space and `Shift+Space` move the open article down and up a screen, stopping at its end (section 19).
- Links inside articles open in a new tab (links to a place inside the article, like footnotes, stay in it).
- Footnote links move to the note inside the article (and back), without leaving nine turtles.
- On a wide window the article sits in the middle of the reading area, like a page in a book, while its actions keep to the pane's right edge. On a computer at least 56px of paper stays beside the text on each side, as Apple Books keeps on a Mac; in a narrow pane the line gets shorter rather than the margins thinner (decision 0044). On a phone the text keeps the one 20px margin.
- Body text wraps normally; only the article's last paragraph avoids a lone last word, so no line is pulled visibly short (decision 0040).
- With no article chosen, the reading area shows one quiet line in its middle, "Time to read something beautiful." (decision 0064; it replaced "No article open"), in the serif at 20px on a 32px line and in muted grey (never the accent, no picture), at the optical middle: a little above half way, about 40% down (decision 0142). When the list has something to open, screen readers also hear "Choose one from the list, or press j." (on touch screens just "Choose one from the list.").
- Images load from the publisher's site, each only as you reach it in the text, so an article with many pictures opens quickly and nothing further down is loaded until you scroll near it.
- **Reading here tells no one more than it must** (decision 0112):
  - Invisible 1×1 "tracking pixels" are removed (any image sized 0 or 1 pixel), so they're never loaded.
  - Pictures from known advertising, analytics and tracking services (a short list nine turtles keeps, such as Google Analytics, DoubleClick, Facebook's pixel, WordPress's and Mailchimp's counters and FeedBurner's) are removed too; the same site's real pictures stay.
  - Links lose their tracking tags (`utm_…`, `fbclid`, `gclid`, `mc_cid` and similar well-known ones), both the article's own link (View original, Copy link and Share) and the links in its text. Any other part of a link stays exactly as the publisher wrote it, so it still leads to the same page.
  - When you open a link or a picture loads, the publisher isn't told it came from nine turtles (no "referrer" is sent).
  - A YouTube video plays from YouTube's privacy-enhanced address (youtube-nocookie.com), and only once you press Play.
- On Night, pictures are dimmed a little (never inverted) so a pale one doesn't glare on the dark page; a linked picture shows at full brightness under the pointer or the keyboard. A caption is clearly smaller than the text (13/16 of its size, with a 1.4 line height), 6px under its picture, in the second ink, which keeps 7 : 1 on the page in every theme. A table starts flush with the text's left edge, its header semibold over a fine rule and its rows over fainter ones (decisions 0050, 0053).
- **A picture can be opened larger** (decision 0107). A picture the page shows clearly smaller than it really is (at least a fifth and 40px bigger in width or height), once it has loaded, and not already a link, takes the pointer's hand cursor and is a button for the keyboard (Tab reaches it) and for screen readers, which hear "Open picture larger: {its alt text}". A picture shown at its full size, or inside a link, stays exactly as the publisher's picture. Whether a picture qualifies is looked at again whenever it loads or changes size: a wider or narrower window, a larger or smaller text size, another line width. A press, a tap, Enter or Space opens it alone on a plain card over the dimmed page (the dim of a sheet), as large as fits with a margin all round but never stretched past its own size, its alt text under it as a caption in the second ink (if it has one), a quiet underlined "Open in new tab" to the picture's own address, and an X at the top right (44px, tooltip "Close (Esc)"). Escape, the X or a press on the dim closes it, and the keyboard is back on the picture (or on the article's title, if a change of window size means it no longer opens larger). The opened picture is read in the article's language, as the article is. It appears and goes at once, with no motion, and a phone's pinch-zoom works on it. By night the picture in the article keeps its slight dimming (full brightness under the pointer or the keyboard, as a linked picture does), and the opened picture shows at full brightness, since the reader asked to look at it. A picture that hasn't loaded (offline, or the publisher's site can't be reached) isn't offered.
- Anything unsafe in the feed's content (scripts, embedded frames, code hidden in styles) is removed before display. A YouTube player embedded in an article leaves a link in its place, "Watch the video on YouTube".
- **YouTube videos play in YouTube's own player** (decision 0072). An article whose link is a YouTube video (from a channel's feed) shows, under the byline, a quiet box the shape of the video (16 : 9; a Short's stands up) in a faint tone of the text, with the video's title set large in it, a round Play mark and "Play video" (or "Resume from 4:00"), and no line under it (the owner removed it, 25 October 2026); its chapters, a folded long description, picking up where you stopped and closing at the end are in section 38. Until it's pressed nothing is loaded from YouTube. Pressing it (a click, or Enter or Space) puts YouTube's privacy-enhanced player in the box's place, at the same size, playing; the keyboard moves into the player, and Tab leaves it. Screen readers hear "Play video: {title}" and, once it plays, "YouTube video: {title}". The video's description from the feed is its text; a video with none is just the video, ending with the closing rule.
- **Podcast episodes play in the browser's own audio player,** from the publisher's own file (decision 0113): a calm player panel sits under the byline, and a Now playing bar at the foot of the app while one plays (section 35).
- The next article is loaded in advance so moving to it with `j` is instant.
- **Your place is kept, like a bookmark** (decision 0060). Leave a long article part of the way through and it opens where you stopped next time, even after closing nine turtles. Only your own scrolling moves the place; it is forgotten once you reach the end (a finished article opens at its top again) or scroll back to the top. It's kept by paragraph rather than by pixels, so it holds when pictures load late or the text size changes, and it jumps there at once, never gliding. Each device keeps its own places (in the browser's storage, for the 200 most recent articles).
- **Start reading** (decision 0210). When nothing is open and there's nothing to pick up (below), and the list starts with an unread article, the reading area has already begun it instead of the quiet line: in the article's own column, its title exactly where the article's title will be, so opening it moves nothing (decision 0218); a small muted line ("Newest unread", or "Oldest unread" with Oldest first), its title and byline set as the article sets them, its first paragraph in the reading face (cut after six lines; a summary's first paragraph too, never two run together, decision 0216), then **Start reading**, the one solid button, which opens it as choosing its row does. Fetching it to show this marks nothing read. Not beside search results, nor with Unread off (section 10). With no reading area beside the list (a phone), the list's first row is begun instead: "Newest unread" above its title, the title larger, and under its line the first paragraph (four lines; an untitled post, already showing its opening in the title's place, skips it) and the look of Start reading, not a button of its own: the whole row opens it, as every row does, so it stays one row for the keyboard, and screen readers hear "Newest unread" and the row as before. It goes once the article is read.
- **Pick up where you left off** (decision 0175). The one article you last left part of the way through is also remembered for you on the server, with its place, so every device you sign in on can offer it. When you come back and nothing is open, the empty reading area's quiet line offers it instead of "Time to read something beautiful.": "You were reading *The long walk home*, by Ana.", in the same muted serif, the title a link underlined in the text color (never the accent). The name is the one you gave the person (section 36), else the article's own author; with neither, the title alone ("You were reading *The long walk home*."). With no reading area beside the list (a phone), the same line sits at the top of the list. Pressing the title opens the article in its feed's list, at the place you stopped, even on a device that never had it open. Small to the eye, the title is 44px tall to touch. The line is never a pop-up, a count or a notification, and it goes once you open another article (or this one), finish it or scroll it back to its top; opening another article also lets that place go for every device (each device's own bookmark for it stays). An article whose feed you've left, or that you've hidden, isn't offered.
- If an article's text can't be loaded (and nothing is saved on this device), the reading area says "This article couldn't be loaded." (announced to screen readers) with a **Try again** button, which asks for it again and keeps the keyboard on the article's title.

**How to check it**
1. Open an article from a full-content blog: it reads well, with headings, quotes and images in place.
2. Open one from a summary-only feed: you see the summary, ending with "Read on {the publisher's name} ›" on a row of its own, which opens the publisher's page in a new tab.
3. Check that links in the text are underlined and not blue, on every page.
4. Press `j` repeatedly: each article appears with no visible wait.
5. Press `Enter`, read, press Escape: you're back on the same row in the list. Press `+` and `-`: the text grows and shrinks.
6. Press Aa above an article: the panel opens under it. Press the large A, choose Night, and Atkinson Hyperlegible: the article changes at once. Choose Paper: the page turns warm cream, its links still the text's color. Each swatch has its name under it: White, Paper, Night, Match device. There is no Theme row. Press Escape: the panel closes and the keyboard is back on Aa. Settings › Look and feel shows the same choices.
7. Make the window very wide: View original, Aa, Star, Write back and More stay together at the reading pane's right edge. Scroll an article: the text fades under the top bar.
8. Scroll halfway down a long article, open another, then come back (or reload the page): it opens where you stopped. Read it to the end, open another and come back: it opens at its top.
8a. Scroll halfway down a long article and reload the page: with nothing open, the reading area says "You were reading {title}, by {writer}." Press the title: it opens where you stopped. Sign in on a phone: the same line sits at the top of the list, and opens it at the same place. Open a different article instead and reload: the line is gone, on every device.
8b. With nothing to pick up, open Unread with nothing open: the reading area has begun the newest unread article (its title, byline and first paragraph) under "Newest unread", with Start reading. Press it: the article opens, and only now counts as read. Search for something: the quiet line is back. On a phone, the list's first row is begun the same way.
9. Open an article with a large picture (a photo from a news site or a blog usually is): the pointer becomes a hand over it. Click it: it opens alone on the dimmed page, as large as fits, its description under it, with an X and "Open in new tab". Press Escape: it closes and the keyboard's outline is on the picture. Press Enter: it opens again; click the dim around it: it closes. A small picture, or one that's a link, does nothing new.
10. Follow a YouTube channel (paste its page address, e.g. youtube.com/@name, into + Add) and open a video: a grey box with Play shows above the description. Press it: the video plays in YouTube's own player, in the same place. Open the next video: it waits for Play again.
11. Open an article whose link came with `?utm_source=…` (many newsletters' and news sites' feeds do this), choose Copy link and paste it: the `utm_…` part is gone, and the link still opens the same article. Hover over a link in the text that had tracking tags: they're gone too.
12. Open a long article with many pictures on a slow connection: the text shows at once, and the pictures further down load as you scroll towards them.
13. Open an article from a WordPress blog that showed a tiny stats picture before (`pixel.wp.com` or `stats.wp.com`): with the browser's developer tools on the Network tab, no request goes to those addresses.
