# 20. Phones and installing the app

*Part of the spec ([index](../SPEC.md)). `docs/DESIGN.md` takes precedence.*

**What it does**
- One column you move through: **Feeds → Items → Article**. The feeds list is the home screen; no hamburger menu. While you follow no one, nine turtles opens on the welcome instead (section 2), one step on from the home screen, so Back goes there (decision 0137). That's only how it opens: once you've moved, even if your feeds arrive a moment later, you stay where you went.
- Top bar: a back arrow labeled with where it goes (e.g. "‹ Tech", drawn as a small arrow icon; screen readers hear "Back to Tech") and the screen title. More beside a list's title; a search icon on the feeds and items screens (with search, Milestone 10).
  - The feeds screen: the large "nine turtles" wordmark alone, with no Refresh beside it: Unread's dot catches up each time you arrive on this screen (section 13); **+ Add** and **Settings** sit together at the bottom, as on a computer. It's the root of the screens, as on an iPhone: no row is marked as selected, and every row (Unread, Saved, Notes, All articles, Everyone you follow, folders, feeds) ends in a small chevron, after its count, because it leads on to a screen of its own.
  - A list: "‹ Home" (screen readers hear "Back to Home"; it was "‹ Feeds" until decision 0217: "feed" is the system's word, and that screen is home), or "‹ Article" ("Back to the article") on someone's list opened from an article's byline, back to that article (section 36, decision 0289), then the list's name (one line, ending in "…" if long) with **Search** (a magnifier) and **More** (…) beside it, More holding Mark all as read, Refresh, the sort order and Edit (section 6). The top bar never scrolls. The search field waits behind the Search button, so the first article sits right under the title (decision 0060): a tap shows the field with the keyboard in it, and it stays until the button is tapped again with nothing searched, Escape, or going to another place (never on its own, so nothing moves under a finger); while a search is on it always shows.
  - An article: the back button with the list's name in words ("‹ Tech"; screen readers hear "Back to Tech"; a long name ends in "…") at the left, and **View original** as an icon at the right, on the same solid bar as on a computer (section 7).
  - Add a person or publication: a page sheet with its title and an × that goes back to the feeds screen, no back arrow (decisions 0052, 0055).
  - The back button sits in the same place on every screen, its arrow the same size, on the screen's 20px edge (decision 0244), so it never jumps as you move between screens.
- One side margin on every phone screen: names, titles and text start 20px from the screen's edge (on the feeds screen, the wordmark, the section names and the icons of Unread, Saved, Notes, All articles and Everyone you follow). A list keeps a gutter for its unread dot, as Books and Mail do on iOS: its title, search field and article titles start 28px in, and the dot sits 10px from the edge (decision 0054).
- The system back gesture and back button go back one step, the same as the back arrow. Opening nine turtles' front page on a phone shows the feeds; an address that names a list opens that list, and its back arrow still leads to the feeds.
- "Settings" sits at the bottom of the feeds screen, beside "+ Add".
- **Swipes** (decision 0274), **swiped right, you go back**; in an article, **pulled up past its end, the next one; pulled down past its top, the previous one**; a row swiped left gets what its menu does. Each swipe is only a shortcut for a button that's on the screen, which stays. Only a finger counts, never a mouse or pen; a stroke more up and down than sideways is a scroll. The screen (or row) follows the finger, and when it lifts, finishes the stroke in a short glide of about a third of a second: far enough, the screen slides the rest of the way off over the one it goes to; not far enough, it springs back. A pull moves the article with the finger, the next (or previous) one hung just beyond it; far enough, letting go carries straight on into it. With the phone set to reduce motion, there's no glide: it lands at once (DESIGN.md → Exceptions log). A swipe counts once it's gone about a third of the width (at most 120px), or when it's a quick flick (at least 20px, faster than 600px a second); shorter and slower does nothing. Once it's far enough, what letting go does shows: the screen it goes back to, lying under the one being dragged, or the article a pull opens, hung beyond the one being read (or, where the screen behind isn't there to show, a quiet card saying where back goes), and sliding back takes it away. A swipe never starts in a field, a slider, a player, a table or code that scrolls sideways, an open sheet or menu (nor with a touch outside an open menu or sheet, which only closes it), after a hold long enough for a row's menu, or while words are chosen; nor while the page is zoomed in with two fingers, when a one-finger stroke moves about the page as usual. Swiping right to go back works only where one screen shows at a time (a phone or a narrow window), and only on that screen; beside other panes (a tablet held wide), each pane's own ‹ is pressed instead.
  - **Right, from anywhere on the screen, not only its edge: back**, exactly as the screen's own back button (it presses it): article → its list, list → Home (or, opened from an article's byline, → that article), Settings and every page with ‹ → where its ‹ says. The list or Home shows under the screen as it moves; a page whose back stays in the same place (a Settings page back to Settings) shows the card instead ("‹ Settings"). Home, with no back button, takes no swipe. The screen's left edge stays the phone's own back gesture in a browser; in nine turtles installed on the home screen, a stroke from the edge counts too, since iOS gives an installed web app no back gesture. The right edge is always the phone's.
  - **Left on a row: what its menu does.** The row follows the finger, and the room it opens shows the icon and word of what letting go does, in the second ink, then in the ink once it's far enough: a little way (a third) **Read** (or **Unread**), all the way (60%) **Save** (or **Unsave**). With Unread off, only Save. It's marked at once, with Undo in the list's note, and the row is back in place at once.
  - **In an article, a pull up past its end: the next article**; **a pull down past its top: the previous one**, as the toolbar's ↓ and ↑ (decision 0276: pulls only, so each way means one thing; a swipe left in an article does nothing). The browser's own pull to refresh is off (Chrome on Android), so a pull down at the top is always the previous article. During the pull the article follows the finger (the phone's own stretch at an end is off there), and the next article hangs just past its end (the previous one just past its top), coming into view with it: "Next" (or "Previous"), its title, and who wrote it (the writer, or the feed's name when the feed names none); once it's far enough (or a quick flick), "Let go for the next" (or "…the previous"), and letting go carries the article on a whole screen into it; short of that, it slides back. Where there's none, it says "That's the last one so far." / "That's the first article here." and slides back (decision 0280).
  - **Down on a sheet:** the Aa sheet and an opened picture close, as their × does, when swiped down from where their own scrolling is at the top.
- Article toolbar at the bottom, within thumb reach and clear of the phone's home indicator, solid in the page's color with a faint line along its top so the text ends at an edge: five quiet icons without words (six for a person: Write back's paper plane, decision 0214), each a 44px tap with its name for screen readers, in two groups by job and in order of use (decision 0225): what you do to this article first, the rarest furthest left, More (…) · share · (write back) · save (Share takes the place Aa had, opening the phone's own share menu, or copying the link where there's none, decision 0293), then a short faint upright hairline, then moving through the list at the right, where a right thumb rests: previous · next. Previous and Next are chevrons up and down, as the list runs, so Previous never looks like Back's left chevron (decision 0182). The icons always share the bar evenly: on the first article Previous (and on the last, Next) keeps its arrow in its place, faded, and does nothing; screen readers hear it as dimmed (decision 0047). More opens upwards and reads from the button outwards, so what's used most is nearest the finger: Text and page just above the button, then Mark unread, Add a note and, furthest away, Hide articles about… (decision 0243), each with its icon before its words (decision 0293); a computer's menus drop down in the same order, first item nearest. A long press on a row in a list opens its own menu (section 6). While an episode plays, the Now playing bar sits just above this toolbar (section 35). Text and page opens the reading panel (section 7) as a sheet along the bottom of the screen. Article text reads a little smaller than on a wide screen, so a line keeps about 40 characters.
- **The bars step aside** (decision 0242): in an article, one tap on the text (not on a link, button, highlight, picture or player, and not while words are chosen) puts the top bar and the bottom toolbar away, so only the writing shows; the text stays where it was on the screen. Another tap brings them back, as do Tab reaching them, opening another article, and scrolling to the article's end. Nothing hides them by itself and nothing slides: a phone's reading is short and its bars are used often, so they stay until you tap (decision 0263). Put away, they're still there for screen readers. The Aa sheet ends with the one line that tells of it: "Tap the text to hide the bars. Tap again to bring them back."
- **Tapping the top of an iPhone's screen** (the clock and battery) scrolls what's on screen back to its top: an open sheet, or else the list, article or page, at once, with nothing animating (decision 0237), as in every app there. The same on an iPad showing one screen at a time (a narrow window); a full-width iPad reads as a computer does and has no such tap here. iOS does this only for a page that scrolls itself, and nine turtles' screens scroll inside, so nine turtles gives the page one spare pixel above the app and rests one pixel down (the app still fills the screen exactly), and treats the page reaching its top as the tap. A move made with a finger on the screen, or while typing, never counts. Android has no such tap.
- Every tap target is at least 44 pixels.
- Can be added to the home screen on iPhone and Android; it then opens full-screen with its own icon: nine small turtles seen from the side, three rows of three with the middle row a little to the right, dark on white (decision 0067). The same turtles mark the browser tab, on white by day and on near-black by night.
- **Settings › Install the app on any device** (decisions 0151 and 0190), a page of its own, says plainly how, and how to follow the blog you're on. First one sentence: "Installed, nine turtles has its own icon and window, like any other app, with nothing to download from an app store. Sign in once on each device; what you follow and what you've read are the same everywhere." Then three tabs, a segmented control named "Device": **Computer**, **Android** and **iPhone & iPad**, the one for the device it's opened on already chosen, guessed from what the browser says about itself (on a phone they fill the width).
  - **This device's tab** is headed "On this iPhone" (or iPad, Android phone, "Mac, in Safari", "Windows computer, in Chrome", "Linux computer, in Firefox"…) and gives the numbered steps for that system and browser, with a line under them saying where nine turtles now is. Where the browser offers to install in one press (Chrome, Edge, Android), **Install nine turtles** sits above the steps and opens the browser's own dialog; once it's done the page says "Installed. Open nine turtles from its icon." Firefox installs only on Windows (Firefox 143 and later, with the web apps button in the address bar); on a Mac or Linux it gets a plain line instead of steps: open the page in Chrome or Edge (or Safari, on a Mac). On an iPhone or iPad any browser gets the Share steps. In the installed app itself, this tab says "You're using the installed app now." instead of the steps.
  - **Another tab** is headed "On a computer", "On an Android phone" or "On an iPhone or iPad" and gives that device's steps for any browser (on a computer, one unnumbered line per browser, since they are alternatives: Chrome or Edge, Safari on a Mac, Firefox on Windows), so a reader on a Mac still sees that their phone can have nine turtles.
  - **Follow the blog you're on**, under the steps in every tab: on Computer, "Drag this to your bookmarks bar, then press it on any blog." with **Add to nine turtles** at its right end (the bookmarklet, section 3; pressed here it only says "Drag it to your bookmarks bar: it works from there."), and under it "No bookmarks bar? Press Command Shift B. Can't drag? Paste the blog's address into + Add." (Ctrl Shift B off a Mac; the second sentence is the keyboard's way, since dragging needs a pointer); on Android, "Once nine turtles is installed, tap Share on any blog, then nine turtles."; on iPhone & iPad, "Copy the blog's address, then paste it into + Add.", or, once the Shortcut's iCloud link exists, "Get the Shortcut once, then on any blog tap Share › Add to nine turtles." with **Get the Shortcut** (Apple's page, in a new tab). Last: "nine turtles always asks before following anyone." The bookmark's raw code and a Copy button are never shown.
  - Nothing else ever offers installing: no banner, and Android's own install bar is held back.
- Without a connection, the app still opens: signed in, with the articles kept on the device for reading offline (section 32); on a device that keeps none, it says plainly that nine turtles can't be reached. The service worker keeps a copy of the app itself and its fonts, never of your articles: those are the app's own copies.
- **Sharing a page to nine turtles** (decisions 0104 and 0138; what happens next is in section 3, "Adding from anywhere"):
  - **Android:** once nine turtles is on the home screen, share any page and choose **nine turtles**. nine turtles asks "Follow <name>?"; press Follow.
  - **iPhone and iPad:** Safari doesn't let home-screen web apps appear in the share sheet (Apple doesn't support web share targets), so a ready-made Apple Shortcut, **Add to nine turtles**, does it (decision 0177). Settings › Install the app on any device, on its iPhone & iPad tab, has **Get the Shortcut**, which opens Apple's page to add it; then in Safari, on any page, tap Share and choose **Add to nine turtles**. The Shortcut opens `https://app.nineturtles.app/add?url=` with the page's address. The owner made it once on an iPhone; until its iCloud link is in the app, that tab says to copy the blog's address into + Add instead. How the Shortcut is made: [`docs/operations/apple-shortcut.md`](../operations/apple-shortcut.md).

**How to check it**
1. On your phone, open the app: you see your feeds. Tap a folder, a feed, an article; the back arrow says where it goes each time.
2. Swipe back from the screen edge: you go back one step. Swipe right from the middle of an article: the card says "‹" and the list's name, and letting go goes back to the list; again from the list, back to Home. On Home, a swipe right does nothing.
3. In an article, press Next and Previous at the bottom: they move through the list. On the first article, Previous shows a faded arrow in its usual place and does nothing; the bar has no gap.
4. Use "Add to Home Screen". Open it from the icon: no browser bars.
5. Open Settings › Install the app on any device on a phone and on a computer: each starts with the one sentence, then its own tab already chosen with its own numbered steps. Choose another tab: its steps for any browser show, and its way to follow a blog. In Chrome, press Install nine turtles: the browser asks, and the page then says it's installed. Open the installed app's Settings › Install the app on any device: it says you're using the installed app.
6. On Android, with nine turtles on the home screen, share a blog from the browser and choose nine turtles: "Follow <the blog's name>?" opens; nothing is followed until you press Follow. On an iPhone, press Get the Shortcut in Settings › Install the app on any device, add it, and share a blog from Safari to **Add to nine turtles**: the same happens.
7. On an iPhone, scroll well down a list or an article, then tap the clock at the top of the screen: it is back at its top at once. Tap again: nothing moves. Drag the top bar down a little: the list stays where it was.
8. In an article, tap the words once: both bars go and the text doesn't move. Tap again: they're back. Hide them and scroll to the end: they come back, with Next and Write back.
9. In a list, swipe a row left a little: "Read" shows beside it, and letting go marks it read, with Undo. Swipe another all the way: the word becomes "Save", and it's saved. A short, slow swipe does nothing; a short quick flick counts.
10. In an article, pull up past its end: the article moves with your finger and the next one comes up under it, named with its writer; pull further and it says "Let go for the next", and letting go carries straight on into it. A short pull slides back; a quick flick counts. Swipe left: nothing happens. Pull down past its top: the previous one. Open Aa and swipe it down: it closes.
